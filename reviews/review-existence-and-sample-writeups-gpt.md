# Cold review of the mean and sample exponents

## Existence of the mean exponent

VERDICT: CORRECT. The weighted branch comparison gives the continuous-time mean exponent and the Yule size bounds transfer it to discrete trees.

1. **Model and time change.** The revised construction determines the ring from the source active strictly before its point. It reassigns sources only after a processed ring or at locally finite deterministic boundaries. Conditional survival probabilities multiply across those boundaries. The first ring at size $`j`$ has an exponential waiting time of rate $`j`$. Its source is uniform and independent of the wait. Conditioning on the source and time leaves the mark uniform. This gives exactly the discrete attachment law. Since the sizes are deterministic along the jump index, the holding times are independent of the embedded chain. Their centred sum converges by square-summability of their variances, and their means have divergent sum, so there is no explosion.
2. **Eligible sites and planting.** When there are at least two cores, the core-tree degree sum bounds the number of branching vertices by the number of endpoints minus two. Isolated bare degree-two sites use two distinct edges apiece, so at least a quarter of the cores are eligible. The sole core of a star qualifies separately. In the planting script, silencing the two neighbours of the designated bare vertex fixes its degree up to its first ring. Silencing the planting site protects its tagged leaf. In the final units each requested receiver has an internal neighbour of bounded degree, and exterior births cannot enter the branch during the site's silence. The lower probability bound depends only on $`k,M`$, including when $`k=2`$. Distinct successful cuts point away from all old core vertices, so their far sides remain disjoint.
3. **Comparison.** Before the first discrepancy, each internal choice at the planted root has probability $`(D_v/(D_v+1))(1/D_v)=1/(D_v+1)`$. The unused choice hits the exterior parent. Its own rings face the opposite boundary test, whose forbidden marks have rate $`1/M`$. The reference tree is generated independently of those marks and the acceptance uniforms. Conditional on its whole history, all acceptances have probability $`\prod D_v/(D_v+1)`$. Every reference core on this joint success event counts in the actual tree. The scope of this inequality matters for interpreting the [exact counterexample](review-existence-counterexample-gpt.md).
4. **Good histories and the root.** Marked rings at the original vertices form a Poisson stream of rate $`k/M`$. As long as all original degrees are below $`M`$, each such ring adds to an original degree. Thus a failure to reach the threshold forces a bounded count in a stream of mean at least $`6s`$. The upper tails of the early and total ring counts and this lower tail give a failure probability of order $`e^{-4s}`$ at fixed parameters. Cauchy–Schwarz and the Yule second moment turn this into an $`O_k(e^{-s})`$ loss of mean. Assigning each good history to one original vertex and averaging the weighted contributions gives a deterministic root for the chosen seed and time.
5. **Iteration.** Conditioning at the first hit of size $`k`$ bounds $`F(s)`$ by $`k`$ plus the largest seed mean. For each eligible site, count all far sides at the preparation endpoint containing no old core. This count at the block endpoint is a functional of the tree path. A site-specific continuation supplies its conditional lower bound, even if two sites would request conflicting rankings. Disjointness of those cuts and linearity of expectation justify summing in the original process. The promotion intensity is at least one after the first birth, so $`F(s)\to\infty`$ and the subtractive constant is harmless. Iterate the block inequality at fixed $`s`$, then take a subsequence attaining the upper exponential rate, and finally send the loss $`\eta`$ to zero. This forces the upper and lower rates to agree.
6. **Discrete size.** At $`t=\log n`$, the probability of reaching size $`n`$ is bounded below uniformly. At $`t=(1-\alpha)\log n`$, the geometric tail bound and the size second moment give $`\mathbb E[N(t);N(t)>n]=o(1)`$. Independence of the size and jump chain makes these bounds apply to the nondecreasing sequence $`\mathbb E C_n`$. Sending $`\alpha`$ to zero gives the same exponent. See [the mean-exponent proof](../proofs/existence.md).

## Equality of the sample and mean exponents

VERDICT: CORRECT after revision. The preparation flags now have the stated exact probability, and the block iteration starts at an almost surely finite index where the core count is positive.

### Two corrections checked in the revised proof

- **Stage 4 of preparation, §2.3.** In unit $`j`$ the first draft required silence from every tagged source indexed by $`r\le j+1`$ except the source of the prescribed birth. The tagged family starts at zero. This event also silenced the auxiliary source $`\sigma_0^{(i)}`$, adding an independent factor $`e^{-1}`$ per unit. For $`k>2`$ its probability over the entire preparation was $`e^{-(k-2)}\hat p`$, contradicting the exact value in Lemma 2.2. I checked that the revised flag explicitly restricts the indices to $`1\le r\le j+1`$ and excludes the designated source $`z(j)`$. Exactly $`j`$ other branch clocks must now be silent. Including the base, the designated ring and mark, and the thinned newborn silence gives $`e^{-(j+3)}/d_j`$ per unit, as claimed. For $`k=2`$ the stage is empty.
- **First positive grid count, Proposition 4.1.** The first version called the event of a birth by a fixed deterministic grid time $`t_{j_0}`$ almost sure. Starting from the edge, its probability is $`1-e^{-2t_{j_0}}<1`$. On its complement the count is still zero and its logarithm cannot start the block iteration. I checked that the revised proof defines $`j_0`$ as the first grid index at or after the first birth. This index is finite almost surely, the count there is at least one, and $`j_0/J\to0`$ almost surely. The same lower rate follows. Both corrections preserve Theorem 2 and its stated exponent. See [the revised preparation and grid argument](../proofs/almost-sure-exponent.md).

### Proof steps

1. **Packing.** Suppressing the bare degree-two chains leaves a tree on at most $`2A`$ vertices, where $`A`$ counts the low-core-degree sites with a leaf. Each suppressed chain leaves at most two spare vertices after disjoint triples are selected. Thus $`C\le6A+3J`$ for $`J`$ triples, and the $`A+J`$ reserved sites give at least $`C/6`$ trials. Star centres are available when there is just one core. Different trials reserve distinct old clocks.
2. **Product law for preparation.** A fixed mark threshold at the sole third-stage site ring guarantees promotion because the leaves occupy the first positions. Exterior arrivals can change the identity of the selected leaf. Its future clock can be sampled from an independent process at selection, since the choice uses only its past. The newborn receives its own clock. Requiring silence through the end of the unit and thinning conditionally on the ring time gives the same success probability at each site. Each third-stage promotion cuts off a branch containing no initial core, even at a site whose earlier flags failed. The later scripts therefore have disjoint interiors, and a successful script has a silent boundary parent. This constructs independent retained flags given the block's initial tree.
3. **Lower contributions.** Each retained site takes an independent reference, acceptance uniforms and its own parent's marked clock. Successful tests isolate the branch for the entire growth period. Its reference core count is then a lower contribution to the actual count, even with arbitrary outcomes at failed sites. Each contribution's conditional law is the same for the chosen seed, root and time. The sum fits inside the endpoint count because successful branches are disjoint.
4. **Concentration and iteration.** For a fixed block, truncate the integrable contribution at a finite level retaining at least half its mean. The variance of the conditionally independent sum grows at most linearly in the number of sites, whereas the squared distance to the chosen threshold grows quadratically. Hence the conditional probability of a failed block tends to zero as $`C\to\infty`$. The divergent conditional sum of promotion probabilities ensures $`C\to\infty`$ almost surely. Centring the block-failure indicators gives bounded martingale differences, whose averages tend to zero. Monotonicity protects the accumulated gain in the vanishing fraction of failed blocks.
5. **Limits.** For positive $`\mu`$, choose the loss below $`\mu`$ and let the block length tend to infinity on a deterministic sequence. A countable intersection permits the final limit of vanishing losses. When $`\mu=0`$, one core suffices for the pathwise lower bound after the first birth. Markov's inequality at integer times and monotonicity give the upper bound. The size-hitting times divided by $`\log n`$ converge almost surely to one by summable centred variances, transferring the limit along each such path. See [the sample-exponent proof](../proofs/almost-sure-exponent.md).
