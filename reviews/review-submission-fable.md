## 1. Recommendation and top three issues

**Recommendation: major revision.**

On this read every proof step of both theorems holds, every finite check reproduces, and every number in the manuscript matches its source. **No step was found false, and no step was found that cannot be justified.** The mathematical content appears correct and the results are significant: existence of the growth exponent (which answers, on the logarithmic scale, Question 1 of Addario-Berry et al.), a two-sided bracket of width $`0.0015`$, and a rigorous separation from $`17/30`$.

The recommendation is not "minor" because of the following three issues.

1. **Neither the paper nor its supplement is self-contained at the standard of the target journals.** The sample-path half of Theorem 1.1 (Section 2.4) is a compressed sketch; its rigorous form (reserved randomness per site, re-driving of the promoted leaf, exact product law of the preparation flags) exists only in the supplement. Section 6 states four results whose proofs are entirely in the supplement, and two of them (everything at exponent $`543/1000`$) rest on a drift inequality for two "baseline" tables whose proof is in an earlier report that is not part of the supplement. A full repair of that gap is given in Section 5.7.2 of this report.

2. **Provenance and independence of the certificate behind the headline bracket.** The motif certificate carries the lower bound $`113/200`$ and the sharp upper bound $`1133/2000`$. It is regenerated by a floating-point program at verification time, pinned by a digest only in the supplement, not bit-reproducible across compiler options (shown in Section 3.3), and verified by a single implementation of its 17.3 million inequalities, whereas the banded certificate has two. The direct tests advertised in Section 5 do not exercise unbounded degrees, pooled labels, saturation, band changes or the second transfer. The claim "below $`17/30`$" is doubly supported; the lower bound $`0.5650`$ and the upper bound $`0.5665`$ are not.

3. **Attribution, bibliography and accountability.** The key reference [AB] has appeared in a journal with different theorem numbering; one attribution to the Strogatz post changes its wording; a discrepancy inside [AB] ($`0.9`$ in its theorem and abstract, $`\sqrt2-\tfrac12\approx0.914`$ in its proposition) should be noted; the open problems of Section 8 coincide with questions posed in [AB] and are not credited; and the production note says that no human has read the proofs. A journal requires an author who vouches for them.

## 2. The claims, in my own words

**Model.** Start from one edge. At size $`n`$ a uniform vertex picks a uniform neighbour, and a new leaf is attached to that neighbour. $`C_n`$ is the number of non-leaves.

**Theorem 1.1 (existence).** There is a deterministic $`\mu\in[0,1]`$ with $`\log\mathbb E C_n/\log n\to\mu`$ and $`\log C_n/\log n\to\mu`$ almost surely. The proof is analytic: embed in continuous time; show that a positive fraction of non-leaves can each, with probability bounded below, grow a prescribed finite seed; compare the growth from that seed with a free copy at a cost $`e^{-\eta s}`$ over a block of length $`s`$; deduce an almost-supermultiplicative inequality for the mean, hence a limit; then repeat the construction at disjoint sites with independent randomness to get the pathwise lower bound, with Markov's inequality for the upper bound.

**Theorem 1.2 (bracket).** For every $`n\ge3`$,

```math
0.8335\,P_{113/200}(n)\ \le\ \mathbb E C_n\ \le\ 4.886\,P_{1133/2000}(n),\qquad \mathbb E C_n\le 393.9\,P_{5657/9983}(n),
```

with the exact rational prefactors of the manuscript and $`P_a(n)=\prod_{j=3}^{n-1}(1+a/j)`$. Both are proved by constructing a functional $`\Phi`$ of the tree with $`n\,\mathbb E[\Phi(T_{n+1})-\Phi(T_n)\mid\mathcal F_n]\le\lambda\Phi(T_n)`$ (or $`\ge`$), comparable to $`C_n`$, and by reducing the drift inequality on all trees to finitely many exact inequalities checked by computer.

**Consequences.** $`0.5650\le\mu\le0.5665<17/30`$, with gaps $`1/6000`$ and $`1/299490`$ for the two upper certificates. Since $`N_{=k}(n)\le C_n`$, the upper bounds also bound the mean number of vertices of each exact degree.

**Supporting results (Section 6, proofs in the supplement).** Almost-sure lower bounds $`\liminf\log C_n/\log n\ge 543/1000`$ and $`\ge 11/20`$ proved without Theorem 1.1; $`\liminf C_n/n^{543/1000}>0`$ almost surely; the mean cumulative degree count $`\mathbb E N_{\ge k}(n)`$ has logarithmic exponent $`\mu`$ in $`n/k`$, uniformly in $`k`$; for fixed $`k`$, $`\log N_{\ge k}(n)/\log n\to\mu`$ almost surely.

**Not claimed.** The value of $`\mu`$; $`\mathbb E C_n\asymp n^\mu`$; convergence of $`C_n/\mathbb E C_n`$; any exponent for the exact-degree counts $`N_{=k}(n)`$; any rigorous status for the simulation estimate $`0.56572`$.

The motif certificate is not an archived file. `cert_build.c` computes it by a floating-point iteration and rounds to integers. I built it five ways.

In my probe a build with `-march=native` returned the same integers, residual and reserve. The second verifier takes the least admissible integer in each row, which is canonical.

#### 5.7.2 Full repair for step 6.7 (and for the upper baseline table used in 5.7.4)

The following argument is what the supplement omits. The state definitions are those of `proofs/supporting-results-audit.md`; I checked each item against `certs9.py` and `certs6.py`.

**Claim.** Let $`f`$ be the lower baseline table, extended by $`f(L,\cdot)=f(L_{\max},\cdot)`$ for $`L>L_{\max}`$ and by $`0`$ for $`c>c_{\max}`$. If every inequality of `certs9.verify` holds, then for every tree with $`n\ge3`$ vertices and every core vertex $`u`$,

```math
n\,\mathbb E\Big[f(x_u(T_{n+1}))-f(x_u(T_n))+\mathbf 1\{W_n\text{ is a leaf of }u\}\,f(x_{\rm new})\ \Big|\ T_n\Big]\ \ge\ \delta f(x_u(T_n)).
```

The same holds with $`\le\lambda f`$ for the upper table extended by the constant $`G`$ for $`c>c_{\max}`$, given the inequalities of `certs6.verify`.

**Proof.**

1. *Only four kinds of event can change $`x_u`$.* The class of a neighbour $`w`$ is a function of $`(L_w,c_w)`$, and that pair changes only by a gain at $`w`$ or a conversion at $`w`$. So $`x_u`$ changes only by a gain at $`u`$, a conversion at $`u`$, a gain at a core neighbour, or a conversion at a core neighbour.
2. *Class transitions.* A pendant of class $`\ell`$ becomes class $`\ell+1`$ on a gain, or an "other" of degree $`K_0+2`$ when $`\ell=K_0`$; on a conversion it becomes class B if $`\ell=1`$ and an "other" of degree $`\ell+1`$ otherwise. A class-B vertex has no leaf, so it cannot convert; on a gain it becomes an "other" of degree three. An "other" has degree at least three; a gain raises its recorded degree by one, or moves it to the top class; a conversion keeps its degree and raises its core degree, so its class is unchanged. The top class is absorbing.
3. *Rates.* Own gain: $`L+\rho`$ with $`\rho`$ in the recorded interval (exact credits $`1/(\ell+1)`$, $`1/2`$, $`1/d`$; each top neighbour contributes between $`0`$ and $`1/D_{\rm cap}`$). Own conversion: exactly $`L/D`$; it adds a class-one pendant to $`x_u`$ and creates a newborn in state $`\mathrm{nb}(L,c)`$. Pendant of class $`\ell`$: gain $`\ell+1/D`$, conversion $`\ell/(\ell+1)`$, both exact. Class B: gain $`1/D+1/D_x\in[1/D,1/D+1/2]`$. "Other" of recorded degree $`d`$: gain in $`[0,d]`$.
4. *Extremes.* Each uncertain rate multiplies a fixed difference of table entries, so the extreme of each term is attained at an endpoint of its interval, and the terms may be bounded separately.
5. *Unbounded $`L`$.* For $`L>L_{\max}`$ the own-gain difference vanishes, the newborn type and every successor entry are independent of $`L`$ (this uses $`L_{\max}\ge K_0+1`$ and $`L_{\max}+1\ge D_{\rm cap}`$, both asserted by the code), and each remaining term is affine in $`L/D\in[(L_{\max}+1)/(L_{\max}+1+c),1)`$ or in $`1/D\in(0,1/(L_{\max}+1+c)]`$. Endpoint checks suffice.
6. *Unbounded $`c`$.* For $`c>c_{\max}`$ the value of $`f`$ is constant along the vertex's future, and only the newborn term remains. It is nonnegative (lower table), or at most the largest newborn weight, which the code asserts to be at most $`\lambda G`$ (upper table).
7. *Upper table.* The same list applies with $`K_0=1`$ and without tracked degrees: each "other" neighbour contributes between $`0`$ and $`1/3`$ to $`\rho`$, and none of its events changes $`x_u`$. The static label $`P\ge4`$ records that the creator was an "other" of degree at least $`P`$ at the vertex's birth. It stays a neighbour of degree at least $`P`$, which justifies the improved upper credit $`(o-1)/3+1/P`$.

Items 3 to 6 are exactly the inequalities evaluated by the two `verify` functions. Summing over $`u`$ gives (H2), and the per-vertex form is what the polynomial-scale argument needs. $`\square`$

### 7.2 Qualification on the comparison with the published $`0.569`$

Additional fits on the manuscript's fixed-size sample (2,400 trajectories, nine sizes, pure power law, jackknife errors):

| Fit | Exponent | Distance of $`0.569`$ in standard errors |
|---|---|---|
| Unweighted, count units (Table 1) | $`0.56603\pm0.00024`$ | $`+12.3`$ |

| Weights $`1/\mathrm{se}_i^2`$ | $`0.56758\pm0.00047`$ | $`+3.0`$ |
| Weights $`1/\text{mean}_i`$ | $`0.56621\pm0.00025`$ | $`+11.2`$ |
| Count units with an additive offset | $`0.56600`$ | — |
| Count units, dropping the first one to four sizes | $`0.56600`$ to $`0.56602`$ | — |

The coefficient of variation of $`C_n`$ is between $`0.507`$ and $`0.528`$ at all nine sizes, so weighting by inverse variance is essentially a fit in log units. That is the default behaviour of common fitting tools when error bars are supplied. Under such a fit the published $`0.569`$, itself rounded to three digits and carrying an unknown sampling error of similar size, is about two standard errors away once both errors are combined. The sentence "remains unexplained" is defensible but too strong. A more accurate statement: the gap is twelve standard errors if the published fit was unweighted in count units and two to three if it was variance-weighted; the caption does not say which.

### 7.3 Other qualifications

- The quoted standard errors are for ratio-of-means estimators with heavy, non-self-averaging run-to-run variation. The jackknife over whole trajectories is the right tool, and it was applied correctly.
- Of the 22,223,928,834 pooled firings, 6,784,007,633 (30.5%) come from the 91 runs that continue past $`t=28`$.
- The sampled mean populations lie about 1.3% below $`3e^t`$ at $`t=4,12,28`$ ($`z\approx-0.6`$ each time; the three values share trajectories). This is unremarkable.
- "Exact in distribution" holds for the simulators only up to the documented Gaussian branch. At late times hubs of degree $`10^{12}`$ or more use it routinely. The relative effect on $`\sum_uL_u/D_u`$ is far below the quoted errors, but the phrase should be avoided in the supplement.

## 10. Exposition

### 10.1 Structure

The paper is three papers in one: an analytic existence theorem, two computer-assisted certificates, and a simulation study, followed by four further results stated without proof. For the target journals I would expect the existence theorem with complete proofs and one certificate presented in full; the second certificate and Section 6 could be a companion paper or a properly edited appendix.

### 10.2 Section 2

Sections 2.1 to 2.3 are terse but complete. Section 2.4 is not (row 2.17). The supplement's versions are clear and rigorous. Import their definitions of an admissible construction, the reserved randomness, and the lemmas "product law" and "what the flags guarantee".

### 10.3 Sections 3 and 4

- **Notation overload.** $`H`$ is a centre domain in Section 3 and the degree cutoff 2400 in Section 4. $`J`$ is a domain, a number of triples and a witness set. $`T`$ is a seed tree and the threshold 8. $`M`$ is a degree level and the extremum operator. $`A`$ is the constant 8, three families of functions, and a tail coefficient. $`b`$ is a constant, a rate, a table index and a pooled mark. $`h`$ is a block length, a parent indicator, a cutoff and a common denominator. $`F`$ is the mean core size, a generic table and a sender charge. $`S_u`$ has two meanings. $`k`$ has three. A table of notation and some renaming are needed.
- State Theorem 1.2 with decimal values beside the fractions, and say how loose the prefactors are.
- Section 3.4 would be easier to check with one fully worked corner, for instance an edge with an inner parent and a tail child.
- Say explicitly in Section 3.5 that the arrays are available only through the builder, and print their digests.

### 10.4 Production note

The note is candid, which is to its credit. Two points for the editor.

- A journal article needs an author who takes responsibility for every proof. The note says the author "chose the question, organized independent attempts and specified the acceptance criteria", and that no human has read the proofs. Before publication the author, or a named collaborator, must have checked the arguments and be able to answer for them.
- The list of systems and roles belongs in an acknowledgement or a statement on the use of AI tools, in whatever form the journal prescribes.
