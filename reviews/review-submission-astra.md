## A consequence for each fixed exact degree

Fix an integer $`j\ge2`$ and let $`D_j(n)=N_{=j}(n)`$. The arguments of §§1–2 give

```math
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb E D_j(n)}{\log n}
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log D_j(n)}{\log n}=\mu,
\qquad\text{the second limit almost surely}.
```

Since an exact-degree count can decrease, the following proof controls an entire time window around the desired size. Use $`M=2`$ and a $`j`$-vertex star rooted at its centre as the prescribed branch, choosing either endpoint as root when $`j=2`$. The attached root has total degree $`j`$, counting the exterior edge. Plant in $`h=j+1`$ units using the disjoint site scripts of lines 131–133. Extend each retained preparation flag by silence of its base and the $`j`$ branch clocks for two further units. These extensions use fresh independent increments. The retained flag probability is a fixed $`q_j>0`$ (one may take the displayed planting lower bound times $`e^{-2(j+1)}`$). On each retained flag the root has degree exactly $`j`$ throughout $`[h,h+2]`$, since no exterior ring can attach across the cut except from its silent base. Different flags produce different roots.

Start a block at the first visit to $`m=\lfloor ne^{-(h+1)}\rfloor`$. Conditional on the tree there, the number $`Z`$ of protected roots is binomial with at least $`C_m/6`$ trials of probability $`q_j`$. At times $`h`$ and $`h+2`$ the population is a sum of $`m`$ independent geometric-family sizes whose respective means per family are $`e^h`$ and $`e^{h+2}`$. Thus the probability of reaching $`n`$ outside that time window is at most $`2e^{-c_jn}`$ for large $`n`$. The two totals have means asymptotic to $`n/e`$ and $`en`$ and each geometric law has an exponential moment around zero. This estimate is uniform in the starting tree. It uses no independence between $`Z`$ and the hitting time.

On the window event, $`D_j(n)\ge Z`$. Since $`Z\le m`$, taking expectations gives $`\mathbb ED_j(n)\ge(q_j/6)\mathbb EC_m-2m e^{-c_jn}`$. For the almost-sure result the binomial lower-tail bound gives, conditionally on the starting tree,

```math
\Pr\{D_j(n)<q_j C_m/12\mid T_m\}
\le e^{-q_j C_m/48}+2e^{-c_jn}.
```

The event $`C_m\ge n^{\mu/2}`$ occurs eventually almost surely, since $`m`$ is a fixed positive fraction of $`n`$ asymptotically and $`\mu\ge113/200>0`$. Intersect each bad event with $`\{C_m\ge n^{\mu/2}\}`$ before summing the probabilities. The resulting series converges. Borel–Cantelli gives $`D_j(n)\ge q_j C_m/12`$ eventually almost surely. This argument needs only the conditional bound for each $`n`$, so no joint choice of auxiliary scripts for overlapping blocks is required. The upper bounds follow from $`D_j(n)\le C_n`$.

A bound on core growth makes these counts comparable in size. The probability of a promotion at size $`l`$ is at most $`C_l/l`$, since each core contributes at most one to the scaled promotion rate. For the rising factorial $`H_r(x)=x(x+1)\cdots(x+r-1)`$, use $`H_r(x+1)/H_r(x)=1+r/x`$. Conditional on the size-$`m`$ tree, for every positive integer $`r`$,

```math
\mathbb E[H_r(C_n)\mid T_m]\le H_r(C_m)\prod_{l=m}^{n-1}(1+r/l)
\le H_r(C_m)(n/m)^r.
```

The last inequality uses $`1+r/l\le(1+1/l)^r`$. Put $`c=C_m`$ and choose $`r=c`$ after conditioning. Since $`H_c(c)\le(2c)^c`$ and $`H_c(x)\ge x^c`$, for $`K=8e^{h+1}`$ and all large $`n`$,

```math
\Pr(C_n>Kc\mid T_m)\le (2n/(Km))^c\le2^{-c}.
```

Restrict again to $`C_m\ge n^{\mu/2}`$ before applying Borel–Cantelli. Eventually almost surely $`D_j(n)\ge q_j C_m/12\ge (q_j/(12K)) C_n`$. For the means use $`r=1`$ to obtain $`\mathbb E C_n\le(n/m)\mathbb E C_m`$ in the earlier mean lower bound. Thus the ratios of the means also have strictly positive lower limits. Adjacent fixed exact-degree counts, for degrees at least two, are comparable almost surely. This answers the first part of question 2 in §7 of Addario-Berry et al. The constants depend on the degree. For this comparability conclusion, the earlier polynomial lower bound in Proposition 6.20 already supplies a summable exceptional probability by replacing $`n^{\mu/2}`$ with $`n^{1/20}`$. The two certificates and the exponent limit are needed only to identify the numerical range and common logarithmic exponent, respectively. Convergence of the proportions and their proposed tail in $`j`$ remain open.
