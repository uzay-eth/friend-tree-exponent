## 1. Gut reaction in one minute

The title on the README sells the least interesting part. Ruling out \(17/30\) is satisfying, but I would read this for the positive almost-sure amplitude and for the absence of escaping hub mass. Neither follows from a log exponent or from convergence of individual degrees. Theorem 9 looks like the cleanest result: count the leaf initiators, then charge newly promoted vertices for the mass they can carry into the tail. Theorem 4 is where I would spend my time. If the all-seed renewal estimate holds, the argument appears to have the right ingredients for a strong limit, rather than just an expectation asymptotic dressed up as one.

## 2. What I would do next

Read Theorem 4 against Lemmas B.1–B.2 and Appendix A. Then reread Theorem 9 on its own, using the older sublinear almost-sure upper bound cited there. It should not need the exact exponent, the renewal argument, the enclosure, or even a hub at an endpoint of every edge. I would write down the two distinguishing facts: \(\sum_v D_v(n)/n=2-2/n\), whereas the leaf-initiator vector has mass \(1-C_n/n\); and coordinatewise convergence of that vector is not enough. The stopped promotion budget is precisely the missing tightness estimate. I checked the stopping convention: the crossing step is included, the active indicator is predictable, and freezing includes the denominator. Thus the budget covers later labels without assuming the conclusion. The weak limit for the conditional recipient degree is size-biased by the same random vector; it is not an almost-sure limit for the degree of the actual next recipient.

I compared the claims with [§7 of the cited arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.20185v1#S7). My map is: **(1)** Theorems 1 and 4 give the exponent and a nondegenerate positive amplitude (8 adds absolute continuity); **(2)** Theorems 4–5 give comparability of consecutive exact-degree counts at fixed degree and a vanishing high-degree *fraction of the core* in the iterated limit; **(3)** Theorem 10 gives the first, zero-threshold comparison including the founder tie, and Theorem 11 does not give stochastic dominance; **(4)** Theorem 9 gives unit mass and also the conditional recipient-law conjecture from §3; **(5)** Theorem 13 gives a conditional negative result for one specified sampling rule, metric and reference law, not the law of the whole deleted forest. Questions (6)–(8) concern other models. I checked the two rational endpoint combinations, the outward rounding, and the strict inequality against \(17/30\) by exact fraction arithmetic. I did not run the verifier or check the exhaustive rows.

## 3. Sentences I would mark

Quotations preserve wording, with mathematical typesetting restored and line wraps removed.

- **Abstract:** “The limiting degrees divided by \(n\) sum to one almost surely.” The division is inside the limit for each fixed label; the sum is outside it. Reading this as a limit of the sum gives two. This is the distinction the theorem resolves, so display \(\sum_{v\ge1}\lim_{n\to\infty}D_v(n)/n=1\) or define the shares first. The README's “The limiting degrees divided by $`n`$ sum to one almost surely” clause has the same ambiguity.
- **Abstract:** “For each fixed degree \(k\ge2\), the fraction of non-leaves of degree \(k\) converges almost surely to a deterministic limit of order \(k^{-(1+\mu)}\) as \(k\) grows, as Krapivsky and Redner conjectured.” Easy to read as one joint limit, or as attributing the precise almost-sure formulation to the physics paper. In [Krapivsky–Redner §5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01514) the normalized counts are ratios of averages; §7(2) of *Random friend trees* records the corresponding almost-sure conjecture. The size limit here comes first, then the tail of the limiting sequence. Section 3 does explain the order of limits; its sentence “Krapivsky and Redner’s prediction [2] in this notation is” still puts the exact almost-sure display under the wrong citation. The README's “conjecture recorded in” is better.
- **§2:** “The finite-size prefactors and an independent degree-band upper bound \(5657/9983\) appear in the original paper, Theorem 1.2.” “The original paper” reads as [4] after §1. The link actually goes to this author's earlier exponent paper. Name that paper instead of making the link carry the attribution.
- **Theorem 4 proof:** “Thus \(Y_n\) is bounded in \(L^2\) and converges to \(X/d_2\), where \(X=d_2W_{\rm size}^{-\mu}M_\infty\) is the pathwise count limit.” The product bound proves boundedness, not the identity of the limit. The identity uses \(Y_n=[\Gamma(n)/\Gamma(n+\mu)]e^{\mu\tau_n}M_{\tau_n}\), with all clocks measured from the same reset. Spell out that line; independence of the size clock and the discrete chain does **not** mean independence of \(W_{\rm size}\) and \(M_\infty\).
- **§4.1:** “For the edge seed one can use the external growth input \(\mathbb E C_n=O(n^\beta)\) for some \(\beta<1\), from Theorem 1 (only the sublinear mean bound is used); Tonelli then gives summability almost surely.” “External” makes this sound like a hypothesis or an outside citation, but Theorem 1 is in this paper. The next sentence gives the genuinely external route, [4, Prop. 6.22]. The distinction matters because that route removes dependence on any new exponent proof or certificate.
- **Theorem 13, (3):** “\(\sup_{0\le x\le1}|\nu_{n,N}(V\le Nx)-x|\longrightarrow0.\)” This is printed as \(Nx\), not \(N^x\). It is a uniform limit for \(V/N\), not for \(\log V/\log N\). The proof substitutes \(x=\log v_n/\log N\), which gives \(V\le v_n\) only with \(N^x\). This is a substantive mismatch between the stated premise, proof, and README description, not a deduction of the log-volume premise from the count law. (The linear-volume premise would also yield a support-size bound, by using \(v_n/N\to0\); that is not the premise advertised.)
- **§6:** “The fixed-degree law and intermediate cumulative law do not settle the pointwise growing-degree law of Krapivsky–Redner [2] throughout the intermediate range.” This doesn't say which intermediate cumulative theorem is meant or what degree sequences constitute the range. Theorems 4–5 in the main text give iterated limits, not a uniform crossover statement. Give the result and range explicitly, and cite the physics formula being compared rather than imputing a precise growing-degree quantifier it does not state.
- **Lemma B.3:** “For a newborn or the initial centre, conditional on the environment its mean degree at age \(a\) is at most \(2e^a\) by degree-rate domination, and core degree is dominated by \(1+\mathrm{Pois}(a)\) from its own promotion clock.” Condition on the history through promotion (or the initial seed for the centre), not on the future environment. Conditioning on the latter could reveal the very rings used in the domination. The needed birth-compensation bound is the former.
- **Appendix C.2:** “At \(d\ge32\) direct excess is zero and \(U(d+1)<L(2)\) by the validated cap \(u=1/(33p_{\rm lo})\), where \(h_{\rm lo}\) is the box lower endpoint.” The formula uses \(p_{\rm lo}\), the text defines \(h_{\rm lo}\), and \(h\) is used for a different quantity below. Small, but easy to misread when matching the analytic tail to the boxes.
- **README navigation:** “Proof and checker map” (link label, not a sentence). The `docs/proof-map.md` link returned 404 on the public repository; so did the same path at the linked release pin. The top-level repo and quick-run script were reachable. The README correctly says the quick run is not the full enclosure; I would not treat its example output as a new replay or as verification of an almost-sure statement.

No further misattribution identified in the passages I checked. In particular, I would not flag the founder-edge citation in Theorem 8: [4, Theorem 3.2] supplies a hub at at least one end, not two; the separately linked hub-children argument supplies a hub–hub edge without asserting independence after conditioning on hubhood. The abstract's numbering checks out against §7, subject to the degree-law and mass wording above. The explicit restrictions on Theorem 6 and on the scope of the certificate replays are welcome.

## 4. First theorem I would check, and can I?

**Theorem 4.** Yes: the PDF gives a checkable proof route without a machine replay of the narrow interval. The heart is (8), an exponential error proportional to initial *core* count with constants uniform over finite seeds, not a fixed-seed equivalent. I would start by checking the signed fringe cancellation in (5), the positive comparison \(\Psi\asymp C\), and the pole-free strip for the finite permanent-mark renewal kernel. I would then check that the one-leaf coupling bounds the jump in the *backward conditional expectation*, hence the squared-jump budget, and that \(2\mu>1\) makes the mesh errors summable. Positivity is a separate step: conditional Cauchy–Schwarz and the earlier path exponent make the conditional probability of a positive limit stay away from zero, and that probability converges to the event indicator. Neither a positive mean nor \(L^2\) boundedness alone would do. Finally I would check the passage from a countable mesh to all times using monotone cumulative counts and then the size epochs, before taking differences for exact degrees. The paper lets me follow all of these steps; this is a reading of the proof structure, not a completed check of every renewal or coupling estimate.
