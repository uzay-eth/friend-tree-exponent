## 1. Gut reaction in one minute

The number is reassuring: it rounds to 0.566, and the upper endpoint is about 0.000930 below 17/30. No amount of looking at a straight log-log plot would have persuaded me of a rational value in the first place. And the paper’s own first bound already kills 17/30. The narrow enclosure is worth having, but what would make me keep reading is that the nucleus has a random amplitude while its *composition* is asymptotically deterministic. That is much more informative than another digit. The degree-law proof also has a physical idea I can recognize: look at a core vertex at a random age, then use the flux through degree \(k\) to get the point probability. That last step matters; differencing two asymptotic tails is not a proof.

## 2. What I would do next

I would put Eq. (1) and §5.1 of *Emergent Network Modularity* beside Theorems 4–5. I want to see exactly where the fixed-degree limit is taken and what survives when the degree grows with size. Then I’d check the age-mixture and flux identities on paper. The test I’d want for the numerics is not a bigger fit with smaller sampling error: put the *effective slope* of the mean nucleus size against size for the 2017 and 2026 fitting windows, with the rigorous asymptotic band drawn across it. The 0.569 in Fig. 6 is a finite-window fit to mean core sizes, not a second measurement at the same asymptotic precision. I would not ask the theorem to explain the discrepancy by itself.

## 3. Sentences that overclaim, confuse, or misattribute

Quotes use the PDF’s wording with line wraps removed; math is typeset inline here.

- **Abstract:** “For each fixed degree \(k\ge2\), the fraction of non-leaves of degree \(k\) converges almost surely to a deterministic limit of order \(k^{-(1+\mu)}\) as \(k\) grows, as Krapivsky and Redner conjectured.” The claim is right as a sequential limit, and the attribution is right as shorthand for Addario-Berry et al.’s Question 7(2). The sentence runs the two limits together, and “as … conjectured” can be read as closing the finite-size scaling law in the original Eq. (1), too. It does not. The supplied README’s “answering the Krapivsky–Redner conjecture recorded in [*Random friend trees*, §7(2)]” is better scoped by its citation, but I’d still spell out the limit order there. The 2017 §5.1 describes a normalized degree law from the scaling ansatz; §7(2) is where the almost-sure formulation is explicit.
- **§1:** “In 2022 Strogatz brought their question to a wider audience and asked whether the exponent could be 17/30 [3].” “Their question” has no clear antecedent. If it means the exact value, fine. If it means the specific rational candidate, the preceding numerical estimate did not propose it. Keep the numerical estimate and the later rational question distinct.
- **§2:** “The finite-size prefactors and an independent degree-band upper bound \(5657/9983\) appear in the original paper, Theorem 1.2.” “The original paper” just after discussing the original physics papers is ambiguous. This points to the earlier Uzay paper, not [2] or [4]. The supplied README similarly says “originally bounded by” without naming the earlier certificate. Also, the supplied README’s “LaTeX source” link lands at the earlier paper source in the public repository, not the source of the PDF in front of me; “Proof and checker map” has no file at that path in the public revision I checked. The public README does label its historical section; it is the handoff from this packet that needs disambiguation.
- **§2:** “Transfers between configurations cancel in the sum.” This is where I need an example or a named identity, not reassurance. Deleting the promoted vertex identifies the old motif that pays for the *newborn weight*. The cancellation used to get the row inequalities is a separate accounting step. The linked proof gives more detail; the PDF sentence alone doesn’t tell me what cancels or why the unsupported motifs do not leave a remainder.
- **Theorem 6:** “Assume the finite-exterior planting and one-edge hypotheses of Lemma B.4. There is a Borel probability \(\pi\) with \(\pi Q_t=e^{\mu t}\pi\) for all \(t\ge0\).” The order makes existence sound conditional too. Lemma B.3 proves existence without those inputs; Lemma B.4 uses them for the norm growth rate and exclusion of other probability eigenvalues. The public README repeats this ambiguity: “Under the planting and one-edge estimates, the mean evolution of a rooted core neighbourhood also has a probability eigenmeasure with growth rate $`\mu`$.”
- **Theorem 9 proof:** “For the edge seed one can use the external growth input \(EC_n=O(n^\beta)\) for some \(\beta<1\), from Theorem 1 (only the sublinear mean bound is used); Tonelli then gives summability almost surely.” Theorem 1 is in this paper; [4, Prop. 6.22] in the next sentence is the external alternative. Not a mathematical gap.
- **§5:** “The count per original non-leaf is asymptotic to \(AN^{-\mu}\log N\), and the shape law approaches that of an inert planted newborn observed at uniform age in \([0,\log N]\), with the exterior parent omitted.” “Approaches” needs a metric and a denominator for the sample, not just the order of limits in the preceding sentence. In particular the simultaneous-cutoff TV result later in the paragraph is not a TV statement at arbitrary growing cutoffs. Theorem 13 itself is clear about its premises and about not establishing a sharp transition.
- **§6:** “The fixed-degree law and intermediate cumulative law do not settle the pointwise growing-degree law of Krapivsky–Redner [2] throughout the intermediate range.” Correct caveat, but “intermediate cumulative law” is not identified here. Give the statement or point the reader directly to it. As written, it is easy to conflate it with the sequential cumulative tail in Theorem 5.
- **Appendix C.2:** “At \(d\ge32\) direct excess is zero and \(U(d+1)<L(2)\) by the validated cap \(u=1/(33p_{\rm lo})\), where \(h_{\rm lo}\) is the box lower endpoint.” Two names for the lower endpoint in the same sentence. Here the endpoint is for the exponent \(p\).
- **Supplied README:** “The [full rebuild and strict replay](https://github.com/uzay-eth/friend-tree-exponent/blob/a0972085ad72ad5685ae7b424988648d1965e73c/round2/verify/gold/README.md) took about 17 minutes to build the two snapshots, then 2 minutes to check them on the recorded Ryzen 7 5800X.” Those are the **Gold2 pair** timings. The linked rebuild command without a preset builds four snapshots, and the default command’s disk allowance is higher. Name the preset in the summary; I should not have to infer it from storage figures.

No other overclaim or misattribution stood out on this pass. In particular, 0.566 is fair to the 2017 report, the 0.569 credit is fair to Fig. 6, and the Question 7 numbering and founder exception are fine. “Independent” for the sampled certificate checks is not presented as an independent exhaustive scan. I did not audit every proof or establish priority independently.

## 4. Which theorem I would check first, and whether the paper lets me

**Theorem 5.** I can check the tail mechanism from the PDF: the rooted cell measure in B.3, the newborn age mixture, the hitting-time identity for the cumulative tail, and the separate crossing flux for the exact-degree law. The exponential moment of *core* degree is what makes the degree-\(k\) crossing rate asymptotic to \(k\) in the requisite estimates. I would check that step before trying to digest the contour argument. For the whole theorem I still have to trace the uniform renewal remainder in B.2 and the de-Poissonization in Theorem 4. The paper gives me that route; reading the route is not a verification of it. Separately, I recomputed the two Theorem 2 fractions from the printed residual table by exact rational arithmetic and obtained the printed endpoints. I did not run the certificate. That arithmetic checks the last implication, not the local inequalities that do the work.

## 5. One edit

Replace the degree sentence in the abstract with: “First taking the tree size to infinity at each fixed \(k\ge2\), the fraction of non-leaves of degree \(k\) converges almost surely to \(p_k\), where \(p_k\sim a k^{-(1+\mu)}\) as \(k\to\infty\), for some \(a>0\). This proves the fixed-degree conjecture as formulated in Addario-Berry et al., §7(2), but does not give the scaling law when \(k\) grows with the tree size.”
