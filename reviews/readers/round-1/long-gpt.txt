## 1. Gut reaction, one minute in

The title on the README sells the decimal; the PDF sells the better result. The compact equation is interesting not because it is the first equation for μ, but because capping the permanent marks gives a 19-state renewal equation without capping any physical degree. That is a credible route from an exponent and constant-factor bounds to a leading coefficient and then, with a separate concentration argument, to a positive almost-sure amplitude. I want to see whether the copy terms really pay for the parent arrows. The decimal is a solid target to check, but 17/30 was already excluded by the first enclosure. The new interval narrows the target; it does not identify the number.

## 2. What I would do next

I would put the two equations on the same page. In the earlier one, the pressure is the exponential rate in generation number of the discounted birth sum in the inert-parent newborn process. In this one, a generation counts direct newborn emissions of an auxiliary residual family; copy events and intrinsic evolution are integrated out. I would compare the two routes to the physical Laplace abscissa, not assume the kernels or their spectral-radius functions agree away from the crossing. Then I would derive the parent-arrow cancellation on a planted fringe with one old core child and trace exactly which uniformity in the all-seed mean estimate is used to build the martingale.

On the arithmetic side, I would ask what the finite determinant actually buys at algebraic discounts. The entries are infinite occupation transforms, not rational functions just because the matrix is finite. Even transcendence of an entry or a signed response as a function would not rule out cancellation at an algebraic argument. I checked the displayed endpoint arithmetic with exact fractions: the endpoints are those printed, the decimals round outward, and the interval still contains 99/175. I would keep that last fact separate from the narrower conditional denominator argument. I did not run either verifier suite.

## 3. Sentences I would flag

**Supplied PDF** (mathematical notation transcribed; line breaks joined):

- “This rules out 17/30 and agrees with Krapivsky and Redner’s estimate of 0.566 to the reported precision.” The agreement is fair, but §5.1 says the estimate seems accurate to within 0.001; it isn't a tight numerical cross-check. The sentence also makes ruling out 17/30 sound like a new consequence that required this enclosure. Theorem 1 already does it.
- “For each fixed degree k ≥ 2, the fraction of non-leaves of degree k converges almost surely to a deterministic limit of order k⁻⁽¹⁺μ⁾ as k grows, as Krapivsky and Redner conjectured.” This is right as the interpretation in Addario-Berry et al. §7(2). Name that interpretation in the abstract: Krapivsky–Redner's original statements concern degree counts and a large-degree scaling law. A reader can otherwise take this as settling the joint growing-k asymptotic that §6 explicitly leaves open.
- “Christopher D. Long first gave a critical equation in discounted generation sums and an effective search and then constant-factor bounds on the mean.” The credit is accurate, not missing. But “effective search” sounds like an implemented search or a heuristic; the earlier result is a *terminating* two-sided approximation theorem, without a practical time bound. And “on the mean” should point forward to the distinction between the earlier Θ bound and the new mean equivalent. The exact point at which the new argument goes beyond the earlier one is more useful than the chronology alone.
- “His equation retains the full birth environment.” Which environment? The earlier equation uses an inert exterior parent slot. It retains the evolving planted configuration governing births, not the full physical environment on the far side of that edge. The contrast is in what gets retained or integrated out in the generation operator, not which proof models a live exterior.
- “The finite-size prefactors and an independent degree-band upper bound 5657/9983 appear in the original paper, Theorem 1.2.” That link is to an earlier Uzay version, not to the prominently cited prior *Random friend trees* paper. Give the author/version in the sentence; “the original paper” is ambiguous here.
- “When a new core vertex appears, removing it from any new configuration identifies the old configuration to which its contribution belongs.” This explains the promotion charge, but it does not explain the transfers in the next sentence. The linked proof also transfers external **leaf-gain** donor terms; those can occur with no new core at all. I couldn't infer the finite rows from this sketch alone.
- “Let X be the space of finite or countably infinite connected locally finite rooted core trees marked by Lₓ ∈ ℤ≥0, with Dₓ = Lₓ + cₓ ≥ 2, in the topology of agreement on marked balls.” Not a mathematical overclaim, but X has just been established as the random amplitude. Changing its meaning at Theorem 6 and then going back to the amplitude at Theorem 8 gave me a needless double take.
- “Assume the finite-exterior planting and one-edge hypotheses of Lemma B.4.” This scopes over the whole statement of Theorem 6, including eigenmeasure existence. B.3 supplies existence independently; B.4 is needed for the operator-norm spectral growth and exclusion of other probability eigenvalues. Put the condition where it is used.
- “Thus on the fixed surviving cut, X = U^μ X_A + (1 − U)^μ X_B.” In Theorem 8 I read this as an identity *in the constructed surviving-cut law* (the specified seed and edge, conditional on no subsequent crossings), not a decomposition with independent components at a randomly chosen last cut. The proof makes the distinction; the line could carry it explicitly so it survives quotation.
- “The response is a function of the discount; Long’s programme asks for the arithmetic nature of its critical value.” The pronoun makes the critical exponent sound like a critical value of the particular signed response just discussed. The programme is about the **critical discount** μ of the growth problem. The response as a function of s is a different object, and a theorem about that function doesn't establish an arithmetic property of μ. The paper says this correctly later.
- “For a newborn or the initial centre, conditional on the environment its mean degree at age a is at most 2eᵃ by degree-rate domination, and core degree is dominated by 1 + Pois(a) from its own promotion clock.” In B.3 specify “conditional on the history at promotion” (or the initial state for the centre). Conditioning on the future environment is not what the domination argument gives.
- “At d ≥ 32 direct excess is zero and U(d + 1) < L(2) by the validated cap u = 1/(33pₗₒ), where hₗₒ is the box lower endpoint.” A notation typo in C.2 but worth fixing in an executable-proof paragraph: the symbol being defined is pₗₒ, not hₗₒ.

## 4. First theorem I would check, and whether the paper lets me

Theorem 3, starting with B.1, then the capped version in B.2. In (5) the child death term is −aᵧ f(Y); the parent's two copy terms are (aᵧ − pₛ)f(Y) + pₛ f(Y⁺). Their sum is exactly the missing incoming-arrow term pₛ[f(Y⁺) − f(Y)]. This is a first-moment identity, not independence of real fringes. Check that the weights keep the residual growth at or below 0.51 under **both** marking choices, then verify that the newborn occupation series diverges iff the Perron radius is at least one, and that positive founder immigration transfers this to the Laplace abscissa of the physical mean from T₃. The latter step uses Theorem 1; the equation is not an independent new proof of existence as written. The supplied PDF has enough to do that check by hand; the public paper link does not lead to that theorem. For the mean conclusion I then have to check the extra uniform renewal estimate and size de-Poissonization in B.2 and Theorems 4–5; one real crossing is not a pole-free strip. The PDF gives those arguments and the linked finite-kernel proof gives a separate route to rational evaluation. That is enough to start a real check, not a claim that I've checked the contour estimates or replayed the certificates.

## 5. One edit

Replace the prior-work paragraph's three sentences about Long and Theorem 3 with a direct comparison, keeping the pinned links at the claims they support:

> For the inert-parent newborn process, Long characterized μ by the pressure of discounted generation sums and proved termination of a two-sided rational bracket search. His later critical eigenfunctions gave constant-factor bounds on the mean. Here we obtain the same critical discount from a different first-moment kernel: the auxiliary families integrate out intrinsic evolution and copy events, and the permanent parent mark can be capped without truncating the degrees. The finite-mark renewal estimate gives a limiting mean coefficient; with a separate concentration argument it gives the almost-sure amplitude. Neither critical equation decides whether μ is irrational.
