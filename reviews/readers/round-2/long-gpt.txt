## 1. Gut reaction in one minute

The headline is solidly framed: an exact exclusion of \(17/30\), not an identification of the exponent. But the result that would keep me reading is the positive random amplitude, with a density. The earlier generation equation identifies the exponent, and constant-factor mean bounds set its scale; neither tells you that the normalized count in one tree converges to something positive. The marked-fringe construction looks like a genuinely different way into that problem, not a relabelling of the full-environment kernel. I would be more excited about that mechanism than about the extra digits.

## 2. What I would do next

I would put the two renewal constructions side by side. In the earlier one a generation counts physical genealogical births; the state at a birth retains the environment. Here a generation counts direct births in an auxiliary family with killing and copies. The equality to check is at the critical **discount**, not between the two spectral-radius functions. Separately, for the signed response, summing the physical birth discounts gives \(F_s(1)=sM_s(J)\) for \(s>\mu\). That is the right comparison with \(U_s(J)\): a scalar identity from the same inert newborn occupation, not equality of operators or convergence of the signed Taylor series at one. The natural arithmetic question is what survives specialization at the critical discount. Infinitely many denominator primes in coefficients of a large-\(s\) expansion do not answer that question.

## 3. Overclaims, confusing sentences, or wrong attribution

**Substantive overclaims or wrong attribution found: none.** The cited earlier results support the generation crossing, terminating rational approximation, numerical upper bound, and constant-factor mean bounds. The earlier two-sided search is not a runtime result or an implementation of the new phase-averaged drift tests. The packet also credits the earlier signed germ and critical-boundary work; the new prime-denominator result is about a *function* of the discount. The paper leaves irrationality and transcendence of the *number* open. The fixed-degree law is stated with the limits in the right order; the forest obstruction in Theorem 13 is conditional, and hub and moment ordering are not passed off as stochastic ordering. The numerical estimate attributed to Sabba is presented as reported, not used as a certificate premise.

**Clarity, not an overclaim:** “This proves the fixed-degree formulation of Krapivsky and Redner's scaling prediction in Section 7(2) of Addario-Berry et al.” On a first pass, the final phrase can sound like the original prediction appeared in §7(2). Krapivsky and Redner supply the scaling prediction; Addario-Berry et al. state the almost-sure fixed-degree formulation. The body and README make that distinction. The stated theorem does not prove the original joint exact-degree scaling law.

**Clarity about provenance:** “He gave a terminating two-sided approximation and a certified upper bound of $0.5664485422$ [7].” Correct, but the juxtaposition can suggest the quoted bound came from running the terminating search. In the cited work it is a separate correlated-weight refinement of Uzay’s earlier motif witness, retaining its tables and all-state drift decomposition. Neither the earlier refinement nor Sabba’s reported interval is an input to Gold2.
