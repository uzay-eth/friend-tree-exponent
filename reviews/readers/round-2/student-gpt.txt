## 1. Gut reaction in one minute

I came for the fraction in the tweet. The answer is **no**: start from one edge, pick a vertex uniformly, and attach a new leaf to one of its uniformly chosen neighbours. If \(C_n\) counts non-leaves, then \(\log \mathbb E C_n/\log n\) and \(\log C_n/\log n\) (almost surely) tend to the same deterministic \(\mu\), with \(0.565713486333\le\mu\le0.565736986679<17/30\). That still rounds to Krapivsky and Redner’s \(0.566\). The result I didn’t expect is that \(C_n/n^\mu\) tends almost surely to a finite, strictly positive *random* limit with a density. Same exponent, different multipliers. I’d keep reading.
