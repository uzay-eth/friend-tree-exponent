## 1. The two clock boundaries

**Stopped next slab, A 588–767 and S 704–810.** Write h=1/128, let f be a row of nodal prices, let σ=(f_left−f_right)/h, and use D to clear the state rate denominator. For fixed physical N and rates r_j, multiply the exact one-step drift by N. If a lower-clock step crosses knot t_k by θ with total length δ=log(1+1/N), the result is
```
−N(δ−θ)σ_in(s) − Nθσ_out(s) + Σ r_j[(f_k(j)−f_k(s))−θ(σ_out(j)−σ_out(s))].
```
This is affine in θ at fixed N and rates. At θ=0 the incoming comparison applies. At θ=δ use the outgoing continuing comparison if available. If the outgoing row stopped, Nδ≤1 and σ_out(s)≥0, so it suffices to check both endpoints of
```
B − θ W ≥ 0,
B = Σ Dr_j(f_k(j)−f_k(s)) − Dσ_out(s),
W = Σ Dr_j(σ_out(j)−σ_out(s)).
```
For every crossing N+1≥m exp(t_k), hence δ≤1/N≤1/[200 exp(t_k)−1]. The code bounds the last quantity up, using the **lower** exponential endpoint and ceiling division, and requires B−ceil(max(W,0)·dt_hi/S₂)≥0. It uses the outgoing slopes stored before the swap. Failure forces the current state to stop. Since the current left price then equals a lower stock floor valid through the right endpoint and is at least the right price, the whole interpolation is a valid stop. This reasoning applies at both extrema of the serial-host growth rate. At the degree cutoff the construction always stops. At the lower time horizon the far row is constant through the extra slab; return value zero and nonnegative positive-stock successors give the terminal return inequality.

**Upper terminal lift, A 785–920 and S 821–895.** Denote by F the stopped prices, including postpromotion's two absorbing prices. At a crossing of terminal knot T on the upper clock, the one-step expected stopped value is exactly F_s+QF_s/N. Its maximum above F_s is bounded by (QF_s)₊/N, with Q the generator conditional on the current exterior tree. A backward terminal value ≥F_s+u(QF_s)₊ is sufficient. The producer takes u≥1/[(m₀−1)exp(T−h)]≥1/N, rounds the rate drift upward and rounds its product upward again. In P, an additional maximum with the already-computed next-host price can only raise the result. In U the targets are the **stopped** P and U rows: after the crossing every target is already at the horizon. Checking both host-growth endpoints works for every current exterior. The incoming interpolation decreases toward the lifted value, so it dominates that terminal value at each possible pre-crossing time. A step landing exactly on T uses the same argument.

At the upper degree cutoff K+1 the right price at a slab is at least the beta or capped-beta stop computed for the slab's left knot. The left price is raised to at least the right price. The terminal cutoff uses T−h. Thus throughout the entire slab the boundary dominates a stop valid at its actual physical size. An absorbing beta stop priced differently on two adjacent slabs may use the smaller of the two upper bounds on the crossing overlap. Both bounds hold after crossing and decreasing an absorbing payoff improves the upper test. Zero-lower-endpoint boxes return the static hub table directly, bypassing the positive-threshold terminal solver.

**Independent boundary test.** For the lower clock the least possible crossing size from m≥200 is ceil(200 exp(t_k))−1. For the upper clock it is ceil(199 exp(T)). I enclosed exp(1/128) by a positive Taylor polynomial and a geometric remainder, multiplied the brackets out on a 45-decimal integer scale and required both ceilings to agree separately at all 1,601 knots. The checker below uses these integer sizes. It reconstructs outgoing slopes from three successive values, clears the rate denominators in the two inequalities above and checks the actual stored terminal values after every lift. It consumes neither the producer's stored slopes nor its time-step corrections. It also checks both sides of the stopped upper degree boundary on every slab. Counts include candidate boxes that the ledger later subdivides.

## 3. Outward rounding and integer range
