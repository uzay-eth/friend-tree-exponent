# Proof of the recipient-bias averaging law

Assume m≥n^ρ for some fixed ρ>0 and n/m→∞, let L=log(n/m), and start physical clocks at T_m. An old host is a label ≤m that is a core or eventually becomes one. Assign it a slot at the restart or when it first promotes. A young leaf means a label >m presently of degree one. A crossing is created by one old host source ring promoting a young leaf. Its new branch is the component on the young side, including the new degree-two root and the leaf just born. Its only exterior edge goes back to its old host. Different crossing interiors are disjoint. Equations (3)–(4) of [the main addendum](NEXT-RECIPIENT.md) assign nonnegative charges to old hosts.

We give details of two estimates needed for (6). The first bounds the total possible contribution of a small exceptional set of old hosts even when they are selected after observing their futures. The second classifies the bulk at a fixed intermediate elapsed time U. Limits in n are always taken with U and all classification cutoffs fixed.

## Uniform square bound per old host

Let W_x be the charges assigned to x after L−1 with weights exp(L−s). There is a deterministic K_0 such that

    Σ_x W_x² ≤ K_0 C_m                                               (A)

except with probability O_a(n^(−a)) on C_m≥m^(μ/2), for every a>0. The same assertion holds for any fixed bounded translation of the lower time limit, with its own constant. It is enough to prove this under any coupling of the physical law.

First, each initial core cell consists of the old core and its initial leaves. Its number of eventual old hosts is bounded by 1 plus the private old-leaf promotion bound in `SMALL-POWERS.md` (reviewed Q4 note, not exported). Conditional on the restart the bounds for distinct cells are independent and have every moment bounded uniformly in the initial leaf stock. Consequently, outside the stated small probability, the sum of the **squares** of the cell host counts is ≤K_1 C_m, and the total number of slots is ≤K_2 C_m for deterministic constants. We use this concentration repeatedly: independent variables with uniformly bounded moments have centered sums of 2p-th moment ≤K_p N^p for N terms, by expansion and elimination of all singleton indices. Apply this also to powers of the variables and take p arbitrarily large. The size gate and polynomial lower bound on m make all the resulting tails summable in n.

The early label's own lifetime charges after the cutoff telescope to at most one. Each branch gives 1/2 at its crossing and later reciprocal drops totalling at most 1/2. Call a branch spoiled if its host ever sends into the young endpoint again. There is a uniform all-moment bound for the number of distinct spoiled branches per slot, with independent upper envelopes in slot order. Here are construction details.

Whenever the host has a young leaf and no current trial, start a virtual linear birth-death population J from one, with rates j and j/2 at j. Give it autonomous births coupled into the physical young leaf births; supplement these at their residual rate. At the host's rate-one rings remove a virtual member on a uniform mark ≤min(1,j/2), and supplement deaths at rate (j/2−1)_+. Put young leaves first. When J equals their true count j, their chance of being selected is j/d_x≤min(1,j/2); otherwise one actual loss preserves domination. Repeat after extinction immediately if a young leaf is available, or wait until one appears. Waiting periods produce no crossings. Use fresh host and trial tapes at each start. The trials are iid before the stopping or waiting decisions. An infinite list may be supplied even when only a finite prefix is used. Disjoint slots require separate supplies of host rings, virtual births and extra deaths; gains donated by other hosts can only supplement a young stock. A leaf promoted to a new core takes fresh residual source clocks. These constructions give the original physical arrow rates.

Extinction in one trial has probability 1/2 by the embedded biased walk. The number of host rings up to extinction, restricted to finite extinction, has moments of every order. In detail, on survival to time s the law of J(s) conditional on J(s)>0 is geometric on the positive integers with parameter a=e^(−s/2)/(2−e^(−s/2)). This follows by solving the one-ancestor generating-function equation ∂_s f=(f−1)(f−1/2), f(0)=z; the positive-state probabilities are proportional to a(1−a)^(i−1). Hence P(s<extinction<∞)=E[2^(−J(s)); J(s)>0]≤a. The Poisson ring count up to the random extinction time on this event has all moments by Cauchy–Schwarz on integer time intervals; its independence from extinction is unnecessary.

On an infinite trial, except up to a random time with exponentially decaying tail on that event, J(s)≥exp(s/4). At integer i the probability of 0<J(i)<4 exp(i/4) is O(exp(−i/4)) by the same geometric bound. From a larger state j the chance ever to fall below j/2 is ≤2^(−floor(j/2)). Sum over i after a given time. Up to this exceptional time, count all host rings. Afterwards put the crossing endpoints born during the running trial last among the non-young-leaf slots. There are at most N(s−) of them, where N is the host's own rate-one Poisson count in that trial. A send to one of them costs a ring in an upper uniform strip of width min(1,N(s−)exp(−s/4)). The total of these candidates has all moments. In integer interval [i,i+1) its expected count is ≤(i+1)exp(−i/4) by predictable integration and its count is bounded by the unit-interval Poisson count. Hölder applied on the event of nonzero count gives an L^p bound summable over i for every finite p. All crossings formed on finite trials were already counted once by the number of rings there. Sum these envelopes up to the first infinite trial in the padded list. The geometrically many failed trials have finite conditional moments of all orders, as does the successful trial's bound. This proves the claimed lifetime bound, including branches created before the eventual successful trial that receive arbitrarily many later sends. In particular the sum of the squared physical spoiled counts is O(C_m) with the tail in (A), by padding to ceil(K_2 C_m) slots.

For all other branches use an independent virgin tape at each candidate host ring. Its signed reciprocal is decreasing; let its positive reward measure consist of 1/2 at birth and every subsequent drop in the reciprocal. Total reward is at most one. Until the host sends back, the physical branch uses the same internal arrows. The virgin's null choice of the exterior slot creates a leaf at the old host in the physical copy, leaving the branch unchanged. Mark an entire rate-one Poisson stream in elapsed calendar time for each slot; use only rings after slot activation and launch only at actual crossings. Unused virgin tapes can be retained as independent padding. Increments of a new slot's previously unused calendar stream after its activation have the correct fresh law. For never-spoiled branches, the physical charges are bounded pathwise by the reward of all immigrants at **all** candidate times of that slot. For spoiled branches bound their whole charge by one apiece. The bound on their physical count established above can be transferred to this coupling.

For a marked ring at u≥0 with reward measure Q(da) on ages a≥0, its contribution to the candidate envelope is Y(u)=∫1_{u+a>L−1} exp(L−u−a)Q(da). It is ≤e and ∫₀^∞ E Y(u)du≤e by Tonelli and total mass ≤1. The Poisson product formula gives a uniform exponential moment of the sum, since exp(zY)−1≤(exp(ze)−1)Y/e for z>0. The full calendar envelopes are iid per padded slot. The three squared bounds (own charge, spoiled count, marked calendar envelope) prove (A). This proof controls the number of distinct affected branches, rather than counting all returns to a frozen host's neighbouring core.

The actual epoch deviations sup_{j≥m}|T_j−T_m−log(j/m)| exceed any fixed ε>0 with probability O_ε(exp(−c_ε m)); a centered sum of independent Exp(j) variables has total variance O(1/m) and centered log mgf ≤O(u²/m) for |u|≤m/2. Use the exponential maximal inequality in both signs and the O(1/m) harmonic-sum error. Thus (A) and Cauchy–Schwarz imply the tail (7) for the discrete correction. All polynomially small failures in this proof are intersected with the size gate, which holds eventually almost surely by C_m/m^μ→X>0.

## Classify the old cells at a fixed time

We use the archived uniform-core local limit and its finite-time clock construction. In the limiting locally finite environment a tag either ends at finite degree with all neighbouring leaf stocks tending to infinity, or has unbounded degree and a stock eventually growing exponentially. For the latter statement, at the first fresh degree hit d a one-leaf birth-death family with rates j,j/d survives with probability 1−1/d and then grows at a positive exponential rate (already rate 1/4 eventually suffices when d≥2, by the preceding geometric argument). Let d→∞. A typical fixed initial tag has only its initial neighbours and at most its own ring count as additional neighbours that are not young leaves. For the finite-degree alternative an eventually bounded adjacent pair is impossible by its positive send rates, and the neighbours of a finite-degree tag therefore have diverging stocks. This applies to initial leaves as well. [Archived physical dichotomy](../../round2/proofs/astra-late/source/round2/open/age-order/late/README.md), [local construction](../../round2/proofs/astra/source/round2/operator.md).

Fix a desired error η>0. Initially discard cells with root degree>D, then cells having an adjacent old core of degree>D'. By local tightness their fraction can be made arbitrarily small with fixed D,D'. By the squared cell count bound above, their eventual number of old hosts can be made ≤η C_m. Each retained cell has at most D+1 original labels. At the intermediate elapsed time U classify the retained original labels as follows.

* Call a core active if its young leaf count ℓ≥K(1+a), where a is its number of neighbours that are not young leaves.
* Call a tag passive if its degree is ≤J and all its neighbours are cores with at least K(1+d_tag) leaves each. Other tags are unresolved.

Take K≥2, so the tests are disjoint. Choose J large first: for the retained finite lists of initial tags the limiting expected number of nonhubs of eventual degree >J tends to zero by bounded convergence. For every subsequently fixed K, as U increases the physical dichotomy implies that the limiting expected number unresolved per original core has limsup ≤this small error. Thus after choosing K sufficiently large for the protection estimates below, choose U sufficiently large to make the unresolved count ≤2η C_m eventually a.s. Constants in these choices may depend on D,D'.

Here is why the fixed-U, bounded per-cell finite-history test obeys the needed almost-sure empirical estimate. Label all vertices present at time zero as old, and retain identities of the bounded list of original leaves of the root. The required tag and neighbour data are in the initial root cell and the cells of its initial neighbours. In the old-cell construction of the cited operator proof, independent oriented old-edge proposals have rate 1/c_z^0 from each source z of initial core degree c_z^0≥1. A time-ordered proposal path from the boundary is necessary for outside information to reach these cells. The sum of expected ordered tuples on simple paths of length l ending at w is at most c_w^0 U^l/l! by simple random-walk path reversal. This is bounded uniformly for the root and its retained old neighbours. The singleton-core case has no boundary. At radius R truncate outside input, and charge the test its maximal value whenever an ordered path across the surrounding annulus could enter the inner cells. This alarm is itself decided by the old drivers in the annulus and has arbitrarily small probability for R large. All old/young flags and leaf identities can be retained by uniform choices among leaves. For fixed R, additionally omit roots with marked balls of radius 2R+4 exceeding a fixed degree cap, losing an arbitrarily small fraction by the empirical local limit. The resulting bounded truncated variables and alarms have a dependency graph of bounded degree given the initial tree: disjoint initial driver balls, with one extra layer for edges, are independent. Colour by at most that degree plus one and apply Hoeffding to each class (deviations at fixed fractions of the total C_m have exponential tails). Their conditional expectations are bounded cylinder functions of initial marked balls, with empirical means tending to their limiting integrals. Sending the deterministic approximation errors down proves the asserted upper estimate. This argument only uses finite U after all the classification parameters have been fixed.

## Protect the classified tags

Condition on the physical state at U. At each active x embed the private young-leaf lower stock from ℓ, with births at its size and one removal at each source ring while positive. The archived bound gives probability at most (√e/2)^ℓ+2exp(−ℓ/8) that this stock ever falls below (ℓ/4)exp(s). Let N(s) count host rings since U. The chance N(s)>√K(s+1) somewhere tends to zero with K by integer-interval Poisson tails. Non-young-leaf neighbours are at most a+N(s). Put them last; on these two good events no host ring hits them if no uniform mark is in the upper strip of width min(1,4(a+√K(s+1))exp(−s)/ℓ). This last failure costs at most 4/K+8/√K by integration. These private sufficient events, made using each active host's own lower stock and marked rings, are independent across active hosts. Consequently the physical number that fail to promote a young leaf at **every** later ring is ≤η C_m eventually a.s. by choosing K large and applying conditional concentration on the slot gate. Each successful active has unbounded degree (infinitely many distinct neighbours promote), hence positive share. Its late branches born after U never receive a return from it.

For the passive list, bound the number that ever gain again. Every donor z adjacent to any listed passive tag has at least K leaves. If z is early, all its early neighbours existed at m, and any such z adjacent to a retained tag has initial degree ≤max(D,D'): for an initially leafy tag this is its initial host, for a core it is an original neighbour. If z is late it has at most one early neighbour. Put the designated passive targets first, at most D_1=max(1,D,D') per donor. With the same lower stock and the uniform strip of width 4D_1 exp(−s)/L_z(U), the chance any one donor ever sends to a designated tag is bounded by a deterministic number tending to zero with K. Sufficient upper indicators from the private stock construction are independent across donor cores (the stock may track all current leaves). Up to the first gain of a passive tag it has only its original donors at U. There are ≤J(D+1) C_m donor incidences and each failing donor is charged at most D_1 tags. Conditional concentration makes the number of failed passive tags ≤η C_m eventually a.s. This may use a separate coupling from active protection, because the asserted failure counts are physical. A successful passive has no future gain, no leaf neighbours and no future crossings. Its existing late neighbours have degrees at least K(1+d_tag), so their remaining total possible reciprocal drop is ≤1/K at this host.

Let A denote the active list at U, an observable set. Its size differs from the physical count H_m of positive-share labels through m by O(η C_m): successful actives are hubs, successful passives are not, and discarded, unresolved, or failed old hosts number O(η C_m). In this count one need only include eventually promoted labels among the discarded cells. Since H_m/C_m→h, the limsup discrepancy between |A|/C_m and h is O(η). The multiplier in this O(η) can be taken absolute by choosing all preceding fractional errors against the stated η. On the exceptional hosts the **physical** weighted charges after L−1 are O(√η C_m) by (A), regardless of how the hosts were selected. Successful passives contribute at most e(D+1)C_m/K. The old-label charge at a successful active is ≤eD/K, by telescoping, since its initial degree is ≤D and its present degree ≥K. Take K large enough also for these errors. It follows already that old-label gains have zero limiting coefficient.

## Young branches of productive hosts

At U give every source in the active list an independent calendar of rate-one rings with fresh virgin marks. Up to any discrepancy, implement the marked internal arrows physically; root-to-exterior choices have no internal effect. On every successful active all post-U rings launch actual branches, and their young-root gains match the indicated marks for all time. The ideal square envelopes for the active list are O(C_m) in sum of squares with the usual high probability by the Poisson calculation in (A). Removing the failed active hosts therefore costs O(√η C_m) on the ideal side as well. This comparison uses the whole list at U, not an independence assertion about future-selected successful hosts.

Handle separately the already crossing edges at U of the successful active hosts. At most O_U(C_m) crossing branches were born up to U by the old-source rate bound and Poisson concentration stopped at the slot gate. All but an arbitrarily small fraction in units of C_m have at most A_0 ordinary vertices at U for a fixed sufficiently large A_0. Indeed a crossing interior of size s receives births at rate ≤s+1 (inside sources plus its sole exterior donor). Different interiors can be dominated at the same time by independent Yule proposals from three at their launches for size plus one. Thin a proposal by the actual total target rate inside that component divided by the proposal population, and choose its internal receiver and source with their conditional weights; use the ordinary transition channels for remaining targets. Launches create a size-two ordinary branch; before promotion the young endpoint is outside the registered core components in question. Incoming from and outgoing to the old host are assigned to their true receiver regions. In particular the promotion of a new crossing is outside every previously registered young branch. This realizes the physical marginal and independent padded proposal tapes. At bounded U their tails for size >A_0 tend to zero; apply independent count concentration to the first O_U(C_m) padded tapes.

At U start fresh inert comparisons in these existing interiors from their actual states; their ordinary starting states of size ≤A_0 form a finite set. No return from a successful active host can change its interiors. In each bounded state the expected reciprocal drop after age L−1−U through infinity tends to zero by monotone convergence of the reciprocal. The independent restart tapes, conditional on the boundary state, give concentration of the sum of these [0,1/2]-valued upper errors (with factor e for the weight). Large interiors cost at most e/2 apiece. This bound applies to the successful-active subset even if its selection depends on the tapes. One may include all existing crossing interiors in these comparisons up to their first inbound send. Thus previously formed branches at time U have negligible contribution in the limiting estimates.

It remains to average post-U marked rings over the measurable list A. For fixed v (enlarge the square envelope's window if v<−1), a ring at u with virgin reward Q contributes ∫1_{u+s>L+v} exp(L−u−s)Q(ds). After integrating u from U to infinity its mean sum per host tends to exp(−v)E Q([0,∞)) as L−U→∞, by Tonelli and dominated convergence. The birth atom has mass 1/2 and the remaining mean mass is 1/2−b. The independent host sums have uniform exponential moments as in (A), hence fluctuations of their average in units C_m tend to zero a.s. by the same centered-moment or exponential bound. This works separately for the nonnegative components and for indicators specifying whether birth preceded L. Countably many fixed v and parameter choices suffice. With the classified estimates, and then η↓0, this proves (6). At the choice of η the bounds for initial arms can be made as small as η after fixing U; no uniform large-age rate of convergence of inert reciprocal degrees is invoked.

The concentration arguments may use separate auxiliary realizations for different physical error bounds or different deterministic starts. They establish the indicated bounds for physical observables by summable probabilities. If a common space is desired, attach each coupling conditionally on the shared physical path. In particular no independence of actual hub labels, or actual cross-edge gains on the full tree, is used.
