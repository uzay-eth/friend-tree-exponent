## A. Actual rates, history and endpoint cases

In continuous time each vertex sends at rate one; each outgoing oriented edge can instead have a fresh independent rate-one stream with independent uniform marks, accepted at `1/D_donor`. At a fixed state this gives total rate `|T|`, with the embedded jump equal to the stated rule. An accepted proposal attaches a brand-new leaf to the *recipient*; the donor does not change degree unless it receives a separate arrow. Only that recipient's degree increases. Independent increments and fresh streams on edges just created implement the same transition rates at every stopping epoch. The resulting Yule size process is nonexplosive. One may sample each next holding time `Exp(|T|)` independently of the next donor/recipient pair and future holding times, so the whole size clock is independent of the embedded tree sequence.

Root at the centre `f` of the size-three tree. Every other vertex begins as a leaf in this rooting (the two initial neighbours of `f` already are leaves at time zero). Upon promotion it has one child, the new leaf. While it stays degree two that child is its unique child forever, and can first get a child only by being promoted. Thus if `v_1,...,v_k` is a nonfounder downward run at observation, the vertices promoted in precisely that order; at each promotion of `v_r` before the next, the descendant subtree of `v_1` consists of those `r` vertices and one leaf, and all earlier `v_i` have remained degree two. For any path not containing `f` the shallowest vertex cannot turn into two children: with its own parent this would be degree three. No assumption about the observed run's exterior degrees is needed. For example, at size three let `f` have leaves `a,b`. Consecutive allowed arrows `f->a`, `a->c`, `c->d`, where `c` and `d` are the successively created leaves, produce a pendant `a,c,d` with leaf `e`. Then `b->f` and `e->d` leave the run `a,c` between the two degree-three vertices `f,d`. Its first hit at threshold two was during the earlier pendant stage. Such bridges are counted, as are subpaths of longer runs, and their own tops are covered by the same argument. Paths through `f` get separate treatment in B.

In the clean stage, extension is the last core sending to its leaf at rate `1/2`. The `2(r-1)` internal directions each spoil at rate `1/2`. The tip leaf sends to its core at rate one, and the exterior parent sends to the top at rate `1/d`. These sum to `r+1/d`; for `r=1` the vacant list of internal edges contributes zero. An exterior parent of a promoted top was already a core (from degree one it could not have had this child and its own parent, and the founder is degree at least two). The reverse, top-to-parent arrow changes no fringe degree; it can lower the external hazard. Once the target length `k` is hit, the bottom may send to its child repeatedly without breaking these first `k` degrees, and the bottom **child** may now be a higher-degree vertex. Its incoming-to-run hazard is then `1/D_child`, not necessarily 1. In general the rate of breakage is `k-1+1/D_child+1/D_parent`; it is bounded uniformly by `k-1` and `k+1/2`. The suppression formula follows by conditioning on the suppressed state history: the only forbidden streams are the two inward boundary directions and the internal directions, all already present, and the probability none has an accepted mark is the exponential of minus their integrated total hazard. In the suppressed trajectory no attachment can introduce another neighbour of the run, which is why this conditioning does not use those same streams to change the edge set.

During clean formation, use local proposal streams with no incoming parent arrow for the upper comparison. A physical successful trial has had no incoming arrow before the first hit, so ignoring all such arrows lets an identical local trial succeed. For the lower comparison forbid all potential incoming proposals of mark at most `1/2`; the parent has degree at least two and cannot lose degree. One can equally use the sharper frozen mark threshold `1/D_parent` at the start. With constant incoming failure rate `a`, the relevant independent exponential race has rates `1/2` and `r+a`; harmless outward sends do not perturb its rates. Hence `q_k(a)` is exactly the product in line 24, and replacing each `r+1/2+a` by `r` or `r+1` gives the factorial bounds. The unique first hit of a nonfounder top occurs when its pendant chain is of length `k-1` and extends. Birth flux times subsequent survival, by the Markov property at this extension, proves the asserted compensator identity (and its discrete version).

## B. A hit at any time, including a founder split

For a path through `f`, there are two initial sides and `k` possible splits `(i,k-1-i)`. If `f` stays degree two it has exactly those two neighbours. Suppress all sends into `f`; until its first physical receipt this cannot change any part of the physical trajectory when driven by the same proposals. In this comparison, f-to-side proposal streams accepted at `1/2` are independent, and there is no interaction between sides through an altered founder degree. On each side retain the first such send (promoting the initial side leaf) but remove all later incoming founder sends. If the side physically succeeded while `f` was still degree two, there cannot have been an additional send into its top before that success: this would permanently raise its degree. Thus a physical path through `f` implies both independent comparison sides hit at least their requested depths, not necessarily simultaneously. For depth zero impose no condition. The sum of the bounds is at most `sum_{i=0}^{k-1} [(i+1)(k-i)]/[i!(k-1-i)!] <= k^2 2^(k-1)/(k-1)!`, by `q_i(0)<=(i+1)/i!` and the binomial formula. This is also a bound for the expected founder path count at an observation epoch.

For each nonfounder top promoted by size `m`, conditional on its promotion history, the upper probability for a hit at **any later time** is `q_k(0)`. Sum over promotion epochs (equivalently, at each size step multiply a promotion indicator by the conditional hit bound). The indicator of promotion by the horizon is known at promotion, so the unconditional sum is at most `E[C_m] q_k(0)`. This gives a bound for a hit at one or more sizes `<=m`, even if that run is gone long before the end of the block. For `m=2^j`, `k=ceil((mu+eps) log m/log log m)` the log of the first bound is `-eps log m+o(log m)`, and the founder term is also summable. ## C. Token supply and genuine independence

A token is a nonfounder degree-two core with a leaf child, initiated at its promotion in the chosen window. A receipt at the token vertex has rate at most `1+1/2`; the leaf child's promotion has rate `1/2`. An attachment at a core can destroy only that core's token; an attachment at a leaf can destroy only its parent's old token and creates a fresh token at the promoted leaf. No event destroys two tokens. Thus assign the transition rates to their unique lost-token classes or to a no-loss class. Independent rate-two proposals per token, thinned using these rates with a recipient selected conditionally in its class, and the remaining no-loss transitions, give the correct tree generator. First proposal waits of all preassigned labels, even unused ones, are i.i.d. `Exp(2)` before revealing the number of promotions. Pathwise, if `J>=M`, each of the first `M` waits exceeding one identifies a surviving token at the end of a unit window. The failure on `J>=M` is therefore a subset of a binomial lower-tail event with parameters `M,e^-2`; an ordinary Chernoff bound gives `exp(-b_0 M)`. No independence between the waits and `J` is needed. This realization may be taken separately for each desired bound; all describe the same tree law.

Using the independent holding times, `tau_n=sum_{r=3}^{n-1} Exp(r)` satisfies `tau_n-log n -> c` almost surely: the centred sum converges by square-summable variances and the means differ from `log n` by a convergent harmonic offset. Consecutive gaps tend to zero, so `|T(t)|e^-t -> Y=e^-c>0` for all real `t`. Claim 6 now gives `C(t)e^-mu t -> X Y^mu` for real `t`, hence `(C(s)-C(s-1))e^-mu s -> (1-e^-mu)X Y^mu>0`. With `M=floor(exp((mu-delta)s))` at integer ends, the binomial failure bounds are summable. This proves the required supply `H(s)>=c_0 exp((mu-delta)s)` eventually on that lattice.

At a trial start, each terminal core and its leaf are disjoint from all others; a terminal core cannot parent another core, and the exterior parent is not a fringe vertex of any trial. For each fringe use its original interior directed streams, both directions of its *own* boundary edge, and fresh streams on its *own* new edges, indexed by the order of local births. These collections are independent conditional on the initial tree, including different boundary edges from a common donor. The event uses an exogenous fixed mark cutoff on the incoming direction, at the start's reciprocal parent degree; allowing outward sends when desired changes only the outside. On this event, no actual parent-to-fringe send can enter; the local history then agrees with the stipulated local history regardless of anything else that happens outside. Thus the sufficient events, not unrestricted physical survival events, are independent. For the fixed-window chain event, integrate the `k-1` ordered extension times in `[0,1]`, each at rate `1/2`, and ban all other outgoing arrows through time 2. The outgoing total intensity on this history is at most `k+1`, and the boundary proposal intensity at most `1/2`; multiplying the two silence factors and the ordered-simplex factor gives probability at least `p_k`. Conditional failure is at most `exp(-H(s)p_k)`; with `k=floor((mu-eps)s/log s)` and `delta<eps`, the product `H(s)p_k` is bounded below by `exp((eps-delta+o(1))s)` on the good-count event. Each success survives over `[s+1,s+2]`. Conditional estimates integrate over the good-count event, and a.s. bad counts occur only finitely often. Borel–Cantelli over integer starts covers every late real time; there is no cross-start independence assumption.

## F. Forest extremes above cutoff two

**Upper.** At block start `s` the old vertices form a connected subtree. Every branch born thereafter attaches to exactly one old anchor, and has only this one boundary edge to the old tree. If a connected retained component meets old vertices, the old intersection `S` is connected: each path joining two old vertices is entirely old. Every new member lies in a branch anchored in `S`. Write `r=|S|` and assume all initial degrees in `S` are ≤`N` (else the event is impossible). If `S` survives at any time in `[s+h,s+h+1]`, its degrees were ≤`N` throughout `[s,s+h]`. Independent old internal edge proposals with marks ≤`1/N` are then compulsory up to `s+h`. The `2(r-1)` directions produce a Poisson count of mean `2(r-1)h/N >=16Nr` for `r>=2` at `h=16N^2`. If `S` survives, these attachments contribute at most `Nr` degree increments; thus the survival event implies a lower tail of the **unconditioned** Poisson count, bounded by `exp(-2Nr)`. For `r=1` use bound one.

By the observation time, at most `Nr` injections of new branches have occurred at anchors in a retained `S`, because each contributes one degree increase. Track the first `Nr` regardless of whether `S` survives. A branch of size `b` has total rate of internal births at most `b`, from its `b` donors, and incremental rate at most one from the old boundary donor. Preassign `Nr` independent Yule trajectories started at two, activated in order at injection. At each Yule ring of a branch, choose whether to realize a birth with probability actual branch rate divided by its envelope population and choose the recipient by its rate. The envelope starts above the branch by one and stays above by at least one, so this constructs the branch rates; injections and all other recipients can use their own remaining rates. This is a marginal bound and can be proved in a different realization than the mandatory-clock bound. Through the common end `s+h+1`, the total possible component size at any observation where `S` is its old intersection is bounded by `r` plus the sum of the `Nr` full-horizon Yule populations (including unused envelopes). No branch born past its first `Nr` at such anchors can matter before a valid observation. With `F_Y(lambda)=E exp(lambda Y_{h+1}^{(2)})`, finite near zero by the geometric Yule law, Markov and Cauchy–Schwarz give for fixed `S` the bound `exp(N-Nr-lambda m/2+r(lambda+N log F_Y(lambda))/2)`. This uses only the inclusion in the intersection of the two bounding events and needs no independence. Choose `lambda` small so the last coefficient is <`1/2`. Each connected old `r`-set in the induced degree-≤`N` forest is the set visited by a depth-first walk of `2(r-1)` steps from a vertex of this forest; there are at most `|T(s)| N^{2(r-1)}` such walks. The resulting geometric series converges since `N-1/2>2 log N` for `N>=2`.

For an all-new component, sum over new branch injections at old anchors. A branch conditionally at injection has probability at most `F_Y(lambda) exp(-lambda m)` to reach size `m` by the common end, by the single-branch envelope, so the union bound is at most this times the expected number of injections, at most `|T(s)|(e^{h+1}-1)`. Taken together these are a conditional bound of `C_N |T(s)| exp(-lambda m/2)` for the supremum over `[s+h,s+h+1]`. The old mark count uses only the interval prior to all observations, and all new volumes are increasing and evaluated at the latest time. Consequently the bounds cover even short-lived components. Use `s=j-h`, `E|T(s)|=3e^s`, and `m=K_N j` with `lambda K_N/2>1` to sum the probabilities over integer `j`. Transfer by the size clock for the a.s. upper bound.
