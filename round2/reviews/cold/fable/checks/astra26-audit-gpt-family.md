## Proof stress points and supplied details

**What is being compared.** At each time a leaf has only one possible sender that can promote it, its parent. At the old-tree stage `r_u` is the sum of actual donor reciprocals. Given first attachment at `u`, the fresh rate at `v≠u` is `r_v−1_{u~v}/[d_u(d_u+1)]`, at `u` it is `r_u+1`, and at the newborn it is `1/(d_u+1)`. Repeated targets contribute exchangeable leaves. Nonadjacent pairs contribute equal masses in two orders, and on an edge the older mark at `u` has net mass `s_v−s_u`. Thus the endpoint at larger `s` is adverse. No pathwise sign is claimed after both placements. All finite unlabelled trees can occur: repeatedly peel a leaf to the edge, then reverse, with every listed recipient having positive probability. The restart proof is uniform in the exact common history because conditional future dynamics depend only on the present tree.

**Classifier directions.** The rescue `B(d,l)` is a lower bound for a mark with a real tracked stock and a permanent core slot. When stock is positive the exact rates of a tracked birth and tracked loss are `l` and `l/d`. An extra gain at the mark supplies a fresh tracked leaf and its target row is no lower, by the rational check. If the mark's real last tracked leaf is promoted, its degree-two neighbour can next send back, before the mark gains elsewhere or the sender reaches degree 32. With mark degree `d` fixed, multiply by sender degree `k` to get rate 1 versus at most `k(k−1+1/d)`. Since `R_d(k)` decreases with `k`, maximal growth is pessimistic. Mark gain elsewhere is success. At stop, either degree increased and a real new leaf permits a fresh bound, or cutoff failure is paid zero. It is not an arbitrary zero-stock lower bound. Positive stock has a monitored hazard at least one; last loss has send hazard at least `1/31` below cutoff. Degree increases finitely often before 20 and between increases stock drops at most finitely often. At degree 20, independent rate-`1/20` proposal removals for each tagged leaf cover its physical promotion; a rejected promotion only discards the tag. Together with rate-one births per tag this gives `1−20^-l` for the survival of at least one tagged family. To avoid assuming “unbounded degree is a hub” with no proof for arbitrary restarts, the tagged population on survival has unbounded leaf stock; the Gamma-minus-Exp estimate of Astra `round2/fringe/hub-spectrum.md:45` from the tagged leaves applies on each stock hit. The probability of zero normalized mass along such hits tends to zero by marginal bounds; physical size Yule normalization is positive a.s. Thus this is an infinite-future lower bound on the correct event.

For a **favourable** parallel leaf use `R_1(p)B(2,1)` for starting parent degree `p<32`, zero otherwise; its own unit arrow to parent is included in the maximum growth rate `k−1+1/1`. On promotion its stock is one at degree two; no independence is needed for the product. In the **serial** middle bonus race, tracked gain/loss have payoffs whose weighted difference from `B(2,1)` is zero. The old-neighbour arrow has hazard `1/p` and buys `B(3,2)−B(2,1)`; gains of the old neighbour simply stop the bonus, leaving the baseline. These hazards can be integrated against the same stopped survival (in particular the gains/losses balance); total hazard ≤`p+3/2+1/p` gives the claimed probability bound. Parent can be arbitrarily high: `g=.16694235` undercuts the uniform baseline gap without any bonus.

For an **adverse** leaf, count other leaves `j`; its final parent has a permanent nonleaf slot *besides* the tag, even in a parallel star (the other placement promoted an old leaf). At every future pending stage `D≥j+2`. At `j≥1` the drift of `1/(j+1)` with send payoff one is `−1/(j+2)+1/D≤0` with the pessimistic minimum immigration; at `j=0` it is nonpositive.
