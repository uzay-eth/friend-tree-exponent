### Independent derivation: why `μ` survives component counting

For the uniform core in `π`, track its promotion age `a` and live newborn environment. The birth identity is `Q_tf=S_tf+∫_0^t Q_s B S_(t−s)f ds` (count the old vertex, then compensate every promotion). With `πQ_s=e^(μs)π`, apply `π`, divide by `e^(μt)`, and let `t→∞`; the old-vertex term vanishes for bounded `f`. Setting `f=1` gives `πB1=μ`, so the newborn environment is `β=πB/μ`, and the comparison age `a` has density `μe^(−μa)` independently of the trajectory. For this tracked core, the degree-gain rate is `D−c+ρ≤D`, so `e^(−a)D(a)` is a nonnegative supermartingale dominated by normalized degree-two Yule growth and has an a.s. limit `W`. On `W>0` its degree is `e^a(W+o(1))`, not simply `e^a` or `e^(μa)`. The latter exponent concerns total core count, not one core's degree. The imported degree law gives `E_β W^μ=A>0` and the Yule bound gives `E W²≤6`.

The component top is the child at a counted boundary edge and does **not** have the uniform-core Exp(`μ`) age law. Its *parent* is sampled from the uniform-core intensity with the factor “number of retained core children”, and the test `D(a)>N`. By the early-history compensation above this factor is `L=log N` to leading order, and the child's normalized age is Lebesgue on `[0,1]`, with the inert kernel at that exact age, for bounded parent-centered ages `y=a−L`. Corrections are negligible in the normalized intensity uniformly on bounded windows and tails are integrable. In particular no extra `D` factor arises; imposing that bias would incorrectly overweight roughly linear degree rather than the logarithmic number of counted edges. There is no independent Exp(`μ`) draw for the child's own age.

After dividing by `N^(−μ)L`, the surviving parent/child measure is therefore

```
μ e^(−μy) 1{w > e^(−y)} dy Lawβ(W)(dw) × [dv P⁰_(Lv)(J,dT)].
```

Put `s=y+log w`, so `s>0` and `e^(−μy)=w^μe^(−μs)`. The mass is `E W^μ=A`, not `A/μ` (the original age density already contained `μ`). After normalization this is a product of the `w^μ`-tilted amplitude, `s∼Exp(μ)` and the independent child kernel in `v`. Equivalently, at `R>r≥1` the same integral starts at `y>log r−log w` and yields mass `A r^(−μ)` times the child probability for each retained rectangle. In particular `R=e^s`, `P(R>r)=r^(−μ)`, and `R` is independent of `V`, hence also of the limiting joint exponents `(μV,V)` using A's same good-coupling estimate for original statuses. The *centered* parent age is `Y*=s−log W*`, which is dependent on `s` (indeed `Cov(Y*,s)=Var(s)>0`, the log moment under the tilted law being finite by the second moment and boundedness of `w^μ|log w|` at zero). At graph level replace `s` by the exact log degree ratio only in the weak mode.

## Independent code, outputs and negative control

`check.cpp` is my C++17 implementation. The inert simulator stores only cores, ordinary leaf stock at each core, and the core edges. Ring selection has a block of weight `1+L` per core and exact uniform neighbor ranks for a core source, including the silent root slot. A leaf source adds a leaf to its core. A root-to-slot proposal advances time but does not change the state. Time steps here are proposal waits of rate `m`, which after thinning give the shape-dependent genuine waiting rate. For the free snapshots I used explicit adjacency (not the inert compressed engine), the exact `T₃` start of the size chain, and DFS after deleting vertices by *original* degree. Assertions independently cross-check marked component counts against top-edge counts, and retained core totals against the original retained vertices. `T₃` time origin is harmless for all sampled nonfounder promotion ages. Compile/run commands below are from this report's directory; all runs used CPU 12, niceness 5 and a timeout below 10 minutes.
