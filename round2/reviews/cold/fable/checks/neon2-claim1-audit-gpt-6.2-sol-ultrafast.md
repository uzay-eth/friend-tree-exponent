### Supplying the proof details that matter

**Stock and null-set counterexample.** For the inert root with `L=l≥3` and `D≥l+1`, gains are at rate ≥`l`, losses at rate `l/D`. The drift of `1/L` until the first hit of 2 is at most `−1/(l+1)+1/[D(l−1)] ≤ −3/(8l)`. Stopped Dynkin, then monotone convergence, give `P(hit 2)≤2/K` and expected integral of `1/L` before the hit ≤`8/(3K)` from stock `K`. Bound the slot kill probability by this integral plus hit probability, obtaining `14/(3K)`. Exactly the same bound applies to a marked outgoing send to a real parent edge from a vertex with a parent and stock `K`: count that send before the stock hit by compensation. To see why R15's converse fails, start inert `J`, first prescribe that the root promotes its only ordinary leaf, leaving the root bare with degree two. Grow a large leaf stock at the new core child by a finite script with no sends to the root. With positive conditional probability the child never sends the root a leaf, by the preceding bound from large stock. Then the root is bare of fixed degree two forever, its reciprocal integral is infinite, and the path has zero weight. This event has positive probability under the inert path law. No assertion that absence of a kill biases nothing about shape is used.

**Amplitude along the arm.** In R35, let `o` and `v` be adjacent old degree-two cores in the arm at distance at least two from its ends and away from the slot; `w` is `v`'s other neighbor. In the first unit, require `o,w` silent and `v`'s first ring to occur and select `o`. Until it does, `v`'s degree stays two because its only two neighbors are silent. This event has probability `(1−e⁻¹)e⁻²/2` and gives `o` a leaf. From time one until its first ring, `o` retains a leaf and exactly two core neighbors, so the probability at its first ring to choose a leaf is ≥1/3. Hence by time two a direct promotion by `o` occurs with probability ≥`(1−e⁻¹)²e⁻²/6`. Spatial-limit §1, (3) is a **positive immigration representation** of the normalized old-cell mean: its direct term is at least `d₂ min_j H(J_j)` times `E ∫ exp(−μt)b_o(t)dt`. Compensate the direct promotions in `[0,2]`; this contribution is at least `d₂ min_j H(J_j)e^(−2μ)` times the preceding probability. This holds uniformly also in every free graft with auxiliary stock `M`; inertia takes over by uniform per-cell convergence and finite-time coupling, exactly the transfer in inert §1. Add these lower bounds over all but `O(1)` old arm cores; do **not** require these flags jointly. Hence `Φ` grows at least linearly while size grows linearly, and `γ_mΦ→∞` along the deterministic arm script. The proof needs the quantitative positive direct-immigration formula, not just positivity of the spatial eigenfunction at each environment.

**Track 3 normalization if used for the gradient.** Its grafted `Φ⁰` is the sum of critical `U_μ` scores and is not simply given as the limit mean in the normalization of the count. From the free resolvent identity in Track 3 Thm. 2(3) and the uniform mean estimate one gets `Φ_free,mean = Ψ⁰(J) Φ_free,Track3` with `Ψ⁰(J)>0`. Compare two free grafts of the same stock `M` where the ordinary planted seeds differ by one leaf. On the Track 3 side the auxiliary-root `f_μ` terms both tend to `1/μ`. On the mean side the ordinary cells converge to the inert means (inert §1); the difference of auxiliary-cell means for the two grafts tends to zero. At fixed elapsed time, couple the auxiliary root clocks and attach paired virgins on rings that promote in both copies. The expected rate of any auxiliary ring selecting a core instead of promoting is ≤`(1+t)/(M+1)` in each copy, so the chance of any such ring on a fixed interval tends to zero. Incoming sends to the auxiliary root change only leaf stock and cannot cause a mismatch of core counts by themselves. All paired virgin descendant branches can agree until the first auxiliary ring selecting a core. The rate-one-per-core moment bound controls the mean error on the failure event, and uniform old-cell convergence transfers the difference to the mean coefficients. This recovers the gradient up to the fixed factor `Ψ⁰(J)` if substituting Track 3. The main proof can instead cite the inert gradient directly and avoid this normalization work.

## Comparisons and precise dependencies

**Fixed event and exact clock law.** Given the fixed seed and edge, the honest event in the original process is `F(n,T₀,e)={T_n=T₀}∩{neither direction along e occurs after τ_n}`. No `Z_u,Z_v` test is included. The killed-product law realizes `F` as an intersection of independent mark-only survival events conditional on `T_n=T₀`. Disjointification then adds only the mark tests described above. Each piece has a Beta law with the parameters of *that* cut, independent of its coefficient pair under the corresponding conditional law. The law is not a fixed Beta mixture merely by conditioning on the union (which has probability one); a single global `U` selected from the pieces is generally a mixture of these Beta laws and can be dependent on the coefficients through the piece. Hub status, time-based cut selection, or independence of the unconditioned physical half processes cannot be inserted into that argument.

**d2 (Fable 2(e), posted at 10:51 UTC before this claim).** d2 establishes adjacent vertices with exponentially growing leaf counts via lower birth–death processes and a conditional-probability martingale. It picks a cut at a large deterministic time using the maximum available minimum endpoint stock. Given the past, d2's two proxies are independent **free** trees with no slots, with exact Yule clocks independently of their embedded free shape limits. Rejection marks couple to the physical future until the first cross decision; the stopped degree hazard gives a mismatch error `O(1/L₁+1/L₂)`. Ordinary free counts at the two roots can differ from slot-counted counts by at most two in total, negligible in the limit. d2 applies the Beta smoothing to the *unconditional proxies given the cut history*, bounds the actual null mass by the mismatch probability, and sends it to zero. There is no need, nor generally a justification in d2's construction, to retain the free proxy product law conditional on its coupling success. Neon-2 uses a fixed-cut countable cover and **planted, survival-biased** coefficients, records the cross decisions as kills, obtains exact equality and conditional independence of the clocks on the surviving event, and sums null masses with no approximation error. Before conditioning, the true birth rates of the side populations are `m_A−1/D_u+1/D_v` and `m_B−1/D_v+1/D_u`; these rates invalidate the naive independent free first-edge construction. d2's a.c. only needs its free positivity/existence (L1) and `0<μ<1`; its other results such as full support involve further hypotheses.

**Astra's separate density proof.** Astra takes the independent unconditioned **inert** product law `Q` and uses `w=exp(−∫(1/D_u+1/D_v)dt)` (elapsed times from the cut) to recover the physical no-cross sublaw. This can bias paths, exactly as R15 needs to acknowledge. It is consistent with Neon-2: for each genuine transition of rate `r` at planted size `m`, the inert joint density is `r exp(−λt)dt`; the survival multiplier over that hold is `exp(−κt)`, giving `r exp(−mt)dt`. In Astra, a first genuine wait is varied under `Q` to prove a.c.; two waits and finite reciprocal integrals give equivalence to Lebesgue. Under Neon-2 the independent full proposal times are available *after* mark selection, unlike the genuine waits under `Q`.

**Strong conclusion: what inputs are used.** The submitted full-support-for-each-surviving-seed route uses the physical inert theorem, §§1–3 (normalized mean, harmonicity, uniform planted one-leaf gradient, count limit identification, positive finite size limit), the quantitative old-cell immigration estimate in Claim 10's `spatial-limit.md` §1, the stock estimate and `1/2<μ<1`. No direct use of Astra Claims 2/5 or 3 except through the earlier renewal/cell/mean inputs; the capped newborn positivity used for the arm is from S6.2 / Claim 10 §1. The broad bounds in the brief more than suffice for `1/2<μ<1`.

**A simpler alternative for just the claimed density** (independently derived; no per-seed full support theorem needed). For any `x>0`, take `0<l<x<h`. Claim 6 gives positive probability of `X<l` and of `X>2^(1−μ) h`. The fixed no-cross cover and the split inequality `min(a,b) ≤ g(u) ≤ 2^(1−μ) max(a,b)` imply that some one-slot seed has positive surviving mass below `l` and some (possibly different) one-slot seed has positive surviving mass above `h`. Join the two roots, reach this free seed with positive probability, and apply the exact split: the two coefficient laws have positive product mass on `a<l<h<b`. Pick a finite point of the support of that product distribution strictly satisfying the inequalities, and a smaller neighbourhood of positive mass. For the chosen coefficients the branch from `a` to the interior maximum covers `x` regularly since the maximum exceeds `b`; as before an interior compact branch proves measure domination by `c_x dy` near `x`. This cut need not be a whole piece in the size-ordered partition, so don't simply add its density to the sum. First build the l.s.c. version `p₀` from the disjoint partition with zero endpoint convention; this needs no lower bounds. For a countable subcover of these open neighbourhoods replace `p₀` by the pointwise maximum of itself and the supremum of `c_j 1_{I_j}` (open `I_j`, each dominated locally by the measure). **Logical strength.** Equivalence to Lebesgue gives a.e. positivity, and one can alter a representative on a null set to force literal pointwise positivity, with no regularity. It does not imply a positive l.s.c. version or local positive domination. Example: a probability density proportional to `exp(−x)|x−1|` on `(0,∞)` is positive a.e.; any positive l.s.c. value at 1 would force a uniform positive lower bound nearby, contradicting arbitrarily small essential infima. Full support plus a.c. does not even imply equivalence: take a positive integrable density proportional to `exp(−x)` off a positive-measure nowhere-dense fat Cantor set in `[1,2]` and zero on that set. Neon-2's conclusion implies both Astra's equivalence and d2's full support, and contradicts neither.
