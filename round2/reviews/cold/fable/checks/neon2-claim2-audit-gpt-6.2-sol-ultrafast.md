## 1. Conditional mean bounds

At size `n≥3`, `a=Σ_{x∼v}1/D_x ∈ [k,d]`, since each of `k` leaves has degree 1. In one step, `D` increases at probability `a/n`, and `L` increases at the same probability, or decreases with probability `k/(nd)` when `v` rings and targets a leaf. The two events have different targets. Therefore

* `E[D'/(n+1)]=(d+a/n)/(n+1)≤d/n`;
* `E[(L'−1)/(n+1)]=((k−1)+(a−k/d)/n)/(n+1)≥(k−1)/n` since `k/d≤1`.

These are conditional calculations. Apply conditional Jensen to the positive part for the second. The bounded sub/supermartingales converge; the first limit is `Z`, and the second limit is bounded by `Z` pathwise. Bounded convergence gives both conditional bounds. At `P3` the centre's lower mean is `(2−1)/3=1/3`. The end's next degree is `1+Bernoulli(1/6)`, so its conditional mean upper is `7/24`. Half their difference is `1/48` by the rooted-law identity.

## 2. Hub lower: tags, escape and the Fable comparison

Start a root with degree `d`, `k` tagged leaf neighbours (all its current leaves), and `c=d−k` permanent initial core neighbours. Tag only new leaves from a tagged leaf's ring. Then with `j` tags, births have rate `j`. Tagged promotions have rate exactly `j/D` where `D≥max(d,j+c)` since degree never decreases and the `c` original core neighbours cannot disappear. All other events preserve the count. Any assertion that *all* untagged neighbours at later times are permanent would be false: for example, an adjacent core may ring into the root and make an untagged leaf. The report only needs permanence of the **initial** untagged neighbours, as it explicitly says.

Couple a lower birth-death count to this tag process. At equality share the rate-`j` births and all physical deaths, add extra comparison deaths to rate `j/max(d,j+c)`; off equality use unit jumps, which cannot cross. The processes are nonexplosive (physical total size is Yule; comparison births are at most linear and deaths are bounded). The rates at any positive count in a finite band admit a uniform positive chance of consecutive deaths, so survival implies reaching every level. With `s_i=f(i+1)−f(i)`, the first-step equation is `s_j=s_{j−1}/max(d,j+c)`, `f(0)=0`, `f(b)=1`. Thus `f(k)=Σ_{i<k}p_i/Σ_{i<b}p_i`, `p_0=1`, `p_i/p_{i−1}=1/max(d,i+c)`. As `b→∞` the hit events decrease to survival (a finite extinction time cannot have infinitely many prior jumps). For `i≥29`, the tail ratios are bounded by `1/M` for `M=max(d,29+c)`, so `Σ_{i≥29}p_i≤p_28/(M−1)`. A second lower chain has per-tag death `1/d`; for `d≥2` the recurrence sums a geometric series giving survival `1−d^(−k)`. Max of the two bounds and the zero convention are valid.

**Positive mass justification.** At any stopping time with at least `b` physical leaf neighbours pick `b` tags. Continue each killed tag's descendants as phantom Yule families; thus the full process `Y` is a Yule count from `b`, the tags `B` are a subset, and the total removal rate is `≤1`. The conditional expected normalized phantom stock up to time `t` is `≤ ∫_0^t e^(−s)ds≤1`; each lost tag seeds one independent phantom family with mean `e^(t−s)`. Let `G=lim e^(−t)Y_t ~ Gamma(b,1)` and `W=lim e^(−t)D_t=SZ`, with `S=lim e^(−t)N_t>0` by the total Yule process. Fatou and `B≤D` give `E[(G−W)_+]≤1`, so `P(W=0)≤P(G<b/2)+2/b≤6/b` by Chebyshev. This bound is uniform over the starting tree, without independence of `G,W,S`. Apply it at the first hit of `b` by the comparison, using the strong Markov property for the complemented process (or stop on the first physical hit). The event of comparison survival and `Z=0` is bounded by `6/b` for every `b`. This supplies the survival-to-hub implication.

**Fable `B(L,c)`.** Use the whole stock instead of tags. Gain rate `j+ρ` where `ρ≥0` comes from core neighbours, loss rate `j/D≤j/(j+c)` since the initial `c` core neighbours remain. At equality share a subset of births and all losses, and the same recurrence gives `a_i/a_{i−1}=1/(i+c)`. With partial through `J`, the tail is `≤a_J/(c+J)` (d1 uses 24); the lead's bound takes `a_40` explicitly and bounds following ratios by `1/(c+41)`. Neon-2's model denominator is at least as favourable at every step (`max(d,j+c)≥j+c`): the lower chain also pathwise dominates the comparator with only `j+c`, so its **true comparison survival** is at least `B(L,c)`. The finite rational tail approximations are different. Fable can take the maximum with `1−upper(w)` via the adjacent-hub theorem, a separate and often substantial improvement. This, and different upper bounds, explain stronger aggregate Fable hub lower certificates at smaller size.

## 3. Arrow upper: derivation and Fable comparison

For each existing neighbour `x` of the root `v`, kill at the first arrow `x→v` (a ring of `x` selecting `v`). Before killing, tag all `r` other initial leaves of `x` and their own ring-born copies. `v` contributes a permanent degree slot whether or not it is a leaf; the `c` other initial untagged neighbours are core. At stock `j`, tag births have exact rate `j`; death and success have **exact** rates `j/D_x` and `1/D_x` with a shared denominator `D_x≥j+1+c`. New untagged leaves and their promotions do not change the tag count. For payoff `f(j)` before success and 1 afterwards the actual drift is

```
j(f(j+1)−f(j)) + [j(f(j−1)−f(j)) + 1−f(j)]/D_x.
```

The numerator is nonnegative for decreasing `0≤f≤1`, hence maximal drift is at minimum degree. For `f=min(1,2/(j+1))`: at 0 drift zero; at 1 birth drift is `−1/3` and all else zero; at `j≥2`, the square bracket is 1, so drift is `−2j/((j+1)(j+2))+1/(j+1+c)≤0` because `j+2≤2j`. Bounded stopped supermartingale at deterministic `t`, then monotone limit of the success event as `t→∞`, gives the upper bound, without an a.s. stopping assumption. To improve on `0≤j≤30`, set boundary `f(0)=1`, `f(30)=2/31`; equate maximal drift to zero on `1…29`. Multiplication by `j+1+c` gives the rates in the report. A tridiagonal system (or the finite absorbed jump chain with payoffs) gives the solution; exact checked monotonicity makes it a physical supersolution. Stop at success or boundary, then apply the already-proved envelope from the boundary. On paths with neither success nor boundary by time `t`, discarded payoff is nonnegative. Finally if `Z_v>0` then its degree must gain again. The source of its first future gain is necessarily one of its current neighbours because it gains new neighbours only on a degree gain. Union bound and clip. A stronger event than necessary is charged: a later `x→v` counts even if another neighbour gave the first gain.

**Fable `F_c(k)` for leaf root.** This compares the whole parent leaf stock `k`, *including* the marked leaf until promotion, so births have rate at least `k`, unmarked losses `(k−1)/D`, and marked success `1/D`. At equality in the rate bounds (`D=k+c`) the equation is `k(k+c)Δ_+ F+(k−1)Δ_- F + 1−F=0`; the `k=1` boundary requires no value at 0. Monotonicity and range make the numerator of the promotion terms nonnegative; extra actual births only reduce drift. For `c≥1`, `f(k)=1/k` has birth term `−1/(k+1)` and combined promotion numerator 1 at `k≥2` (zero at `k=1`). Thus the d1 tighter boundary `1/61` at `k=61` is valid. For any `c≥0` use `g(k)=1/max(1,k−1)`. At `k≥3` the birth term is `−1/(k−1)` and the marked-leaf promotion numerator is `1+1/[(k−1)(k−2)]`, bounded by `k/(k−1)`; at `k≤2`, use `g=1`. Thus the lead's cutoff boundary `1/40` at `k=41` and d1's star boundary are valid. In a star before the first unmarked promotion, births have exact rate `k` and core ring rate 1; after unmarked promotion the leaf root sits at a parent of `k−1` leaves and one core neighbour. This yields d1's backward mixture exactly with a boundary upper bound. **Wrong intermediate estimate in d1 report line 17:** the claimed bound `−1/(k−1)+1/k` is not the drift upper in the star: at `k=3`, birth contributes `−1/2`, promotion `+1/2`, so drift is zero, not `≤−1/6`. The correct nonpositive-drift calculation is in its next sentence and above; d1's certificate survives.

**Fable core-root bound `1/max(1,L_w−1)`.** For a *core* target no marked leaf counts in the stock; along a chosen neighbour arrow, all `k` leaves are possible losses at rate `k/D_w`, success `1/D_w`, and `D_w≥k+1`. Births are at least `k`. At `k≥3` the same birth term is `−1/(k−1)`, while the promotion numerator is `(k²−3k+4)/((k−1)(k−2))≤(k+1)/(k−1)` because the difference in numerators is `2k−6≥0`. At `k≤2`, `g=1` handles the boundary. This proves the upper envelope, independently of edge positivity. The classifiers invoke it for a bare core (and use 1 otherwise); the same arrow argument in fact applies to any core. Neon-2 instead tags a subset of births and uses the finite boundary solve for all targets, without an edge-theorem improvement in its lower endpoint. Its bound for a leaf root may be weaker because excluding its contribution to parent's Yule growth ignores a guaranteed rate-one birth until its promotion.

## 5. Monte Carlo diagnostics

Until all tracked labels are born, direct discrete births with independent rate-`n` waiting times produce the correct marked state. After this, each rate-one core ring clock is independent of all unsampled leaf rings. A stock of `k` surviving leaves produces a total of `k+NB(k,e^(−Δt))` leaves over an untouched interval of duration `Δt`; this follows by independent geometric rate-one Yule families. The gamma-Poisson sampling has scale `e^Δt−1`, giving the same negative-binomial law. Touch the stock exactly when an intervention modifies a count, promotes a leaf, or needs a degree for routing; a mark lives at its same parent until chosen for promotion, and conditional on total stock all current leaves are equally selectable by the core. A promoted vertex receives one leaf, switches to a core clock with exponential residual waiting time, and is an extra core neighbour. Sum all existing leaves plus core count for `N`. The expected population `2e^13=884826.784…` follows from total rate equal to population. The claimant CSV's mean is within one nominal SE of this value. Its `D≥20` and threshold columns are finite-time, and the diagnostic demonstrates neither full adjacent stochastic dominance nor a bias-controlled estimator for `P(Z>0)`. The tiny-time early-return branch of the simulator counts absent tracked labels with degree 0; at the production cutoff the probability of not yet reaching 25 is vanishingly small, and the degrees of those present are sampled correctly.
