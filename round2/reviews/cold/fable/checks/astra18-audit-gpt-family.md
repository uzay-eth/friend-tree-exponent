### Size 204: an independent rational certificate

Reconstruct the star centred at 1 through size 203, then attach 204 to leaf 2. The star history has probability `∏_{j=2}^{202}(j−1)/j=1/202`; the last attachment has probability `1/(203·202)`. Now `D₁=202`, `D₂=2`, and the parent of older leaf 203 has precisely 201 leaves including 203 and always at least one core neighbour. Use d1's marked-leaf classifier: at current stock `k`, set `f(k)=1/k` with value 1 at marked promotion. For `k≥2` its gain drift is at most `−1/(k+1)` from unit transmitting leaf clocks; unmarked and marked promotions together add exactly `1/D₁`, since `1−f(k)+(k−1)(f(k−1)−f(k))=1`. This is at most `1/(k+1)`. At `k=1` the success value already equals `f`; optional stopping bounds eventual promotion by `1/201`. Thus `P(Z_203>0|T_204)≤1/201`.

Independently, suppress all rings of the younger leaf's parent (2) up to its first rate-one ring, which is independent of the suppressed process. The younger leaf cannot be promoted meanwhile; `E D₂(s)≤2e^s` by the degree gain rate. At that ring the promotion probability, averaged over time and Jensen's inequality, is at least `∫₀^∞ e^(−s)/(2e^s)ds=1/4`. After promotion the younger vertex has `(l,c)=(1,1)`. The true leaf count has upward rate ≥ its current leaf count and loss rate `L/(L+c_t)` with `c_t≥c` always. A chain with rates `j,j/(j+c)` and zero absorbing is coupled below by matching unit jumps at equality. The first-hit difference recursion gives escape probability `B(l,c)=Σ_{j<l}a_j/Σ_{j≥0}a_j`, `a_j=c!/(c+j)!`. Nonabsorption forces arbitrarily large leaf counts, implying a hub by the phantom bound, or by d1's unit-removal stock and the stopped-martingale estimate. In particular `B(1,1)=1/(e−1)>4/7` since `e<11/4` (sum through degree 3 and bound the remainder by a geometric series of ratio `1/5`). Therefore `P(Z_204>0|T_204)≥1/7>1/201`. ### Upper endpoint and comparison with stars

Every fixed label has a positive tail at every `x<1` by the append proof, although no label puts mass at 1 by its Pólya cap. For a seed label a centred star exists with probability `1/2` at size 3 (not determined by label alone). For later labels a full star centred at that label is unreachable after its birth: old edges persist, and in particular 1–2 is not incident to it. In fact the smallest possible defect `B=n−L_u` among its core states is `u` for `u≥3`: at its birth every older vertex other than its parent is outside its neighbour set forever, and its parent is already core after the birth; itself also counts toward the defect. The proposed star-at-1 then promotion, followed by appending to `u`, achieves that defect. The defect can remain finite while size tends to infinity in the threshold-dependent histories. Thus even if `x` is arbitrarily close to 1, the fixed older label has a uniform polynomial **lower bound** and the later label's beta exponent tends to infinity. A sharp endpoint exponent is not proved. No single all-append infinite history of positive probability is needed; even for a seed-centred star the probability of staying such a star forever is zero (finite star probabilities `1/(n−1)`).

Independent endpoint check without the gamma calculation: at the reached pure-append state the bounded discrete leaf submartingale gives `E[1−Z_u|T_n]≤(B+1)/n`. Simply take `n=max(m,ceil(2(B+1)/ε))`; conditional Markov bounds `P(Z_u≤1−ε|T_n)≤1/2`. This yields the same strict-tail polynomial lower bound by the append product, even with a smaller `A_u`. Thus the endpoint comparison does not require any interpretation of gamma ratios for the physical tree.
