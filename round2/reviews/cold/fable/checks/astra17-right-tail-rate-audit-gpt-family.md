### Why the Gamma clock does not change this proof

The hold of rate `j` is independent of the arrow at size `j` and all other arrows, by the total vertex ringing rate `j`. The *whole* holding-time sequence is therefore independent of the embedded growth. From a deterministic size-`m` seed, `W_m=lim_t e^{-t}|T(t)|~Gamma(m,1)` and `Z_T=W_m^μ X_T`, so `Φ(T)=Γ(m+μ)E_T X_T/Γ(m)`. This gives exactly `Y_m=E[X|F_m]`; the edge-to-three wait gives `Φ(edge)=2Φ(T₃)/(2+μ)`, not identical continuous amplitudes at sizes two and three. The proof never estimates `P(X>x)` by dividing two rare continuous-time events. Conditioning on physical time or replacing the clock by a typical value would not preserve a tail constant.

For comparison only: if `−log P(X>x)~I x^p`, the independent Gamma product yields `−log P(Z>z)~λ z`, with `λ=min_{v>0}{v+Iv^{−μp}}=pI^{1/p}(p−1)^{−μ}`. Set `W_2=vz`; then `x=z^a v^{-μ}`, and the Gamma density has exponential cost `v z`. For a proof, bound the integral by compact subintervals in `v` using the uniform tail squeeze `(I±η)u^p` above fixed `u_0`; the part `v<ε` costs at least `(I−η)ε^{-μp}z` and `v>M` at least asymptotically `M z`. A small interval about the minimizing `v` gives the reverse bound; send `η` and interval width to zero. Thus a **certified** continuous-time tail slope could test `I`, but is not equal to `I` and is not provided by the log of finite-time samples.

## What the 335,000-run log tests

The production log reports finite-time `Z` and implied mean, variance and skewness of `X`, not individual large `X` values. It contains no raw runs. Even for directly observed `X`, the 95% binomial upper probability from zero exceedances in 335,000 draws is `8.942444×10⁻⁶`. For the *observed `Z`* at `z=5`, the reported cost `6.675` corresponds to about `423` exceedances, with successive fitted slopes still moving `1.442→1.856` over the displayed range. This is not evidence of stabilization to the product-law slope. If the rate theorem holds, integration of the two Weibull bounds with rates `I±ε` implies `(Γ(1+k/p)/E X^k)^(p/k)→I` as `k→∞`. Plugging in the log's first three implied moments gives `1.5963, 1.5245, 1.4810`; these are low-order diagnostics, neither inequalities nor asymptotic estimates. No credible test of `I` from this log: there are no high-order moment estimates with tail control and no demonstrated tail regime, even for `Z`.
