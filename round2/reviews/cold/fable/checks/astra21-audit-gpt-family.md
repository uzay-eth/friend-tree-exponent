## Common limiting argument and exact inputs

Use `ε=+1` lower, `ε=−1` upper, `λ` the appropriate original Neon trial and `s` the new trial (take `s=λ` at `abff32c`). Let `P` denote the version's potential and `Q` the sum of its retained nonnegative physical rewards. The statement to prove on finite trees rooted at the first core is

```
ε (ℒ−s) P(T) ≥ m(C(T)−1) + Q(T).
```

Here `m=0` at `abff32c`, is positive at `80fe026`, and is negative at `02d0bd8`. Write `F(t)=E P(T_t)` and `M(t)=E C(T_t)`. For any fixed `a>0`, integrate on `[t,t+a]`, divide by `M(t)`, and use the common normalized mean limits. The boundary term tends to `(exp(μa)−1)πP`; the integrated potential and charge terms tend to `πP` and `πQ` times `∫₀ᵃ exp(μu)du`. The same integral multiplies `m`, since `M(t+u)/M(t)→exp(μu)` and `1/M(t)→0`. Thus

```
ε (μ−s) πP ≥ m + πQ ≥ m + R = N.
```

The finite-tree Dynkin identity is legitimate on compact time intervals: `n(T_t)` has Yule moments, each jump rate sum is `n`, and `P` is bounded by a constant times `C≤n`; even the crude `O(n²)` absolute drift bound is integrable. The asymptotics and boundedness give a uniform dominating bound on each fixed time window. **Only after `N>0` has been established** may one replace positive `πP` by an upper ceiling `B` and deduce `ε(μ−s)≥N/B`. In particular the upper witness gives `μ≤s−N/B`, and a negative pointwise margin does not need to have any pointwise sign repaired. One averages the additive inequality, not a quotient of instantaneous quantities. This is the argument in `renewal/README.md:31`; Claim 9's Laplace comparison is an analogue, not a needed stronger assertion. In Laplace form multiply the integrated identity by `z−μ` and let `z↓μ`, with the finite initial boundary term vanishing.
