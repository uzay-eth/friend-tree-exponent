## Independent exact checks and lower-bound mechanism

For a state of ordinary size `v`, all `v` rate-one clocks are feeding births except the root's choice of its silent slot, a channel of instantaneous rate `1/D_root`. Hence until the next genuine event the holding time is exponential of rate `v−1/D_root≥v−1/2`. Every visited size occurs in one uninterrupted interval; each next jump succeeds a.s. and there is no explosion by Yule domination. Integrating occupancy to infinity therefore gives `E[(v−1/D_root_at_entry)^-1]≤1/(v−1/2)`. Integrating only through `L=log N` and dividing by `L` proves the point-mass bound. For `v=2` the exact truncated mass is `(2/3)(1−N^-3/2)/log N`. For `v=3` the first real birth is a top gain with probability `2/3` (new rate `8/3`) or a leaf promotion with probability `1/3` (new rate `5/2`), giving full occupation `(2/3)(3/8)+(1/3)(2/5)=23/60`. Our independent code expands the finite-time occupancy as a rational combination of exponentials on chronological labeled states; analytic integration gives exact full occupations and also allows evaluation at finite `L`. No use of the claimant's shape oracle.

## Comparison with Fable

**Sampling and ages.** Fable Claim 4(a) samples uniformly from *all cores*. Their promotion ages have tail `C(t-a)/C(t)→e^(-µa)`; this is not age since physical birth. The intensity of new cores in the full population is growing exponentially on this scale. The uniformly counted cutoff component, by contrast, is usually made at a promotion after `s0` by a deleted parent, from a selected set of hubs already present at `s0`. From just those clocks there are `K` initiating rings per unit time over an interval of length `L-o(L)`, so the conditional age intensity is approximately constant. Integrating it gives `KL`, and `log(volume)≈age` explains the uniform log law. These two age laws do not concern the same population (or the same choice of root). In particular, selecting a surviving core biases the component by its number of cores, favoring large/old branches. Selecting an ordinary vertex without conditioning on the component containing a core instead sees an isolated hub leaf with probability tending to one at fixed cutoff. The Fable report's fixed-age high-parent coverage bounds are for *core mass* and allow vanishing coverage even while young components occur with positive finite-size component frequencies. The Fable report's typical-core escape statements are consistent, but beyond what its own Claim 4 claims.

**Coupling.** At a promotion stopping time, with actual parent degree `d`, future `parent→that child` arrows are contained in a rate-`1/d` Poisson thinning because degrees increase. For known horizon `a`, probability of the first incoming arrow is at most `1−exp(−a/d)`; outgoing root-to-parent arrows do not change the intrinsic path. Neon-2 uses exactly this first-incoming-arrow mechanism, plus a missed mark whenever the same initiating clock chooses *any* current core neighbor rather than a new leaf. Its envelope `4K_* t_-^-3 K_+` is per **all** hub-to-core sends, from `c≤2K_*t_-` and `d≥t_-^4/2`; for an individual parent-to-child path, the corresponding amount is at most `2a/t_-^4`, consistent with `1−exp(−a/d)≤a/d`. The factor 2 on sends counts two distinct potential discrepancies, so no factor-of-two disagreement in per-child coupling probability. Astra 20 uses a deterministic lower degree at time `b` to obtain the weaker linear version `t e^{y-b}` before removing future selection. The stronger Fable all-ages leaf-stock estimate is unnecessary here. Fable's prospective data had no incoming event in either sample; intervals for other fringe differences are far too wide to resolve a coupling error of order `10^-5`, and retrospective groups are subject to final-state selection.

**Which numerical quantities are comparable.** Fable `sim.cpp` lines 182–219 counts `K=M` by exactly the same original-degree rule, retaining singleton cores. The tables of `M` and `C` in `summary.txt` can thus test the finite-size trend `M/C≈A N^-µ log N`. From those means, the inferred `M N^µ/(C log N)` for the fixed cutoffs 10,30,100,300 is `.6828,.6752,.6845,.6807` at `10^6`, and `.6851,.6843,.6829,.6892` at `10^7`. These are ratios of means (not means of ratios). My independent fixed-cutoff means of the per-tree ratios were `.6805,.6758,.6769,.7026` at `2^20`, and `.6830,.6779,.6800,.6775` at `2^24` (tree SEs `.0027,.0037,.0060,.0119` and `.0032,.0029,.0033,.0082`). They are close on this finite range. Persistence of approximate scale across cutoffs and sizes is consistent with Theorem 1, not proof for growing cutoffs. Fable doesn't export per-component ordinary volumes; `sz` is accumulated but then summed before output. Its core-count weighted/unweighted histograms, depth, total volume, pooled recent-fringe pairs, and age/mark summaries cannot reconstruct the CDF of `log ordinary V/log N` and cannot test the asserted shape TV. `young root` is corroborative only: for fixed `N=300` at `10^7` it is `.550`, close to the idealized ratio `3/log 300≈.526`, but promotion age of the real top is an extra field not present in Theorem 1, and `N` here is fixed. There is no empirical apples-to-apples volume CDF between the supplied Fable tables and the Neon-2 theorem.

## Gaps and limits of the result

1. **No blocking theorem gap found.** Line 191 must be restricted as in the audit; subsequent formulas already respect the needed restrictions. At line 180 the independent tolerance with fixed bracket is necessary for the stated little-oh, although a bound proportional to the bracket width also proves the final squeeze. The countable coupling extension and the conditional-at-promotion estimates above supply written details, not new stochastic hypotheses.
2. **Unsupported board parenthetical.** `neon2.md:109` adds “isolated surviving singleton cores receive `Θ(K)` mass from early non-hubs”, with `K` on the `(n/N)^µ` scale. Nothing in the pinned proof gives that two-sided claim, or identifies its origin. The available early-branch and error estimates are only `o(K log N)`, and the ideal mixture has exactly zero singleton mass. One can conclude `ν(V=1)→0` under Theorem 1, but no fixed-shape intensity times `K^-1` from this proof. I did not disprove the parenthetical; its absence from the formal theorem matters.
3. **TV regimes are separated.** No sharp boundary between `c0 log n` and `n^{µ/(1+µ)}`, no proof of volume-TV convergence below the polynomial obstruction exponent (outside what log-cutoff full TV gives), and no assertion of equality in the TV lower bound except the forced `α=1` consequence. The label `explicit c0` gives a formula in the symbolic spectral gap; no certified numerical value of that gap is given here.
4. **Sampling stays component-uniform.** No simultaneous limit for a core-weighted or vertex-weighted component shape, component extrema, exact age/degree-mark joint law, fixed-shape intensities, or a concentration rate for all growing cutoffs follows from the two statements. Neither the proof nor replay tests resolve original-paper variants that take `N/n` bounded away from zero or hold `N` fixed while asking for closeness to the uniform inert mixture; the fixed-`N` empirical stationary law is Astra 19's separate input.
