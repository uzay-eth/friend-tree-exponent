## 1. Rates and weight (S2.2; applies also to the cap)

**1.1 — PROVED.** Every added physical vertex is a leaf with an older parent. A leaf can only first become a core when that parent sends to it; at that instant the parent is a core. Parent degrees never decrease, so for every subsequent time the assigned mark satisfies $`1/D_{\rm parent}\le a_v`$, both with and without the cap (initially this is equality without the cap, and follows from $`m(D)\le D`$ with it). Intrinsic additions and copied fringes preserve the constraints: adding a root leaf only weakens the child inequalities, and declining to track the parent of a copied root only removes a constraint. Thus all claimed rates are nonnegative. In the following write $`m(k)=k`$ for the uncapped case.

**1.2 — PROVED.** For a particle configuration use mass $`X=\sum_S |S|`$, including multiplicities. For a particle with size $`n`$, intrinsic rate is $`n-p_S`$: count all rings at unit rate except root selections of the slot. Intrinsic jumps add one to mass. Death rate is at most $`1/2`$; direct births have rate at most one and add two. For paired copies of a child of size $`n_Y`$ the rate-weighted added mass is $`a_Y n_Y+p_S`$; its sum over children is at most $`n`$, since both coefficients are at most $`1/2`$ and the child subtrees are disjoint. Total rate is therefore bounded by a constant times $`X`$; any positive jump adds at most $`X`$. For each integer $`k\ge1`$, $`(X+v)^k-X^k\le k(2X)^{k-1}v`$ for $`0\le v\le X`$. Hence the generator of $`X^k`$ is at most $`C_kX^k`$. Stop at mass $`\ge M`$; the finite stopped jump process gives by integration and Grönwall $`\mathbb E X_{t\wedge\tau_M}^k\le X_0^k e^{C_kt}`$. Bounded mass implies bounded total jump rate; the mass hitting bound then proves non-explosion and Fatou gives the unstopped moments. The same argument for physical size uses rate $`n`$, increment one; all moments on bounded time intervals are finite in both systems. The bounds for stopped processes also give uniform integrability of stopped variables with any fixed polynomial growth by using larger $`k`$.

**1.3 — PROVED.** The normalised weight has root contribution at least $`(7/10)/(1/2+d)=14/13>1`$. Each core has a core parent counted in $`c`$ (the slot for a planted root), so $`h\le1`$ on all planted cores; also $`0\le w_x\le1`$ and $`a_S+d\ge d`$. Consequently $`W\le n/d`$. Since $`W(J_a)=1/(a+d)`$, the direct-birth drift is $`BW=b_S/(1/m(D_{\rm root})+d)\le 1/d`$.

**1.4 — PROVED.** All leaf additions without promotions leave the weight unchanged. When a vertex of degree $`k`$, core-neighbour count $`c`$, and path factor $`w_x`$ promotes a leaf, its unnormalised weight increment is exactly $`w_x[h(c+1)-h(c)+1/(1+d m(k))]`$: its own count goes up one and the only new core has $`h(1)=1`$. Its promotion rate is $`(k-c)/k`$. It suffices to bound the product of rate and the bracket by $`q h(c)`$. For $`m(k)=k`$, multiply the desired gap by $`k(20+3k)`$; the result for $`c=0,1,2,3,4`$ is respectively

| $`c`$ | Polynomial | Check |

|1| $`153k^2/100-41k/4+17`$ | values $`131/50,1/50,12/25`$ at $`2,3,4`$; successive differences are $`(306k-872)/100>0`$ for $`k\ge4`$ |

In rows with a negative discriminant the leading coefficient is positive. For $`c>4`$, the weight increment bracket is the same positive number as at 4 and the promotion rate decreases. With the cap and $`k\ge20`$, the bracket is at most $`1/4 < q(7/10)=63/250`$. This proves $`L^0W\le qW`$ and also $`L_{\rm phys}Z\le qZ`$, with the founder's $`c=0`$ allowed. The latter has $`Z(T_3)=2`$; since $`Z\le 2|T|`$, compensation and Grönwall give $`\mathbb E Z(T_t)\le2e^{qt}`$.

**1.5 — PROVED.** A root leaf addition does not affect $`W(Y)`$, so $`W(Y^+)=W(Y)`$. Write $`H(S)=(a_S+d)W(S)`$. The path-factor definition gives $`H(S)=h(c_{\rm root})+\sum_Y a_Y W(Y)`$. Thus the two copies together pay exactly the second term in this formula; subtracting death gives $`RW=L^0W+dW-h(c_{\rm root})\le\rho W-h(c_{\rm root})`$. The full bound on the generator is $`(R+B)W\le(\rho+1/d)W`$ by $`W\ge1`$. Compensate the sum of the weights in a particle configuration and integrate the respective drift bounds (or apply the integrating-factor supermartingale stopped as in 1.2). This gives $`R_tW\le e^{\rho t}W`$ and $`P_tW\le e^{(\rho+1/d)t}W`$.

## 2. Physical first moment (S2.3; applies also to the cap)

**2.1 — PROVED.** Write $`F_f(T)=\sum_{v\ne o, \ v\text{ core}} f(S_v)`$ and $`i_f(T)`$ for the founder expression inside $`I_tf`$. Then
```math
L_{\rm phys}F_f=F_{(R+B)f}+i_f.
```
To see this pointwise, for each preexisting nonfounder core, intrinsic changes account for the change in its own score from all rings in its fringe except selection of the parent slot (that selection does not change its own fringe). The only external ring that changes that score is a send from its parent to its root, at actual rate $`p_{\rm parent}`$; the required change is $`p_{\rm parent}[f(Y^+)-f(Y)]`$. Charge this to the death debit $`-a_Yf(Y)`$ in its own term and the two copy credits $`(a_Y-p_{\rm parent})f(Y)+p_{\rm parent}f(Y^+)`$ in its parent's term. For founder children the same credits are in $`i_f`$. A promotion adds the promoted vertex's own score $`f(J_{1/m(D_{\rm parent})})`$, paid by the direct birth of its parent (in $`i_f`$ at the founder). The changes of the scores of its preexisting ancestors are already counted intrinsically. In particular a ring may change several scores at once: these are increments of separate summands, not independent physical events. These are all ways the sum can change.

**2.2 — PROVED.** The founder copies have weight sum $`\sum_Y a_Y W(Y)=Z(T)-h(c_o)`$. Its direct birth costs at most $`1/d`$. By 1.4, $`I_tW\le1/d+2e^{qt}`$.

**2.3 — PROVED.** For $`|f|\le C W`$, the semigroup bound gives $`|P_v f|\le C_tW`$ on $`0\le v\le t`$. First-event conditioning gives the backward derivative $`\partial_v P_v f=(R+B)P_v f`$. More explicitly, first use the Markov property on particle sums over an initial interval of length $`\varepsilon`$. For a fixed starting type the first and second possible jump neighborhoods are finite and all the rates there are bounded. Contributions following two jumps are $`O(\varepsilon^2)`$ by the weight expectation bound from those neighborhoods. This proves continuity and the right-derivative formula; the right side is continuous at each type (finite neighbors), so the derivative is two-sided in the interior. All derivative terms are bounded by $`C'_t|S|^2`$ using the rate and successor-mass bounds in 1.2. Apply time-dependent jump compensation to $`u\mapsto F_{P_{t-u}f}(T_u)`$ on $`[0, t]`$. For bounded-size stops the formula is finite; removal of stops is justified by 1.2, since the score, derivative and rate-weighted changes grow at most polynomially in physical size. The time derivative cancels $`F_{(R+B)P_{t-u}f}`$ in 2.1. The initial sum is empty and the final sum is $`F_f`$. Thus $`M_tf=\int_0^t I_uP_{t-u}f\,du`$, with absolute integrability also following from 2.2 and 1.5.

## 3. Compactness and criticality (S2.4 and S5.1)

Use the label function $`\lambda(S)=1/m(D_{\rm root})`$; it is $`p_S`$ without a cap. Use $`\mathcal A`$ as parameter space without the cap, and the nineteen-point newborn label set with it. Define the positive direct-birth kernel $`D_tg(a)=R_t[b\,g(\lambda)](J_a)`$, and write $`D_t(S, db)`$ for the analogous finite measure for arbitrary source $`S`$. Also write $`V_t(S)=R_t1(S)`$.

**3.1 — PROVED.** $`\|D_t\|\le e^{\rho t}/d`$ by 1.5, and the same bound holds for $`V_t`$ on newborns. For equicontinuity in $`a`$, run the same intrinsic history of the initial source for all initial root marks, and the same residual emission opportunities and descendant families. The source's root mark only changes the rate of its own independent exponential death; it never enters an emission's type or its rate, and the source's instantaneous $`b, \lambda`$ are independent of that mark. On the initial path, the source-at-time-$`t`$ contribution is multiplied by $`e^{-at}`$ relative to $`a=0`$; for a branch born directly from the source at time $`u\le t`$, the factor is $`e^{-au}`$, regardless of future changes in its family. The difference of either factor between marks is bounded by $`t|a-a'|`$. Taking absolute values and using the nonnegative total at $`a=0`$ bounded by $`e^{\rho t}/d`$ proves the uniform Lipschitz bound $`|D_tg(a)-D_tg(a')|\le \|g\|_\infty t e^{\rho t}|a-a'|/d`$; the same proof works for $`V_t(J_a)`$. For a cap the comparison can use an auxiliary mark zero or simply finite dimensionality. Integrating proves that $`K_s`$ maps into continuous functions; the image of the unit ball is bounded and equicontinuous on compact $`\mathcal A`$. **STANDARD:** Arzelà–Ascoli gives compactness. The positive operators are norm-continuous in $`s>\rho`$ by dominated integration, using $`e^{-(s_0-\rho)t}/d`$ when $`s\ge s_0>\rho`$.

**3.2 — PROVED.** Nonnegative continuous $`g\not\equiv0`$ is positive at some nonzero label (continuity handles a possible positive value at zero). From any newborn, there is positive probability to reach a star of that root degree using only root leaf additions at successive leaf rings, with the initial particle surviving. The root can then stay in that type for positive time, with $`b>0`$. In the cap, label 20 uses degree 20. This argument has positive time spent even over events entirely inside any prescribed time interval of positive length in $`(0, \infty)`$: fit the finite path before that interval and then prescribe holding there. Therefore $`K_sg`$ and $`(K_s-K_{s'})g`$ for $`s<s'`$ are positive everywhere; by compactness and continuity their minima are positive.

**3.3a — PROVED.** The spectral-radius formula applied to $`K_s^n1\ge(\min K_s1)^n1`$ gives $`r_s>0`$.

**3.3b — STANDARD.** Krein–Rutman for a positive compact operator with positive spectral radius on the Banach lattice $`C(\mathcal A)`$ (or the finite-dimensional lattice) gives a nonzero nonnegative eigenfunction $`h_s`$ at $`r_s`$. Positivity and compactness were checked in 3.1–3.2, and positive radius in 3.3a.

**3.3c — PROVED.** By 3.2, $`\min h_s>0`$. Here and below $`h_s`$ denotes an eigenfunction on labels, distinct from the core-count function $`h(c)`$. For any continuous $`v`$ with positive minimum, $`\min K_sv/v\le r_s\le\max K_sv/v`$: iterate the pointwise bounds (for the upper bound use $`|f|\le \|f\|v/\min v`$), then take $`n`$-th roots in the spectral-radius formula. Testing at a fixed $`h_s`$ under norm perturbations gives continuity of $`r_s`$. Testing $`K_s`$ at $`h_{s'}`$ for $`s<s'`$ and using the strictly positive difference in 3.2 gives strict decrease.

**3.4 — PROVED.** The full population consists of successive residual families: group individuals by their most recent direct-birth edge in their genealogy, with the initial source as generation zero. A residual family of type $`S`$ has surviving mean $`V_t(S)`$ and direct offspring intensity $`D_t(S, db)\,dt`$. In the discounted expectation starting from a newborn, the contribution of direct-generation $`k`$ is $`K_s^k v_s`$, with $`v_s(a)=\int e^{-st}V_t(J_a)\,dt`$; integrate over birth time and use independence of descendants. Tonelli applies since all terms are nonnegative. The same decomposition with an arbitrary starting type has basal term $`\int e^{-st}V_t(S)\,dt`$, plus one first-generation measure $`\int e^{-st}D_t(S, db)\,dt`$ followed by the newborn sum. The basal newborn score is bounded above by $`1/[d(s-\rho)]`$ and below by $`1/(s+1/2)`$ because the original particle survives its constant death clock. Hence it is bounded between positive multiples of $`h_s`$. The newborn sum is finite and uniformly bounded when $`r_s<1`$, and infinite at every label when $`r_s\ge1`$. In the former case the arbitrary-source sum is at most a constant times $`W(S)`$ by 1.5. In the latter case founder immigration includes positive mass at $`J_{1/2}`$ at every time $`u`$, by the physical event of no rings through $`u`$. In either case, using 2.3 and Tonelli, $`\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\mathbb E_{T_3} C(T_t)\,dt`$ is finite exactly when $`r_s<1`$, for $`s>\rho`$. The constant founder term has finite integral, and the finite-case immigration integral converges by 2.2 since $`s>q`$.

**3.5a — GIVEN.** The only round-1 inputs used are $`\mathbb E C_n=n^{\mu+o(1)}`$ and $`113/200\le\mu\le1133/2000`$.

**3.5b — PROVED.** Derive the continuous-time exponent rather than using the almost-sure result: size, starting from 3, is a pure birth chain with rate $`n`$ in state $`n`$; the holding times are independent of the discrete attachment chain by competing rate-one clocks and the Markov property. ## 4. Two-sided mean estimate (last claim of S2.4)

**4.1 — PROVED.** Let $`h_*=h_\mu`$ be any strictly positive Perron eigenfunction; in this section this symbol does not denote the capital $`H_*`$ of S5.2. The kernel
```math
 Q_a(dt, db)=e^{-\mu t}\frac{h_*(b)}{h_*(a)}D_t(J_a, db)\,dt
```
is a probability kernel by its eigenfunction equation. Start with $`(\tau_0, A_0)=(0, a)`$; from label $`A_k`$, choose the next holding time and label by $`Q_{A_k}`$ to get $`(\tau_{k+1}, A_{k+1})`$. Let $`\eta=\mu-\rho>0`$. The conditional holding-time density is bounded by $`C e^{-\eta t}`$ by 3.1 and the bounded positive eigenfunction. Uniformly over labels there is some $`\epsilon>0`$ probability a holding time exceeds 1: use the initial source in $`[1,2]`$ while it stays a newborn, with direct intensity $`\frac12e^{-(a+3/2)t}`$ or larger before tilt. In fact the probability of that source's having no intrinsic jump and not dying is exactly the exponential used here; births do not kill the source. The time sequence diverges almost surely: conditionally at every step the probability of at least one hold longer than 1 in the next $`m`$ steps is at least $`1-(1-\epsilon)^m`$; repeat after each success.

**4.2 — PROVED.** Uniformly in $`a, x\ge0`$ (with $`a`$ a label), the expected number of renewal epochs in $`[x, x+1]`$ is at most $`1/\epsilon`$: after the first index at or above $`x`$, either it overshot the interval, or every further epoch inside requires a new step of length at most 1, with conditional chance at most $`1-\epsilon`$. For any $`x\ge0`$, the probability that the first epoch at or above $`x`$ exceeds $`x+L`$ is at most $`C_1 e^{-\eta L}`$. On this event some epoch $`\tau_k<x`$ is followed by a hold larger than $`x+L-\tau_k`$ (at $`x=0`$ overshoot of the first epoch is impossible). Sum the conditional tail bounds over these epochs, and partition their distances below $`x`$ into unit intervals; the preceding occupation bound makes the resulting geometric sum uniform. Choose $`L`$ so this probability is at most $`1/2`$. Then for $`t\ge L`$, there is an epoch in $`[t-L, t]`$ with probability at least $`1/2`$.

**4.3 — PROVED.** The same direct-generation decomposition as in 3.4, now at a fixed time, gives
```math
\frac{e^{-\mu t}P_t1(J_a)}{h_*(a)}
=\mathbb E_a\sum_{k:\tau_k\le t}
\frac{e^{-\mu(t-\tau_k)}V_{t-\tau_k}(J_{A_k})}{h_*(A_k)}.
```
The factors $`h_*`$ telescope along each sequence of births. The summand at age $`v`$ is at most $`C_2e^{-\eta v}`$ by 1.5; the unit-interval occupation bound yields a uniform upper bound. At ages in $`[0, L]`$ it is bounded below by a positive constant since $`V_v\ge e^{-v/2}`$. Thus 4.2 gives a positive lower bound for $`t\ge L`$; for $`t<L`$ use epoch zero. In particular $`P_t1(J_a)\asymp e^{\mu t}`$ uniformly. Starting from arbitrary type $`S`$, the basal term is at most $`W(S)e^{\rho t}`$; integrating the first direct birth intensity (at most $`W(S)e^{\rho v}`$) against the uniform newborn upper bound gives $`P_t1(S)\le C_3W(S)e^{\mu t}`$. Formula 2.3 and 2.2 imply the physical upper bound $`\mathbb E C(T_t)\le C_4e^{\mu t}`$. For the lower bound when $`t\ge1`$, integrate founder direct newborns over $`0\le u\le1`$: if no rings occurred, their rate at $`J_{1/2}`$ is one. Their contribution is at least a positive multiple of $`e^{\mu t}\int_0^1e^{-(3+\mu)u}du`$. The founder itself handles $`t<1`$.

**4.4 — PROVED.** Let $`F(n)=\mathbb E C_n`$, nondecreasing since each transition either preserves or increases the core count. Independence in 3.5 and the continuous upper bound at $`t=\log n`$ give $`F(n)\le C_5 n^\mu`$ using the positive lower bound for $`\mathbb P(|T_{\log n}|\ge n)`$. For a positive constant $`B`$ and $`t=\log(n/B)\ge0`$, the continuous lower bound gives $`c e^{\mu t}\le F(n)+C_5\mathbb E[|T_t|^\mu; |T_t|>n]`$. The last term is at most $`C_5 e^{\mu t}\mathbb E[(e^{-t}|T_t|)^\mu; e^{-t}|T_t|>B]`$, uniformly $`o_B(1)e^{\mu t}`$ by the geometric tail. Fix $`B`$ to make this at most $`c e^{\mu t}/2`$, and obtain the matching lower bound $`F(n)\ge (c/2)(n/B)^\mu`$ for $`n\ge B`$.

## 5. Series, truncation and numerical bounds (S5.2)

**5.1 — PROVED.** Count intrinsic rate as in 1.2. To integrate the residual family, distinguish each particle's continuation after an intrinsic jump from each extra branch due to a copy. In a state $`S`$, discounting and survival against intrinsic jumps and death give the factor $`e^{-(s+a_S+r_0(S))t}`$ up to the next such change, independent of extra copies that retain the source. Expected money from one visit is its intensity $`B_j(S)`$ integrated against this factor. Each successor state's expected discounted arrival is its continuation-plus-copy intensity $`t(S, S')`$ integrated against that same factor: for the continuation use the competing jump law; for copies use compensation over the visit. Iterate along paths of visits. Every contribution in a finite time population has a finite number of preceding visits by non-explosion. Tonelli then gives precisely the stated series and factors including the empty path. Absolute convergence follows from $`B_j\le W`$ and $`\int e^{-st}R_tW\,dt\le W/(s-\rho)`$.

**5.2 — PROVED.** Retained types form a finite set (finite cap and bounded size); the only transitions leaving it increase size to $`N+1`$, since intrinsic changes add one and every copy has size at most the source size. Kill each continuation at an exit and suppress extra branches that leave the set. The residual first-moment matrix of this killed system is $`e^{tR_N}`$ by its finite backward equations, with precisely the stated subtraction and retained additions. It is nonnegative and its action on the restricted $`W`$ is at most $`e^{\rho t}W`$, by domination by the full process. Thus $`\int_0^\infty e^{-st}e^{tR_N}dt=A_N^{-1}`$ by integration of the matrix differential equation. The matrix entries of $`A_N`$ are rational for rational $`s`$, implying rational inverse. Integrating births before the cut gives $`K_N^-`$; at exits, the expected continuation-and-descendants score in column $`j`$, from exit type $`S'`$, is at most $`W(S')/(s-\rho)`$. By compensation, paying that bound for each exit gives exactly the column addition $`u_N`$. Hence the enclosures hold. The lower estimates increase with $`N`$. The upper estimates decrease: the potential $`V=W/(s-\rho)`$ satisfies $`(s-R)V\ge W\ge B_j`$; the expected discounted payments consisting of $`B_j`$ up to any lineage cut plus $`V`$ at the cut are at most initial $`V`$. This follows by compensating discounted active $`V`$ plus accumulated scores and exit payments, at bounded stops, and passing by Fatou and positivity. For a larger cut, apply that inequality to the continuation of each first exit from the smaller cut; the replacement costs at most its former boundary payment. The entrywise order implies spectral-radius order by iterating nonnegative matrices and using the spectral-radius formula.

**5.3 — PROVED.** Put $`W_\delta(S)=n(S)^\delta W(S)`$. Copy jumps in the first-moment generator only add offspring scores and their sizes do not exceed $`n`$. Intrinsic jumps always go from $`n`$ to $`n+1`$. Consequently, by concavity of $`x^\delta`$ for $`0<\delta\le1`$,
```math
 RW_\delta \le n^\delta RW+n^\delta\frac\delta n\sum_{S'} r^0(S, S')W(S')
 \le [\rho+\delta(1+q/2)]W_\delta.
```
Here the sum is $`L^0W+r_0W\le(q+n)W`$, and $`n\ge2`$. At the assumed $`s`$, discounted active $`W_\delta`$ plus discounted $`W_\delta`$ paid at each exit is a nonnegative supermartingale: compensating the exit payment restores the term of the full generator lost at the cut. Localize and apply Fatou as in 5.2 to bound the expected sum of exit payments by $`2^\delta W(J_{1/i})`$. Exit size is $`N+1`$, so replacing each payment by $`W/(s-\rho)`$ proves the stated bound for $`u_N(i)`$.

**5.4 — PROVED.** On $`[l, u]`$ all entries of $`K`$ are at most $`H_*`$. They are at least $`e_*>0`$: use in the series the single path through stars of root degree 2 up to degree $`j`$, using leaf rings with rates $`k-1`$, and collect $`b=(j-1)/j`$ at the last star. For a degree-$`k`$ star, $`r_0=k-1+(k-1)/k`$; each denominator is at most the denominator in $`e_*`$. For a right Perron vector $`v_s`$ normalized by $`\max_i v_s(i)=1`$, the row-sum bound gives $`r_s\le19H_*`$, and the entry in a column where $`v_s=1`$ gives $`r_s v_s(i)\ge e_*`$. Thus $`v_s(i)\ge g_*`$. Take $`\delta=1/24`$: $`\rho+(1+q/2)/24=671/1200<l`$. By 5.3 each difference $`K_N^+-K`$ or $`K-K_N^-`$ is nonnegative entrywise and at most $`H_*(2/(N+1))^{1/24}`$. Comparing both matrices on $`v_s`$, their radii differ from $`r_s`$ in the respective directions by at most $`19H_*(2/(N+1))^{1/24}/g_*`$ (iteration bounds as in 3.3, with a negative lower bound treated as trivial). This proves the width bound, including at $`u`$.

**5.5 — PROVED.** As $`s`$ increases to $`s'`$, each kernel entry decreases. Already the length-zero path for column 2 decreases in row $`i`$ by
```math
\frac{s'-s}{2(s+1/i+3/2)(s'+1/i+3/2)}\ \ge\ \frac{s'-s}{2(u+2)^2}.
```
The remaining paths are all nonincreasing. Applying the difference matrix to $`v_{s'}`$ gives coordinatewise at least $`g_*(s'-s)/(2(u+2)^2)`$, which is also at least that number times $`v_{s'}`$. Apply the lower iteration comparison to $`K(s)`$ on this vector to prove the stated separation bound.

## 6. Exact computational checks

The uncapped root mark has infinitely many values even at size 2: use 0 and five positive representatives numerically; all other root values reduce exactly to the same drift checks because multiplying $`L^0W\le qW`$ and the equivalent residual drift bound in 1.5 by $`a_S+d`$ removes root-mark dependence, $`BW`$ is independent of it, and the uniform pointwise bounds are given directly in 1.3. For the cap every legal root mark is enumerated. Test the actual jump rates and exact rational weight inequalities, including the drift behind the semigroup bounds and the rational component of the exit estimate. Check all founder-rooted states through size 7, 140 random larger planted states in each variant, and a high-degree nested example. A separate signed-law recursion applies the physical generator to the full tree; an exact backward recursion applies the branching generator, and the beta integral identifies the convolution coefficient of order $`k`$ as $`\sum_{i+j=k-1} I_0^{(i)}(R+B)^jf/k!`$. Compare orders 0–6 for $`1,(D_{\rm root}^2+L_{\rm root})\mathbf1_{D_{\rm root}\le9}, a_S+\min(c_{\rm root},9)/7`$, all bounded scores. These coefficients are genuine one-sided Taylor coefficients at zero: for each fixed order, restrict both jump processes to a finite number of jumps from any fixed start; the omitted contribution is of one higher order by the finite first-jump neighborhoods and polynomial moment bounds of 1.2. For immigration the same argument applies first to the physical chain, and then to the branching continuations from its finitely many bounded-jump states. The cap of 20 cannot bind at these orders; a cap of 3 is also tested to exercise actual capped marks in the coefficient calculation.
