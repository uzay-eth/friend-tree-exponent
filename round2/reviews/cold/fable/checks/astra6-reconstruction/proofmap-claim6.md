## §1. Exponential renewal remainder

1. **Capped kernel and derivative bound (lines 15–19).** Inputs: claim 5, `finite-kernel.md` §1 and (2), namely $`m(D)=\min(D,20)`$, actual reciprocal send rates, and $`r(K(s))=1`$ exactly at $`\mu`$; claims 2/5, residual $`R_t W\le e^{\rho t}W`$, $`\sum_Y a_YW(Y)=(a_S+d)W(S)-h(c_{\rm root})`$, and $`W\ge1`$. With $`B_0g(S)=b_Sg(m(D))`$ and $`k(t)=JR_tB_0`$, the source proves $`\|RB_0g\|_W\le C`$ for $`\|g\|_\infty\le1`$: below degree 20 the gain rate is at most 20 and increment at most 2; at or above 20 the label is fixed and $`|\Delta b|=c/[D(D+1)]`$ with gain rate at most $`D`$; promotions have rate at most one and increment at most 2, killing rate at most $`1/2`$, and copies cost at most $`(a_S+d)W(S)`$. Quote: “$`k(t)g = JB_0g + \int_0^t JR_u R B_0g\,du`$” (line 17). Localize the residual jump formula, then integrate by parts for $`K(z)=O(1/|z|)`$ on $`\Re z\ge\rho+\varepsilon`$ (line 19). **Delicate:** check absolute *nonzero increment* compensator in each particle (not its total event rate), domination and removal of stopping, and the $`O(1/|z|)`$ estimate uniformly over the whole half-plane. Indeed derivative is bounded by a constant times $`e^{\rho t}`$ on the finitely many newborns, giving a bound of form $`(C'+C''/\varepsilon)/|z|`$.

2. **Only one pole on a closed half-strip (lines 21–24).** Inputs: step 1; claim 5 positivity and strict decrease of real Perron radius; claim 3, `asymptotics.md` §2, domination by $`K(\mu)`$, equality in the triangle inequality and an interval density of births of label 2; simple Perron eigenvalue and analytic perturbation. Invert by domination on $`\Re z>\mu`$ and by a uniformly convergent Neumann series at large frequency on $`\Re z\ge\rho+\varepsilon`$. On the finite remaining line segment, local invertibility and isolation of the pole give a $`\sigma\in(\rho, \mu)`$ with no other singularities on $`\Re z\ge\sigma`$. Quote: “$`D=g\otimes\ell/[-\ell K'(\mu)g]`$” (line 23), with $`\ell g=1`$ and negative derivative from positive birth times. **Delicate:** a zero-free line does not give a uniform strip by itself; check the high-frequency bound and invertibility neighbourhoods covering the bounded segment, and the local rank-one derivative at $`\mu`$.

3. **Invert the renewal series (lines 26–30, (2)).** Inputs: steps 1–2, matrix convolution, Fourier inversion and Cauchy's residue theorem. Define $`v=\sum_{n\ge1}k^{*n}`$, locally uniform by bounding each power on a compact time interval by the corresponding exponential-series term. Nonnegative integration for real $`s>\mu`$ gives absolute transform convergence on its vertical line. The transform of $`v-k`$ is “$`(I-K)^{-1}K^2`$” (line 26), $`O(|\Im z|^{-2})`$ on both vertical lines and uniformly on the high horizontal segments between $`\sigma`$ and any fixed $`s>\mu`$. Quote: “$`v(t)=e^{\mu t}D+O(e^{\sigma t}), \quad t\ge0`$” (2); $`k(t)=O(e^{\rho t})`$ is absorbed. **Delicate:** verify the subtraction of the $`1/|z|`$ term, $`L^1`$ Fourier inversion for the continuous discounted convolution remainder, and that both horizontal edges vanish with constants uniform between the lines; the exponential error is bounded by the $`L^1`$ norm on the new line.

4. **Propagate to all planted first moments (lines 32–41, (3)).** Inputs: step 3; claim 5 direct-birth decomposition; $`R_tW\le e^{\rho t}W`$ and $`|B_0g|\le\|g\|_\infty`$. Quote: “$`JP_tf=h_f(t)+(v*h_f)(t)`$”, $`h_f(t)=JR_tf`$ (line 32); the leading coefficient is $`d(f)=\ell\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu u}h_f(u)du/[-\ell K'(\mu)g]`$ (line 35). The rest is $`O(e^{\sigma t})`$ for $`|f|\le W`$ with a leading-term truncation cost $`O(e^{\rho t})`$. Apply “$`P_tf=R_tf+(b*JP_\cdot f)(t)`$”, $`b(t)=R_t B_0`$ (line 37), obtaining “$`|P_t f(S)-e^{\mu t}H(S)d(f)|\le Ce^{\sigma t}W(S)`$” and $`H=\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu u}R_u B_0g\,du`$ (3). **Standard:** the pointwise bound on $`b(u)`$ is $`e^{\rho u}W(S)`$ as an operator on input columns of norm at most one, so convolution costs at most $`(\sigma-\rho)^{-1}`$ in the discounted norm, uniformly including unbounded degrees and all admissible marks. The newborn weight is bounded by $`1/d`$.

## §2. Physical eigenfunction

5. **Arbitrary initial tree and immigration (lines 45–57, (4)).** Inputs: step 4, claim 2 `renewal.md` (2)–(6) (physical signed-to-positive cancellation and backward jump formula), and claim 5's unchanged identity under capping. For any finite rooted core seed with compatible marks, $`\sum_{v\ne o}W(S_v)\le C_0 C(T)`$ by the geometric factor (e.g. $`2/[d(1-10/13)]`$ suffices), $`Z(T)\le2 C(T)`$ and $`\mathbb E_T Z(T_t)\le e^{qt}Z(T)`$. Quote: “$`I_{T, t}W\le 1/d+e^{qt}Z(T)`$” (lines 47–48). Add the initial term $`\sum_{v\ne o}P_tf(S_v)`$ to the immigrant convolution. In (4), “$`|\mathbb E_T\sum_{v\ne o} f(S_v(t))-e^{\mu t}\Psi(T)d(f)|\le C_1 C(T)e^{\sigma t}`$”, with $`\Psi=\sum_{v\ne o}H(S_v)+\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu u}I_{T, u}Hdu`$. The founder contributes at most one for $`f_k`$; $`d_k=d(f_k)>0`$ by positive occupancy of degree at least $`k`$. **Delicate:** check the physical identity for all finite seeds and all compatible marks, including root immigration and the extra initial term, and that the weighted immigration integral/tail is bounded by $`C(T)`$ rather than initial size. The convolution uses $`q<\sigma`$, the leading tail $`q<\mu`$, and there is no initial-degree cutoff.

6. **Two-sided physical comparison (line 59).** Inputs: step 5, $`H\le C W`$ and claim 2 `renewal.md` §5; `proofs/existence.md` Lemma 2.1 (at least $`C/4`$ eligible). Quote: “$`\Psi(T)\le C_2 C(T)`$” and “$`C(T)\le C_3(1+\Psi(T))`$.” Eligible means $`c\le2, L\ge1`$ or bare ($`D=c=2`$) with a bare core neighbour. For a nonfounder of the first kind, the one-unit no-death/root-first-ring lower bound for $`H`$ is $`e^{-(\mu+1/2)}(1-e^{-1})\min g/3`$. For a bare vertex with a bare child, the one-unit forced incoming ring produces a leaf, giving a uniformly positive lower bound by a second unit. A bare eligible vertex without a bare child can be charged to its bare parent, with at most two such children per parent, yielding at least $`C/12-1`$ good nonfounders. Positive founder direct immigration gives $`\Psi(T_3)>0`$. **Delicate:** check the eligible-vertex scripts operate intrinsically with parent slot silent, the bare child's degree is unchanged until its forced choice, the death bound is still $`1/2`$ under capping, and all counting losses including the founder are absorbed by the added 1.

7. **Orientation-free eigenfunction and martingale (lines 59–64, (5)).** Inputs: step 5 with $`f=1=f_2`$, ordinary Markov property, finite-time Yule integrability. Quote: “$`\Psi(T)=d_2^{-1}\lim_{u\to\infty} e^{-\mu u}\mathbb E_T C(T_u)`$” (line 59), hence independent of the arbitrary marks/orientation. Applying the uniform error at $`T_h`$ gives “$`\mathbb E_T\Psi(T_h)=e^{\mu h}\Psi(T)`$” (5); the integrated normalized error is at most $`C e^{-(\mu-\sigma)u}\mathbb E_T C(T_h)`$. Nonnegative martingale convergence gives a finite limit of $`Z_t^*=e^{-\mu t}\Psi(T_t)`$. **Standard.**

## §3. Variance, $`L^2`$ and positivity

8. **One-leaf influence via dirty cores (lines 68–73, (6)).** Input: physical arrows, especially rate-one core rings and rate-$`L`$ leaf gains at their parent. Seed two dirty copies of the changed core parent, or two dirty copies of the old parent plus the new core if the extension is itself a promotion. Clean pairs use synchronized choices. A dirty ring adds at most two to the combined dirty count (dirty birth adds one; discovery of a clean recipient dirties both copies); leaf gain with dirty parent creates no new dirty core. Grönwall with localization gives at most $`3e^{2u}`$ combined expected dirty count. Quote: “$`|\mathbb E_{T^+}A_k(T_u)-\mathbb E_T A_k(T_u)|\le 3e^{2u}\quad(k\ge2)`$” (6). **Delicate:** check both marginals have the correct rates, clean recipient pairs remain identifiable even when adjacent to dirty sources, paired clean leaf stocks/degree slots are preserved, and differences cannot propagate faster through the unbounded aggregate leaf clocks.

9. **Finite-horizon martingale variance bounds (lines 75–83, (7)).** Inputs: step 8; backward conditional mean martingale, compensated jump isometry and finite-size Yule moments. At time $`v`$ jump bound is $`3e^{2(b-v)}`$ and total rate is $`n(T_v)`$ with mean $`e^v n(T)`$. Quote: “$`\mathrm{Var}_T(A_k(T_b))\le9 n(T)\int_0^b e^{4(b-v)+v}dv`$” (7). For $`g_h=d_2^{-1}e^{-\mu h}\mathbb E_T C(T_h)`$ use the same bound over $`v\in[0,1]`$ with horizon $`1+h`$. Quote: “$`\mathrm{Var}_T(g_h(T_1))\le C_4e^{4h}n(T)`$” (line 82); one can take $`C_4=3e^4(1-e^{-3})/d_2^2`$ from this estimate. **Standard:** localization removes square-integrability concerns; the backward score is bounded by the conditional future Yule size, and its stopped jump bracket by the same expected rate bound.

10. **Approximate score and variance transfer (lines 84–87, (8)).** Inputs: steps 5, 9; one-unit Yule domination of core count by initial $`C(T)`$, since its promotion rate is at most its size. With $`\eta=\mu-\sigma`$, “$`|\Psi-g_h|\le C_5e^{-\eta h} C`$”, $`\mathbb E_T C(T_1)^2\le C_6 C(T)^2`$ (e.g. $`C_6=2e^2`$). 11. **Iteration for the full martingale (lines 89–100, (9)).** Inputs: steps 6–7, 10. With $`C(T_j)^2\le C_8(1+\Psi(T_j)^2)`$ and $`\mathbb E_T n(T_j)=e^j n(T)`$, the coefficient on the previous second moment is “$`1+C_9(j+2)^{-2}`$” and the additive term is “$`C_{10}[(j+2)^{4/\eta}e^{-(2\mu-1)j}n(T)+(j+2)^{-2}e^{-2\mu j}]`$” (lines 91–92). ## §5. Support of the limit

15. **Discrete drift and $`L^2`$ transport (lines 121–130).** Inputs: steps 6–7, 11, first holding time at size $`m`$ is independent of transition and has rate $`m`$; gamma recursion; optional sampling/Jensen. Quote: “$`\mathbb E[\Psi(T_{m+1})\mid T_m]=(1+\mu/m)\Psi(T_m)`$” (line 123). From the full continuous-time eigenfunction one may differentiate at zero using the finite successors and Yule moment bound. Then $`Y_m=\Gamma(m)\Psi(T_m)/\Gamma(m+\mu)`$ is a nonnegative martingale. “$`\mathbb E e^{-2\mu\tau_m}=\prod_{j=3}^{m-1}j/(j+2\mu)\asymp m^{-2\mu}`$” (lines 127–128); independence of all holding times from the jump chain factors $`\mathbb E(Z_{\tau_m}^*)^2`$. The latter is bounded by $`\mathbb E(Z_\infty^*)^2`$ via the closed $`L^2`$ martingale, hence $`\sup_m\mathbb E Y_m^2<\infty`$. **Standard:** optional sampling applies to a uniformly integrable closed martingale at the finite size times; stopping first at bounded times and Fatou suffices for the inequality.

16. **Arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large limit (lines 130–134).** Inputs: steps 6, 14–15, $`\Gamma(m+\mu)/\Gamma(m)\sim m^\mu`$, and positive finite-path probability of stars and paths. Quote: “$`\mathbb E[X\mid T_m]=d_2\Gamma(m)\Psi(T_m)/\Gamma(m+\mu)`$” (line 132), since $`X=d_2\lim Y_m`$. A star has one core and the conditional mean is $`O(m^{-\mu})`$. A path has $`m-2`$ cores and conditional mean at least a positive constant times $`m^{1-\mu}`$ for large $`m`$. Since $`X\ge0`$, a conditional mean below $`\varepsilon`$ implies positive conditional probability of $`X<\varepsilon`$, and a conditional mean above $`M`$ implies positive conditional probability of $`X>M`$. **Elementary.**
