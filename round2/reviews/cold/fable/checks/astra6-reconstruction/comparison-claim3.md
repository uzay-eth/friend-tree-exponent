## 2. Estimates in Astra's proofs that the reconstruction does not re-derive

**U1 — First-original-particle sharper row estimate (Renewal (8), L69–73; used at A L25).** The bound on the intrinsic expectation of the absolute bracket in (8), if $`\|g\|_\infty\le1`$, is $`1+e^{qt}/(\Re z-\rho)`$. In the intrinsic process the unnormalised subtree weight $`Z_S=(a+d)W(S)`$ has $`\mathbb E Z_S(t)\le e^{qt}`$ from $`J_a`$, because $`Z_S(0)=1`$. The copy weight is $`Z_S-h(c_o)`$, and $`\lvert U_z g\rvert\le W/(\Re z-\rho)`$. Integrating the bracket against $`e^{-(z+a)t}`$ gives precisely the claimed independence except for survival, and row Lipschitz constant at most $`(\Re z)^{-2}+[ (\Re z-\rho)(\Re z-q)^2]^{-1}`$ by integrating $`t`$. The bracket for $`jG_z f`$ has the same form with $`f`$ and $`G_z f`$ substituted. The broader real-generations (Renewal (9)) proof of the transform is also not R's route: the lower bound on its forcing for $`1`$ is $`(s+1/2)^{-1}`$ (original lifetime); termwise real domination gives the complex series in row 2. No finite-capped matrix computation is needed here.

**U2 — Exact raw-measure and tilted-probability convergence (A L68–72).** For $`\nu_x`$ as in row 7 put $`Z_x(s)=\int e^{-sy}d\nu_x`$. Its limit is $`d_k^*s^{-b}`$ for $`b=\mu+2`$. The probability $`e^{-y}\nu_x/Z_x(1)`$ has transform $`Z_x(1+s)/Z_x(1)\to(1+s)^{-b}`$ for $`s\ge0`$. The gamma probability has density $`e^{-y}y^{b-1}/\Gamma(b)`$, as is checked by substituting $`(1+s)y`$ in its integral. Thus for $`0<a<b'`$ the bounded, a.e. continuous test $`e^y\mathbf1_{[a, b']}`$ against the tilted laws gives $`\nu_x([a, b'])\to d_k^*\int_a^{b'}y^{\mu+1}dy/\Gamma(\mu+2)`$. This proves the interval estimate Astra uses for weighted monotonicity; the alternative power-series theorem in R gives the same conclusion without this construction.

**U3 — Exact cutoff prefactor and exponent (O (3), L23–37).** Use permanent boundary slots in the truncated intrinsic rates so outbound choice probabilities are not changed until an actual discrepancy has entered a cell. Preallocate even the suppressed inbound drivers from old vertices outside. Any dependence originating outside $`B_R`$ and reaching $`w\in B_k`$ requires a time-ordered path of old proposals of at least $`R-k+1`$ edges; take its final $`j=R-k+1`$ edges. The same event on these prescribed drivers covers **all** larger truncations, including one initial infinite input compared along its exhaustion. Removing excursions from a causal walk on a tree preserves an increasing subsequence of times on its simple path; for distinct edges the Poisson product intensity integrated over the ordered simplex is $`s^j\prod 1/c_x^0/j!`$. Detailed balance gives each forward product as $`c_w^0/c_{\rm start}^0`$ times the probability of the reversed walk, hence their sum is at most $`c_w^0`$. A singleton has no path. A ball about a root-cell member can only reach cells of original bases in $`B_k`$; edges born later cannot shorten an old distance, so agreement of those entire cells suffices. On failure, bound each of the two expected cell counts restricted to the event by $`\sqrt{2}e^s\sqrt{p}`$ separately; a truncated cell also obeys the Yule bound. This gives exactly $`p=\min(1, s^j\sum_{B_k}c_w^0/j!)`$ and (3). Only a finite augmented old ball (marks and boundary degrees) is needed to evaluate the truncated answer, and dependence of a fixed number of bounded tests follows by the same coupling, as in row 10b. This is stronger than the $`R-k-1`$ version written in R, but for a different boundary convention.

**U4 — Explicit tilt normalizer and moment constants (O (4)–(5), L43–49).** The exact TV error (dual norm on $`\lVert f\rVert_\infty\le1`$) is bounded by
```math
 \max_{\pm}|e^{(\mu\pm\delta)h}-e^{\mu h}|+e^{\mu h}Z^{-1}\int_0^h w(u) F_3(u)du.
```
The integral is $`O_h(w(0))`$, using $`F_3(u)\le e^u`$ and $`w(u)\le w(0)e^{-(\mu-\delta)u}`$. Integrate R §7's age bound $`Be^{\kappa a}`$: founder cost is at most $`B w(0)/(\mu-\delta-\kappa)`$ by $`w(a)\le w(0)e^{-(\mu-\delta)a}`$; births contribute at most $`B\int w(u)F_3(u)du/(\mu-\delta-\kappa)`$. Divide by $`Z`$ to get (5). Each fixed choice $`\delta`$ is normalizable because the time rate is $`\mu`$. The $`[T, T+1]`$ lower bound for $`Z`$ follows from monotonicity of $`F_3`$, and yields the claimed diagonal sequence even without the sharp time asymptotic.

**U5 — The weighted finite-seed lower bound and explicit planting script (O (6), L59–65).** Exact ingredients where used to bound $`Q_{s+h}1`$:

- In the free reference from a $`k`$-vertex tree, take $`b=7/\log(1/\epsilon)`$ and $`k\ge6M/\epsilon`$. The Poisson upper tails for original source counts in $`[0, \epsilon s]`$ and $`[0, s]`$ at $`bs`$ and $`8s`$ are at most $`e^{-6s}`$ and $`e^{-5s}`$ per vertex respectively: $`b\log(b/\epsilon)-b+\epsilon\ge6`$ and $`8\log8-8+1>5`$. With a fixed original neighbor ranked first at each original source, rings with a mark at most $`1/M`$ increase the sum of original degrees as long as none reaches $`M`$. Fewer than $`kM`$ such rings can then have occurred by $`\epsilon s`$. Their Poisson mean is $`\ge6s`$; the bound $`e^{-6s}(6es/(kM))^{kM}\le e^{-4s}`$ holds for sufficiently large $`s`$ at fixed $`k, M, \epsilon`$. Thus the bad event costs at most $`(2k+1)e^{-4s}`$, and Cauchy–Schwarz with the size-$`k`$ Yule second moment bounds its mean core contribution by $`\theta_k(s)=\sqrt{k(k+1)(2k+1)}e^{-s}`$.
- On good paths choose the lowest-labelled original vertex $`v`$ whose degree is $`\ge M`$ at time $`\epsilon s`$. The comparison across the cut has lower mean $`e^{-s/M}\mathbb E[C_T(s)\prod_{\text{rings of }v}D_v/(D_v+1)]`$. Couple using a separate uniform to reject the reference choice at $`v`$ with probability $`1/(D_v+1)`$; reject the entire comparison also if the exterior parent's source has any point marked at most $`1/M`$. Its independent no-point probability is $`e^{-s/M}`$, and having no such point suffices to exclude an inbound arrow because its degree stays at least $`M`$. ### Continuous time and discrete size

Write $`F_j`$ for the continuous-time threshold mean from start size $`j=2,3`$. Once size 3 is reached, the unlabeled tree is deterministic, and the holding times are independent of the embedded jump chain. With $`r=1-q`$ and $`q=e^{-t}`$, both texts use $`F_2=q^2\sum_{n\ge2}(n-1)r^{n-2}a_{n, k}`$. The corresponding path formula is $`F_3=q^3\sum_{n\ge3}\binom{n-1}{2}r^{n-3}a_{n, k}`$. For the continuous-time Tauberian step, in the Laplace-function version rescale by $`x=\mu t`$. ### Why cumulative regular variation becomes a point law here

Strict positivity, including for every atom, follows from the exact residue and residual occupancy of a planted star of precisely degree $`k`$. The uniform resolvent domination needed for normalization is $`\sup_{a, k}jG_\mu f_k(a)\le1/[d(\mu-\rho)]`$, since $`W(J_a)=1/(a+d)`$. This proves tightness of the limiting ratio distribution directly; the spatial moments provide an independent time-tilt tightness argument. $`D_o`$ is read from the root leaf count and its core neighbors, so bounded degree indicators are continuous even with no degree cutoff at the neighbors. $`\pi`$ is a subsequential time-tilted, annealed root law, and its entire degree marginal is fixed for every admissible tilt limit. The exponent is the **unique value** of any positive probability eigenmeasure of $`Q_1`$; both texts leave open whether there is a unique such measure. The strict moment intervals are the same in both texts; positive moments pass to the limit by bounded continuous truncation. R's uniform bounds on normalized means at all times and $`C'e^{\mu t}`$ cell growth make the eigenvalue argument shorter but import the capped branching estimate.

For Astra's flux proof, set $`f=\mathbf1_{D_o\ge k+1}`$ and $`g=(L_o+\rho_o)\mathbf1_{D_o=k}`$. For an arbitrary initial root cell, its threshold count changes only at the crossings counted by $`g`$, never at a birth. Thus $`Q_tf-f=\int_0^t Q_sg\,ds`$ by direct compensation, since $`g\le k`$ and $`Q_s1\le e^s`$. This is **not** an application of (7) to an unverified class of tests. Integrating with $`\pi`$ gives $`(e^{\mu t}-1)H_{k+1}=(e^{\mu t}-1)\pi g/\mu`$. R obtains exactly the same identity by the fixed newborn crossing's discounted compensator and the exponential-age mixture. Its boundedness by $`k`$ legitimizes the integral on the infinite time interval after discount. Since $`\rho_o\le c_o/2`$, either cutoff gives $`\pi g=kp_k(1+o(1))`$ at the relevant scale: for fixed $`\delta>0`$,
```math
 \frac{\mu H_{k+1}}k\le p_k\le \frac{\mu H_{k+1}}{(1-\delta)k}+\pi(c_o>\delta k),
```
and $`\pi(c_o>x)\le \pi(e^{\theta c_o})e^{-\theta x}`$; send $`k`$ to infinity, then $`\delta\downarrow0`$. R uses $`\delta=1/\sqrt{k}`$. By contrast $`H_k\sim A k^{-\mu}`$ alone is insufficient: distribute a power-tail law only at even $`k`$ (masses proportional to $`(2j)^{-1-\mu}`$, $`k=2j`$) to get the same tail order and convergence of the normalized tail but no pointwise equivalent at odd $`k`$. - **Astra Claim 3 uses exact marks** in A: the input compact space is countable plus the accumulation point 0; outputs have no atom at 0. - **R uses exact marks for the same sharp mean** through §5. The only new given among S6.1–S6.3 used in R is **S6.2 in §8**, to prove the full spatial spectral assertions by a distinct uniform cell bound. The upper bound for all starting types follows by taking $`f=W`$ and bounding $`H\le C W`$; for $`f=1`$ its leading coefficient on each of 19 newborns is positive and the error $`O(e^{\sigma t})`$ is uniform, giving a common eventual lower bound. - **No circularity.** The role of S6.1 in proving S6.2 is analytic and finite-mark. On bounded labels the root part of $`RB_0g`$ has uniformly bounded increments times rates: high-degree gains do not change the capped label, and $`D\lvert\Delta b\rvert\le1`$. The positive child weights bound copies. This gives the time derivative bound and $`O(1/\lvert z\rvert)`$; the no-event nonlattice argument works on its own for the capped matrix and does not use an already-proved exact mean. On a pole-free line $`\sigma<\mu`$, the transform of $`v-k`$ is $`(I-K)^{-1}K^2`$, integrable on vertical lines and $`O(|\Im z|^{-2})`$ uniformly on the horizontal segments used for the shift; inversion gives the exponential remainder in AS (2). Referring to A for the nonlattice *argument* is not invoking its conclusion. In particular exact-mark and capped $`g, \ell`$ and unscaled $`d`$'s are not interchangeable.
- **Why not silently import a capped generator bound to exact marks?** On a planted star of degree $`D=N`$ and $`c=1`$ with root mark fixed, choose continuous $`g`$ of sup norm one taking values $`+1,-1`$ at $`1/N,1/(N+1)`$ and zero at all other marks including 0. This does not contradict exact discount analyticity; it explains why A's pointwise boundary argument and compactness are appropriate there.
