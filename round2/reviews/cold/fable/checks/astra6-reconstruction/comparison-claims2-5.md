## 3. Estimates in Astra's proofs that the reconstruction does not re-derive

These are the 12 grouped omissions at the level of Astra's auxiliary formulas. Each is recoverable, including the distinct exact-order proof. They do not leave a missing statement of S2/S5.

1. **R:49, uncapped large-degree shortcut in (4):** `(1−c/D)[h(c+1)−h(c)+(1+dD)⁻¹]≤1/16` for `D≥100`. If the bracket is negative the left is nonpositive; otherwise use `1−c/D≤1`, `h(c+1)≤h(c)`, and `1+dD≥16`. P §1.4 proves (4) by all-degree quadratics, not this cutoff estimate. The cutoff's exact maximal left-hand side is actually `3/50`, at `(D,c)=(100,4)` (see §4).
2. **R:69–73, (8) with its distinct bound:** writing `E^0` for one original intrinsic particle, the line says `U_sg(J_a)=∫ e^{−(s+a)t} E^0[b_Sg(p_S)+Σ_Y((a_Y−p_S)U_sg(Y)+p_SU_sg(Y^+))]dt`. The bracket expectation in absolute value is `≤1+e^{qt}/(s−ρ)` for `‖g‖∞≤1`; used for compactness and for continuity of the forcing in (9). Original-particle survival is independent of the intrinsic path; independently integrate the reward from all its emitted copies, at their actual times of emission. The copy part is bounded by `E^0[Σ a_YW(Y)]/(s−ρ)≤(a+d)E^0W(S_t)/(s−ρ)≤e^{qt}/(s−ρ)` since `(a+d)W(J_a)=1`. The argument also works for the occupation forcing with immediate score 1 and future score `g_s`. The TV coefficient here can be taken as `1/s²+1/[(s−ρ)(s−q)²]`; use `|e^{-at}−e^{-a't}|≤t|a−a'|`. P uses a time-`t` coupling instead, and does not derive (8) in this form.
3. **R:85–87, harmonic resolvent:** `H=U_μg`, `H(J_a)=g(a)`, `R_tH=e^{μt}H−∫₀ᵗR_vB[e^{μ(t−v)}H]dv`. Indeed `BH=b g(p)` and `R_tH=e^{μt}∫_t^∞e^{−μv}R_vBH dv`; subtract the segment before `t`. Used to exhibit the full semigroup eigenfunction.
4. **R:89, full eigenfunction:** `P_tH=R_tH+∫₀ᵗ R_vB P_{t−v}H dv=e^{μt}H`. The first equality splits by the first direct birth in the genealogy, exactly as a reward version of P §3.4/4.3. **Stopping.** R:53 says “While the count is bounded, deaths have bounded intensity and the number of copy births is bounded by the count plus the deaths.” In the full process the same count equation bounds *all* particle births (copy plus direct) by the count plus deaths. Formal way to localize: before first hitting count `m` or event cutoff `k`, discounted total weight is a nonnegative supermartingale by the drift of the stopped process (the states reached in `k` events from a fixed initial type have finite possibilities). For horizon `T`, the probability of hitting count `m` by `T` within that event cutoff is at most `W(S)e^{β T}/m`, with `β=ρ` or `ρ+1/d`. Let `k→∞`. On explosion before `T` with bounded count, the killed population still has bounded death intensity up to explosion; compensation or rate domination shows finitely many deaths almost surely. There are then finitely many births. Finitely many particles each have intrinsic jump rate at most their size and one-vertex increments, so cannot give infinitely many intrinsic jumps in bounded time. Therefore explosion would require unbounded count; let `m→∞`. Fatou gives the stated weight expectation. The physical compensation steps can likewise stop on size, then use Yule moments. For `|f|≤CW`, at fixed time each planted state has only finitely many first-jump successors (or resulting particle configurations), and local in time the derivative estimate follows by bounding the difference quotient using that neighborhood and the weight expectation. One may then use dominated continuity of both physical generator and time-derivative terms after localization; for ordinary fixed `f` the same argument makes the stated forward expectation derivative valid (right derivative at initial time).

**Physical accounting, event by event.** If an old leaf rings, only a root leaf gain at its core parent occurs, already intrinsic in every old summand containing that recipient. If a core rings and sends to an old core child `Y`, the sender's and older ancestral scores see their ordinary full intrinsic transition, and `Y`'s own score misses the input. The mismatch is precisely `p_parent(f(Y^+)−f(Y))`. It is paid by `−a_Y f(Y)` and `(a_Y−p_parent)f(Y)+p_parent f(Y^+)`; if the sender is the founder, the credits are in `I_t`. A core sending to its own core parent leaves the sender fringe alone (the slot arrow is inert), and is internal in every changed score that actually contains the recipient as well as the sending child. A core sending to a leaf creates one new summand and changes all preexisting containing scores by intrinsic promotion; the newly promoted child has one leaf, one parent slot and the birth mark, so the additive newborn birth is paid in the promoter row, or at the founder. These cases exhaust the arrows. Degrees never decrease and the parent degree is unchanged at promotion; even with the cap, `1/D_parent(t)≤1/D_parent(birth)≤1/m(D_parent(birth))`. All child marks other than the auxiliary root are positive; copying removes the exterior constraint on the copied root and preserves all interior constraints. Adding a leaf can only weaken the constraints on the copied root's children. For capped auxiliary types one can restrict to marks that also satisfy `a_Y≥1/m(D_parent)`; this stronger condition is preserved and holds on the reachable set. The identity needs only the weaker true-send inequality. There is no artificial death at the physical founder and there is no initial `M` source at `T_3`. The founder's direct/copy credits are separate from its role in the unnormalised `Z`.

### Topology, criticality, and the differing mean proofs

`A={0,1/2,1/3,…}` is compact, and the only continuity issue for a row is its limit at zero. The only effect of changing the starting mark is the independent death of the original source. In the time-`t` coupling, each contribution from an original source that is still present is reweighted by `e^{−at}`, and each contribution of a side family emitted from that source at time `v≤t` by `e^{−av}`, relative to `a=0`. Their differences are at most `t |a−a'|`; for a test function of supnorm 1 use the total reward bound at `a=0`. This gives the P Lipschitz bound explicitly, `‖K_s(a,·)−K_s(a',·)‖_TV≤|a−a'|/[d(s−ρ)^2]` (TV norm for supnorm-1 test functions). The original-source integral of item 2 gives a second bound. Both are uniform on compact subintervals of `s>ρ`; `e^{−(s₀−ρ)t}/d` dominates times uniformly for `s≥s₀>ρ`, so norm continuity in `s` follows. Compactness is *operator compactness by Arzelà–Ascoli* on this space; positivity alone would not imply it. Strict positivity is the positive minimum on nonzero positive continuous inputs, not a mass at the endpoint 0. The star path at any positive time works even for `a=0`; all finite intrinsic sequences involved have strictly positive probability, followed by a positive residence time and reward.

Strong Krein–Rutman in R:75 is applicable: positive minimum lies in the interior of the cone in `C(A)`, and `K_s1≥m_s1` for some `m_s>0` gives positive radius by iteration. Independently, after conjugating by the strictly positive eigenfunction and dividing by `r_s`, the kernel is Markov and uniformly minorizes a positive atom at `1/2`: the initial unchanged newborn gives `K_s(a,{1/2})≥1/[2(s+2)]` and the eigenfunction ratio has positive minimum. Thus the oscillation of any real function under this Markov kernel contracts by a uniform factor `<1` (remove the common atom and apply the interval bound to the residual Markov kernel). Its fixed functions are constants, likewise for complex functions by real and imaginary parts. No generalized eigenvector can map to a nonzero constant under `P−I`: iterating would grow linearly while a Markov operator is power-bounded. An invariant probability follows by averaging iterates of any starting probability and taking a weakly convergent subsequence on compact `A`, since the kernel acts continuously on `C(A)`. Undoing the conjugation gives the positive dual eigenmeasure and simple eigenvalue in R. P does not claim these extra spectral properties; neither is necessary for criticality. Its min/max iteration comparison suffices to show radius continuity and strict decrease on positive eigenfunctions, exactly as used on the finite matrices.
