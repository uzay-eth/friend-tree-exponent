**E1 — Astra's nonquantitative compactness strip (A L21–24).** The conclusion is: some $`\rho<\sigma<\mu`$ has $`(I-K(z))^{-1}`$ analytic on $`\Re z\ge\sigma`$ except for the simple pole with $`D=g\ell/[-\ell K'(\mu)g]`$. Used at the contour shift. The a proof uses a quantitative strip instead. For the Astra route, fix $`\sigma_0\in(\rho, \mu)`$. The integration-by-parts bound makes $`\|K(z)\|<1/2`$ on $`\Re z\ge\sigma_0, |\Im z|\ge R_0`$ for large enough $`R_0`$. On the remaining finite segment, continuous invertibility away from $`\mu`$ and an isolated simple zero at $`\mu`$ give a single leftward width: otherwise a sequence of additional zeros with real parts increasing to $`\mu`$ and bounded imaginary parts would accumulate on this segment. On $`\Re z>\mu`$ compare with the real kernel using monotonicity or a weighted norm. The simple zero and residue follow since the scalar Schur complement has derivative $`-\ell K'(\mu)g>0`$. **E4 — Dirty cores and $`\mathbb E D_{\rm dirty}(u)\le3e^{2u}`$ (A L68–73).** Used for (6)–(7). Represent a physical state by its core tree and stock of leaves per vertex. Identify old cores, maintain matched clean core neighbours *slot by slot* (including edges to dirty cores by their old identities); a dirty core need not have matching degree or stock. A clean source shares a ring and a uniform choice among its matching core slots or equally many leaf slots. On leaf selection promote corresponding leaves and pair the new cores; on send to a clean core add to both leaf stocks. Leaf returns at a clean parent share a clock at the common stock rate; those at dirty vertices alter only their own stock. Dirty source clocks run individually. A dirty send to a clean core unpairs both copies of the recipient, before further rings; a dirty promotion creates one dirty core, and if the promoted leaf at a clean source is selected there is no conflict because the parent is clean and leaf stocks are paired. No other event changes a clean degree, stock or slot asymmetrically. These rules preserve both marginal rates and the invariant; nonexplosion is inherited from the Yule marginals. Initially there are at most three dirty cores: a core recipient pair, or a promoted leaf and the old-parent pair (that parent has a core when the tree has size at least three). Each dirty core rings at rate one and creates at most two combined marks; compensate stopped increments, apply Grönwall, then Fatou to get $`\mathbb E D_{\rm dirty}(u)\le D_{\rm dirty}(0)e^{2u}`$. ## 3. Differences that matter

### Variance weight and the refined influence, line by line

1. **p L17–23:** the paired induced graph is a connected tree with at least two vertices. It grows only when newborns are paired after a common target. At a paired source the probability of choosing the same paired target is $`d_0/(d_0+\max(a, b))`$, the sum of the common masses; subtract exactly those masses from each marginal. On failure pair neither newborn. An unmatched component of a side has exactly one boundary edge (at least one by connectivity of the full tree, at most one by acyclicity); hence $`\sum(a+b)=J\le B`$. For integer $`m=\max(a, b)\ge1`$, $`2m/(d_0+m)\le m\le a+b`$, and for $`m=0`$ it is zero. Thus drift $`B+2\sum p\le2B`$ (here $`p`$ in the sum is the failure probability).
2. **p L25:** to bound the signed degree-count difference, a positively contributing paired vertex has an unmatched neighbour on the positive side. Reserve one full-tree leaf in that neighbour's unmatched component for it. Such a leaf exists even for a singleton; for a larger component take a farthest vertex from the boundary endpoint. Different positively contributing paired vertices cannot use the same component, and the reserved leaf does not count at $`k\ge2`$. Therefore all positive contributions fit in the unmatched count on the positive side. The same holds for negative contributions on the other side; $`|\Delta N_k|\le B`$. Taking expectations gives (2.4).
3. **p L27:** before the first failed pair ring $`S`$, there are only plus-side unmatched leaves of $`x`$; each of their rings adds another leaf to $`x`$ without failing a pair ring. Before failure only the paired degree of $`x`$ can affect the count disparity. Since its minus degree is nondecreasing and initially $`d=d_T(x)`$, the absolute disparity is $`\le\mathbf1_{d<k}`$.
4. **p L29–37:** before failure the unmatched stock $`L`$ increases at rate $`L`$ and can be coupled below a rate-one Yule process $`U`$ from one, killed only on the comparison side at failure. The sole failure intensity is $`L/(D_0(x)+L)\le L/(d+1)`$; the failed pair ring adds two to $`B`$. By the post-failure drift bound, the conditional expected disparity at $`u`$ after a failure at $`s\le u`$ is at most $`(L+2)e^{2(u-s)}`$. Use predictable pre-ring $`L`$ and compensate the first failure. With $`\mathbb E(U^2+2U)=2e^{2s}+e^s`$, the integral divided by $`d+1`$ is $`e^{2u}(2u+1-e^{-u})/(d+1)`$. This is precisely (2.3). Conditional compensation and localization are justified by the positive integrand and this moment bound. There is no monotonicity-under-leaf-extension premise.
5. **p L45–52:** $`q_x=\sum_{y\sim x}1/d_T(y)\le d_T(x)`$, $`\sum_xq_x=n`$. Both (2.3) and (2.5) are correct.

### Explicit $`\sigma`$: the claimed value survives a norm repair

Astra needs only existence (E1). In a step 6 let $`s_0=(\rho+l_*)/2`$, $`M_j=j!/[d(s_0-\rho)^{j+1}]`$, $`E=e_*`$, and $`B=u_*+2`$. The prescribed high-frequency deficit and width are
```math
\delta_0=\frac1{2B}\left(1-\frac B{\sqrt{B^2+(r/2)^2}}\right), \qquad
\eta_{\rm a}=\min(r/4, E\delta_0/(2M_1)).
```
The no-event path used in a L23–27 produces **output label 2**, not output $`i`$ from input $`i`$. This would by itself give only a weighted row deficit $`\ge E\delta_0`$, and a weaker bound for $`\alpha=-\ell K'(\mu)g`$. Thus
```math
|K_{ij}(\mu+i\tau)|\le \frac{B}{\sqrt{B^2+\tau^2}}K_{ij}(\mu),
\qquad -K'_{ij}(\mu)\ge K_{ij}(\mu)/B.
```
The derivative bound follows by differentiating the positive path terms (or taking monotone difference quotients). It implies $`\alpha\ge1/B\ge1/(2B^2)`$. The complex bound implies $`\|K(\mu+i\tau)\|_g\le1-2B\delta_0\le1-\delta_0`$ for $`|\tau|\ge r/2`$. With $`\|K'\|_g\le M_1/E`$, the shift by at most $`\eta_{\rm a}`$ costs at most $`\delta_0/2`$.
