# S3: sharp means and the degree law

Notation: $`\mu`$ is the discount exponent, and $`\rho=0.51`$ is the residual growth bound; $`\rho_o`$ below is a spatial incoming rate. All physical counts ignore artificial parent slots, but a planted root's degree includes its slot. Use exact marks in steps 1–5, and capped birth marks only where explicitly indicated in step 8. The spatial topology always uses leaf-count marks.

## Exact-mark constants and fixed-size asymptotics

**1. GIVEN — Inputs.** Model and notation, S2 and S5, including $`r(K_\mu)=1`$ and $`\rho<\mu<1`$. The only capped analytic estimate needed below is S6.2 (in step 8); no exact-mark version of S6 is assumed. The given logarithmic continuous-time count bound implies integrability of $`e^{-zt}\mathbb E_{T_3} C(T_t)`$ on $`\Re z>\mu`$.

**2. PROVED — Exact-mark transforms and renewal identity.** On scores with norm $`\|f\|_W=\sup |f|/W`$, $`G_z`$ is bounded by $`(\Re z-\rho)^{-1}`$; $`\widehat I_z`$ is bounded on the same score space for $`\Re z>q`$. The power series obtained by expanding the discount converge in these norms in any disk of radius smaller than the distance to the relevant bounding line. Write $`B_0g(S)=b_S g(p_S)`$, so $`B=B_0j`$. Then $`U_z=G_zB_0`$. The restrictions $`jG_z f`$ for bounded degree-only $`f`$, and $`K_zg=jU_zg`$, belong to $`C(\mathcal A)`$. Indeed, start the residual family at $`J_a`$. Conditional on the intrinsic trajectory of the initial particle, the only dependence on $`a`$ is its lifetime: its own score at time $`t`$ is weighted by $`e^{-at}`$, and the mean descendant contribution from each of its copy emissions at time $`u\le t`$ is weighted by $`e^{-au}`$. The emission rates, emitted types, and all descendant scores are independent of the initial particle's mark. The same holds for the initial score itself for these choices of score. Thus changing $`a`$ to $`a'`$ changes the time-$`t`$ mean by at most $`t|a-a'|R_t|f|(J_0)`$, or the same bound with $`|B_0g|`$ in place of $`|f|`$. This also proves norm holomorphy in discount after restriction.

Decomposing at the first direct-birth edge in a particle genealogy gives
```math
P_t f=R_t f+\int_0^t R_u B_0 jP_{t-u}f\,du.
```
Indeed, particles with no such edge belong to the residual family; each direct birth from that family initiates an independent full family. For large $`\Re z`$, the bounds in S2 permit absolute Laplace transformation and imply $`\|K_z\|<1`$. With $`\widehat M_f(z)=\int e^{-zt}M_t f\,dt`$, S2.3 gives there
```math
\widehat M_f(z)=\widehat I_z(G_z f)+\widehat I_z\big(U_z(I-K_z)^{-1}jG_z f\big). \qquad\text{(1)}
```

**3. PROVED / STANDARD — Perron eigenvectors, pole, and absence of other boundary singularities.** For any finite degree $`j\ge2`$ the initial particle can reach the planted star of degree $`j`$ by consecutive leaf rings, with positive probability, and can remain in that state for a positive duration without death. There $`b=(j-1)/j>0`$. Thus every output coordinate $`1/j`$ has positive kernel mass for each input $`a`$, including $`a=0`$. Any nonzero nonnegative continuous function on $`\mathcal A`$ is positive at some such coordinate, even if its first known positive value is at 0. Hence $`K_\mu`$ is strongly positive (the positive continuous output has positive minimum on the compact space). **STANDARD:** the strong-positive compact Krein–Rutman theorem applies to the real Banach lattice $`C(\mathcal A)`$, with solid cone and spectral radius 1. It gives algebraic simplicity, $`g`$ of positive minimum and strictly positive dual $`\ell`$, represented by a finite Borel measure by the Riesz representation theorem. Normalize $`\ell(g)=1`$. Differentiation inserts the factor $`-t`$; the positive no-event contribution on $`t>0`$ implies $`-\ell(K'_\mu g)>0`$.

Use the equivalent supremum norm weighted by $`g`$. If $`s>\mu`$, then $`|K_{s+ib}h|\le \|h\|_g K_s g`$ and $`K_s g<g`$ pointwise by the strict discount on positive times. Compactness of the input space makes this a strict norm contraction. If $`s=\mu`$, $`b\ne0`$, view $`K_{\mu+ib}`$ as a row of complex coefficients at $`1/j`$, each formed from the corresponding positive time measure after multiplying by $`e^{-ibt}`$. The coefficient at $`1/2`$ suffers strict cancellation: its positive measure dominates the no-event density $`\tfrac12 e^{-(\mu+a+3/2)t}dt`$ on $`t>0`$. The contribution of that coefficient to the bound $`g(a)`$ is therefore reduced by a strictly positive continuous function of $`a`$, using continuity from step 2 (the coordinate test at $`1/2`$ is continuous). All other coefficients satisfy the non-strict triangle inequality; there is no output mass at 0. This again gives a strict norm contraction. Consequently the resolvent extends holomorphically across each point of the boundary line other than $`\mu`$, by openness of invertibility.

At $`\mu`$, split $`C(\mathcal A)=\mathrm{span}(g)\oplus\ker\ell`$. The second diagonal block of $`I-K_\mu`$ is invertible by compactness and simplicity. Both off-diagonal blocks vanish at $`\mu`$. Eliminating the second block gives a scalar with value zero and derivative $`-\ell(K'_\mu g)`$ at $`\mu`$. Block inversion therefore yields a simple pole with residue $`(g\otimes\ell)/[-\ell(K'_\mu g)]`$. This argument needs no uniform large-frequency or exact-mark $`RB_0`$ estimate.

**4. PROVED / STANDARD — Continuous-time means.** For $`f=f_k`$, the left side of (1) is holomorphic on $`\Re z>\mu`$ by step 1. **STANDARD:** apply Wiener–Ikehara in Laplace–Stieltjes form: for nondecreasing $`A`$ supported on $`[0, \infty)`$ with transform $`\int e^{-zt}dA(t)`$ convergent on $`\Re z>\mu>0`$, analytic extension of the transform minus $`R/(z-\mu)`$ across the boundary gives $`A(t)\sim (R/\mu)e^{\mu t}`$ for $`R>0`$. **5. PROVED / STANDARD — Edge start, and Yule de-Poissonization with the exact constant.** The first ring from the edge has time $`H\sim\mathrm{Exp}(2)`$; the unlabeled tree after it is always the path. Condition on $`H`$, use boundedness of $`e^{-\mu t}\mathbb E_{T_3}N_{\ge k}(t)`$ (from the asymptotic and local boundedness), and dominated convergence to get $`d_k^*=d_{f_k}\mathbb E e^{-\mu H}=2d_{f_k}/(2+\mu)`$.

For each jump, the minimum clock at size $`n`$ is $`\mathrm{Exp}(n)`$, independent of the uniform ringer and its neighbor choice; successive holding times can be sampled independently of the embedded discrete tree. The size process from two has law $`\Pr(Y_t=n)=(n-1)x^2(1-x)^{n-2}`$ for $`x=e^{-t}`$ (substitution in the pure-birth forward equations with initial size two, or sum of two independent geometric Yule families). With $`a_n=\mathbb E N_{\ge k}(n)`$, the exact identity is
```math
\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)a_{m+2} r^m
=(1-r)^{-2}\mathbb E_{T_2}N_{\ge k}(-\log(1-r))
\sim d_k^*(1-r)^{-\mu-2}.
```
**STANDARD:** the Hardy–Littlewood–Karamata power-series Tauberian theorem for nonnegative coefficients applies, with exponent $`b=\mu+2>0`$, giving partial sums $`S(M)\sim d_k^*M^b/\Gamma(1+b)`$. The coefficients $`B_m=(m+1)a_{m+2}`$ are nondecreasing: this follows by following one discrete tree across steps. For $`0<\varepsilon<1`$, backward and forward discrete averages of $`S`$ over $`[(1-\varepsilon)m, m]`$ and $`[m,(1+\varepsilon)m]`$ sandwich $`B_m`$ (excluding $`m`$ on the forward side if desired). Dividing by $`m^{b-1}`$, the limiting bounds are $`d_k^*[1-(1-\varepsilon)^b]/[\varepsilon\Gamma(1+b)]`$ and $`d_k^*[(1+\varepsilon)^b-1]/[\varepsilon\Gamma(1+b)]`$. Sending $`\varepsilon\downarrow0`$ gives $`a_n\sim d_k^* n^\mu/\Gamma(\mu+2)`$. In particular the gamma factor includes the Yule initial size two. Step 4 gives $`c_k>c_{k+1}>0`$ and $`c_k\to0`$. Subtract the two mean asymptotics at fixed $`k`$, divide by the mean core count, and telescope to obtain $`p_k>0`$, $`\sum_{k\ge2}p_k=1`$, and the claimed ratios. These are ratios of expectations.

## The spatial semigroup and its eigenmeasure

**6. PROVED — Construction, locality, and Feller property.** Work on the core tree with leaf counts. Give every core a rate-one marked proposal clock for promotion (accept at rate fraction $`L/D`$); give its leaves independent rate-one birth clocks aggregated as a leaf-gain rate $`L`$; and give each oriented core edge out of $`x`$ Poisson proposals of rate $`1/\max(2, c_x(0))`$ if $`x`$ is old at time zero, or $`1/2`$ if it is newborn. Accept an edge proposal with the ratio of the actual rate $`1/D_x(t)`$ to its proposal rate. These produce exactly the stated rates. For a finite computation one may realize the rate $`L`$ using a shared Poisson measure on time and a nonnegative intensity coordinate. All descendant labels and clocks can be allocated by successive birth addresses at each initial vertex. At any individual old or newborn root $`c`$ increases only by promotions of that vertex at rate at most 1; its total degree increases by unit jumps at rate $`L+\rho_x\le L+c=D`$. Thus on any bounded interval its own degree has no explosion and can be dominated by a rate-per-capita Yule birth chain. The number of cores carrying one old label is dominated by a Yule chain started at one, since each member has promotion rate at most 1. Both dominations can be made by adding the missing jump intensity; alternatively they give the same tail bounds by stopped comparison of jump times. All finite initial constructions are nonexplosive, also by attaching the actual leaves and comparing with the physical total size process.

For infinite initial trees, truncate only outside a ball of radius $`R`$ of initial cores, choosing admissible marks at its boundary; for example replace cut edges there by leaves. If initial states agree through a ball of radius $`R`$, couple all clocks of common old vertices and their newly born descendants, including their edge proposal clocks wherever the starting degrees and edges are specified strictly inside the ball. Disagreement in an old vertex or its descendant colony can only be imported from another old colony across an initial core edge; descendants created after time zero never connect different colonies. Disagreement entering a colony inside the ball must have a time-ordered chain of edge proposals into that colony from the boundary. One may take a simple inward path, by tracing the first time each old colony differs; or erase excursions from any such causal path in the tree. For a target initial vertex $`x`$ well inside the ball, and an inward path of length $`l`$ whose sender vertices are all interior, the probability of its necessary proposals by time $`t`$ is at most $`t^l/l!`$ times $`\prod_y 1/\max(2, c_y(0))`$, by counting ordered tuples of points on distinct edges. If the last (outermost) sender lies on the altered boundary, discard the outermost link and use the necessary inward subpath ending at the target with all senders interior. The summed weights of inward paths of length $`l\ge1`$ into $`x`$ are at most $`c_x(0)`$: each terminal sender contributes at most 1, and at an internal sender the number of outward choices is at most its core degree, cancelling its own reciprocal bound, by induction from the outer end. Hence for a fixed initial ball of radius $`r`$ a union bound on disagreement in any of its old colonies up to time $`t`$ is at most $`\sum_{x\in B_r}c_x(0)\, t^{R-r-1}/(R-r-1)!`$ for $`R\ge r+2`$. The bound works without any global bound on the initial degrees or on the size of balls.

Couple the successive truncations with these same clocks. The preceding bounds are summable in $`R`$ for each fixed $`r, t`$; thus all finite sets of old colonies stabilize on compact time intervals almost surely (countably many integer $`r, t`$ suffice). They define the process, with the given local rates and independently of the boundary truncation. Use Poisson measures with an intensity coordinate also for edge and promotion proposals, so that a larger dominating rate just exposes extra points that would never be accepted under a tighter valid bound. Independent increments give the Markov property at deterministic times and stopping times: the independent post-stopping Poisson property holds for any finite list of coordinates in the filtration of the entire family, hence for the countable family. Restarting with the tighter proposal bounds from the current degrees gives the same paths locally by the coupling argument. This justifies, in particular, conditioning on configurations immediately after a promotion. If initial rooted states converge locally, the same estimates couple paths on every fixed finite set of central old colonies with probability tending to one. In particular the initial root's label cell agrees as an entire finite family on any bounded time interval with probability tending to one. A marked ball of radius $`r`$ about any member of that cell involves only the initial old vertices within $`r`$ of the original root and descendants of such vertices, since adding pendant cores never shortens old distances. Thus the joint finite marked neighborhoods of its members converge in probability. The cell count is uniformly integrable, bounded in distribution by the same Yule law for each input. Bounded continuous scores then converge in expectation under $`Q_t`$: one can choose increasing coupling radii and, from any subsequence, further indices with summable failure probabilities, all coupled to a common limit process. On that subsequence the cell and all fixed-radius balls eventually agree almost surely, so continuity and uniform integrability give convergence of expectations. The same argument for a fixed old root gives the Feller property of $`S_t`$. The label cells at a restart partition all future cores according to their ancestor at restart, so the Markov property gives $`Q_{u+t}=Q_uQ_t`$. We have $`Q_t1\le e^t`$. On $`C_b(\mathcal X)`$ this asserts a semigroup of bounded operators, without requiring strong continuity in the supremum norm.

**7. PROVED / STANDARD — Tightness, the prescribed tilt, and identification.** Put $`F(t)=m_t(1)`$ and $`\nu_t=m_t/F(t)`$. Step 4 gives constants $`0<a\le b<\infty`$ with $`a e^{\mu t}\le F(t)\le b e^{\mu t}`$ for all $`t\ge0`$; on bounded intervals use $`F(t)\ge1`$ and the finite total size moment. The expected measure of promotions at time $`u`$ is at most $`F(u)du`$. From a promotion, the newborn starts with $`D=2, c=1`$; the original root at time zero starts with $`D=2, c=0`$. For an age $`v`$, the degree is dominated by the Yule chain begun at 2, hence its $`\alpha`$-moment is at most $`2^\alpha e^{\alpha v}`$ for $`0<\alpha<1`$ by concavity. The core degree is dominated by $`1+\mathrm{Pois}(v)`$; its exponential moment at $`\theta`$ is at most $`e^\theta\exp(v(e^\theta-1))`$. These statements hold conditionally on every configuration at promotion. Summing the original root's contribution and then integrating contributions at promotion times, using the Markov property and the promotion compensator, bounds the two $`m_t`$-moments by constants times
```math
e^{\gamma t}+\int_0^t F(u)e^{\gamma(t-u)}du,
\qquad \gamma=\alpha\text{ or }e^\theta-1.
```
After division by $`F(t)`$ the bounds are uniform for $`\gamma<\mu`$.

For tightness of marked balls, use finite-tree mass transport under $`\nu_t`$: summing a nonnegative two-vertex function over ordered pairs of cores, rooting uniformly in the expected sum and reversing the pair, gives equal means. Suppose that up to distance $`r-1`$ the degree bounds along each layer $`j`$ are finite cutoffs $`M_j`$. For $`r\ge1`$, the expected number of paths of length $`r`$ to a vertex with $`D>M_r`$ satisfying these prefix bounds is at most
```math
\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1} M_j\bigg)\nu_t[c_o\mathbf 1_{D_o>M_r}].
```
Indeed, reverse the path: there are at most $`c_o`$ first choices from the new root and at most $`M_j`$ choices at a restricted internal vertex at original distance $`j`$; the restriction at the final vertex may be dropped. The expectation tends to zero uniformly in $`t`$ as $`M_r\to\infty`$, by Cauchy–Schwarz, the uniform second moment of $`c_o`$, and the uniform degree tail bound. The initial layer uses the root-degree tail alone. Successively choose $`M_r`$ so failures at the first violating layer have summable probabilities, with total at most any prescribed $`\varepsilon>0`$. These constraints give compact sets: at each radius there are finitely many possible balls (all branching and integer marks are bounded on the finitely many relevant layers), and diagonal extraction gives a consistent locally finite limit; each degree constraint is closed, observable from balls one radius larger. The space itself is Polish for the agreement ultrametric: Cauchy sequences have coherent eventually constant balls at every radius, giving a rooted union with finite degrees and verified degree-at-least-two conditions; countable valid finite balls give a countable base and separability. **STANDARD:** Prokhorov now gives weakly convergent subsequences, including for mixtures of the $`\nu_t`$'s.

Take, for instance, $`\delta_j\downarrow0`$ and $`T_j=1/\delta_j`$, discarding terms with $`\delta_j\ge\mu`$. The specified tilt has $`Z^{\rm tilt}\ge a(2-e^{-\delta_j T_j})/\delta_j`$, so $`w(0)/Z^{\rm tilt}\to0`$. More generally for any such sequences in the question, each fixed interval $`[0, h]`$ has vanishing tilted probability, since $`w(t)\le w(0)e^{-(\mu-\delta)t}`$ and $`F`$ is locally bounded. The identity $`m_t Q_h=m_{t+h}`$ follows from partition into the labels of all cores alive at time $`t`$. For the tilted measure, after change of variables $`s=t+h`$, the weights satisfy for $`s\ge h`$
```math
e^{(\mu-\delta)h}w(s)\le w(s-h)\le e^{(\mu+\delta)h}w(s).
```
Thus the normalized difference of $`\pi_{\delta, T}Q_h(f)`$ and $`e^{\mu h}\pi_{\delta, T}(f)`$ vanishes for each bounded score as $`\delta\downarrow0`$ and $`w(0)/Z^{\rm tilt}\to0`$. For bounded continuous scores the Feller property permits passage to a single weak subsequential limit $`\pi`$; it satisfies $`\pi Q_h=e^{\mu h}\pi`$ for every $`h\ge0`$. Equality extends to Borel scores by equality of the two finite Borel measures (bounded continuous functions determine them). Moment bounds pass by continuous truncation and monotone convergence. Degree tail and atom indicators depend on a finite ball and are continuous; the late-time ratios from steps 4–5 therefore give $`\pi(D_o\ge k)=c_k/c_2`$, $`\pi(D_o=k)=p_k`$. This identifies the needed degree marginal of every such weak limit, without asserting uniqueness of its full spatial law.

**8. PROVED / GIVEN — Spectral claims in S3.2, with uniform cell bounds.** Use capped marks here, so that the given S6.2 supplies the growth bound $`P_t W\le C e^{\mu t}W`$ and the lower bound $`P_t1(J_a)\ge c e^{\mu t}`$ for all capped newborns when $`t\ge t_0`$. To verify it in this setting, test the current sum over non-founders by a time-dependent score $`F_u=P_{t-u}f`$. ## Newborns, the degree constant, and point probabilities

**9. PROVED — Newborn representation.** The promotion compensator within the root label cell, with the Markov property after the promotion, gives for bounded nonnegative Borel $`f`$
```math
Q_t f=S_t f+\int_0^t Q_u B^{\rm sp}S_{t-u}f\,du.
```
In this identity the first term accounts for the old root, and each other member is counted exactly once at the event of its own birth, then scored at time $`t`$ with that particular root kept fixed. The rate of all births is at most the cell size; the Yule bound justifies taking the compensator in expectation. This is valid on infinite states by the same local construction; the kernel is measurable (finite trajectories can be approximated by the truncations). At a site with $`b_o=0`$, define $`B^{\rm sp}f=0`$ without performing an invalid promotion. Apply $`\pi`$, divide by $`e^{\mu t}`$, and use step 7:
```math
\pi f=e^{-\mu t}\pi S_t f+\int_0^t e^{-\mu a}\pi B^{\rm sp}S_a f\,da.
```
The first term vanishes as $`t\to\infty`$ and the integral converges by monotone convergence. Taking $`f=1`$ gives $`\pi(b_o)=\mu`$. Thus $`\beta=\pi B^{\rm sp}/\mu`$ is a probability, supported on roots with $`(L, c)=(1,1)`$, and the claimed representation holds. Equivalently, sample a state from $`\beta`$, run the fixed-root process, and independently sample an age of law $`\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)`$.

**10. PROVED / STANDARD — Limit of normalized degree, its integrability and positivity.** Consider the fixed root from $`\beta`$, abbreviated $`D(t), c(t), b(t), \rho(t)`$. Degree jumps are unit increases with intensity $`D-c+\rho`$, with $`0\le\rho\le c/2`$. Thus $`X(t)=e^{-t}D(t)`$ is a nonnegative supermartingale by the stopped compensator and nonnegative conditional Fatou. **STANDARD:** nonnegative supermartingale convergence applies to this adapted càdlàg process (use the usual filtration), giving a finite almost-sure limit $`W^{\rm deg}`$. Domination by the rate-per-capita Yule chain started at 2 yields a uniform bound on $`\mathbb E X(t)^2`$: if $`Y_t`$ is the sum of two independent geometric variables of parameter $`x=e^{-t}`$, then $`x^2\mathbb E[Y_t^2]=2(1-x)+4=6-2x`$. Hence $`X(t)\to W^{\rm deg}`$ also in $`L^1`$.

Using the degree compensator without stopping is justified on finite intervals by the degree expectation bound. It gives
```math
\mathbb E X(t)=2-\int_0^t e^{-s}\mathbb E[c(s)-\rho(s)]\,ds.
```
The core-degree compensator gives $`\mathbb E c(s)=1+\int_0^s\mathbb E b(u)du`$, since a promotion at this root is the only event incrementing $`c`$. By Tonelli and the nonnegative incoming rate,
```math
\mathbb E W^{\rm deg}\ge 2-\int_0^\infty e^{-s}\mathbb E c(s)ds
=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\mathbb E[1-b(u)]du>0.
```
Indeed $`c(u)\ge1`$ and $`D(u)<\infty`$ almost surely, so $`1-b(u)=c(u)/D(u)>0`$. Thus $`\Pr(W^{\rm deg}>0)>0`$. Taking the $`\mu`$-moment and using concavity yields $`0<A\le(\mathbb E W^{\rm deg})^\mu\le 2^\mu`$. This proof does not need almost-sure strict positivity of $`W^{\rm deg}`$.

**11. PROVED / STANDARD — Hitting times and the cumulative tail.** The representation and monotonicity of the degree give, with the infinite hitting time convention,
```math
\pi(D_o\ge k)=\mathbb E_\beta\int_0^\infty \mu e^{-\mu a}\mathbf1_{D(a)\ge k}da
=\mathbb E_\beta[e^{-\mu T_k};T_k<\infty].
```
If the degree is bounded on a path, then $`W^{\rm deg}=0`$ and the hitting-time expression is 0 for all sufficiently large $`k`$. If the degree is unbounded, all levels hit exactly (unit steps), and $`T_k\to\infty`$ by nonexplosion; thus $`k e^{-T_k}=X(T_k)\to W^{\rm deg}`$ almost surely. The variables $`k^\mu e^{-\mu T_k}\mathbf1_{T_k<\infty}`$ are dominated by $`(\sup_{t\ge0}X(t))^\mu`$. This is integrable: the standard maximal inequality for a nonnegative right-continuous supermartingale (optional stopping at first passage, or finite-grid maximal inequality followed by dense grids and increasing horizons) gives $`\Pr(\sup_t X(t)>u)\le 2/u`$. Integrating this bound against $`\mu u^{\mu-1}du`$ above 2 uses $`\mu<1`$. Dominated convergence gives $`k^\mu\pi(D_o\ge k)\to A`$. Any of the admissible tilt limits gives the same value of $`A`$, since step 7 identifies the full degree marginal.

**12. PROVED — Flux supplies the pointwise regularity.** Since $`D(0)=2`$, for each $`k\ge2`$ the unique possible jump from $`k`$ to $`k+1`$ at the sampled newborn root occurs at $`T_{k+1}`$ if this time is finite. Its counting process has predictable intensity $`(L+\rho)\mathbf1_{D=k}`$ evaluated just before time $`a`$; left limits can be dropped under the time integral. Discount its compensator and use the age representation and step 11:
```math
\mu\pi(D_o\ge k+1)
=\mu\mathbb E_\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu a}(L(a)+\rho(a))\mathbf1_{D(a)=k}\,da
=\pi[(k-c_o+\rho_o)\mathbf1_{D_o=k}].
```
The intensity of this crossing is bounded by $`k`$, so the identity holds also in expectation over the infinite age range. Write $`H_k=\pi(D_o\ge k)`$, $`p_k=\pi(D_o=k)`$. The upper bound $`k-c_o+\rho_o\le k`$ gives $`p_k\ge \mu H_{k+1}/k`$. On $`c_o\le\sqrt{k}`$ the rate is at least $`k-\sqrt{k}`$, so the same identity gives
```math
\frac{\mu H_{k+1}}k\le p_k\le
\frac{\mu H_{k+1}}{k-\sqrt{k}}+\pi(c_o>\sqrt{k}).
```
By step 7 the final term is $`O(e^{-\theta\sqrt{k}})`$ for a fixed $`\theta>0`$, hence negligible even relative to the point scale $`k^{-1-\mu}`$. Together with step 11 and $`A>0`$ this gives $`p_k\sim\mu A k^{-1-\mu}`$. The extra ingredient beyond a cumulative estimate is exactly the flux identity with effective rate asymptotic to $`k`$ off an exponentially small core-degree exceptional set. A cumulative estimate alone cannot be differenced termwise. All $`n`$-limits here are for fixed $`k`$; the large-$`k`$ limit is applied to the already limiting probabilities.
