**Mark calculation.** Under the spatial birth law the pre/post-promotion parent has the same degree and `β(mark>K)=π[(L/D)1_{D>K}]/μ`. The deficit from `π(D>K)/μ` is at most `π(c)/[μ(K+1)]` (or use `π c=2`, since the parent orientation and mass transport make mean children equal to one). Thus `β(mark>K)∼(A/μ)K^(−μ)`. It also holds for the root observed at stable age, since the mark is permanent. Among **counted** tops with birth mark `>N`, the final parent is automatically high. Loss from child cut is `o(β(mark>N))`: split its independent exponential age at a fixed `a`, use the uniform newborn degree mean bound `2e^a` at all ages `≤a` and then send `a→∞`. Dividing by Claim 20's density gives top mark fraction `∼1/(μ log N)`. This is one extra inference under the same allowed inputs; it does not identify the mark law seen at a uniform surviving core's component top. In the independent `10^7` simulation the uniform-core mark tails were `.34732, .18893, .09494, .05184, .02685` vs predicted `.35243, .18930, .09580, .05146, .02604`; d5's corresponding observed tails `.34960, .18962, .09749, .05209, .02620` are consistent in scale. The once-counted fractions of tops with mark `>N` were `.65400, .50577, .38987, .32463, .28179` vs leading predictions `.76768, .51971, .38384, .30991, .25589`.

## Verdict and list of gaps / nonclaims

1. **No fatal gap in the board statements.** The proof should explicitly define the clipped downward test on the larger event after replacing the parent indicator. The companion proof already does. The positive mass of the high-promoter event and the oriented use of the spatial birth identity are supplied above.
2. “Conditional on its environment” must refer to the adaptive physical rates/initial environment. A future-conditioned rate law is not justified. All comparisons in this proof can and do use only adapted rates.
3. The given tail constant is for large multiples of three, in the **limiting unconditioned core law**. Rounding changes the prefactor by the elementary monotonicity argument; conditioning on survival changes it by `1/p_N`. Neither estimate is a finite-`n` uniform-in-`N` bound, an extremal result, nor a sharp decay estimate.
4. Claim 20 is sequential. It does not give an approximation for a growing threshold observed at finite size, a threshold-uniform rate, or a correction term to the count. The finite-cutoff deficit is real at the simulation precision and does not by itself tell whether the asymptotic coefficient is wrong.
5. The TV claim is for the ordinary rooted component with the parent and original promoter mark omitted. It does not silently carry over under core/ordinary-size bias, to the whole forest jointly, to the ordinary RFT started from an edge, or to a process in which deleting a vertex at its first crossing changes subsequent physical dynamics. Neither joint age/parent mark extensions on the board nor inert-seed core-growth claims were audited here. These two claims alone do not determine the log-core-count distribution of a counted component.
