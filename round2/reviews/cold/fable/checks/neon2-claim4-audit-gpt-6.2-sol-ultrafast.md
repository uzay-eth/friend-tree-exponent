## Full formal-to-analytic bridge and scope

Let `z=1/s` and let `f(z)` be one of these analytic functions evaluated on the positive real ray near `z=0`. The prefix argument gives expansions to every integer order, with rational coefficients `F∈z Q[[z]]`, and an error `O(z^(k+1))` after the `k`th term for every fixed `k`. Sums and products admit the corresponding formal operations: replace each factor by its truncation to the desired order, bound the errors using the boundedness of every factor near 0, and repeat for every order. If `f` were algebraic over `C(s)=C(z)`, clear all rational coefficient denominators to take a nonzero polynomial `P(z,X)` vanishing on the ray. The expansion of `P(z,f(z))` is `P(z,F)`; if the latter had a first nonzero coefficient at `z^m`, division by `z^m` and the positive-ray limit would contradict the identically zero function. This is uniqueness of ordinary asymptotic expansions. **A convergent Puiseux expansion for an algebraic branch is true but unnecessary.** If used, its convergent fractional powers on a local sector are uniquely determined on the positive ray, and comparison to the complete integer-power expansion forces any noninteger terms to vanish by the first unmatched power. No analytic extension of the actual jet is assumed.
