NEON CLAIM 1: HOLDS

## 1. What is certified

I audited `ft2-neon@12202c2`, with a clean status for the proof and certificate sources. Let the induced core be oriented away from a core root. Put `h=0` at the root and `h=1` everywhere else; `k` counts core children, `L` counts leaf children, `D=L+k+h`. The core is connected because the interior of every path between degree-at-least-two vertices has degree at least two. In the growth process the root is the middle of the deterministic unlabelled three-vertex path, and never ceases to be core.

The eight potential tables store signed integer scores divided by `S`. At a vertex use `f(min(L,LF),k,h)` when `k<=KF` and `f(VSTAR_h)` otherwise. The embedding for all other tables uses the exact `(L,k,h)` while `D<D0`; for `D>=D0` use indices `INF`, `T1(κ,h)`, `T2(κ,h)`, `T3(κ,h)` with `κ=min(k,KT+1)` and weights `(s³,3ts²,3t²s,t³)`, `t=D0/D`, `s=1−t`. In particular the seam `D=D0` uses `T3` alone. Each embedded slot is interpolated separately (multiply the slot weights). Motifs are:

| Table | Occurrences and restrictions |
|---|---|
| `f` | Each core vertex as above |
| `g` | Each oriented core edge `(p,w)`; `g(INF,INF)=0` |
| `hV` | Each directed two-edge path `(p,u,w)` with middle `1<=k_u<=KT`, automatically `h_u=1`; zero on middle `INF` |
| `hC` | Each apex `u` with `2<=k_u<=KT` and each unordered pair of distinct core children; zero on apex `INF` |
| `qP4` | Each directed three-edge path `(a,b,c,d)`; `b,c` nonroot, each `1<=k<=KP4`, `D<DP4`; other slots embedded |
| `qYt` | Each `(p,u;{w1,w2})` with two distinct core children, `h_u=1`, `2<=k_u<=KYT`, `D_u<DYT`; parent/children embedded |
| `qYb` | `u` has distinct children `w1,w2` and `w2` has child `c`; fixed `u` has `2<=k<=KYB` with either `h`, fixed `w2` has `h=1`, `1<=k<=KYB`, both `D<DYB`; embed `w1,c` |
| `qS3` | Apex with either `h`, `3<=k<=KS3`, `D<DS3`, and each unordered triple of distinct core children; children embedded |

The rate of attaching at a physical `w` is `r(w)/n`, `r(w)=Σ_{v~w}1/D_v`; the rates `r` sum to `n`. At a core this yields gain at rate `L+ρ`, where `ρ=Σ_{core neighbors}1/D_v`; promotions of its leaves have total rate `L/D`. A promotion holds the old parent's `D` fixed, changes `(L,k)` to `(L−1,k+1)`, and gives one new core `(1,0,1)`. The conditional mean drift is `ℒΦ(T)/n`. For all rooted cores the exact upper check gives `ℒΦ−(P/Q)Φ<=0` and the lower check gives `>=0`. The comparison sweep gives `c0 C<=Φ<=C0 C`; all constants on **both production sides** are positive, although generic lower certification only needs `C0>0` and `Φ(T3)>0`.

At `n=2` there is no core and neither certificate starts there. Either first attachment gives `T3`, with `C3=1`, `Φ(T3)=f(2,0,0)` (no higher motifs). Taking expectations gives `EΦ(T_{j+1}) <= (1+λ_hi/j) EΦ(T_j)` on the upper side and `>=` with `λ_lo` on the lower. Iterate positive deterministic factors, including the empty product at `n=3`. Upper: `c0 E C_n <= EΦ_hi <= Φ_hi(T3) product`; lower: `C0 E C_n >= EΦ_lo >= Φ_lo(T3) product`. At `n=3` these immediately say `prefactor_lo <= 1 <= prefactor_hi`; the prefactors are verified in §4 below. Since `log(1+λ/j)=λ/j+O(j^-2)`, the logarithm of each product is `λ log n+O(1)`. This already bounds liminf and limsup; the established existence of the mean exponent gives the bracket as stated. No prior numerical interval enters any implication. The builder **does** start its float search at `0.5657237` (`cert_b3f_build.c:385`); it is a guess used to find bytes, not a bound assumed by the verifier. The verifier's only exponent input is the candidate `P/Q` in the row residuals, and the rate/tail inequalities below use degree and caps only.

## 2. Named audit steps

### Exact transfers and finite coverage

- **§2.1 / §3.1 lines 87–91, follows.** The four controls of reciprocal degree are `(0,1/(3D0),2/(3D0),1/D0)`; their expectation is `t/D0`. For an inner `f`, transfer `(1/D_v−rbar_{u,v}) Δ_L f` to its incident edge; `f` itself retains the centered gain. For tail `k<=KT`, the same positive edge term uses a degree coefficient `ψ=c2/D²+c3/D³` and the `f` residual gains `(Rbar−ρ)ψ`. With `B2=3t²s` and `B3=t³`, the two edge controls are `c2/(3D0²)` and `c2/D0²+c3/D0³`. Their weighted sum is `c2 t²(s+t)/D0²+c3 t³/D0³=ψ`; summing over incident edges cancels against `f`. This does not assert `ψ=Δ_L f`; for `L<LF` and bounded `k`, C 1167–1187 checks the remaining `ρ(Δ_L f−ψ)` at both endpoints. For `k>KT` no such one-body transfer occurs (C 1189–1195, 1247–1285).
- **§3.1 lines 92–96, follows with the incidence argument.** An extra parent or child at the updating end of an existing edge creates a unique path or fork. Fix the included endpoint's basis state; `a=F0−F1` and `Yref` are functions of that basis state. Transfer weighted `(ρ_ext−Rbar_ext)a/D0` on `T2`, and weighted `(ρ_ext−Rbar_ext)Yref` on `T3`. Each omitted neighbor contributes the matching receiver summand, by reciprocal-degree reproduction. In `hV` there are two possible sources (outgoing edge with omitted parent; incoming edge with omitted child); in `hC` each child serves once as the omitted neighbor of the other child edge. C 1319–1339, 1384–1432 and 1539–1585 agree. At `k=KT` the **current** middle/apex still receives the transfer. A bud at that vertex sends unrestricted slots to the overflow label, kills its old restricted slots and creates no new restricted triple at that site; the weights do not change. With `k>KT` transfers in the old edge's drift at that endpoint are disabled.
- **§3.1 lines 97–100, follows with the incidence argument.** On `P4(a,b,c,d)` take `hV(a,b,c)` updated at `c` with omitted `d`, and `hV(b,c,d)` updated at `b` with omitted `a`. On `Ytop`, take `hC` with omitted parent and each of the two paths with the other child omitted. On `Ybot`, take `hV(u,w2,c)` updated at `u` with omitted `w1` and `hC(u;w1,w2)` updated at `w2` with omitted `c`. On `S3`, take each child pair with the third child omitted. The newly fixed slots are exactly the inner cut domains checked on the donor side; other external neighbors keep the interval `[0,1/2]` per neighbor. This covers root/nonroot and symmetric state repeats because the incidence is between physical vertices.
- **§3 line 82 / newborn accounting, follows with the deletion argument.** Delete the promoted newborn core from a newly created motif. It is terminal and deleting it leaves a unique existing motif, of one smaller size. The vertex equation charges newborn singleton plus edge; edge rows charge newborn path at the child end or fork at the parent end; path rows charge newborn chain at the bottom, Ytop at the middle, Ybot as a new short sibling at the top; fork rows charge newborn S3 at the apex or one Ybot for each long-child direction. Eligibility uses the post-bud state for the promoting slot, and unaffected old slots keep their states. The `F0b=F0` assertion is valid also when birth terms were assigned to the bud controls: restricted newborn motifs have zero control at that location's `INF`. The verifier's `Fjb` terms and `nb_*` terms implement this distinction. For existing motifs whose cutoff is left, use zero exactly at the exited slot (or `T3` for an unrestricted inner-to-tail gain).

- **§4.3 lines 131–132, follows.** The four cubic controls for `ψ(x)` on `[0,x0]` are `[0,0,c2*x0²/3,c2*x0²+c3*x0³]`; the prose lists the distinct values. - **Coverage inference (needed between §4 and §5), follows with an argument.** Assign each tail slot its own gain/bud and the gain credits it sends to inner neighbors *in that same motif*. Such inner rows omit the tail-neighbor contribution, which is restored by `t B3(d;t)/D0`. Tail-to-tail credit is already in the first slot's `ρ` interval when its own gain is computed. For one such term fix all other motif slots' bases and interpolate; bounded rate intervals used there contain the physical rate for every such choice. Interpolate this slot with nonnegative T1/T2/T3 weights; `INF` supplies zero for its own term but can participate in other slots' terms and transferred sources. Centered sources are exact products of independent slot evaluations, so they expand directly by multilinearity. Summing the separate contributions gives a product-weighted sum of the verified row bounds, without assuming any independence of physical states. In a restricted slot the omitted `INF` row has zero value, slot derivatives and source; any entering inner-to-tail state is exact T3. The sole skipped `g(INF,INF)` row has checked potential zero, no own-transition term from either slot, and no f-source there. The checks include even basis labels that can only appear by interpolation, not only labels achievable at a single integer degree. Consequently unbounded `D`, `L`, and `k`, multiple tail slots, and non-reachable combinations in individual boxes do not invalidate the implication.

### Zero-sum re-charging

**Follows for all trees in the theorem.** Every core edge has one parent and one child, so the edge contributes one to `k` at its tail and one to `h` at its head, hence its signed contribution is zero. Root `h=0` by convention; with one core `k=h=0`; the one-edge tree has empty core and gives empty-sum zero, but supplies no `f` initial value or drift step. This depends on orienting the **core**; e.g. setting `h=1` for the first core because the physical graph is rooted at a leaf would be invalid and is not what the verifier does. Each bottom charge is genuinely local: at most one `g/hV/P4` of each kind, and in `Ybot` the long-child and its apex are forced by the bottom's parent chain, giving exactly `k_apex−1` siblings when eligible. Each apex charge has the corresponding binomial number of child pairs or triples. Thus extrema by charged slot, including zero for optional bottom charges and missing `INF` apex, bound each total charge. Change the lower per-core charge by `+(k−h)/4` and take its minimum; the sum is unchanged. C 850–859 applies it to all finite core states, 862–879 uses `KF+1` as a lower bound on the actual `k` for VSTAR, and the upper comparison does not need the shift. Saturating `L` at `LF>=D0` does not switch any of the non-f domains under comparison: it retains the same tail set of extrema. The re-charging is used only here, not in the drift. For production, both lower minima have witness `(L,k,h)=(0,1,1)` in the *relaxed local charge* envelope; this does not say there is a physical tree attaining either comparison constant.

To avoid quadratic work on a 100,000-leaf star, siblings that are physical leaves reuse one successor score: permuting those leaves fixes the core root and maps each successor to the same rooted tree. ## 6. Verdict, gaps, and comparison with existing reviews

I find no mathematical gap in the production proof and no concrete counterexample. One wording nit: proof line 16 says “there exist exact positive rationals $`c_0, C_0, \Phi_{\mathcal C}(T_3)`$” without immediately narrowing that assertion by mode. Generic lower code at 889–890 accepts a possibly nonpositive `c0`; the line-19 lower conclusion uses only the valid `C0, Φ(T3)` positivity, and both supplied files have positive `c0` independently confirmed. Proof line 107's `min(1,D0/c)` also needs the usual `c=0` convention; C uses the correct bound. Neither wording issue affects Claim 1. For production, native pre-GMP arithmetic is on x86-64 LP64 and all header parameters fall within the hardening limits; `D^4` with finite production `D<=14181`, native slot differences bounded by the entry guard, and scaled centering sums are within 64-bit range. I do not infer portability to an ABI where GMP's `long` argument is only 32 bits.

Only after deriving the above and running the production evaluator did I read `round2/checks/b3f18_sol.md` and `attacks/astra-on-neon-1.md`. I agree with both on the production result. Sol audited the previous generic-input overflow and offset issues; they are not failures on the production bytes, and I independently reproduced the *correct* byte-60 rejection on the hardened pin. Astra notes a rounded entry count in the earlier proof; hardened line 23 now gives the exact count. My incremental evidence: a fresh binary layout reader and physical attachment evaluator through 100,001 vertices, exact enumeration through n=10, both opposite-endpoint failures already at T3, independently regenerated comparison values, and a mutation of exactly the production evaluator's T3 score (read overlay). Unlike Sol and Astra's direct C coefficient probes, my algebra exercise tests the published rational formula by generating the Bernstein basis independently, and my source audit ties those terms to the C blocks; my production result relies on the existing exhaustive GMP logs and the lead's replay for all rows. These roles are distinct from testing 105 trees per side.
