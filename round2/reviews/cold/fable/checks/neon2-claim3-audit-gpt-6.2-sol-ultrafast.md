### C. Pathwise form of the hub-mass calculation

From Fable Step 5, let `A_r=1{τ_K≥r}`, predictable for the step `r−1→r`. The conditional upper drift of the frozen normalized late-promotion sum is bounded by `2 A_r E[C_r−C_{r−1}|F_{r−1}]/r`. This is an **expected drift** bound, not a sample-path inequality on the tracked mass: on a path an already promoted core can keep receiving leaves, increasing its normalized degree without any new promotion. Pathwise, the controlling budget is the increasing immigration compensand `Σ 2A_r(C_r−C_{r−1})/r=2Σ(B_r−B_{r−1})/r`. Its summation-by-parts upper bound by `2Σ_{r>m} B_r/[r(r+1)]` holds on each trajectory because `B_n/n≤Σ_{r≥n}B_r/[r(r+1)]`. After stopping, this tail is bounded by `2(K+1)` and tends to zero, so its **expectation** tends to zero as well; the stopping indicator cannot be replaced by `{τ_K>r}`, which is not predictable and omits the crossing immigration. Then apply Fatou to the nonnegative late mass restricted to no stop. This is strictly weaker as a growth assumption than an a.s. power bound: e.g. a nondecreasing integer sequence of order `n/log²n` meets summability and no fixed sublinear power. It uses no unconditional expectation hypothesis. The Fable expectation version using deterministic `a_n=E C_n` is its analogue but a.s. summability by itself need not bound those unconditional expectations.
