### Bounds at every label and degree dichotomy

For a core target `w` with `L` leaves, the weighted postbirth promotion bound contributes at most `1+ρ_w`, where `ρ_w=Σ_{core x~w}1/D_x`: divide `L+ρ_w` by `L+1` for a nonstar, and use `L/L=1` for a star. Leaf targets contribute at most `Σ_w L_w/D_w`. The donated-core credits sum to `Σ_w c_w/D_w`; hence the two credit terms add to exactly `C`. This gives the factor 2 on every conditioning tree, including the star at size three.

For the lower bound, suppress all rings of the newborn's parent until its independent rate-one first ring. The suppressed degree mean is at most `d e^s` from postbirth degree `d`. The tag is still a leaf, so Jensen gives selection probability at least `∫e^(−s)/(d e^s)ds=1/(2d)`, followed by the uniform `B(1,1)=1/(e−1)` hub lower bound. The remaining prebirth weighted sum is `Σ_w r_w/(D_w+1)`. In a core tree of size `C≥2`, let `J` be the cores with a leaf and core degree at most two. Every core-tree tip belongs to `J`, and the tree degree identity bounds the number of core degrees at least three by `|J|−2`. All other cores are degree-two leafless cores, forming a set of size `H≥C−2|J|`. They have at least `max(0,4H−2C+2)` internal directed edges: cross-edges cannot exceed the total core degree outside that set. The disjoint leaf payments and internal directed-edge payments give at least `|J|/4+max(0,2C−8|J|)/6≥C/16`. For a single core the star contributes at least `2/3`. Thus the lower constant is `1/[32(e−1)]`. The asymptotic exponent here uses the **mean** core-count theorem.

The same hitting estimate shows a nonhub has bounded leaf stock almost surely. If a core has a leaf arbitrarily late, take successive leaf-present stopping times separated by one time unit. At each, the core can stay silent and one chosen leaf can ring at least `K` times in the next unit with fixed positive conditional probability. That leaf then remains unpromoted and supplies at least `K` leaves. Conditional iteration rules out infinitely many trials with no hit. Thus a nonhub that enters the core eventually has no leaves, and any later degree gain would create one. A vertex that never enters the core already has degree one; eventual constant degree implies zero share directly. A countable intersection gives the assertion for all vertices simultaneously.
