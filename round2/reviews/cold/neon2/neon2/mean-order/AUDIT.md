## Lemma 1 — embedding, limits, and stopping (README 10, 39–68)

- For a tree on `m >= 2`, all degrees are positive. The conditional rate sum for attachment to `v` is `r(v) = sum_{u~v} 1/D_u`; the discrete attachment probability is `r(v)/m`. Thus `E[D_v(m+1)/(m+1) | full history] <= D_v(m)/m` by `r(v) <= D_v(m)`. The normalized degree is bounded by 1, so almost-sure convergence also gives `L^1` convergence. For a future label this argument begins at its birth, not before it.
- At size `m`, racing the `m` clocks gives an `Exp(m)` holding time independent of the uniform caller. Neighbor choice is independent of that holding time. One can construct the entire jump chain with fresh caller/neighbor marks independent of the holding-time sequence, since the holding-time parameter depends only on the size. Consequently `W_N` depends only on holding times, and `Z_v` only on the embedded chain. The pure birth process is nonexplosive, and its size tends to infinity. For completeness, starting with one individual the Yule population has geometric law of parameter `e^{-t}` on `{1,2,...}`; starting with `N` gives a sum of `N` independent copies. The normalized size is a martingale with second moment `N^2+N(1-e^{-t})`, hence converges a.s. and in `L^2`; the scaled geometric law tends to `Exp(1)` and the sum to `Gamma(N,1)`. Its limit is positive a.s. and has mean `N`.
- The claimed limit in (3) is justified in `L^1`: with `X_t=D_v(t)/N(t)`, `|e^{-t}D_v(t)-W_N Z_v| <= |e^{-t}N(t)-W_N| + W_N |X_t-Z_v|`. The first term tends to zero in `L^1`; the second does by ordinary dominated convergence with integrable `W_N`. Independence gives (4). For deterministic `t`, conditional expectation contracts `L^1`, and the Markov property evaluates the conditional future normalized degree as `e^{-t}` times the restarted normalized degree expectation. Statewise convergence for every finite tree then identifies the conditional limit. The resulting càdlàg process `e^{-t}M(T_t,v)` is the UI conditional-expectation martingale closed by `W_N Z_v`. The corresponding signed difference is UI as well. Work with the usual augmentation of the filtration.
- The generator of degree is `r(v)`. The count `L_v` increases by one on births to `v`; it decreases by one on births to a current leaf neighbor of `v`; these events are disjoint. A leaf neighbor can receive a birth only from `v` choosing it, so total decrement rate is `L_v/D_v`. When `L_v=0`, a gain takes `g=(L_v-1)_+` from zero to zero; drift is zero. When `L_v=1`, a gain adds one to `g`, a promotion leaves it at zero, hence drift is nonnegative. When `L_v>=2` (including `L_v=2`, where promotion reaches `g=0`), drift is `r(v)-L_v/D_v >= L_v-1 = g`. At `D_v=1`, only `L_v=0,1` are possible. For the edge seed both vertices initially have `(D,L)=(1,1)`; a birth at `v` adds a leaf while its old neighbor remains a leaf, whereas a birth at the old neighbor removes the leaf from `v`'s count without changing `D_v`. There is no exception in (5).
- To remove localization rigorously, stop both discounted processes at the first size `K'` (an auxiliary size cutoff, distinct from the certificate's `K`). The stopped discounted generator signs are those of `L f-f` before the stop. For each fixed time horizon `t`, the stopped values, for **all** `K'`, are dominated by the same integrable `N(t)` because `e^{-s} <= 1` and population is increasing. A claim of mere integrability of separate stopped values would not suffice. Passing to limits in the conditional inequalities (or multiplying by events in the past and using dominated convergence) proves the supermartingale for degree and submartingale for `g`. Taking expectations and using `g<=D` proves (5).
- General stopping argument used below: stop the discounted local table at the first exit, taking the prescribed terminal table entry or payoff on the exit jump. On bounded horizons the compensated generator formula applies, by size localization if necessary. The affected coordinates before exit have bounded rates under the stated cutoffs, and the stopped discounted local value is uniformly bounded by the max absolute (finite) table/terminal value. A birth changes the tree only once; clocks do not tie a.s. Apply the stopped drift inequality at `t∧tau` and take `t→∞` by bounded convergence on the local process, with limit zero on `{tau=∞}`. Separately for the closed martingale, optional sampling yields `M(T',v)=E[1_{tau<∞} e^{-tau}M(T_tau,v)+1_{tau=∞}W_NZ_v]`, and the same for differences. On a no-exit event where the relevant degrees stay bounded, `Z_v=0` because the population tends to infinity. This applies even when the stopped local lower bound is signed. It does not require the exit to be a.s. finite or a bound on the full tree at exit. A degree cutoff written as equality `D=DM+1` agrees with `D>DM` when started inside and because degrees jump up by one; the clipped `U` bound for starts already above cutoff instead uses the global estimate.

## Lemma 2 — exact pairing and endpoint split (README 72–105)

Condition on the entire second-birth tree; the Markov property and (4) divide each terminal `M` difference by `n+2`. The two transition probabilities supply the other factor `1/[n(n+1)]`. This also defines the conditional expression for a tree used as a seed, regardless of whether a separately specified history reaches that labeled tree.

1. First attach `a` to `x`. Its sole neighbor `x` now has degree `D_x+1`; the scaled weight for a second birth at `a` is exactly `r_T(x)/(D_x+1)`. It produces `x-a-b` with the displayed orientation.
2. A second birth at `x` creates two twin leaves, whose rooted trees are isomorphic by swapping the labels; `M` is unchanged by relabeling, so the difference is zero even when the probability itself is large.
3. A second birth at a distinct non-neighbor `y` sees no change in `r_T(y)`: the new leaf is at `x` and the changed degree `D_x+1` is not at a neighbor of `y`. Both orders have scaled weight `r_T(x)r_T(y)`; their signed payoffs are opposite by swapping the labels on the same final shape.
4. A second birth at a neighbor `y` sees just one altered term, `1/(D_x+1)-1/D_x = -1/[D_x(D_x+1)]`. For the order `x,y`, subtracting the reverse weight leaves `q_y-q_x` where `q_z=r_T(z)/[D_z(D_z+1)]`. Multiplying by `M(a_x)-M(a_y)` is orientation invariant. This includes `T_2` where the edge coefficient is zero; no denominator vanishes. These four cases exhaust the second attachment site.

Expanding each edge payoff gives `-q_x M(a_x)-q_y M(a_y)+q_y M(a_x)+q_x M(a_y)`. The last two are nonnegative by (4), so dropping them gives (7) after grouping incident edges. This applies even if the edge coefficient or the payoff difference is negative. Summing rates over targets counts each caller's total neighbor-selection mass exactly once, giving `sum_x r_T(x)=n`. The bound `D_x+1<=n` gives `sum_x r_T(x)/(D_x+1)>=1`, including equality at `T_2`.

## Lemma 3 — all six local certificate processes (README 109–133)

In general the tree process is Markov; its projection to these coordinates need not be. The drift proof is valid because the inequalities hold for the generator of the **full tree** at each interior state. At a fixed row the drift is affine in all uncertain rates. Checking interval endpoints (or all box corners for two intervals) bounds the drift even when the actual rates are correlated. Unchanged projected states contribute zero to the jump part. The discounted processes are sub- or supermartingales according to sign; there is no assertion needed that they have identically zero drift.

- **`v,w` (112–117).** Core status persists. `0 <= L <= D`, including a star center with `L=D`. A birth to the core changes `(D,L)` to `(D+1,L+1)`; the rate is exactly `L` plus the sum over `D-L` core neighbors, each contributing at most `1/2`. A birth to one of its leaf neighbors changes the state to `(D,L-1)` at total rate `L/D`; when `L=0` the term vanishes. At the degree cutoff `41`, the boundary inequalities sandwich `M` by Lemma 1. If the cutoff is never reached, degree is bounded and the stopping argument gives zero terminal Doob value. The verified drift signs give the claimed lower and upper bounds respectively.
- **`U` (119–124).** Before promotion, the neighbor `x` remains core and `a` remains a leaf; the latter generates births at `x` at rate 1. All other contributions to the increment of `D_x` are nonnegative. Promotion of `a` has intensity `1/D_x(t-)`; on promotion `a` has `(D,L)=(2,1)` as `x` is core and the new neighbor is a leaf. A birth at `x` cannot be the birth at `a`; no double exit. With `H(t)=P(t<tau_a)`, the compensated counting process for the first promotion gives `H(t)=1-∫_0^t E[1_{s<tau_a}/D_x(s-)] ds`. At deterministic times one can equivalently use the pre-stop indicator; endpoint conventions do not affect this integral. Thus `H'(t)>=-H(t)/d` a.e., so `e^{t/d}H(t)` is nondecreasing and `H(t)>=e^{-t/d}`. By `1-e^{-tau_a}=∫_0^{tau_a}e^{-t}dt`, including `tau_a=∞`, `E[e^{-tau_a}]=1-∫_0^∞ e^{-t}H(t)dt <= 1/(d+1)`. This uses predictable intensity, not an assumption that conditioning on the random degree path makes the promotion time exponentially distributed. UI stopping and `Z_a=0` on no promotion give the global `w(2,1)/(d+1)` bound; `w(2,1)` is positive both by (5) and by the replay. At `d=41`, and for starts with greater degree, this bound is at most `w(2,1)/42<=U(41)`. For degrees 2–40, the one-step stopped payoff is `w(2,1)` for promotion, `U(d+1)` for a birth to `x`. The worst supermartingale drift occurs at the minimum degree-increment rate of 1 because `U` is nonincreasing. The checked inequality is exactly that drift. Even if neither cutoff occurs, `a` stays degree 1 and contributes zero in the limit.
- **`phi2` (126–127).** Each tracked core has the other core as a neighbor throughout, so `0<=L_a<=D_a-1` and `0<=L_b<=D_b-1`. Core-neighbor contributions to the gain rate of `a` are exactly `1/D_b` plus a sum over `D_a-L_a-1` other cores, in `[0,(D_a-L_a-1)/2]`; plus `L_a` from leaves. Same with `a,b` exchanged. Each promotion category has rate `L_i/D_i`. A birth can affect a coordinate only by attaching to its core or to one of its leaf neighbors. Neither core is a leaf of the other, and the leaf sets are disjoint (a leaf has just one neighbor), so a single birth cannot affect both pairs. All destinations preserve the domain; zero-leaf promotions are absent. At first degree 15, both degrees are within `v,w` coverage, so the boundary `v(a)-w(b)` is a valid lower payoff. The simultaneous cutoff corner is unreachable in one birth and checked anyway. If no cutoff, both degrees stay bounded. There are `sum_{D=2}^{14}D=104` coordinate states per core; `104^2=10816` interior rows and four rate corners per row, including coincident corners.
- **`phi1` (129–130).** Before exit, `b` remains a leaf and a distinct core neighbor of `a` remains core, so `1<=L_a<=D_a-1`. Birth at `a` increments both counts at rate in `[L_a,L_a+(D_a-L_a)/2]`; the ring of `b` is included in its leaf part. Other leaf promotions remove one leaf at rate `(L_a-1)/D_a`, zero when `L_a=1`. Birth at `b` has rate `1/D_a`, removes exactly one leaf from `a` without changing `D_a`, and makes `b` core of degree 2 with exactly one leaf. Its destination is therefore `phi2(D_a,L_a-1;2,1)` even at `L_a=1`. No single birth can hit both exit faces; the degree-15 exit has `b` still leaf and is bounded by `v(15,L_a)-U(15)`. No exit implies both degrees bounded. There are `1+...+13=91` interior rows. In every `x-a-b` extension, `x` is core even for a leaf `x` in the original tree, and the start is `(2,1)`.
- **`phi1_x` (132–133).** The tracked `x` stays core and distinct from leaf `b`. The `a` gain rate includes exact `1/D_x`, plus `L_a`, plus the other core contributions bounded by `(D_a-L_a-1)/2`. The exact birth rate at `x` is `1/D_a+sum_{u~x,u!=a}1/D_u`, between `1/D_a` and `D_x` (indeed at most `D_x-1+1/D_a`). In the `T_2` extension `x` has one leaf neighbor and incoming rate `1+1/2=3/2`, correctly in `[1/2,2]`; replacing `D_x` by a core-only bound would be unsafe. A birth at `x` cannot change `a`'s leaf count because `x` is already core, even if the caller was `a`. Rates/destinations for other leaf promotions and `b` promotion are the same as for `phi1`. All four changed-state categories are disjoint. At `D_x=9`, use `phi1(D_a,L_a)` regardless of `x`'s degree; at `D_a=15` use `v(15,L_a)-U(15)`. If both thresholds are assigned to a formal boundary cell, the `phi1(15,L_a)` value is itself bounded by this last expression; that corner is never hit from interior by a single birth. No exit implies bounded degrees of `a` and `b`. There are `7*91=637` interior rows, four rate corners each. For the path start `D_x=D_T(x)+1`, `D_a=2,L_a=1`, so this applies for `D_T(x)<=7`. Larger degrees do not need this refinement.

### Independent generator and boundary attacks

I used actual graph births to evaluate each row, rather than reimplementing the verifier's rate intervals. For each selected tracked vertex or ordered tuple in a concrete tree `g`, I recomputed the local coordinates from `attach(g,y)` for every `y`, including choosing the correct exit payoff for `U`, `phi1`, and `phi1_x`. In integer ticks the tested expression was `sum_y rate(y)*(value(attach(g,y))-value(g)) - value(g)`, where `rate(y)` was independently accumulated from caller marks. Equivalently, the discrete one-birth drift with discount `n/(n+1)` has the same sign (divide this CT expression by `n+1`). In addition to all 24 small trees, I used the 8-vertex broom, a degree-40 star and two double stars with central degrees `(14,14)` and `(14,8)` to hit the actual exits. All rows passed: `v=66, w=66, U=172, phi2=78, phi1=112, phi1_x=84` (578 total). These are concrete-tree regressions; the all-row corner check is what certifies every tree.

Additional exact one-tick attacks on the generated tables: lowering `U[41]` by one was rejected as `U boundary violation`; raising `phi2` at `(15,14,2,1)`, `(2,1,15,14)`, and `(15,14,15,14)` was rejected on the corresponding boundary; raising `phi1[15][14]` was rejected; raising `phi1_x` at `(9,2,1)`, `(2,15,14)`, and `(9,15,14)` was rejected on its corresponding boundary. The stock replay also rejected all eight stock mutations. The independent script used for these checks is `neon2_mean_audit.py` in this workspace.

### Zero-float replay and verifier limitations

Command (from `[local path]`): `python3 -B round2/neon2/mean-order/cert_mean_order.py`. It exited 0 with `ALL_EXACT_CHECKS_PASSED`; parameters `SCALE=1000000000, DM=40, K=14, DXM=8`; counts `858,858,39,10816,91,637` (13,299 rows); 24 signed/endpoint trees; 8/8 planted controls `REJECTED`. I scanned the AST for float/complex literals and confirmed recursively that all generated payload leaves were exactly Python `int`. Each true division on the replay path has a `Fraction` operand; formatting and JSON only handle integer/string output. Thus the documented replay performs zero floating-point arithmetic. The independent command `python3 -B neon2_mean_audit.py round2/neon2/mean-order/cert_mean_order.py` exited 0 and produced the concrete checks and attacks above.

I also tested the limits of treating `verify_tables_exact` as an API for untrusted external inputs:

- Replacing the zero boundary tick `v[41][0]` by `0.0` passed the normal verifier, confirming that it does not enforce integer tick types. The CLI has no external input path and its generator emits integers; this is an API contract weakness, not a float in the replay.
- Raising `U[2]` by one tick passed with the early-gap lower bound smaller by `1/(6*10^9)`. This perturbation is **valid** for the drift and boundary conditions, so accepting it is right for a general table verifier. It demonstrates that comparing exact output with the published constants is necessary to verify the literal numerical claim for replacement tables.
- With `python3 -B -O` using `runpy.run_path`, increasing `v[41][5]` by one **passed the standalone verifier**, as `assert` statements are removed under optimization. In the same probe `run_negative_controls` raised `AssertionError: Planted defect v_interior was NOT rejected!`; hence the bundled CLI would stop before printing success. The documented replay uses no optimization. For a reusable verifier, enforce types and use explicit raises if optimized imports must fail closed.

## Theorems and constants (README 12–32, 137–152)

For every original vertex `x`, both extensions raise its degree from `D_T(x)` to `D_T(x)+1`. In an edge extension the leaf `a_x` is adjacent to that core, regardless of `y`'s degree. In a path extension the new `a` has one core neighbor `x` and one leaf `b`; this is true even when `x` was originally an endpoint. The replay values and the resulting pointwise margins are:

| Original degree | Path lower bound | Edge-leaf upper bound | Exact difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | `95490419/200000000` | `367808441/1000000000` | `54821827/500000000` |
| 2 | `49433051/125000000` | `70025291/250000000` | `28840811/250000000` |
| ≥3 | `834963/3125000` | `14205589/62500000` | `2493671/62500000` |

The third leaf bound follows by the clipped `U` bound and its nonincreasing property. The third margin is smallest. Its excess over `1/26` is `1167723/812500000`. Because each pointwise margin holds for **every** neighbor, averaging over `D_T(x)` neighbors preserves it; all weights `r_T(x)/(D_x+1)` are nonnegative and sum to at least 1. This proves (1) with no finite-size restriction. In the path distribution from the original edge seed, label 2 is center with probability `1/2`; when it is a leaf, it is symmetric to label 3 and contributes zero expected difference. In the center case label 3 is a leaf, and division by size 3 converts `M` to mean share. Thus the factor is exactly `1/6`; `(1187553139/1000000000 - 367808441/1000000000)/6 = 409872349/3000000000`, exceeding `2/15` by `9872349/3000000000`. The decimals in the headline are correct at the shown precision. Edge-seed symmetry gives `m_1=m_2`; taking expectation over the size `u-1` tree gives every later strict comparison with the stated bound, including `u=3` from `T_2`.
