## Common completion, physical conditioning and exact upper tails

The old tree has size `b−1`. Pair both placements and compare continuation tests at size `b+1`, even if the earlier label has already promoted. The continuation test is the exact indicator-tail probability at `1/3`, not a moment or a smoothed step. The common tail scale at size `n` is `u^(n−1)`, `u=2/3`; one more physical birth multiplies the normalized successor by `u`. The factorial denominator from the two-birth placement identity is `b(b−1)`, not `(b+1)b`.

For a pending tip with parent degree `k` and `j` *other* leaf neighbours there are four distinct channels. The parent promotes the tag at rate `1/k` and a sibling at aggregate `j/k`; the latter does not add a leaf at the parent. The parent gains at rate `g`, with the `j+1` leaf senders contributing one each and the remaining `k−1−j` core neighbours contributing at most `1/2` each. Their incoming arrows cannot promote the pending tip or its siblings. Thus `g∈[j+1,(k+j+1)/2]`. Remaining births preserve the two tracked coordinates and still increase size by one. The integer recursion is the maximum at these two corners, with the correct common denominator `3 k n` and ceiling. It does not assume any of the rounded rows is monotone. Taking the minimum with the current locked urn and using a locked cap at `b+15` is safe by finite backward induction.

In either case the locked comparison follows from a nondecreasing degree deficit `Σ_neighbor(1−1/D_neighbor)`: new neighbours add zero initially, and old terms do not decrease.
