## Reconstruct the first bad coefficient

Put `z=1/s`, use size excluding the silent slot and degree including it at the top, and write `w=(p-2)!(p-1)`. Intrinsic moves add one vertex; diagonal terms add none; either child copy has size at most the old size, even with the shifted leaf. Direct productions reset to a two-vertex newborn. At size less than `p`, no degree supplies a `1/p` denominator. At size exactly `p`, degree `p` is possible only at the top of the star. From a virgin in precisely `p-2` moves that star has only the pure top-growth route; its rates are `1,…,p-2`, independent of the initial mark. Marks reflect the promoter's degree capped at 20; only the mark, **never** the live reciprocal, is capped.

* **Unmarked `U`, `p>=5`.** The terminal top-star promotion reward gives `w/p` at `z^(p-1)`. Every earlier coefficient is integral at `p`. The signed feedback action is size-nonincreasing, so terms containing it cannot reach the required star in time. Independently the whole-tree identity gives `U=G/(1+G)` with `G` intrinsic total-promotion occupation. At this first bad order every nonlinear product uses only earlier coefficients of `G`, hence cannot alter the residue. The reward at a nonroot cannot have degree `p` at size `p`.
* **Trigger `F`, `p>=5`.** The star has zero trigger reward; the first bad order is one later, `z^p`. The diagonal and all intrinsic successors yield zero against the terminal trigger, and there is no child to copy. Only the *independent* direct birth at rate `(p-1)/p` lands on a virgin with reward `1/2`, yielding `w/(2p)`. For `p<=19` the mark `1/p` cannot already exist before this last move, since generating it takes a promoter of degree at least `p`; it is irrelevant to the last virgin's mark-blind reward. For `p>=23` the direct newborn is `J_20`, but the promotion rate still uses `p`.
* **`K_i,20`, `p>=23`.** The top-star terminal reward gives `w/p` at `z^(p-1)` in every row. No older coefficient contains `p` in the denominator because even the terminal size is then `<p` and all marks are at most 20.
* **`K_ij`, `3<=j<=19`, `p>=23`.** At `p+j-3` actions (coefficient of `z^(p+j-2)`), at most `j-1` rate factors can be singular. Each individual rate factor has at most a simple `p` denominator in this size window (`N<=p+18`, hence all relevant degrees `<2p`). To earn all `j-1` factors, the prefix has to be the unique size-`p` top star. Every later action must be singular, so a top gain (which has integral rate here) cannot occur. A post-star child starts with degree two by promotion; within the remaining actions its degree stays well below `p`. A singular child copy must be last, because its small subtree cannot acquire a degree `p` in the remaining budget. To finish at degree `j`, the only maximal term is one top promotion, `j-3` sends at `1/p` from the top to that child, and the shifted copy at `1/p`. Unshifted needs an extra gain, and diagonals, extra promotions or sends to another child spend actions without increasing the chosen child's degree. The residue of `p^(j-1)` times the coefficient is `w(j-1)/j mod p`, nonzero. Parallel arrows to interchangeable leaves are already counted by the initial promotion factor `(p-1)/p`; there is no second multiplicity to insert.
* **`K_i,2`, `p>=23`.** At `p` actions (order `z^(p+1)`), the only two-singularity term paying a degree-two reward is a top promotion followed by the *unshifted* copy. The child's permanent mark is `1/20`, so its full nonnegative rate `1/20-1/p` contributes the **negative** residue `-1` after multiplication by `p`. The shifted copy has degree three, and the positive singular diagonal cannot land at degree two. The residue of `p²` times this coefficient is `-w/2 mod p`, nonzero.

### Why these are the first bad orders, not just first maximal residues

Before the first action whose source contains any degree `p`, follow the actual lineage (including its intrinsic ambient ancestors before child copying). Suppose the vertex with degree `p` at that source is `v`. It needed `p-2` degree gains before the singular action, plus a separate promotion if it was not the original root. Copying prunes ancestors, but no surviving vertex's counted degree falls; the copied root replaces its old parent by a counted slot. The final top `u` of degree `j<p` cannot be `v`. There are two cases. If `v` was the original root, `u` needs a promotion, `j-2` gains and a copy to become top; a shifted copy can combine with a gain only for `j>=3`. Those are disjoint from `v`'s `p-2` gains, so the total is at least `p+j-3` for `j>=3`, or `p` for `j=2`. If `v` was nonroot, `u` could even be its ancestor. When `u` is the original root, its `j-2` gains are disjoint from the `p-2` gains and the promotion of `v`. A root gain might itself be the first singular action when `j>2`, so the lower count is `p+j-3`; for `j=2` there are no root gains and a separate singular action is required, giving `p`. When `u` is also nonoriginal, count the two distinct promotions as well as the two sets of gains: this gives at least `p+j-2>=p`. The terminal rewards in these columns are `p`-integral, and the larger-prime range excludes singular mark denominators. This gives genuine first-index statements, not merely a vanishing leading field residue at shorter lengths.

## Analytic-to-formal check

For `R` and the trigger generator, use the positive chronological expansion with denominator `s+α(S)`, where `α=N-1/D+1/i`; all direct births/copies are numerator transitions. Stop after any fixed number `m` of numerator transitions. The reachable prefix is finite, and each denominator is `O(1/s)` at positive infinity. The continuation is `O(1/s)` uniformly on that finite set by the inherited weighted semigroup estimate (for the trigger, use its own positive bound). Thus the omitted part is `O(s^(-m-1))`. Expanding the retained rational factors recovers precisely the diagonal insertions in the signed generator powers, to every fixed order. For intrinsic total-promotion occupation the same reasoning uses `E N_t<=N_0 exp(t)` and `total_b<=N`; then the Möbius identity proves the expansion for `U` without any assumption about convergence of its formal series or about a signed infinite path sum.

If an analytic relation over `C(s)` holds, clear denominators and substitute `z=1/s`; uniqueness of each finite-order asymptotic expansion makes the polynomial vanish formally. The relation descends to `Q(z)`: take the finite list of monomials from a complex relation; their rational coefficient rows span a finite-dimensional space over `Q`, with the same rank over `C`, so a nonzero rational kernel vector suffices. For a square-free minimal polynomial after descent, clear denominators to `P∈Z[z,X]` and let the first nonzero coefficient of `P_X(z,f)` be `c z^r`. For `n>r`, linearizing around the prefix of `f` to degree `n-1` makes the quadratic remainder start at order `2n>n+r`. The coefficient equation at `n+r` therefore gives `c f_n` in the ring generated by the earlier coefficients. Only primes in `c` and the finite initial block can appear as denominator primes. The residues above exhibit infinitely many primes for each individual response, contradicting algebraicity. This proves no independence of the different responses and no assertion about rational or algebraic zeros of `F(s)-1` or the determinant.
