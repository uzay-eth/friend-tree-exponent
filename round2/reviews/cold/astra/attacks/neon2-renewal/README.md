## 1. Analytic fringe resolvent and occupation normalization

Write `K_s = Δ V_s C`, where `C` here is the immediate-child operator, and denote the core count by `N`. The input paired estimate bounds the integrated absolute difference before the complex phase; hence the same majorant applies for `Re s ≥ ell`. Norm analyticity of `V_s` follows by integrating the exponential series against the contraction semigroup, without assuming a strongly continuous semigroup on all bounded scores. The first-jump equation puts its range in the bounded pointwise generator domain; stopped Dynkin applied to a bounded `f` with bounded `L⁰f` gives the converse. The inverse is locally analytic even at each point of the boundary line. Solving first for `Δ W` shows uniqueness of the bounded solution of `(s−L⁰−CΔ)W=ψ`.

Let `T` sum over downward core fringes, including the top. In `L⁰ T w`, an existing fringe sees its intrinsic changes and its single incoming parent arrow. Regroup the latter by the sender as `T C Δw`; new virgin summands enter as `w(J)T b`. There is no incoming arrow through the inert slot. Thus `(s−L⁰)T W=Tψ−W(J)L⁰N`. For real `s>μ`, stopped Dynkin has vanishing terminal by the first-moment bound and `|TW|≤||W||N`. Starting from `J`, `N(J)=1`, so the transform of `L⁰N` is `sM_s(J)−1`. Consequently the denominator is `sM_s(J)` in the equation for `W(J)` and `M_s(J)` in the equation for `𝓜_s(ψ)=sW(J)`. Both the resolvent and the normalized physical occupation formulas have the signs and birth terms claimed. The latter is an actual Laplace occupation ratio only for real `s>μ`.

Real fringe size `n` grows at rate at most `n+1/2`, conditionally also after a promotion, since the external core parent sends at rate at most `1/2`. With size two at birth, Jensen gives the age bound `sqrt(5/2)e^(age/2)`. The initial root is another such cohort. Compensating subsequent births and integrating gives the factor `1+[sM_s(J)−1]` in the unnormalized moment bound. Uniform tightness follows on the finite sets of sizes `≤m`. Norm continuity then gives a countably additive probability at `μ`, the moment bound there by bounded truncations, and convergence even in total variation. This does not infer positivity from termwise signs of the Neumann series.

For identification, the inert mean route can be made explicit without assuming projected balance on an unspecified domain. In Astra `fringe/spatial-limit.md` §§1–2 take the planted root of `J` as the only initial core. The root's send to its slot is suppressed, and the slot sends nothing; its ordinary child immigration row is otherwise unchanged, with degrees including the slot. The path-weight proof of (2) still applies: the slot is an exterior core edge that adds no member to the cell, the root's actual promotion rate is unchanged by suppressing slot selections, and the starting weighted mass is bounded by two. Apply `fringe/almost-sure.md` **only §1, (3)** (the first-moment expansion) to the compatible permanent-mark descendants, lifting a bounded unmarked score by forgetting marks. The leading amplitude for their count and for their sum of scores is the same root-immigration integral times `d(1)` and `d(ψ)` respectively; the original root contributes a bounded term. This follows by integrating the `O(e^(σt))` remainder against the `O(e^(q₀t))` immigration bound. The count amplitude is positive by the imported critical residue. The asymptotic ratio is `d(ψ)/d(1)`, which is the spatial downward marginal by the distinguished-descendant construction in `spatial-limit.md` §2 (a given finite downward type is decidable from a finite marked core ball; the probabilities exhaust mass one). Abelian passage now identifies `𝓜_μ`. No equality between the inert and mark-`1/2` entrance laws is required. Alternatively the projected-balance route is valid given that balance on the asserted domain.

The evaluations follow from `W_1=1/s`, `W_b=U_s`, and differentiating the bounded resolvent (`V'_s=−V_s²`), which gives `W_{U_μ,μ}=−U'_μ`. Thus `𝓜_μ(U_μ)=1/Ψ⁰(J)`. Testing the inverse on `(s−L⁰−CΔ)f` gives `s f(J)` before adding the rank-one birth term, confirming (4).

## 2. Free rerooting and the inert graft

**Free rerooting holds.** On the root component the bounded resolvent pays the root promotion intensity and all root-to-core-child differences. In the non-root sum, the root-to-child arrows cancel that latter forcing; all promotions contribute `U_s(J)` while the bare `b` forcing contributes once per promoter. The resulting source is `(1−U_s(J))𝓛N`. Uniqueness of the discounted occupation representation for the at-most-linear-in-size aggregate starts at real `s>1` by Yule domination, not by using the target growth estimate. Analyticity extends the root identity to the claimed domain. To justify the real formula down to `s>μ`, a fixed free seed can be reached from `P₃` and a fixed inert seed from `J`: retain the appropriate starting path or root-neighbor edge, grow the rest by ordinary leaf attachments, and stop the clock history in a bounded time interval. All required rates are positive. The Markov property gives an upper mean bound for each fixed seed, with a seed-dependent constant. This justifies the residue and positivity claims. Linear lower comparability uses the prior permanent-mark **mean** eligibility comparison for large counts, and the printed two-unit leaf stock script gives a uniform positive minimum for the remaining counts. Neither step uses the a.s. limit under review.

**The free-to-inert gradient holds.** For a core insertion reroot at the recipient; for a leaf insertion reroot at its old core neighbor, which now has a single extra virgin fringe. All other non-root summands are unchanged. For an inert tree and its extension, graft both to an auxiliary core with the same `M` extra real leaves. At this free root the sum over non-root cores is exactly `T U_μ`; there is no miscounted auxiliary virgin. The graft root stays at degree `≥M+1` and has core degree `≤1+Pois(t)` pathwise using its tracked ring count. Its resolvent forcing differs from 1 in expected absolute value by at most `(1+||H_μ||)(1+t)/(M+1)`. The two root resolvents therefore have the same uniform limit `1/μ`. Subtract the two exact decompositions and pass to the limit in the free gradient. The published constant `<94` follows. In fact on the positive real line `0≤V_s b≤1/s`, so `||ΔV_s b||≤1/s` rather than `2/s`; the same proof gives `<49.200` at `μ` in both models, in the normalization `T U_μ(J)=1`.

**Coboundaries hold.** Centering `W` at `W(J)` removes the virgin reward and contributes exactly `−sW(J)` to the per-core source. The fixed nonlocal root score `f_μ−1` makes the uncentered aggregate exactly `Φ−N` at every root. With the derivative identity above, multiplying the critical nonlocal coboundary by `−Ψ⁰(J)` gives `N−Ψ⁰(J)Φ`, without a marked-to-inert identification of the entrance law.

## 3. Exponent endpoints

Neon-2's formula gives an independently evaluable **criterion**: at a prescribed real `s`, `𝓜_s(b)−s=s[U_s(J)−1]`. The sign is useful if the unlimited score is certified strictly to one side of `s`. The equivalence and orientation are inherited from Astra Claim 14; for `s>μ` the physical ratio also gives `𝓜_s(b)−s=−1/M_s(J)`, but this ratio does not extend as an occupation evaluation below `μ`. The stationary equality at `μ` and the slope/residue alone supply no independent trial sign. The prior-dependent centered enclosure at `.56572365` still straddles one.

A viable absolute target remains the unchanged Astra Claim 12 degree-two return `F=(s−Rhat)^−1 h(j)`. Here `Rhat` omits only true triggered direct production; its occupation is positive and has an independent tail budget in principle. At `s=.56572365`, an unlimited `|1−F(s)|≤1.94872894e−9` would certify `|s−μ|<5e−9`: the virgin first residence implies `(s+2)F(s)` is nonincreasing, hence `|s−μ|≤(s+2)|1−F(s)|` on either side. The signed alternative uses the full-window inverse factor `471522`, so a conservative scalar budget is `5e−9/471522`. At the worst discount `ell`, 239 Neumann terms suffice to spend less than half that budget **on series truncation alone**. This supplies no error bound for approximating the occupation terms by finite tables; the killed coupling and the prior-dependent enclosure do not supply it. Neither trial observable was evaluated to endpoint precision here. An absolute radius is also distinct from fixing rounding to eight decimals, which requires checking the final enclosure against the rounding boundaries.
