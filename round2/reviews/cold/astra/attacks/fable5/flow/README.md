## Why the certificates are actual flows

- **Endpoint law.** With `J_n=lcm(1,…,n−1)`, each outgoing attachment numerator is the sum of `J_n/D_x` over actual donors adjacent to the target. The common normalizer multiplies by `n J_n`. Canonical shape propagation can forget chronological labels except the mark: rates and the canonical rooted successor depend only on the rooted shape. Every attachment target is included. The newborn law comes from the free-tree law and rooting the successor at the newly attached leaf. The older law is rooted at its birth and advanced once at fixed root. The size-three special row is the literal equal-weight centre/end law for label 2.
- **Cancellation and universal supplies.** Common rooted mass can be left on the diagonal. In an existing transport it can also be peeled off by joining incoming and outgoing routes through any common shape, using transitivity. At the younger birth census only leaf-rooted shapes can be demands; an older mark that has ceased to be a leaf has the last newborn as a leaf child (at size three use the centre). Such a source matches every leaf-rooted demand. Thus `leaf supply + universal supply = demand`, and saturating leaf supply gives an ordered coupling after arbitrary fill by universal mass. Full saturation is maximal by total source capacity, independently of any early-stop criterion used on a nonsaturating case.
- **Direction and pruning.** A deletion at an even-depth parent is a stronger→weaker arc; at an odd-depth parent its reverse is stronger→weaker. The matching of the remaining vertices by identity proves an individual arc even at different sizes. For a comparable pair, delete unmatched A branches at even parents and unmatched B branches at odd parents to obtain the same matched shape. Both original leaf roots retain their unique edge. Hence source-even closure and sink-odd closure have a common shape exactly when the endpoints are comparable. This validates the lead's restricted closure as well as the child's full network. A route may visit smaller shapes, but only its endpoints represent an initialization of the coupled *physical* future, at equal size. Directed structural arcs are acyclic: the first changed level in the vector of vertex counts with positive odd and negative even entries decreases. Each path decomposed from a feasible structural flow joins comparable endpoints.
- **Residual updates.** The lead stores forward arcs as unbounded and reverse capacities as current flow, with source residuals and sink residuals explicit in capacity arrays rather than graph nodes. A successful DFS updates each directed residual along a path and decreases one sink capacity and one source capacity by the same integer. Re-routing over a reverse arc cancels existing flow; the BFS levels exclude cycles in an augmentation. The child uses a distinct explicit-source/sink residual Dinic and an exhaustive set of leaf-rooted words. Both stop only after reaching the requested saturation or exhausting augmentations. The achieved amounts on the receipts are all saturating; this is more than a sign in a weighted score. At size 20 the normalizer is 269 bits (301 at size 21), below the six-limb signed limit even allowing `2 Den` intermediate bounds. Feasible forward structural flow is at most the amount sent because the structural graph is acyclic. The checked sizes also fit the packed word and arc indices.

The post-transport implication uses the alternating matching in the physical steps: at an even match the larger tree includes all counterparts of the smaller tree's neighbour set, each opposite-parity counterpart has no larger degree, and the corresponding reciprocal sends give the rate inequality. At odd matches the roles reverse. Pair the smaller rates and take any coupling of the equal-mass residual laws; the only extra matched-site leaves occur on the permitted side. Iterating at equal total sizes gives the claimed degree path order. This review does not treat strictness or tail exponents as inputs to that implication.
