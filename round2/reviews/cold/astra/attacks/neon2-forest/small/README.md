## Engine B: score, coupling, and almost-sure upgrade

1. **Two different bounds.** The actual component test has `|f|≤1`, and the total score is bounded by `C(T)`, not a universal constant. Its downward score `φ` can be unbounded: e.g. put arbitrarily many virgin children under a deleted parent. This does not obstruct the signed weighted mean estimate: each child contributes at least `(20/13)(1/4)(7/10)=7/26` to `W`. The founder is omitted both as a possible top and as a possible deleted parent; the latter costs up to `c_o`. Its new core children arise only on its own rate-one clock. For genuinely unbounded component tests no such gradient holds: at `N=2`, take a path of size `v`, add a leaf to its endpoint, and set `f(F)=2^|F|`. Both trees are entirely retained and the jump is `2^v`, while `max c≤2`. Thus the unit-ball condition is essential, even at lag zero.
2. **Dirty-boundary gradient.** A clean-only component can change when a dirty child crosses the cutoff; charge it to that dirty core's unique upward edge. Downward incidences charge the components newly exposed below a deleted dirty vertex; a component containing a dirty core charges directly. Clean identities and leaf counts then pair every remaining component. Summing the two copies before erasing their identities gives the stated bound, with slack in the factor 2. To control the dirty-weight sum without any independence, truncate own-clock ring counts at `K=log |T|+3r+1`. For `Z~Pois(r)`, the exact identity `E[Z;Z>K]=r P(Z≥floor K)` and the exponential Markov bound yield `C e^(2r−K)` since `3>e`. Sum this at initial vertices and at stopping-time births using the remaining time at birth and expected population growth. No conditioning on the future dirty set is needed. The resulting gradient is `O((max c+log |T|+r+1)e^(2r))`. The fourth moments of `max c` and `log |T|` are `O((1+s)^4)` at epoch `s`; Cauchy–Schwarz with the Yule-size second moment and backward jump isometry give (7). Finite-time square integrability also follows directly from `|M(f)|≤|T|`.
3. **The fixed-cutoff L¹ argument is only for identification.** At fixed `N`, `M(f)/C→M_N(f)` almost surely by the sequential result. Multiplying the bounded difference by `e^(−µt)C` and using its uniform integrability identifies the coefficient in the conditional mean bound. This does not upgrade L¹ convergence along changing cutoffs to a.s. convergence. The latter comes from summable Chebyshev on deterministic blocks, not an interchange of limits. Specifically with `d=2µ−1`, the centered squared error decays as `poly(j)e^(−.7dj)`. Mesh, cutoffs, tests and weight `N^(2µ)` add at most `(5+2µ)γ₀j`. The martingale-tail estimate follows independently from Astra (8) with lag `κm`: sum the integer increment variances with exponents `d−4κ` and `2ηκ`, and use the decreasing L² tail at intermediate times. The same mesh for `C` yields `e^(−µt)C−d₂Z∞*=o(1/j)` uniformly per block. This rate is needed because `N^µ d_N=O(j)`; bare convergence of the denominator is insufficient.
4. **Physical interpolation.** The *receiving* rate at a core is `Σ_(y~x)1/D_y≤D_x`. At still-deleted receivers the emitted new leaf is an isolated non-core and the forest is unchanged. A promotion has total rate at most `C` across all parents, whether or not the parent survives. The crossing of a surviving receiver removes a component, adds at most one ancestral remnant, and exposes at most `c_x` child components, so the single-jump count-vector ℓ¹ bound is `2+c_x`. Count relevant events only until the stated stopping time, using the predictable left-limit rate. A conditional exponential counting bound gives the stated Poisson Chernoff comparison, including a jump at the stop. The pathwise comparison to twice the mesh-start core count follows from the positive continuous-time normalized limit. The inter-mesh error after weighting is controlled by `poly(j)N^(1+µ)e^(−3γ₀j)` plus an exponentially small normalized `j²` term. Countable deterministic size multipliers absorb the random Yule time change.

## Both TV statements

**Logarithmic convergence.** `R_N` contains all ordinary **rooted, unlabelled** types with at most `N` real vertices, including the singleton. It does not include vertex identities, plant slots, degree labels, or birth marks. Sorted child encodings inject into balanced-parenthesis strings of length `2m` at size `m`, giving `|R_N|≤4^(N+1)`. Actual degree cutoff does **not** bound ordinary component size; explicitly control the tail instead. On a deterministic open `Y` bracket with upper endpoint `B`, eventually `n e^(−τ_n)<B`. Thus `N≤c₀(j+1+log B)` when `τ_n∈[j,j+1]`; choose the deterministic test family only on this restricted range. At `c₀=γ₀/(2 log 4)`, squaring the extra weight `4^(N+1)` adds `γ₀j+O(1)`, so `(6+2µ)γ₀≤.4d<.7d`. Multiply the deterministic mean error by the number of shapes, but charge the common martingale and denominator errors against total mass `d_N`, not the number of shapes. For interpolation, use the vector jump bound directly, and for the exponential time factor use `Σ_R M_v(1_R)≤C(T_v)`. This yields a summable mesh argument for ℓ¹, not merely expectation or pointwise convergence on a growing shape set. Divide by `d_N∼ A N^(−µ)log N` and the asymptotic empirical mass. The inert probability beyond size `N` is at most `(log N)^(−1/2)+2e^(−sqrt(log N))` by splitting its uniform age window; sequential TV transfers this to the stationary target. Convergence on the finite set and total mass one then provide the empirical tail, completing full TV.

**Polynomial breakdown, conditional on Theorem 1.** In the inert process of ordinary size `v`, all `v` real vertices ring at rate one; only selection of the root's exterior slot is null, at rate `1/D_o`. Every other selection adds exactly one real vertex, so physical holding rate is `v−1/D_o≥v−1/2`, not `v` and not a rate of the marked/killed renewal process. The entrance state is fixed during null rings, so thinning gives one exponential residence at `v`. Rates are bounded above by the nonexplosive Yule rates and bounded away from zero at each finite size. Truncating the residence at `log N` only lowers its expected integral, so `μ_N(V=v)≤1/[(v−1/2)log N]` for `v≥2`. The empirical support has at most the **number of components** many distinct sizes, irrespective of multiplicity; singleton sizes are allowed. The TV supremum is evaluated pathwise, so it permits the random test consisting of the observed sizes greater than `v_n=ceil(M log log N/log N)+1`. Its reference mass is at most `1/log log N`. Its empirical mass equals `ν(V>v_n)`. As `(n/N)^µ log log N→∞`, rounding is negligible, and the count asymptotic gives `log v_n/log N=µ(log n/log N−1)+o(1)`. The uniform CDF bound applies at this random argument since it is eventually in `[0,1]` under the claimed strict exponent condition. Taking limsup in the threshold gives liminf TV at least `1−µ(α^(−1)−1)`; at `α=1` this forces TV to one. No stationarity of the sampled support or independence of components is used.
