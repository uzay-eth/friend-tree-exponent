## 2. Mask, Gamma gluing and summation

**Mask.** `H_k+h` is an increasing sequence of stopping times, and tie order at a given time uses only the observed tree. Conditional on the physical history to the current cut, all earlier edge labels and earlier crossings up to that cut are known. For an earlier edge with no recorded crossing, either the edge is deleted in the present cut and cannot cross on this piece, or its endpoints are in the *same* current component (a tree). Future occurrence of a directed send along that edge is a property of that component's marked embedded chain. No global ordering of births or comparisons of time after the cut are involved. Thus the first-success indicator is a function of history and marked chains only, even though it can couple all side shapes. The retained mass for level `k` is at most the miss at `k−1`; this is not a product of late miss probabilities. This is the precise restriction that distinguishes these masks from a selector that observes a future time or amplitude.

**Likelihood and shares.** With all deleted slots kept, at component size `n` one may sample an independent Exp(`n`) hold and follow either a genuine internal marked arrow or a killing slot arrow, with probabilities proportional to the original rates. Merging these killed components recovers the free process up to the first crossing. Surviving arrows are the normalized genuine chain with the product `∏(1−d_i(n)/n)`; finite local-step cylinders, then monotone limits give the infinite survive identity, still on marked paths. Each component has an ordinary edge and nonzero internal rate. The predecessor mask is multiplied by this weight; their product is independent of reference holds conditional on the marked chains and history. The independent Yule Gamma masses give Dirichlet shares; within-pair ratios are independent Betas after conditioning on pair masses and the remainder. Neither independence of surviving *shapes* after disjointification nor independence of physical no-cross events is required.

On positive product weight, convergence of the whole free count and size limits holds for almost every reference set of clocks. With paths and all other clocks fixed, pick two first waits for one component from its full-measure fibre. Subtract the convergent total discounted core counts; after the time shift this gives `F(t)−qF(t−d)→b`, `q=e^(−μd)<1`. Nonnegative summands bound `F` by a convergent total, and bounded limsup/liminf give the same limit `b/(1−q)`. Divide by the component's positive normalized size and evaluate along its births: its fixed-size coefficient is a function only of the embedded path. This handles the many-slot remainder without importing a many-slot positive limit theorem.

**Gluing.** The joined fixed seed is two copies of the *same* rooted one-slot seed with the slot incidences made into one true edge. Every original half has exactly the same slot degree, marked internal law and likelihood in the joined experiment. On the joint event of each half amplitude at most `ε`, the glued ratio is at most `2ε` for every share. The event has mass `ν([0,ε])²` *before* the additional mask, using the factorized weights. By reachability the joined seed is covered by the free theorem, and its core count can be made arbitrarily large. The CT negative moment is converted using `Z=W^μX` and independence of the free Gamma clock; choose `2d>80μ` at order 80. This yields weighted tail order 40 and moments strictly below 40 for a half, with no weighted zero atom. For increasing derivative order choose the glued negative order strictly above twice the required half order, and increase `d` accordingly. Positive half moments follow from the reverse inequality `X≥P^μa`, the same prior product identity, and strictly positive weight mass on the other half (the leaf protection provides this directly). Multiplying these moments under an arbitrary post-mask product would be invalid; Hölder is applied **before** imposing the extra mask.

**Kernels and summation.** On each fixed path and pair-total fibre, `−g''≥μ(1−μ)(a+b)` for the Beta phase. A derivative strip has length `O(v/(a+b))`; the two complementary branches admit an integration by parts with bound `O(1/(|ξ|v))` from the BV Beta weight and monotone inverse derivative. Hence each pair pays a half-power of Fourier decay, even at coinciding coefficients, where the fold is real. Four pairs pay the bounded-density cost, five pay a `C^{1,1/20}` cost. With `λ=n_*^μ min(T_i)^{μ} min(1,a_i: i small)`, a norm of scale `λ^(−s)` averages to a cost `C (N/n_*)^(μs) B^(−s)` by the Beta negative moment. In the pre-mask weighted measure Hölder exponents `9/8,16,144/7` leave strictly positive mass power `7/144`; at `μ≤.5665` the history costs for `s=11/10,2,41/20` are below `1,4/3,4/3`, respectively, and `16s<40`. The subexponential history factor sums against exponential miss even after counting up to `k` possible bases. In particular the high-order derivative estimate for star exits is not paid out of the one-core waiting budget.

**Interior derivatives of arbitrary order.** For a derivative of order `v≤ℓ`, put `w=v+1≤s=ℓ+1` and use `r>2s` pairs. On `x≥ε` insert the kernel support bound `1_{M≥ε}≤(M/ε)^s`; the support is below `M=a_R+Σ_i (a_{2i−1}+a_{2i})T_i^μ`. Rewrite the cost as `C ε^(−s)(NP)^(−μw) B^(−w)(N^μ M)^s N^(μ(w−s))`. The last factor is at most one. For free seeds the jump bound and bracket estimate stopped at `e^(−μt) Φ≥R` give bracket `O(1+R)` and passage probability `O(exp(−cR))` for `R≥C C(T)` by the bounded-jump martingale inequality. Thus the scaled free amplitude `N^μ X_T` has moments `O(C(T)^p)` using independent Gamma. The corresponding remainder moments under the cut weight follow from `X_T≥P_R^μ a_R` and the uniformly positive Beta positive moment of `P_R` (its first shape parameter is at least two). Positive small-side moments and bounded moments of `NT_i` pay the other terms in `N^μ M`. ## 3. Endpoint and comparison with Astra Q4

The last-star **predeparture** size `j` has mass `2/[j(j−1)]` and the departure seed has size `j+1`. Therefore `V_j=j^μ X_{S_j}` differs from Astra's departure-size normalization by `(j/(j+1))^μ`, which tends to one. The Fourier densities of `V_j` are uniformly bounded in `C^{1,1/20}(R)`, and the lower support `λ` implies `f_j(z)≤C min(z,1)` from the weighted inverse square scale. Local Arzelà–Ascoli with the fundamental theorem of calculus gives local uniform convergence of both `f_j` and `f'_j`: weak convergence identifies the only possible density, and the derivative is then fixed. The two-core CT tail is uniformly exponential by the free stopped-bracket argument, and transfers to `V_j` using the independent Gamma multiplier with a uniformly positive chance to be bounded below. A uniform Lipschitz constant converts exponential probability tail to exponential density-value bound; a global Hölder bound on the derivative converts that value bound to exponential derivative decay by integrating over a right-hand interval where the derivative keeps sign and half its magnitude. There is no differentiation of a CDF equivalent.

With `h=x^(1/μ)` and `y=jh`, the density and differentiated density series, divided by their claimed powers of `x`, are mesh-`h` sums with respective summands before the mesh `2[j/(j−1)] y^(μ−2) f_j(y^μ)` and `2[j/(j−1)] y^(2μ−2) f'_j(y^μ)`. Near zero both are dominated by `C y^(2μ−2)` (integrable), and at infinity by `C y^(μ−2)` or an exponentially decreasing envelope, respectively. Derivative termwise summation is justified away from zero by uniform exponential decay. Changing variable to `z=y^μ` gives the first constant `(2/μ) E V^(−1/μ)=κ/μ`. For the derivative, the integral is `(2/μ)∫ z^(1−1/μ) f'_V(z) dz`. Integration by parts has no contribution at infinity or zero, where the boundary product is `O(z^(2−1/μ))→0`; it gives exactly `(κ/μ)(1/μ−1)`. For global Hölder, the star sum split at `j=δ^(−1/μ)` bounds the lower part by `Cδ Σ j^(2μ−2)` and the upper by `C Σ j^(μ−2)`. Both are `O(δ^(1/μ−1))`; positivity of `κ` rules out any better exponent at zero. No higher-order endpoint equivalents follow here.

**Strict comparison.** Astra's frozen `13/278` and audited `747/11494` full-law `W^{s,1}` cutoffs are both surpassed by Fable's proof, not merely by changing the regularity norm: the uniform bounds on `f'_j` near zero and exponential tails give `sup_j ||f'_j||_1<∞`. The derivative of `j^μ f_j(j^μ·)` costs `j^μ ||f'_j||_1` in `L¹`, and `Σ 2j^μ/[j(j−1)]<∞`. The mixture therefore converges in **`W^{1,1}(R)`** for the zero extension, with `||τ_h f−f||_1≤|h| ||f'||_1`. No boundary delta arises because the density vanishes continuously at zero. More generally the derivative equivalent and decay at infinity give `W^{1,p}` for `1≤p<1/(2−1/μ)`, with failure of strong derivative integrability at equality. For the tail here sum the uniform star-exit derivative decay, using `j^μ x≥3^μ x` to factor an exponential in `x≥1` and another in `j^μ`. Thus Fable also supplies the local boundedness that the Astra Q4 cutoffs left unresolved. The key new hypotheses realized in the proof are a clock-safe predecessor mask and exponentially likely fixed small sides with real weighted moment estimates, rather than a uniform bound on a two-side fold kernel.
