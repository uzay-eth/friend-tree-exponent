## Proof findings

- **Embedding and limit:** Size-`m` holding time is `Exp(m)` independent of the sender and neighbor; fresh races give independence of the full size clock and embedded chain. The degree share is a bounded supermartingale since the attachment rate is at most degree. The single-founder geometric law and independence of Yule families give the positive Gamma limit and a uniform second-moment bound. Consequently discounted degree powers are UI for `0<p<=1`, and the terminal conditional expectation is the discounted amplitude from the current tree. This gives a closed UI martingale for each tracked vertex, including at an infinite stopping time.
- **Upper comparison (reconstructs the first Claim 9 citation):** Set `x=(D+L)/2`. The Gamma ratio divided by `Gamma(p+1)` is increasing in `x` by convexity of `log Gamma`; promotion lowers `x` by 1/2. A gain increments `x` by 1 and increases the value by relative amount `p/x`, at rate at most `x`. This gives the correct supersolution. The Gamma bounds `(x+p-1)^p <= Gamma(x+p)/Gamma(x) <= x^p` follow by log-convexity at the two indicated mixtures of arguments a unit apart. The core-neighbor count increases only at self rings, so is `O(t)` a.s., and the discounted comparison function converges to the same normalized degree limit. Nonnegative localization and Fatou give the claimed upper bound.
- **Lower comparison:** `a(L)=L*q_p(L)/(L+2)` is increasing. For `L>=2`, at the worst gain rate `L`, normalized gain minus discount is `2(L+p)/[(L+2)(L+3)]`. The normalized promotion loss increases in `p` on `[0,1]` and is strictly below `L(L+3)/[D(L+2)(L+1)]`; the sufficient inequality `(L+3)^2 <= 2D(L+1)` holds for `D>=12` (for `L>=6` use `D>=L`, and for `2..5` use `D>=12`). `L=0,1` have nonnegative drifts directly. At fixed time the size-stopped expressions are dominated by a constant times `N(t)^p`, allowing removal of size localization. The discounted values are UI as deterministic time tends to infinity and converge by the Gamma bounds and `D-L=O(t)`; on zero share the upper squeeze suffices. For the leaf bound, the pre-promotion intensity is at most `1/d`: the survival function is bounded below by `exp(-t/d)`, so `E[exp(-p*tau); tau<infinity] <= 1/(1+pd)`. The state at finite promotion is `(2,1)`, and no promotion gives zero terminal share.
- **Finite hierarchy:** Since the tracked coordinates may not be Markov, use the full generator. Every jump is one attachment; in particular promotion of the distinguished leaf is a *joint* successor to the two-core layer. An `a` gain multiplies the `q_p(D_a)` basis by `1+p/D_a`; changes of the other core or parent do not. The discounted drift after basis division is separately affine in `p` and the allowed send rates; for the nonnegative upper leaf tables the worst discount is at `p0`, and the monotone degree-only table uses the least gain rate 1. The boundary monotonic-product ratios have their extrema at the stated endpoints, with the ceiling of `(D+L)/2` in the upper bound conservative. The bare parent really has two core neighbors, so its tighter rate cap is safe. Localized active plus immediate handoff values are bounded; use bounded convergence and the closed amplitude martingale at the exit. On infinite exit the tracked degrees relevant to the payoff stay bounded and both the discounted table and terminal payoff tend to zero. The line saying `M~=0` on infinite exit should be read as zero *discounted terminal* payoff, not zero initial/finite-time conditional amplitude. The comparison does not require the latter statement.
- **Old-edge pairing (reconstructs the second Claim 9 citation):** Unnormalize the two birth probabilities by `n(n+1)`, and condition at size `n+2` for the common Gamma factor. A second birth at `a` gives the path weight `r(x)/(D_x+1)`; at `x` it makes twins with equal amplitudes. Nonadjacent old targets cancel under reversed order. For adjacent targets `x,y`, the first attachment to `x` changes the send rate into `y` by `-1/[D_x(D_x+1)]`; reverse order therefore gives the signed coefficient `r(y)/[D_y(D_y+1)] - r(x)/[D_x(D_x+1)]`. At size 2 that is identically zero. Otherwise expand each product and discard only the two nonnegative cross terms, charging the leaf on each endpoint against that endpoint's path weight. The total path weight is at least 1 since `sum r=n` and `D_x+1<=n`. The leaf in the edge charge is on a parent of degree `D_x+1`; the path's original leaf becomes bare at size `n>=3`. The numerical minima cover size 2, bare, every tracked parent degree, and the monotone high-degree charge. The smallest interval margin is at `[11/20,29/50]`.
- **Founder gap:** At size 3, label 2 is the center with probability 1/2, and in the other case it is exchangeable with label 3. The size-3 amplitude-to-share factor `2/[(p+1)(p+2)]` cancels the half and the `q_p(2)=1+p` table factor, leaving at least `(alpha[2][2]-Q[2][2])/[S(p+2)]`.
