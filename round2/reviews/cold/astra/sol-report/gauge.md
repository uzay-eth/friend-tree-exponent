2. **Binary and dormant.** The underlying binary rows and positive excess rows are holomorphic for `Re z>0.54`; exceeding `0.54` is sufficient for the Laplace majorant, not a claim of singularity there. The positive representation of the excess is important: `|d_β(z)|≤d_β(Re z)`. Therefore the common-return quotient is holomorphic on `Re z>0.56470` using the elementary certificate, or on `Re z>0.5630` using the all-depth certificate. The latter improvement requires the fixed-N controlled boundary and the reviewed weight/potential argument. The quotient provides a **meromorphic representation** on the larger `Re z>0.54` domain where the entries are holomorphic, with possible poles only among the zeros of `1−d_β(z)`; no global assertion about their locations, cancellation, or the sharp abscissa follows. A real residual semigroup upper bound is not the true physical exponent. Binary rows (`finite/gauge/binary-row-bounds.md`) · Dormant (`finite/gauge/dormant-return.md`) · Excursions (`finite/gauge/recursive-excursions.md`).
3. **All-depth numerical continuation.** “Continuation” in the boundary estimates is a payoff at a cut, not analytic continuation in the complex parameter. At `0.54001` the finite, outward-weighted anchor suffices for Hölder; the printed `N→∞` size estimate applies at `s≥0.565`, not at that anchor. The first-promotion `₃F₂` is an exact sum for an individual star's direct phase, and its displayed integral uses real `λ>0`. It does not evaluate post-promotion physical response or imply continuation of the complete transform by a classical function. First promotion (`finite/gauge/first-promotion.md`) · [Review](../checks/excursions-hy4.md).
4. **Generic 19-mark common-survival scalar.** With `D_s=K_s−1 k_s`, the bound near `μ` is qualitative and follows without the anchor. 5. **Genuine degree-two trigger.** The column-complement argument defines `F` without `μ` at each fixed trial discount once the complement is subcritical. Even the earlier independent six-group result suffices for the weaker half-plane `Re z>0.5649`. The stronger `0.976433602362` comparison at `0.56564` uses the narrower physical prior. Both the dormant and trigger scalars at criticality have an immediate reward with a positive time density, so their real roots are simple and no other root of their equation `F(z)=1` lies on `Re z≥μ` (strict phase cancellation on the line except at `μ`). This neither locates roots left of that line nor proves a rate for a finite boundary scheme. The published trigger endpoint signs obtained from the existing physical bracket are **prior-assisted**; neither the small killed sector nor its generation-tail bound independently obtains these signs. Trigger (`finite/gauge/physical-trigger.md`) · [early referee scope](../checks/physical-trigger-hy4.md).
6. **Transfer and arithmetic.** The deletion conjugacy is pointwise. No proof there places the literal constant-mark residual's abscissa below `μ`. Exponentially bounded time response plus all finite generator derivatives gives a large-positive-`s` *asymptotic* series; convergence at infinity is unnecessary and unproved. For any large prime `p` outside the fixed mark denominators, at order `p−2` only the all-gains path to the `p`-star can contribute a terminal factor `1/p`; dormant emission resets size and activation preserves it. Formal algebraicity would force only finitely many denominator primes by the finite-order derivative of the minimal polynomial. The degree-two trigger uses terminal reward `h`, not `b`; the printed star argument does not establish its transcendence. Even the dormant response's transcendence over `C(s)` gives no arithmetic conclusion for a particular zero of `K̃(s)−1`. No contradiction with the first-promotion integral or an unknown variable-coefficient ODE.
