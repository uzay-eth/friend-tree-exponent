# Review of the seven-bin occupation denominator

The denominator argument in `../renewal/README.md` (`renewal/README.md`) checks out. It uses an upper bound on the *potential charge*, not on the signed drift row. For a nonfounder with at most one core child there are no apex charges. There is one mandatory incoming edge, at most one vertical triple and one vertical four-chain, and at most one Y-bottom group. The last group shares the bottom's parent and grandparent, so for a fixed grandparent state its size is at most `k_grandparent−1`; maximizing that product over admissible grandparent rows bounds the sum even though different siblings can have different states. Zero is needed for the optional terms, including a Y-bottom group whose members have negative scores; it is not needed for the mandatory edge. Multilinear Bernstein embedding is a convex average of these finite controls and preserves each ceiling. The grouped universal `C0` is a per-charge ceiling from the production comparison, stronger than merely knowing `Φ≤C0*C`; this is why `C0−ceiling` is a valid improvement on the seven selected states.

Write `A(x)` for the actual charge and `G(x)=C0−ceiling(x)` on the seven bins, zero elsewhere. Then `A≤C0−G` pointwise on nonfounders. The finite Bellman bound for the nonnegative `G` is valid under time-varying receiving rates: its generator inequality holds at every allowed rate by affine numerator and denominator. With the stable-age law it gives `πG≥LO*V(newborn)`. Bounded local charge and negligible founder terms give `barΦ≤C0−πG`. Both witness signs use this upper denominator because the priced numerator is strictly positive and the dimensionless potential has a positive mean. The conversion does not require differentiating the asymptotics: integrate the signed drift over a fixed time interval and take the mean spatial limit of the bounded charged observables.

After authenticating both originals with the standalone crown replay, I ran the renewal denominator extraction and the arithmetic replay. All fourteen extracted integer ceilings matched the seven-per-sign constants in the renewal check.
