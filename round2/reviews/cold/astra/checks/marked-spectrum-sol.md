## 1. Finite-window centering under shared future

Fix a deterministic cohort window `(s0,s1]`, observation time `T>s1` and a terminal degree cutoff at least two (true eventually in the stated scaling). Write `A` for the finite cohort success count, `g(s)` for the post-insertion terminal-success expectation averaged against the rates of *all* initiating arrows, and `I=∫ g`. There is a useful stronger way to see exactly what cancels. For any deterministic `s∈[s0,T]`,

```
E[A−I | F_s] = E[count of successful cohort births at or before s | F_s]
               − ∫_(s0)^(min(s,s1)) g(w) dw.
```

Indeed condition the future-cohort part first at each actual birth epoch *after* `s`, taking the post-jump state (and the actual elapsed time `T−birth`). Ordinary predictable arrow compensation, with the terminal success replaced by this post-insertion forecast, gives that remaining conditional count as the conditional mean of the remaining integral. This use of the tower is valid with common future noise, even when two terminal successes are strongly dependent. It starts with the usual jump history, not a filtration revealing terminal successes. The accumulated integral is continuous. The conditional mean at `s0` is zero (the left endpoint is excluded); at `T` the martingale is `A−I`.

At a possible arrow at `s`, evaluate the pre-jump conditional means using the pre-jump state **at the same time `s`**, not the earlier birth time of the inserted vertex's parent. In the `A` forecast include the inserted newborn if eligible. In the remaining `I` forecast consider births **strictly after** `s` in both states; the inserted newborn is not counted there. The newborn can still affect the future evolution in that forecast. This also follows directly from the display: the jump is the change in the forecast for the already-born cohort, plus the immediate inclusion of the eligible newborn. The two backward forecasts have square-integrable compensated-jump representations: on finite size cutoffs the first-jump equation gives the continuous time drift between jumps, with possible deterministic window endpoints causing no jumps of conditional expectations (there is no birth exactly there). Remove localization by the finite-horizon Yule bounds (`A≤N(T)`, `I≤∫ N`). No drift remains in the martingale after compensation.

The dirty-core coupling controls each difference of forecasts. Maintain a matching of the leaves of every clean pair, with their birth times, and synchronize the aggregate leaf gains by matching initiators. Clean promotions choose among matched leaves, so the resulting clean cores have matching marks even when the promoted leaves were born during the cohort. A birth aimed at an old leaf in the perturbation creates one dirty core and dirties both copies of the old parent; a birth aimed at an old core dirties only its two copies. Any dirty core can initiate at rate one and creates at most two counted dirty cores (either by infecting a clean pair or by promoting privately); dirty leaf rings cannot create a core. A clean core may send into a dirty neighbour but this does not infect another clean pair; at the clean sender the degree and the matched incident slots are still the same. Incoming discrepant sends are charged to the dirty initiator. All unmatched terminal contributors are dirty cores because the terminal threshold excludes leaves. These facts control both already-born tags and not-yet-born tags, despite differing jump labels after insertion: cohort membership uses actual birth **times** and every matched birth gets the common time of its joint jump. At time `T` the expected combined dirty-core count is `≤3e^(2(T−s))`. This argument would not give the same bound for a cohort of all leaves without the threshold restriction.

Thus the difference of the two martingale jumps is bounded by `6e^(2(T−s))`; the direct already-born formula can also give a tighter bound, not needed. The full physical rate is `N(s)` and `E N(s)=3e^s` from `T3`, so the squared centered error is at most

```
36 e^(-2mu t) ∫_(t+l)^T e^(4(T−s)) 3e^s ds
≤ 36e^(-3l) exp(−(2mu−1)t+4(T−t)).
```

In particular for `T−t=2 log(t+2)` it is summable on each fixed mesh. This is a rate for the finite-window centered error only. An adverse physical history is already possible from `T3`: attach labels 4 and 5 to the center, then use the last two births at size seven to promote both leaves, only one, or neither. The two terminal tests use the same future and compete on individual jumps. The fixture finds exact nonzero cross terms for separately birth-centered residuals in three windows, and exact nonzero conditional mean for the earlier such residual on some history at the later birth. In contrast subtracting the two backward forecasts gives zero drift and the exact jump variance identity. Reincluding an inserted mark in the post-jump remaining forecast creates positive drift in all three tests. The fixture uses finite **size** windows; the continuous-time predictable representation above is a separate analytic step.

## 2. From terminal degrees to simultaneous physical rectangles

The normalized finite-age cutoff requires promotion uniformly on each fixed window once `T−t` is large. Thus the per-core birth scores are bounded by seven, also for large stock where individual leaf-source coefficients are large. The empirical LLN under its a.s. event applies via bounded sequential convergence on rooted inputs; this holds along every sequence of times and integrates over the fixed finite `z` window by domination by the a.s. bounded normalized core count. There is no empirical convergence rate claimed for the limiting cohort mean.

The threshold at birth time `t+z` is measured relative to *that* insertion: the condition `e^(-(T−t−z))D(T)>r e^z` cancels to a common terminal degree cutoff `r e^(T−t)`. Conversely the physical continuous-time masses, conditional on the history at `T`, obey the gamma comparisons label by label. A union bound over all existing vertices uses `E N(T)=3e^T`; it does not multiply gamma probabilities. With `k=(t+2)^2`, the bound on a history-gated failure is summable because for every fixed margin and `r>0` it decays as `exp(−c k+T)`. The gate `max c_v(T)=O(T)` holds eventually a.s.; no failure probability bound for the gate is needed. Comparing degree cutoffs at `(1±epsilon)r` and then letting `epsilon` decrease on a countable mesh proves convergence for rational finite windows and thresholds.

No monotonicity of a moving window is assumed. For `t=s+δ` with `0≤δ≤h`, the window `(s+l+h,s+d]` at threshold `r e^(-s)` is contained in the moved event; it is contained in `(s+l,s+d+h]` at threshold `r e^(-s-h)`. The normalization ratio is between `e^(-mu h)` and one, and threshold replacements can be made rational. Use arbitrarily fine countable meshes, then bracket both endpoints and the threshold by rationals; continuity of the limiting finite-window integral allows all real parameters on one event. For instance even without pointwise atomlessness of a generic mark measure, integration in the time coordinate kills each positive mass-boundary line; here the endpoint atomlessness is available as well.

The random change of coordinates is pathwise. With `v e^(-B_v)→Y>0`, the error in `B_v−t−log(xv/Y)` is uniformly small for `xv` bounded away from zero and infinity, since all labels in that compact range go to infinity. The converse for finite time windows follows the same way. The correct threshold for the clock mass is `rY e^(-t)`, not `r e^(-t)`, and the density change gives `Y^(-mu)H=X`. Random endpoints and threshold can be used only after the simultaneous real-parameter statement; this argument makes no independence assertion about `Y` and the marked process. For endpoints zero and infinity, subtract interior compact counts from the unmarked total and exhaust the label axis. Strict total thresholds follow from the non-strict Claim 11 by squeezing with a fixed multiplicative perturbation. The Mellin identity matches the limiting total, so no mass is lost through a label endpoint at fixed positive mass cutoff. Rectangles determine the vague limit, and after restriction to `Z>x`, their limits and total normalization give tightness of the realized probability law and weak convergence. ## 3. Annealed truncation and what `L¹` covers

At the true promotion size `M`, the urn starts from degree two with fresh uniforms conditionally on the entire stopped history. Its tail `Q_M` decreases in `M`, so the product upper estimate uses the probability of **finite promotion**, not the probability of positive mass treated as a separate independent event. At birth `v` the all-time promotion bound and `E C_(v−1)=O(v^mu)` then give `O(v^(mu−1)(1+vx)e^(-vx))` for `v≥3`; finitely many older labels are negligible after `x^mu` normalization. The compact-profile Riemann argument at `y=xv` holds uniformly on compact label bands, and the envelope has integrable left and right ends after scaling. Support of the finite intensity is positive marks only; one need not normalize all newborn events to a finite measure at mark zero.

Uniform integrability of `x^mu N_x` uses two ordered truncations. First discard labels `v>M/x` in expected normalized count. Then among the rest keep only promoters up to deterministic size `floor(RM/x)`, bounding the weighted retained count by `x^mu C_floor(RM/x)` (plus initial labels). The uniform late finite-delay estimate at `floor(Rv)` bounds the expected discarded positive count by a constant times `M^mu(1+log R)/sqrt(R)+o(1)`. This takes `M` fixed before increasing `R`; infinite promotion sizes were never counted. For these fixed cutoffs the retained count has bounded second moment. Approximation in `L¹` by such families proves UI as `x↓0`; an a.s. core-count limit without the `L²` input would not prove this. Comparing `L¹` total convergence to the expected Riemann sum gives `K=(1/mu)∫u^mu ν(du)`.

For each fixed `r>0`, every rectangle is bounded by `x^mu N_(rx)=r^(-mu)(rx)^mu N_(rx)`. This proves its `L¹` convergence for arbitrary deterministic `x↓0` (not just a time mesh) and works for all label endpoints, including zero and infinity. It likewise gives `L¹` for deterministic continuous compactly supported tests by the same domination. It does not bound the whole untruncated measure in `L¹` as `r↓0`, nor does it establish a uniform rate in all rectangles. The annealed tree bias `N_x/E N_x` converges in total variation as a *density on trees* to `X/E X`; this does not imply total variation of the empirical marked laws. Changing coordinates `y=u/r` in `K^-1 1_(0<y<u)y^(mu−1)dy ν(du)` gives `K^-1 u^mu ν(du) r^(-1−mu)dr`, hence the Pareto factor and the power-biased mark. In-tree sampling yields the same limiting mark law by dividing out the a.s. `X`; the two tree sampling laws themselves are different.

## 4. Every fixed positive adjacent moment

At final size `m=n+2` the new endpoints all have degree one or two. Therefore for `y<n`, each endpoint probability of `Z>y/n` belongs to the same interval `[0,Q_(n+2)(y/n)]` (use the promotion-time cap for a leaf). A *difference* of any two such probabilities has absolute value at most this upper endpoint. Pair the old-target orders before bounding: reverse orders produce a tagged reversal on the same final graph, nonedges and twins cancel, and the serial middle versus tip term is unpaired. The absolute oriented coefficients, grouped per old core and its old leaves, are at most `2r_o/(D_o+1)+L_o/D_o≤3`. With the exact denominator `n(n+1)` this yields the scaled signed envelope `C(1+y)e^(-y)` since `(1−y/n)^n≤e^(-y)`. A naive difference of two separately bounded single-label tails would miss a factor `n`. The single tail uses finite promotion at rate `O(n^(mu−1))` and `Q_(n+1)`, giving `C(1+y)e^(-y/2)`. Both probabilities are zero outside the physical `y<n` range.

Layer cake is justified after this signed absolute bound, for every fixed `alpha>0`: the factor `y^(alpha−1)` is integrable against the envelopes both near zero and infinity. The derivative identity from the scaled telescope gives `B=(1−mu)A−y A'`; integrate on compact subintervals first. The boundary term `y^alpha A(y)` vanishes at both ends by boundedness and the tail envelope, so the coefficient is `(1+alpha−mu)b_alpha`. The index convention `n=v−1` changes no equivalent, but it would not be legitimate to take adjacent differences of the single equivalent. For `alpha=mu`, `b_mu=mu K E X`; the conditional hub moment divides by `A(0)`. No simultaneous bound over all positive powers or early-label sign follows. At finite restart the continuous mass equals `S Z`, with `S` gamma and independent of the embedded jump chain and `Z`, not independent of `S Z`; if using continuous moments rather than the discrete layer cake, the exact divisor is the gamma moment, not the size to power `alpha`.
