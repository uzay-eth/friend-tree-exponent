# Q4 cold audit: deterministic-time cuts and full-law integrability

**Holds:** the compact-cut proof (`open/limit-law/smoothing/compact/README.md`), reviewed at blob `e8c258bc65ed7e2cf4417093fa8bb43cbeca0945`, proves the advertised global set bound `4/247` and strong `L^{65/64}`. The same inputs give a stronger global exponent **`1/(41+μ)`**, hence in particular strong **`L^{42/41}`**. No locally bounded, continuous, or globally bounded full density follows from the stated smoothing estimates. That is a limitation of the bounds, not a singularity claim for the actual law.

## What the actual failure clock measures

At each restart, select the oldest eligible core-tree endpoint and one leaf by label (any measurable tie rule works). Until the endpoint first rings its tracked setup leaf cannot be removed, since only the endpoint can promote it. If the endpoint wins the first rate-two race, process its physical ring and restart **there**, without adding the subsequent wait. On a leaf win, process the added leaf, then wait for the endpoint's next ring. Its stock is at least two and its core adjacency has not grown, so its conditional leaf-choice probability is at least `2/3` even conditional on this waiting time and the pre-ring history. At success both cores have a private leaf. The split birth/death-proposal drivers give two independent `1,1/2` lower families conditional on setup, without assuming anything about the physical tree given their survival. Restart at the first extinction actually observed. Old lower-family drivers can be disabled on abandonment, recovering the original physical rates by thinning or merging residual channels with fresh drivers. For the union estimate one can continue abandoned lower families with private independent drivers; this does not insert their future into the physical restart filtration. The physical process is nonexplosive, and the initial race times also preclude any accumulation of retries in finite time.

Let `T1,T2` be lower-family extinction times (possibly infinite), `U=min(T1,T2)`, and `w=e^(−t/2)`. The one-family defective tail is `q=w/[2(2−w)]`. Therefore

```
P(t<U<∞) = (1/2+q)^2 − 1/4 = q+q².
```

Subtracting `1/4` is essential: using the full survival probability as a failure tail would make its exponential moment divergent. The displayed paper's union bound by `w` is valid. Its `F=37/44` at `r=1/24` and restart moment `ρ=11736/11891` follow by tail integration and the two setup waits. The second-wait term uses the worst conditional probability of setup failure against the smaller defective family weight; it does not assume setup success is independent of wait duration. Each failed restart is a stopping time in the enlarged filtration, so iterating conditional defective moments is valid. The first never-failed setup completion `S` is not a stopping time. Its tail certificate is obtained by summing over failure prefixes, with the last attempted setup wait bounded by `a_r b_r` on the never-failed subevent. The expected number of attempts ever reached is at most `(1−ρ)^−1` by the same defective bound at `r=0`.

At deterministic time `t`, the never-failed setup is used only as an **existence witness**. For each reached setup at time `s≤αt`, use the unconditioned finite-age small-positive stock probability at age `t−s`, which is `≤K exp(−(1−α)t/2)` per family; sum over attempts. Setup and the event `s≤αt` are observed at the actual setup. Neither nonextinction forever nor future crossing information is given to the selector. On the real time-`t` tree, select a labelled-first eligible edge with `K` genuine leaves at each anchor, or retain nothing if none qualifies. Given this history, a first-send barrier with **zero return immigration** for a receiving core costs at most `2/(K+1)` per direction. The authors' `α=4/5`, `K=ceil(exp(t/24))` give all three loss exponents at least `1/30` (`7/120` for the small-stock term). Starting from the three-vertex shape changes no fixed-size amplitude law from the edge seed.

## Why the cut is not size biased and where inverse weights enter

Freeze the actual eligible history and chosen edge, then use the no-cross sublaw with exact shape-only weight `L≤1` under the two-half clock reference. Initial sample distribution is the **ordinary deterministic-time physical history**, not its distribution under eventual absence of crossings. No conditioning on the retrospective surviving trial appears in the cut reference. Conditional on embedded shapes in this reference the split is `Beta(n1,n2)`, and `g(P)=aP^μ+b(1−P)^μ` with nonnegative shape coefficients. Integer beta density is `(n1+n2−1)` times a binomial point probability, giving the bound `C(n1+n2)/sqrt(min(ni)−1)` at its mode by Stirling. The actual edge guarantees `ni≥K+1` even in an unbalanced split. The factor `|T_t|` is paid by its **unconditional** mean `3 exp(t)` after dropping eligibility; calling this a size-biased law or factoring eligibility and size is unnecessary. The image bound uses `−g''≥μ(1−μ)(a+b)` even if one coefficient vanishes. On an image hitting `[l,∞)` use `a+b≥l`, avoiding any negative moment under the cut shape mixture. Testing all Borel sets, the exceptional derivative strip and two inverse branches give `C sqrt(|A|/l)`. Adding the physical discarded mass gives `C exp(−ct)+C exp(dt) sqrt(|A|/l)` with `d=1−(log stock growth rate)/2` (i.e. `d=1−γ/2` when `K=ceil(exp(γt))`). The low-end mass costs `Cl^(1/μ)` by the edge-seeded left-tail theorem. Balancing gives compact exponent `c/[2(c+d)]` and global `1/[2(c+d)/c+μ]`. For the published constants these are `4/243` and `4/(243+4μ)≥4/247`.

Three independent pair kernels conditional on fixed shapes and Dirichlet pair sums have `L^(3/2)` norms controlled by `C [M_j/(A_j s_j^μ)^(1/2)]^(2/3)`, from the two-way superlevel bound `min(1/H,B²/H²)`. Young gives the supremum of their convolution, and translation continuity in `L^(3/2)` gives continuity. Integrating the **pair-sum** factors `∏s_j^(−μ/3)` is legal for `N_j>μ/3`, giving exactly `Γ(N)/Γ(N−μ) ∏Γ(N_j−μ/3)/Γ(N_j)`. This does **not** integrate the shape factors `∏A_j^(−1/3)` or the random beta maxima. Positivity of the full amplitude is not by itself positivity of every pair sum; this convolution estimate is only used when its three pair sums of coefficients are positive. Even a positive full-law left-tail moment does not control one tiny pair when another carries macroscopic mass. The equal-size equal-coefficient concentration example makes size-independent conditional density bounds impossible. Neither obstruction is a counterexample in the physical mixture. A bounded density for the full law needs more than these estimates: a rate-controlled bounded-density exhaustion or suitable inverse-coefficient/size estimates plus control of discarded paths, without dropping arbitrary future masks.

## A larger full-law `p`, using the exact minimum tail

Expand `q=w/(4−2w)`. In `q+q²` the first two coefficients are `1/4` and `3/16`; the remaining coefficients are nonnegative and total `3/4−1/4−3/16=5/16` at `w=1`. Thus the defective tail is at most `w/4+3w²/16+5w³/16`. With `r=1/16`, tail integration gives

```
F(r) ≤ 3/4 + r[(1/4)/(1/2−r) + (3/16)/(1−r) + (5/16)/(3/2−r)]
     = 1307/1610,
ρ ≤ [1 + (1/3+(2/3)F(r))/(1−r)]/(2−r) = 373424/374325 < 1.
```

Choose `S≤4t/5` and `K=ceil(exp(t/20))`. Then the late setup, small stock, and first-send errors each have rate `c=1/20`, while the kernel exponent is `d=39/40`. The compact exponent becomes `1/41` and the global exponent `1/(41+μ)>1/42` without requiring a sharper numerical value of `μ`. The set inequality implies weak `L^{(41+μ)/(40+μ)}`, and strong `L^p` below that endpoint by testing the density superlevel sets and integrating their volumes. In particular the larger `p=42/41` itself lies strictly below the stronger endpoint since `μ<1` (so strong `L^{42/41}` holds as well). A strong endpoint at `(41+μ)/(40+μ)` is not obtained. For perspective, a normalized density proportional to `x^(ξ−1)` on `(0,1)` satisfies the same abstract all-set bound with exponent `ξ` and can be unbounded even after translating its support into a positive compact interval. This illustrates why a positive set exponent alone cannot settle boundedness.
