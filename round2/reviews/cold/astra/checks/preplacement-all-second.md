## Stochastic inputs at arbitrary exposure

**Exact two-birth cancellation.** Conditional on the actual old tree, use a *common unlabelled final graph* for the two orders of distinct old recipients `u,v`, with the same two tagged positions. The second rate at `v` after choosing `u` is `r_v−1_(uv edge)/[D_u(D_u+1)]`. The rate at `u` gains one, but repeats pay no gap by leaf exchangeability. In the reversed order the same test changes sign. Nonadjacent terms cancel; an unordered edge contributes `(s_v−s_u)(h_u−h_v)` with `s_u=r_u/[D_u(D_u+1)]`. When the second recipient is the older newborn, the rate is `1/(D_u+1)`, so the serial term has coefficient `w_u=D_u s_u`. This identity holds before inequality or rounding and has denominator `n(n+1)`. No independent coupling of the two true endpoints is needed.

**Retry is restricted to a real last promotion.** Track actual leaf stock, starting with the serial middle's fresh leaf, or after a selected tip's promotion. At positive stock `l`, the tracked gain and promotion rates are exactly `l` and `l/d`. Extra gains give genuine fresh leaves, with nonnegative drift by the checked `B(d+1,l+1)≥B(d,l)`. A last tracked promotion makes a degree-two neighbour *at that moment*: this supplies a real return edge and is not a lower payoff on an arbitrary bare initial core. Until the next gain at the mark, its degree is `d`; at the promoted neighbour's degree `k` the return rate is `1/k` and the competing growth rate is at most `k−1+1/d`. Other gains at the mark are success, not failure. The monotone return recursion is pessimistic against this maximal growth, and its zero at 32 deliberately discards later success. It has an a.s. exit by the positive return rate while below cutoff. After a gain the mark has the physical degree `d+1` and one new tracked leaf, so the product with `B(d+1,1)` follows by a conditional restart bound, not by independence or by counting a removed leaf twice. At degree 20 the disjoint linear birth–death comparison (per-leaf death proposal rate `1/20`) gives the terminal payoff; survival implies positive degree fraction by the physical hub characterization. There are only finitely many monitored stages; no optional-stopping limit assumes eventual hubhood under the surrogate.

**Old-neighbour increment and favourable tip.** For a pending parallel tip, the return race starts at its actual parent degree `p`, with the tip still degree one and its own unit return immigration included in the competing rate bound. Promotion creates the stock needed for `B(2,1)`; using zero at or above cutoff is valid. In the serial extra first-gain credit, while the initial local mark and old-neighbour degrees are fixed, tracked births and tracked promotions have rates `1` and `1/2`; their payoffs balance exactly by the rescue recursion. The incoming old-neighbour send earns the increment from `B(2,1)` to `B(3,2)`. An old-neighbour gain stops this bonus race but retains the baseline. Its intensity is at most `p−1+1/2`; the upper bound `p+3/2+1/p` on the total competing rate is conservative even with arbitrary changing remote arrows. The gain probability is bounded using this total hazard on the pending interval, not by declaring the surrounding future frozen. The lower stock value after a loss remains the just-proved retry value. This is why the extra serial credit is legitimate before rounding.

**Adverse tip even with no siblings.** Each nonstar placement exposure used in the ledger retains the tag slot and another permanent core slot at its parent. With `j` other leaves the specified send is `1/d`, the loss of others is `j/d`, and their addition rate is at least `j+1`; the pending tip's own clock supplies the one. The domain `d≥j+2` remains invariant. The scalar upper's gain monotonicity makes *additional* outside input harmless. At cutoff use the supersolution `1/(j+1)`; at zero stock its drift is nonpositive too. A pending comparison exits by the tip's unit clock if it has not sent. This yields an upper bound for hubhood by paying full credit upon promotion. Old core–leaf parallel placement consumes the old neighbouring leaf, so the two stocks tested there are `0` at the promoted leaf and `l−1` at the old core. Old core–core placement keeps the stock `l`. Star-centre adverse placement also has the newly promoted old leaf as its permanent core slot. The width-one rounding of `U` should not be read as literally preserving `U≤1/(j+1)` for arbitrary rows: use the **unrounded physical** envelope in analytic tails, or add `10^−12` to the rounded bound. Finite rows use the rounded table directly. The high-exposure tail `1/86<.012`, the large-peer outgoing bound at `d≥113`, and the vanishing stocked toll against `L(2)>1/4` have ample slack for this distinction; `U(d+1,·)` at `d≥113` is already the analytic barrier before rounding.

## Simultaneous mixed ledger

On direct core edges actual pairing pays only one orientation; the opposite site's coefficient is at least `1/[d e(e+1)]` from the literal incoming reciprocal, even if it is bare. Thus one baseline `.017` and the two directed excess upper bounds suffice. For low sites, the actual low reciprocal list is retained, high exposures range over the sum interval, and each degree-two port reserves the maximum of direct excess and suppressed-chain payment. This is a convex cost and the credit is concave in aggregate receive. Direct vouchers `.00356` are counted on true high-to-degree-three/four edges only; a high endpoint pays all such vouchers (including ones dropped at the recipients). No voucher is claimed through a suppressed path.
