# Exact right-tail rate — independent cold audit

**Verdict:** The right-tail rate limit and finite-block variational formula (`open/limit-law/right/README.md`) hold, conditional on the all-free-seed finite-time gradient, the embedded martingale and uniform concentration, and the stated core/eigenfunction comparisons. I found no counterexample to either formula. I would qualify the prose claim about repeating *any* rare block: the proved `o(m)` likelihood overhead is uniform over blocks of cost `O(m)` at densities bounded away from zero, not over arbitrarily rare scripted histories. The variational argument uses exactly that qualified regime.

## 1. Joining, arbitrary scaffold and additive error

Fix component colors; only the original `k` joins cross colors, because a newborn inherits the receiver's color. Assume every cut component, including the scaffold, is a connected tree of at least two vertices, as in the construction. At a source with `d` internal and `c` cross neighbours, the sum of losses of internal directed rates relative to the disconnected generator is `c/(d+c)`, and the sum of the new cross rates is the same. Thus the **absolute** difference of the separate arrow rates is at most `2 Σ_source c = 4k`, even when cut edges share endpoints or are attached to vertices with highly unequal degrees. After projection to receivers it can only shrink. There is no comparison of the size-normalized physical and disconnected rates in this step.

For the test `f_u=Σ_color E_free C_u` evaluated on the current cuts, every arrow changes exactly one summand by at most `L_f exp(μu)`. Applying the time-dependent generator formula with `u=t-s` cancels the disconnected generator, leaving the physical-minus-disconnected residual. To remove the size stop, the remaining test value on `s<=t` is bounded by `exp(t)` times the current total size; finite-time Yule moments give uniform integrability. The residual is bounded by `4k L_f exp(μ(t-s))`. The physical versus free-cut core-count discrepancy is at most `2k` at observation time, since every untouched vertex keeps its degree on cutting. Divide by `exp(μt)` to get `Gk`, with `G=4L_f/μ`, independent of the topology, port degrees, block count, and block size. The free edge is included in the input gradient and normalization. If a singleton scaffold were intended in the lemma's scope, it needs a free `deg=0` convention or separate treatment; the actual scaffold has size at least three.

After growing any cap-three scaffold, condition on its entire history and choose distinct anchors. In stage one an original neighbour used as sender gains at most one additional neighbour from its own anchor duty, even if it serves several other anchors; a send changes the **receiver** degree. The arrows for distinct targets are disjoint. In stage two, a promoted leaf gains the new vertex and its anchor does not increase degree. The two setup stages together have lower probability ` (k!)²/(320 k²)^k >= (320 e²)^(-k)` from any such scaffold. Including the cap-three construction one may take `c_0=3 log 12+log 320+2`. Thus this proof uses neither a rare prescribed scaffold shape nor a special favorable choice of anchors. In fact the deterministic joining inequality also survives repeated anchors; the stated setup bound uses distinct ones.

## 2. Unconditional trimming on exponentially rare events

Conditioned on the prior history the identity of the next source is uniform on the current labels, since each source's arrow weights sum to one. The root label is fixed before the free histories are sampled. For deterministic local size `j`, its source indicator has conditional probability `1/j` even given the previous targets; induction gives independence of these indicators across deterministic steps. This is not a statement of independence from the final amplitude event, the root degree, or any conditioned path. Given auxiliary color schedule, the free histories are independent because the size weights are deterministic functions of that schedule. The balance schedule and the root-trim event do not need to be imposed on the physical process with those same independence properties.

For `s=ceil(m/sqrt(log m))` the elementary Chernoff bound has exponent `-s log(s/(e log m))=-(1+o(1)) m sqrt(log m)`. For **all** events with probability at least `exp(-Dm)`, subtraction of this unconditional bad mass gives retention at least one half eventually, uniformly for fixed `D`. One does not conditionalize the Chernoff argument, and the discarded event may contain every path of some much rarer set. The fixture scripts a comb from the rooted edge: alternate a send from the original root leaf into its neighbour and a send from that neighbour into its newest leaf. The original root stays a leaf, and up to rounding the comb grows one core per two births, so reverse comparison makes the terminal state's eigenfunction linear in size. The specified labelled arrow history has root visits `~m/2` and probability `exp[-Θ(m log m)]`; it is entirely discarded by the trim for large `m`. Even without trimming, joining the block multiplies the likelihood of this **exact** arrow history by `2^(-R_m)` on a fixed schedule, because the free root degree is always one. Thus its joining penalty is genuinely linear in `m`. This refutes an unqualified history-level repetition assertion, not the theorem about bounded block quotients for the upper tail: the one history neither exhausts `H_m(b)` nor has finite exponential cost per vertex as `m` grows.

Avoidance and balance are at a fixed total embedded size. The cost of avoiding the scaffold is at most `3k Σ_(i<L)1/(2k+i) <= 3/2+3k log(m/2)` for `L=k(m-2)>0` (and zero when `L=0`). For each composition `l` the conditional urn probability is `∏(l_i+1) / binom(L+2k-1,2k-1)` by summing schedules. Equal counts maximize that product. The number of compositions is at most `(em)^k`, for example by bounding `binom(L+k-1,k-1)` with `binom(L+k,k)` and applying the usual binomial bound. The physical likelihood relative to the urn equals the product of `d/(d+1)` for each actual block-root send. Cross arrows are excluded in the histories being compared. No other source's internal rate changes, the same total size divides both rates, and the physical ordered histories are disjoint (setup is conditioned on, and each post-setup arrow determines its color). Thus one can take `c_2=c_0+8` in the displayed cost formula, independent of `k,m,b`; that also pays the factor of two lost by trimming. The formula uses a linear repetition inequality for the chosen block; it does not invoke a Fekete lemma or assume an `O(1)` subadditivity constant independent of a growing block size.

## 3. Embedded normalization, uniform forecasting and quantifiers

Let `a=1-μ`, `p=1/a`, and use actual core-count normalization `Φ=d₂Ψ`. The eigenfunction equation gives `E[Φ(T_(m+1))|F_m]=(1+μ/m)Φ(T_m)` from the exponential holding time of rate `m` independent of the next arrow, or directly from the generator. Thus the exact forecast is `Y_m=Γ(m)Φ(T_m)/Γ(m+μ)`, with `γ_m m^μ→1`. It converges to `X` with no factor of `d₂` left over. If one instead takes the continuous-time martingale terminal value from `T₃`, it equals `W_size^μ X` and need not have this same tail. The comparison never conditions on a physical clock. Conditional on a successful possibly rare history at deterministic size, the future variance and concentration bounds are still uniform: the scaled centered increment has width at most `2L γ_(j+1)` in every state, not just under the unconditioned law. A first crossing of another process is unnecessary.

For fixed `m,b`, the repetition lower bound on `Φ` is `k(bm-G)` at `n=k(m+3)`; the scaffold eigenfunction is nonnegative. If `b_*=(bm-G)/(m+3)>0` and `k` is the least integer giving `b_* n^a >= (1+ε)x`, the ceiling costs at most one extra block. The uniform forecast error probability at gap `εx/2` is at most `4V n^(1-2μ)/(ε²x²)=O(x^-p)` for this fixed block, regardless of the exponential rarity of the past. In particular this factor is multiplicative, not an absolute subtraction from the exponentially small joint event. Letting `x→∞` first and `ε↓0` next yields `I_+<=J_m/[(m+3)b_*^p]`. Relative to `m b^p`, this denominator is `(1-G/(mb))^p (1+3/m)^(1-p)`, tending uniformly to one for `b>=δ`. Select near-minimizers along a sequence attaining the finite liminf; the quotient bound gives `-log H_m(b)<=D m` since `b<=B`. The constant is allowed to depend on the bound and `B`, not on the chosen history or repetition count. Thus `I_+<=V(δ)`. In particular a claim of uniform repetition for `δ→0` concurrently with `m→∞` is unnecessary.

Conversely pick a tail sequence attaining `I_-` and deterministic `m=ceil(M x^p)`. The exceptional event below the forecast threshold has **unconditional** probability at most `exp[-c ε² M^(2μ-1) x^p]` by the uniform conditional bound. Increasing fixed `M` is effective because `μ>1/2`; take it also large enough that the limiting density `(1-2ε)M^-a` is strictly below `B`. The exponent identity is `2+p(2μ-1)=p`. The error is `o(P(X>x))`, so `H_m(b)` retains this mass at `b=(1-2ε)x/m^a`; the normalization is **exactly** `m b^p=(1-2ε)^p x^p`. For any `δ` smaller than the limiting positive density, the liminf over all sizes is bounded above by this subsequence. Strict `>` in both events causes no boundary atom issue: the lower-forecast error includes the equality case and the repeated state leaves a fixed forecast margin. The correct order of limits is first the subsequence in `m`, then `δ↓0`, then `ε↓0`. Hence the squeeze gives `I_-=I_+=lim_(δ↓0)V(δ)`, with positivity and finiteness from the inherited right-tail order. Even if the latter is phrased using `>=`, evaluating its lower probability bound at a fixed larger threshold gives the needed strict-tail bound.

The conditional localization is division of the two-sided unconditional concentration by the actual tail probability, with `M` chosen sufficiently large using the rate. The strict overshoot estimate follows from the ratio of the two tails at `x` and `(1+ε)x`; it does not address which trees realize the rate. Finally Hoeffding's width proxy is `L² Σ γ_(j+1)²`; using `z=B t^a` the lower-rate objective is proportional to `z^((2μ-1)/a)(1-z)²`, maximized at `z=2μ-1`. The cap-three upper bound uses fixed `b<b_cap` and has sufficient uniform margin for strict `Φ>bm`. Neither estimate proves an optimizer for the block problem or a tail prefactor.
