## Endpoint strips, one coefficient and two

Use `S=a+b>0`, `p=sin²θ`, and `F(θ)=g(p)`. The angular input density peaks once, vanishes at the two endpoints and has both supremum and variation `O(√N)` by the half-shifted Stirling cancellation. This is not a unimodality argument for the output density. On `g<S/4`, concavity and `g(1/2)≥S/2` imply the one-sided derivative bound `|g'|≥S/2` with the correct sign towards the midpoint. The trimmed `sin(2θ)` turns it into an angular slope of order `Sε`. This localizes the critical strip to `F≥S/4`, a connected interval. There the curvature identity bounds `F''` above by `−cS` whenever `|F'|≤H` for sufficiently small `H/(Sε)`. At each strip level the derivative crosses only downwards. Thus the intersection is one interval (or empty), with length `O(ε)`; an endpoint of the trimmed domain can only shorten it. There is no requirement that `F'` be monotone on the *entire* angular interval.

Cut the complement further at zeros of `F''`. After dividing by the positive factor `2μ cos^(2μ)θ`, the sign polynomial in `x=tan²θ` is precisely `a x^(μ−1)(2μ−1−x)+b((2μ−1)x−1)`. It is strictly convex for `a>0` (even when `b=0`), so has at most two zeros; for `a=0`, it is affine. One positive coefficient gives a monotone map, but still need not give a monotone *slope*; both positive coefficients give the interior fold and can produce two angular inflections. On each remaining piece `F` and `|F'|` are monotone with `|F'|≥H`. Variation of the inverse Jacobian is at most `1/H` on each piece. Multiplying by the angular input density and extending each pushforward by zero costs at most `C√N/H`, including the two endpoint jumps per piece. The endpoint strips and the critical strip lose at most `C√N ε` in mass. Thus the translation estimate is `C√N[ε+|h|/(Sε)]`; using `ε=√(|h|/S)` or the trivial norm bound two proves it for all shifts. No uniform global bound on the angular curvature, nor on either individual coefficient, is needed.

## The exact weight and the full-law bound

Write the retained measure as `ν_t=∫ L(ω) K_ω dQ(ω) dH_t`, where `H_t` is ordinary deterministic-time physical history, `ω` is the embedded-path data, and `K_ω` is the *unmasked* Beta pushforward at those paths. Translation is linear on measures: `τ_h ν_t−ν_t=∫ L(ω)(τ_h K_ω−K_ω) dQ dH_t`. The word “commutes” refers only to the scalar `L(ω)` while integrating the clocks, not to multiplying by a function of the output and translating. An extra mask on `X` is not scalar on the Beta fibre; such a mask can introduce arbitrary variation. A simple finite control is a uniform kernel on `[0,1]` with gate `[0,1/2]`: translate the gated measure to the right by `1/2` and it is disjoint from the measure obtained by gating the translated kernel with the old gate.

The no-cross Likelihood is the exact product over embedded component jumps; rate-change and crossing factors have already cancelled, with the unfinished holds included in that cancellation. The `S^(−1/2)` cost is transferred **with** this weight: on the retained physical law `X≤S`, so its history-weighted `√N` integral is at most `E_phys[√N X^(−1/2); retained]≤C exp(t/2)` by Cauchy–Schwarz and `E X^(−1)<∞`. No independence of `N` and `X`, or inverse moment of an unweighted planted coefficient, is asserted. The missing measure and its own translate cost at most twice their common mass. With `c=13/252`, choosing the cut time `log(1/|h|)/(1+2c)` makes both errors `O(|h|^(13/278))`. The translation integral for `W^{s,p}` tests `||τ_h f−f||_p^p`; interpolating against the strong `L^r` bound from the joint-coefficient angular set estimate gives exponent `(13/278)(1/p−1/r)/(1−1/r)` **on the norm**, so the power `p` cancels in the seminorm test. Passing `r↑278/265` gives the stated strict region; it does not use strong integrability at the limiting `r`. The fold for two positive coefficients remains after a positive scalar weight, but its location depends on the shape. It is not a lower bound on the mixture at a fixed point. The finite-state next-cross lower bound also checks out: no neighbour has rung before the proposed sender, so its degree is unchanged regardless of exterior growth.

## Separate conditional proposal: refine a gated planted side

There is a real side-law gain for the factorized **stock-floor plus no-anchor-to-core-send gate** of the six-side note, not an unconditional renewal for the free law. Let `A` denote one side's intrinsic size-epoch coefficient, `n` its ordinary starting size, and let `ν` be its unnormalized shape law including the original port no-send weight and that side's gate. The cited weighted inverse bound at order one applies by choosing `p_*>μ`, so `∫A^(-1)dν≤C Γ(n)/Γ(n−μ)≤C'n^μ`. The measure is equivalently the physical inert planted ratio restricted to no original slot send and the other gates, with the slot's null choices retained in the graphical construction.

Apply the two-anchor retry construction **inside that planted tree**. At setup choose a deepest core oriented away from the original port, or the root if it is the sole core; its only possible nonleaf slot is its parent, real or inert, and its real leaf sends behave normally. Both family laws and the failed-clock budgets are unchanged. At a deterministic secondary time, select an eligible internal edge and retain no secondary crossing. The missing mass in the gated law is at most the unconditional inert-law failure bound `C exp(−ct)`, since the original gate is only a restriction (not a normalized conditioning). The first-send barrier for the new edge uses no return immigration and is unaffected by an additional null slot. On the nested cut the original port is still a retained slot on its side. Ban both original and new slot sends by the same holding-time cancellation. The stock-floor and no-anchor-to-core-send restrictions on the original anchor continue to be shape-only: its stock changes only within its component and on a deleted edge both directions are already banned. The old prefix must also satisfy those gate conditions up to the secondary time. It is not legal to replace the original gate with an arbitrary earlier-failure or amplitude mask. The multi-slot identity supplies the two resulting shape coefficients without assuming a new positive-limit theorem for all multi-slot seeds; at least one is positive on the retained sublaw.

Both the angular set and translation kernels at the secondary cut can therefore be averaged with the remaining weight intact. Their inverse cost is bounded by `(E_inert |T_t|)^(1/2)(∫ A^(−1)dν)^(1/2)≤C n^((1+μ)/2) exp(t/2)` since the split image is the *same* `A`. Optimizing the two errors gives set and translation bounds `C n^(c(1+μ)/(1+2c)) δ^(13/278)` for `δ=|E|` or `|h|≤1`, respectively (use the trivial norm bound if the formal cut time is negative). Thus this particular gated *side* measure has quantitative strong `L^r` for `r<278/265`, by the all-set estimate. This argument uses only its existing **weighted** negative moment and never treats the terminal weight as a bound on the derivative of a density. If both planted sides at a fixed outer cut carry these factorized gates, condition on the outer Beta proportion and use Young's inequality on the two scaled side densities. It yields `L^{r/(2−r)}` on that fixed doubly gated sublaw; the dilation factor averages by a finite joint inverse Beta moment at powers `μ(1−1/r)`. For translations one can likewise put a translate on one factor before convolution. This is a conditional improvement over using only inverse-coefficient bounds at that outer cut. It does **not** upgrade the full-law exponent automatically: the unrestricted sides lack the gated inverse bound used here, and the probability loss of imposing the extra gates at outer cuts and the size-dependent norms must still be paid. Repeated first-success subtraction does not preserve the exact factorization. A one-side renewal with an arbitrary dominated terminal weight is insufficient, since domination preserves set bounds but not translation bounds (a mask on the output can oscillate arbitrarily).
