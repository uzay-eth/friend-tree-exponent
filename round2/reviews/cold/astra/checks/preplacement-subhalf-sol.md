## What the numerical coefficient actually uses

Given the initiator-colour endpoint `θ` and first blue mark-sender index `f`, count the red choices **including** the string preceding choice `f`. The red stock is `1+S` with the negative-binomial law of `f+1` geometric counts. The tag chooses a uniform red leaf on a hit, and the lower-bound construction chooses a uniform dummy on a miss. In either case no uncharged leaf was damaged at this arrow. The choice occurs before exposing any limiting family percentage. The conditional future family word is Pólya within the red choices, so the charged share `W` is Beta conditional on the census (an atom at one when `S=0`). For `w<1`, weighting the size law by the density `s(1−w)^(s−1)` gives `S−1` negative-binomial with `f+2` geometrics and survival parameter `z=θ(1−w)`. The `f+2`, rather than `f+1`, is essential. The new uncharged-versus-blue strings dominate additional geometrics of parameter `z`, since deleting charged-red choices gives parameter `z/(u+z)≥z`. Consequently the expected reciprocal before blue choice `j>f` is bounded by `(1−z)/(z(j+1))`. All these comparisons keep the red/blue and within-colour drivers distinct; no independence of mark degree and stock is invoked.

Summation starts at `j=f+1`. Replacing `b+j` by `b+j+1` costs the factor `1+1/(b+f+1)`. With `v=j+1`, convex midpoint integration starts at `v=f+3/2`, including `f=0`. This gives the claimed smaller coefficient `C`. Under `θ>x`, the scaled charge is the uncapped Pareto-type size until its truncation at `bθ/u`; that cap is greater than `t=b(θ−x)/u` and all larger values give zero payoff. It is therefore legitimate to use its untruncated distribution for this mixed payoff. Its conditional law given `f` no longer depends on `θ`. Markov is applied **after** the exposure (charging subsequent losses only). The payoff increases in `t` and decreases in `a` and `C`.

Uniformity in `b` genuinely needs two cases: the exact floor at `B`, and the continuous bound for `b≥B+1`. The floor divided by `b` is not monotone. The lower bound `min(h,(Bh+1/2)/(B+1))` is used only in the second case and handles both signs of `1/2−h`. The exponent `min(v0,v1)` follows from the displayed log bound, yielding a lower CDF. Right endpoints of uniform bins worsen both the charge and the family-size CDF; the last uniform bin is dropped. The lower CDF used in the code is monotone: the exponent is nondecreasing in the family-size argument, and the upward fixed-point survival table is nonincreasing and bounded by its initial value. So its **increments**, used as masses at right endpoints, are nonnegative. This is a stochastic lower-CDF discretization with leftover mass assigned to infinity, not a claim that every finite bin mass is lower than its true counterpart. Truncation is conservative, and left endpoints in the `t` bins and outward exponential/charge rounding have the correct directions. The producer quadrature replay passed all three coefficient floors.

## Additional family exposures: valid kernel, no additional certified band

At a checkpoint retain the finite physical history, original blue probability `u`, uncharged probability `p`, live red stock `r` and physical mark degree `D`. Charged senders remain a separate class and can send to the mark, but cannot promote a live leaf: that requires the blue mark itself. The next actual mark arrow hits a uniform live founder with probability `r/D`; this choice precedes the exposure of its share. If sampled, that share is `Beta(1,r−1)` (`1` if there is only one), by Pólya disintegration. Conditional on the exposed share, the future active-versus-charged class word is iid with the updated total `p(1−V)`, and the within-live sender driver is fresh. Dirichlet neutrality preserves this driver under the exposure; at a later finite checkpoint one may start a **new** vector of unit counts at the actual surviving census, including freshly born leaves. Conditioning on whole eventual paths, or on the target of a later arrow, is not the same conditioning. Covariance `−1/[r²(r+1)]` for two initially distinct founders rules out independent repeated draws at unchanged `r`. When `r=0` the branch stops; conditioning on a hit at `r=1` leaves no uncharged probability. On a miss the exact law leaves `p,r` unchanged and still processes the physical target and degree update if needed for the next checkpoint.

The residual mean charge is legitimate at an **adapted finite checkpoint in the disintegrated process**: delete charged-colour decisions to obtain geometric live strings of parameter `p/(p+u)`. The blue count before choice `f+k` is `b+f+k`, not the count of tagged blue decisions. The reciprocal of live stock has the integral as printed, and for `k≥2` the `r=1` value is bounded by `u/[p(k−1)]`. With `L≥1` the remaining sum is bounded at its first term plus the integral from `L` of `1/[v(b+f+1+v)]`; this gives the printed truncation bound. Its Markov payoff is nondecreasing in both `p` and `r` for fixed `u,f,b`: for fixed `r` larger `p` stochastically increases each count, and `p/(p−x)` decreases for `p>x≥0`. For larger `r` the reciprocal bound improves pointwise. This checks that the dummy replacement at a miss is conservative for a terminal scalar payoff, not a proof that it preserves a multi-checkpoint physical law.

At the first mark arrow `R+1≤D≤R+1+f`. The lower miss bound `1/(R+1)` follows from the former inequality, and is attained on an arbitrarily long all-red prefix before blue choice zero. Thus no positive stock-independent miss rate is valid under exact prefixes. The thinning factor `D/[(D−R)(R+1)]` retains that much genuine miss mass conditionally on the census, inducing the stated non-uniform tilt of `R`; its positive integral may be used inside a weighted expectation. For further checkpoints neither the Dirichlet exposure alone nor this first-miss debit gives the new joint law of `D,r` at threats. In particular a dummy charged trajectory is not the true hit branch; one must either propagate the physical degree/stock history or supply a verified bound for it. No additional band is certified by §§2–3.
