# Second audit: the fine forest diagonal and its slow-cutoff obstruction

**Verdict: both claims hold in their stated scopes.** For each deterministic `N(n)→∞` with `N(n)/n→0`, the fixed rooted-shape intensity and parent-mark limit hold in probability. There are deterministic diagonals in that range, with `n/N(n)` eventually below any prescribed divergent upper envelope, on which the pair census is zero infinitely often almost surely. At a fixed positive cutoff fraction, the pair census even has a probability of zero bounded away from zero at deterministic observation sizes. The proof below does not require atomlessness of a fixed label's limiting degree fraction.

The inputs are the physical stable-age law (`degree-law.md`), core/local LLNs (`fringe/spatial-limit.md`), core moments (`fringe/almost-sure.md`), intermediate-degree spectrum (`fringe/hub-spectrum.md`), the fixed-radius cylinder rate (`open/forest-age/diagonal/README.md#1-uniform-physical-cylinder-estimate-at-deterministic-sizes`), and the sequential fixed-shape constants (`open/forest-age/fixed-shapes/README.md`). This audit reconstructs the new arguments in the diagonal proof (`open/forest-age/fixed-shapes/diagonal/README.md`) and the [first audit's counterexample](forest-fine-diagonal-sol.md), rather than re-proving those inputs.

## 1. Boundary, no-return and deterministic blocks

**Finite candidate set at a proportional boundary.** If a vertex starts at degree 1 at size `v`, couple its degree to the red count `R_n` in a Pólya urn from `(1,v−1)`: the physical increase probability is at most `D/n`. The square of `R_n/n` is a submartingale, with terminal second moment `2/[v(v+1)]`. Hence the probability that a new label ever reaches fraction `c/2` is at most `8/[c²v(v+1)]`. Summability, with no independence assumption, bounds all eventual high sets at any sequence `N(n)/n→c>0` by a single random finite set of labels. For a regular `c` this proves the author's eventual stabilization by fixed-label convergence. At a boundary atom `Z_v=c`, neither convergence of the fractions nor monotonicity of the degrees proves stabilization; the exact fixture exercises that failure. The argument below needs only finite containment, so it also covers those thresholds. Atomlessness of the numerator in the continuous clock would not by itself imply atomlessness of its ratio with the random size mass.

**No persistent cut-inside pairs.** Fix a deterministic finite label set `H` and consider deleting it at all sufficiently late epochs. The exceptional component tops are the founder and the surviving children of vertices in `H` whose branch contains another vertex of `H`; there are finitely many such tops, since each lies on a finite path to `H`. The component of each such top is increasing after all labels in `H` are present. If two adjacent retained vertices remained a component of size two forever, each would have bounded original degree: all its other neighbours would have to belong to `H`. The positive arrow intensity across their edge forces an addition to the retained component, a contradiction. So eventually none of those exceptional components is a pair. Intersect over the countably many finite masks; for any deterministic `k`, the resulting last-exception time can be maximized over the finitely many subsets of the first `k` labels. This works with an actual high set that switches among those subsets. A nonexceptional pair is an intact promoted virgin whose parent lies in `H`. It cannot return to size two once it leaves, and its first new leaf's clock destroys the pair at rate 1 even if no other effective clock does so.

**Recurrence in the real jump chain.** From size `k` onward preassign independent rate-one clocks to the first `k` vertices, and attach independent `Exp(1)` lifetime marks to *every* point of these clocks. For a promotion, the mark supplies the first waiting time of the newly attached leaf; for any other ring it is a dummy. This is an unconditional graphical construction. The always-admitted immigration–death queue with input rate `k` and these lifetimes dominates all new intact pair candidates from those sources. Old pair candidates are finite and disappear. A stationary version obtained by extending the Poisson input to negative times is empty on a unit interval with probability `exp(−k) exp(−k)` (empty at its start, no arrival during it). The stationary marked Poisson process is ergodic under unit shifts, so there are infinitely many such intervals a.s. Physical holding times at size `n` are `Exp(n)` marginally; summing their tails shows the gaps tend to zero a.s. Thus sufficiently late empty intervals contain actual size epochs. Intersect the argument over `k` and then choose the realized finite cover. Neither the queue nor its clock was conditioned on which labels eventually get cut.

**A genuine macroscopic zero atom bound.** The recurrence alone would not rule out convergence in probability. For a fixed `c>0`, condition instead on the positive-probability finite star history through a large deterministic size `m`, rooted at its centre. At this restart each other current label has degree 1, and future labels start at 1. By the urn maximal bound, the conditional probability that any noncentre ever exceeds `c/2` beyond size `m` is at most `(8/c²)[(m−1)/(m(m+1))+1/(m+1)]`. On its complement the cut either deletes only the centre or deletes no one. In the former case every pair is a virgin promoted by the centre after restart; in the latter case there is no pair for `n>2`. The centre's stationary dominating queue is empty throughout `[log(n/m)−1/2, log(n/m)+1/2]` with probability `e⁻²`. Under the restart, the elapsed size epoch minus `log(n/m)` tends a.s. to `log(m/Y_m)`, with `Y_m∼Gamma(m,1)`. The limiting bracket-miss probability is at most `16/m` by Chebyshev on `|Y_m/m−1|>1/4`. Taking `m≥ceil(1024/c²)` bounds the sum of the two errors by `1/32<e⁻²/2`. The intersection bound uses subtraction of probabilities, not independence. Thus `liminf P(pair count=0)` is at least the star history probability times `e⁻²/2`. This applies to any deterministic `N(n)/n→c∈(0,1)` and rules out the positive fine pair limit in probability there. It complements the author's closed-finite-atom argument for parent marks and scalar lattice obstruction.

**Block selection.** Take deterministic `c_j↓0` in `(0,1)` and any deterministic envelope `g(n)→∞` (it need not increase). After the `j`th search start `b_j` is fixed, choose finite deterministic `B_j≥b_j` with probability of no zero at cutoff `floor(c_j n)` in `[b_j,B_j]` at most `2⁻ʲ`, using the a.s. recurrence for that *fixed* cutoff fraction. Then choose `b_(j+1)>B_j` so large that `c_(j+1)b_(j+1)≥j+1` and `g(n)≥2/c_(j+1)` for **all** `n≥b_(j+1)`. Make the analogous choice for `b_1`. Keep cutoff fraction `c_j` on the entire interval `[b_j,b_(j+1))`, including all waiting time. Throughout it the floor is at least `j`, and `n/floor(c_j n)≤2/c_j`. The search windows are preserved and failure probabilities summable; independence of blocks is not required. The zero sizes are random witnesses **inside a deterministic cutoff schedule**. Since the fine pair target is `2AX/3>0` a.s., this defeats almost-sure convergence. No monotonicity of the cutoff sequence at block seams is claimed or required.

## 2. Full sublinear range: checks on the two mechanisms

**Small cutoff.** The physical top-edge score of a high parent is a downward cylinder at radius depending only on `|R|`: explore retained low children and reject at `|R|+1` ordinary vertices, retaining the original degree at each boundary. Count only components with an original core; a leaf exposed by deletion is not a singleton in this census. Truncate the parent's core arity at `B log n`. The stationarity loss is exponentially small in `B log n` by the core exponential moment, and an integer `B` large enough eventually excludes nothing on each realized path. Apply the cylinder error with this norm (and a countable intersection over deterministic `B`). When `N≤n^(η/300)`, multiplying the error by `N^μ` makes both exponents negative (`μ<1`, `μ>11/20`, and the given `η=2⁻³⁴⁸`). The founder component divided by the fine scale vanishes. This gives the small-power a.s. result using the sequential constants, even for oscillating schedules in this regime.

**Recent intact branches and retrospective no-return bias.** At fixed observation interval `[t,t+ω]`, choose parents at `t−h` by degree. For `N>n^(η/300)` and the deterministic time brackets used below, the intermediate spectrum and finite-time Yule upper/lower comparisons sandwich the high-parent *mark-tail* sets, simultaneously at both endpoints, by a fixed small relative margin and the deterministic factor `e^ω`. This uses the core-degree cap `O(t)` and a union over `O(e^t)` expected vertices and births, with exponentially small error in `N` for fixed positive margins. Future births cannot enter those high sets. Proxy parents number `O_P(e^(μ(t−log N)))` and diverge at any rate of `n/N→∞`.

At all their clock points preassign fresh ideal inert virgins, including dummy marks at nonpromotions. Their internal drivers on admitted fresh branches can be used physically until a mismatch; outbound child-to-parent arrows affect the exterior but not the ideal branch. A parent clock send to an existing core can burn its own proposal and disrupt one previously admitted mark. Before a deterministic core-cap exit its expected count per proxy parent is `O((h+ω)t e^h/N)`; stop and use Markov, rather than condition the independent ideal law on cap survival or absence of returns. There is no thinning by retrospective physical success in the Poisson variance calculation. The control in the companion shows that conditioning on even one absence of return can shift the unmasked inert mark probability from `2/3` to `8/11`. Large internal degrees in recent physical branches are excluded by the same finite-horizon Yule comparison, because every core there was still a leaf or not present at the start. Thus no internal deletion is silently treated as a recent intact branch. The conditional Poisson means and variances for the **unmasked** ideal counts are of order the number of proxy parents. Dividing by the square of the fine scale gives concentration in probability; this does not give a summable error at arbitrarily slow divergence.

**Finite histories for old cuts.** At focal promotion time `s`, use the actual `L/D` insertion rate, and condition on the full post-promotion input. Cap the number of read neighbours and early children, and use the common deterministic snapshot after the promotion window. For the endpoint score, a bounded-radius admitted read can disagree only near a dirty core, including the core that promoted a disputed leaf. In either copy each dirty core is within a bounded number of admitted reads: paths to an admitted focal vertex have capped degrees, and the cap test at a boundary can be made with one more level. The backward mean jump is thus at most a fixed multiple of `e^(2u)` at remaining lag `u`. The forecast for the remaining insertion integral is the conditional remaining terminal count **excluding** promotions already made; the integral accumulated so far has no jump. The two forecasts start at the same conditional mean by insertion compensation. Their difference has variance `O(e^a)` on a fixed window around `a=t−log N`, by jump isometry and the Yule mean. Dividing by `e^(2μa)` gives a vanishing error even when `a` grows arbitrarily slowly. Old-cell continuity of the capped endpoint read gives the insertion forecast limit by the shifted local LLN, with multiplier `H μ e^(μz) dz` at shift `z=s−a`, `H=Y^μX`. A fixed bound on early offspring gives uniform integrability by the core-count second moment, and the excess is paid by the Poisson clock tail. No limit in a growing observation horizon is needed here.

**Singletons and temporary pairs.** Test a finite old top snapshot, with degree `≤q` and at least `j` other leaves at each recorded neighbour, including the parent. Such a candidate is bare. Up to its first gain this list cannot expand; the physical first-send barrier and union bound give failure at most `2q/(j+1)`. The stock comparison from `j` leaves dominates independently driven comparison families with rates 1 and `1/2` (the actual neighbours need not be independent). Their joint extinction probability for one stock is `2⁻ʲ`; on survival the lower stock tends to infinity. Thus a growing top, or a frozen top with a surviving neighbour with growing stock, cannot give a fixed-size component except on the paid errors. An old singleton needs *all* its final neighbours cut, not just the parent. The independently computed finite example contains a genuine inherited pair with one ordinary leaf, then three surviving vertices, then a singleton: the final cut does not make the earlier pair an intact virgin. Under the limiting newborn law, bounded tops eventually stabilize and have only unbounded neighbours, and unbounded cores have diverging leaf stock. For any small tolerance, take `q` large to cover bounded tops, then `j≫q`, then the finite horizon large at fixed `q,j`. Undecided reads have vanishing limiting mass by this order of choices. This removes temporary old pairs asymptotically rather than denying their existence at finite times.

For a snapshot neighbour of degree `d` and core degree `c`, `e^(−u)D(u)` after restart has compensated bracket in expectation at most `d`, and its nonpositive drift has expected total variation at most `c+1` (integrate `e^(−u)(c+u)`). Hence the all-future expected error relative to snapshot degree is bounded by `2sqrt(d)+c+2`, multiplied by `e^(−(D+B))` in the note's time units. The truncated bound tends to zero along the stabilized finite lists, as degrees grow at most on the exponential scale and core-degree growth is linear. With finite early-count truncation this is a bounded finite-read error payment. Fixed margins above and below the degree and mark thresholds close after integrating the parent offset `y`: for each trajectory any positive mass equals a moving threshold `e^(−y)` for at most one `y` (and the same holds for a fixed relative parent threshold). The resulting test uses the minimum of the parent's and all final nonparent neighbours' masses, in the **same parent clock**. It produces the sequential shifted-exponential singleton mark; for `|R|≥2` only the recent inert occupation survives.

**Tail estimates and random cut.** For a focal parent of promotion offset `y=t−log N−s`, the uniform bounds are `O(e^y)` on the negative tail and `O(1+y)` on the positive tail. The first uses the intact age/second-moment bound with the high-parent probability and the `6e^(2y)` high-descendant bound. The second splits late descendants at focal age `2y` and charges earlier tops to its rate-one clock. Inside a compact window, a late cut-inside component has a distinct high descendant in its branch, paying at most `6e^(2y−b)` for tops promoted after focal age `b`; older intact candidates are paid by a Poisson lower bound `3/2` on internal genuine birth intensity. Multiplying by `e^(−μy)` gives integrable tails. For a bounded observation interval charge high descendants at its right endpoint and size-at-most-`|R|` for already present intact branches at its left, plus all births within the interval (with the endpoint high-parent gate). This bounds the *union of candidates* over the interval, not just the two marginal counts.

Write `τ_n=log n−log Y+o(1)`. Use finitely many deterministic brackets for `log Y` on a set of arbitrarily high probability, allowing a small slack at their endpoints for the `o(1)`. Prove each comparison unconditionally at the associated times; do not condition on `Y`, the final high set, or the size epoch. The recent lower envelope uses inert paths resident in the shape throughout the interval; the upper uses visits, including late arrivals. The residence loss is at most `O_R(ω)` per parent by the holding rate; the extra arrival-time exposure is at most `ω` per mark that hits the shape, since size increases at every genuine inert jump. For old candidates check all high inequalities using the common mass margin at both endpoints. This handles the random degree cut itself: a vertex reaching strict `D>N` late may expose a new component, so bracketing the *full fixed-shape count* by endpoint counts would be false. Let margins and bracket width decrease after the in-probability bounds. The time scale `e^(μ(t−log N))` divided by `(n/N)^μ` is `Y^(−μ)` up to an `e^(O(μω))` factor. It cancels the `Y^μ` in the continuous-time coefficient. Atomless parent-mark limits convert tail rectangles and total masses to the bounded continuous tests in the claim. This proves the statement for each deterministic schedule, using the small-power argument on the remaining indices. It does not establish an arbitrary path-adaptive cutoff law.
