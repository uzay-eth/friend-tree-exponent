## Physical balances and seams

Use occupation `η=π/μ`, so `η(1)=1/μ` and `η(b)=1` for promotion rate `b=L/D`. A nonfounder core has `L=D−1−k` ordinary leaf children. The exact gain rate is `L` from those leaves, plus reciprocal rates of all core neighbors. For a mother–child edge the internal sender on each side is explicit, and only the exterior mother of the upper vertex and each endpoint's spare core children belong to ports. Each promotion changes the parent's arity without changing its degree, updates every old incident embedding, and creates one virgin and one newborn edge. The edge uses the **postpromotion** parent census; there is no factor of `k`. The virgin receives source one, even if its mother is omitted. There is no corresponding stationary source from a founder-only edge.

For a high axis write `t=H/D`, `a=1+t/H`, and `W=∏a_i^m` over the high axes. Store Bernstein coordinates of **both** mass and actual reciprocal port flux divided by `W`. A coefficient must multiply its raw test by the source's weight `W`. The discount split in a physical lift uses the same `μ` on every mass block: the lanes add to the unsplit stored mass, and endpoint-weighting their sum multiplies it by `μ`. Slabs use `S t−s` inside slab `s`, but the test is in global `t`. A shared endpoint may be assigned to either adjacent slab provided its whole atom, ports included, uses the same assignment. Internal slab crossings need no explicit transport term: balance keys omit the slab, and the gain term evaluates the global successor test from the source slab.

At a gain within the high sector the old-minus-new numerator for exponent `e` is `t^e(a^m−a^(m−e))`. It is zero when `e=0`, and for `e≥1` it has a zero of order at least two at `t=0`. Thus the leaf part can multiply by `H/t−(k+1)` after cancellation. Each untouched high axis has degree at most `2m` before any reciprocal factor; adding that factor or a promotion factor costs at most one more. Stored degree `2m+2` suffices separately in each coordinate. At the explicit `H−1→H` seam, the changed coordinate has raw test value one and **no high source weight**; an unchanged high axis still uses its own weight. Test values vanish on high arity overflow. The code leaves the loss in place at gains or promotions that exit to overflow, and does not drop the exterior rates supplied by omitted vertices. For fixed model parameters the weak generator integrands after cancellation are bounded (also for the edge counted at its child); a discounted physical root or child/edge calculation yields stationarity without assuming an integrable ordinary degree or separating the two potentially unbounded leaf-gain terms.

## Projection and founder boundary

On the same physical arrow let `Q` be the nonnegative Bernstein selector on the root axis in a specific slab, divided by that root's `W`. Retained edges project the same `Q` by multiplying by the eliminated donor's weight on the stored edge measure; its reciprocal multiplies that factor in the flux. Write `F` for the selected root port, `P` for projected retained flux, `C` for projected retained count and `M` for selected root mass. The implemented rows are `P≤F≤P+(cM−C)/H`, where `c=1` or `k`. In the stationary image every unresolved donor has `D≥H` and `k>K`, so this is a pointwise bound per missing arrow. Comparing independently fitted means would not justify these inequalities. The nonshared boxes use the valid per-core send cap `1/2`.

In a literal finite planted tree a founder mother is unmatched by an edge whose apex is nonfounder. In the four-vertex path `(-1,0,0,1)` the virgin has mother flux `1/2`, projected retained flux zero, and an upper-row residual `1/2−1/H` (e.g. `1/6` at `H=3`). Keep the actual founder send in all finite balance checks. For a finite upper projection add the selected excess `Q·(1/D_founder−1/H)_+` on each such missing arrow. This exception is bounded in aggregate by a constant depending only on `H`: it can be positive only while `D_founder<H`, when the number of her direct children is itself bounded by `H−1`. The founder term vanishes per core. More generally the number of direct founder promotions up to time `t` is dominated by a rate-one Poisson clock; founder-related bounded local scores and normalized balance terms do not furnish an additional stationary source.

## Global constraints and rational repair

Write `M,U,B,G` for omitted mass, omitted arity mass, omitted promotion mass, and omitted incoming **core** flux, in occupation units. By mass transport, full arity mass is `η(1)`. Full incoming core flux is total outgoing core-to-core flux, `η[(1+k)/D]=η(1)−1`. This is why the incoming omission is `total−1−kept`, not `total−kept`. The birth omission is `1−kept`. All four omissions are nonnegative; `U≥(K+1)M`, `B≤M`, `M−B≤(M+U)/H`, and `G≤(M+U)/2`. Any omitted edge has an omitted endpoint: charge one edge per omitted child, and at most `k` per omitted mother. Hence omitted edge mass is at most `M+U`, including when both ends are omitted.

The physical age law is `Exp(μ)` and the postbirth promotion process is dominated by the rate-one outgoing proposal count. One may extend the common age to `Exp(low)` and use `N` equal to the number of unit-rate proposals through that age. Then `P(N≥K+1)=r^(K+1)`, `r=1/(1+low)`; by geometric memorylessness `E[N;N≥K+1]=r^(K+1)(K+1+1/low)`. The omitted event requires `k>K` as well as `D≥H`; discarding the degree condition gives both implemented envelopes. This does not require independence of physical degree and arity. The global constants are stationary identities; the verifier compares the exact row images on finite trees, not feasibility of finite trees for those rows.

For minimization with exact equalities `Ax=b`, uppers `Gx≤h`, and `x≥0`, take arbitrary rational `y,z` with `z≤0` and set `r=c−Aᵀy−Gᵀz`. Per group `g` with `Σx_i≤B_g`, the certified lower bound is `b·y+h·z−Σ_g B_g max(0,max_{i∈g}(−r_i))`; negate the result for minimization of `−c` to bound the maximum. `optimize` divides rounded marginals by the objective scale and clips the upper multipliers before the exact residual. Tensor Bernstein coordinates sum to one and `1/W≤1`, so summing each storage group costs at most its raw mass or port flux. Root and edge raw masses are each at most `1/low` (charge each edge to its child); use factors `1/2` for mother ports and `max(K,H−2)/2` for spare-child ports. The two lanes share that bound. The conclusion applies to the physical image, even if a solver point violates group budgets that are not explicitly imposed. For conversion to per-core units, all four endpoint products with the `μ` interval handle either sign.
