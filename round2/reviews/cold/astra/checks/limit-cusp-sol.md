## Sharp first differences and the distributional derivative

Set `α=1/μ−1`, `p*=1/(1−α)`, `A=κ/μ`, and use the continuous zero extension. Fable proves the two equivalents `f(x)~A x^α` and `f′(x)~Aα x^(α−1)` separately. Its uniform exit bounds on `v_j,v′_j` and their exponential decay justify differentiating the star series on every compact positive interval. For `x≥1`, use `j^μ x≥(3^μ x+j^μ)/2`; summability survives the factor `j^(2μ)` from differentiation. Thus `f,f′` decay exponentially at infinity. Fable §8 separately proves interior smoothness; using its support-weight bound for order `ℓ`, with `s=ℓ+1`, gives an `O(ε^−s)` bound on derivatives through `ℓ` for `x≥ε`. Taking larger `ℓ` gives arbitrary polynomial decay at infinity for any fixed derivative order, with order-dependent constants. This does not supply higher-derivative asymptotics at zero.

Integrate the classical derivative from a positive cutoff and let it decrease to zero. Since the derivative is locally integrable and `f(0)=0`, the weak derivative is exactly `1_(x>0) f′(x)`, with no boundary delta. It lies in strong `L^p` exactly for `1≤p<p*`; at the endpoint it is in weak `L^{p*}` and has `lim_(u→∞) u^(p*) |{|Df|>u}|=(Aα)^(p*)`. Indeed all sufficiently large level sets lie in an arbitrarily short positive interval, where the derivative equivalent traps their lengths between the corresponding power-law intervals; no monotonicity is needed. There is no locally finite signed-measure second derivative across zero: on any compact neighborhood a locally finite second derivative would make the first derivative a BV function and locally essentially bounded there, which contradicts its cusp. No specific higher singular expansion is claimed.

- For `p<p*`, average translates of the weak derivative and use strong `L^p` translation continuity, including `p=1`. The coefficient is the nonzero norm of the full derivative.
- At `p=p*`, the crossing and inner region `[-h,Rh]` contributes `O_R(h^p)` to the integral, and `x≥ε` gives `O_ε(h^p)`. On `[Rh,ε]`, integrating the derivative equivalent gives the lower relative factor `(1−η)(1+1/R)^(α−1)` and upper `1+η`. The comparison integral is exactly `h^p(Aα)^p (log(1/h)+log(ε/R))`.
