# Cold audit: proportional-cutoff fixed-shape forest (Q5)

**HOLDS.** For each fixed deterministic `c>0` and deterministic `N_n/n→c`, the finite vector of marked rooted shape counts (ordinary size at least two) has the claimed **stable** limit relative to the full physical history. Conditional on that history in the extension, different hub labels and distinct shape categories have independent Poisson cells of means `I_R`; a nonfounder atom has limiting parent mark `Z_h/c`. The original-core singleton ledger stabilizes pathwise and can be carried jointly. The result is for each deterministic `c`, not a simultaneous regularity assertion for all `c` on one path, nor total-variation convergence of a conditional law given the full history. ## Future-dependent cut and positive atomlessness

- On a hub event the outgoing intensity into existing cores is integrable: personal rings give `O(1+t)` such neighbours, including the initial orientation exception, whereas the degree is asymptotic to `Y Z_h e^t`. The statement about finitely many arrows follows by compensation stopped on the integrated predictable intensity, not by compensating after conditioning on a hub event. In particular a hub-to-hub edge has a last cross-arrow in either direction. The inherited hub dichotomy and offspring law (`fringe/hub-children.md`) provide a positive-mass child and ensure both endpoints can be taken to be cores at the restart.
- To exclude `Z_v=x>0`, union over deterministic integer restart sizes and labelled cut edges. At any such restart, generate two independent **unconditional** inert continuations retaining the cut slot on each endpoint, plus the cross directions. Until the first cross arrow only internal genuine arrows change either side; each suppressed direction can be represented by its own hazard `1/D_sender`. This construction need only agree with the physical process until the first cross arrow. It is not a factorization of the law after requiring that there be no cross arrows forever. Equivalently, this split may use separate internal and cross jump channels; it need not reuse the initiating-clock construction used later for marking.
- On either side, add a Yule proposal slot at every proposal, assign a real vertex only when an internal genuine birth occurred, and choose uniformly among all current slots. A virtual slot or a boundary-to-slot choice adds only a virtual slot. Independently generated holding times of rate equal to the proposal count give gamma limits `G_A,G_B` independent of the two proposal embedded chains. The ratios `a_A=D_v/Q_A`, `b_i=M_i/Q_i` at the limit are measurable in those chains; `b_i>0` by the inert ordinary-size limit (`open/forest-age/inert/README.md#3-ordinary-size-and-the-high-cutoff-joint-law`). Thus the inert ratio is a strictly increasing function of the continuous beta split when `a_A>0`, and zero otherwise. The unconditional event of equality at `x>0` already has probability zero, so intersecting it with no future cross cannot create an atom. No independence of the true continuous degree mass from the true Yule mass is assumed.
- At actual vertex birth after the initial edge, the degree starts at one and increases with probability at most `D_v/n` at the next size. Monotone urn comparison and Doob at `p=2` give `2/[a²v(v+1)]` for ever reaching fraction `a`. Sum over late labels with `a=c/2`; on the finite remaining list, the positive-atom exclusion and convergence settle the strict cutoff. This works for cutoffs approaching `cn` from either side, including `c=1`. For `c≥1` the high set is empty and eventually there is no component of any fixed finite size.

## Root boundaries, old pairs and the persistent singleton

After all high vertices exist and deletion stabilizes, a component containing a fixed retained vertex only grows. If it remains finite with at least two members, any internal edge joins two vertices whose original degrees are bounded: all their other neighbours must be members of that finite component or of the fixed finite deleted set. A direction on that edge then has integrated arrow intensity infinite, forcing unbounded growth. This is pathwise with the original compensated arrows (or the original rate-one personal rings and fresh choices). It does not assume the no-cross law conditional on the future cut. In particular an old pair can look intact or have cuts hidden below both ends, but never remains a pair at late sizes.

Any nonroot component top has immediate ordinary parent in the high set. Any component with a cut below its top has top on one of the finite ancestor paths of the high labels. These tops, and the orientation root and initial label 2, form a fixed finite exceptional list, so contribute no size-`s≥2` occurrences eventually. In particular label 2 can be a hub while its **orientation parent** label 1 is still a leaf. Sending into 1 can produce a pair rooted at 1 but it is a fixed orientation-root exception, not a new downward immigrant with a parent mark. If 1 is a hub and the initial label 2 becomes its promoted leaf, 2 is likewise an old fixed exception in the eventual argument. After both founders are cores, every selected hub promotion into a leaf is downward: a noninitial vertex's birth parent was already a core immediately after its birth. The original core criterion includes a singleton at 1 with no parent mark and excludes isolated ordinary leaves. Any singleton core is adjacent to at least two distinct members of `H_c`, hence in their finite spanning subtree. It remains singleton exactly when every final neighbour is high. Conversely, if that condition holds all the finite neighbours have appeared by some finite time; the singleton then persists. This gives eventual equality, not just a singleton intensity. In particular no positive singleton atom is asserted on every history.

## Immigration coupling, returns and random size time

- At the early deterministic time `b`, select only known core labels up to `K` by their current fractions. The observation window is then fixed conditional on that history. In the finite window the fresh initiating clocks of the proxies are independent Poisson streams. At a proposed promotion the selected vertex must be a leaf, so it has no existing core branch; refresh the unused post-promotion drivers of it and its newly attached leaf. Keep the parent's slot in the child's degree. Branch root-to-parent choices are **internal nulls but genuine live returns**: the added leaf belongs to the parent, not the branch. It may later be selected for promotion, at which point it gets a fresh mark. It has never been used inside the returning branch. Proxies are already cores and cannot be reused as promoted leaves; their newly promoted branches are disjoint even when the proxies themselves are nested or adjacent. A predetermined proxy clock is not driven by a branch mark. These statements use causal revelation of the independent drivers, not a filtration in which the physical process knows the unrevealed marked trajectories.
- A proxy choice of any existing core is an allowed physical arrow but a comparison failure. This includes all high-parent input into a matched branch. No extra failure for a return from the branch is needed. Compensate against the **early** measurable proxy list and times; only split the expected integrated failure rate afterwards by proxy agreement with the eventual finite hub list. Bounded convergence on agreement and the trivial `K(l+2ε)` bound on disagreement give the error claimed. Neither the inert marks nor the streams are asserted to be independent conditional on the eventual hub list or on coupling success.
- The Yule forecast `t_b=log n−log(e^{-b}M(b))` uses only early data. The physical `τ_n−t_b` tends to `log(Y_b/Y)` at fixed `b`, then this error goes to zero as `b→∞`. No substitution of deterministic `log n` for the physical time without the Yule shift is made. To transfer across the bracket, bound jumps of the **observable** in the ideal immigration system: total rate at most `K` plus `s` times the number of branches then of size at most `s`. At each specified time their expected count is at most `K∫B_s`, by compensation of the marked arrivals; the wording “at any time” here is a uniform bound on the mean at a specified time, not a bound on the expected temporal supremum. The resulting `2ε K(1+s∫B_s)` controls all possibly relevant jumps, even when `τ_n` depends on these same branches and returns. The many physical Yule size jumps are irrelevant to this estimate.
- For every live virgin, internal genuine rate is `m−1/D_root≥3/2` with a retained slot. Incoming arrows can only add more vertices. A branch still at size `≤s` after elapsed time `r` requires at most `s−2` genuine internal changes, giving the uniform conditional envelope `B_s(r)`. Before `b` there are finitely many actual children of the finitely many eligible parents; after `b` and before the start of the recent window, apply the envelope **at each promotion stopping time** and compensate its intensity at most one. Only later discard the event of a mismatched high set. The old bounded pair layer is therefore not silently absorbed into the limiting Poisson means. For the inert pair `I_pair=1/(2−1/2)=2/3` since size never decreases and the only null is its one boundary slot.

## Stable convergence and scope

At the forecast time the inert marks are assigned to a single present-shape category (or the unused category). Independent thinning gives the finite-window conditional Poisson cells at each proxy, with mean `∫_0^{l+ε}P(T^0_u=R)du`. For a bounded full-history test use `E[U|F_b]`; its `L¹` error tends to zero. Fix `K,l,ε` for `n→∞` then `b→∞`, send `ε↓0`, then `l→∞`, then remove `K`. This respects the factors of `K` and `l` in the error estimates. The finite-label localization and tight counts allow replacement of the true parent marks by their early forecasts for continuous mark tests. The pathwise singleton result then yields joint stable convergence with its limiting ledger and marks. Given the full history at finite `n`, the count is measurable and its conditional law is Dirac; the nondegenerate Poisson kernel where there are hubs is a **limit in the stable-test sense**, not an assertion that such Dirac laws approach it in total variation. Marginal stable convergence also does not prove infinitely many empty samples; the separate recurrence argument is needed for that conclusion.
