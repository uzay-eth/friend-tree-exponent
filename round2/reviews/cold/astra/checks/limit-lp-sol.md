# Cold audit: the full-law `L^p` upgrade

**Verdict: accept**, using the fixed-cut Dirichlet identity (`open/limit-law/smoothing/README.md`) and the edge-seeded lower-tail bound (`open/limit-law/left/README.md`). The mixture proof (`open/limit-law/smoothing/mixture/README.md`) gets a power bound for **all small Borel sets**, not merely intervals. This distinction is needed when testing superlevel sets of the density. The proof uses no retrospective conditioning on a never-failing pair or on a last crossing. The original argument is unchanged by this audit.

## Deterministic mesh and crossing error

Start the clock at the deterministic three-vertex shape. The two-stage setup keeps an original spare leaf and a virgin leaf at the two anchors. At the first core ring in the second unit the chance to choose a leaf is at least `3/4`, because the active anchor has at most one old core neighbour and at least three leaves. Silence both anchors for the remainder of that unit. By the restart property the extra silence costs at most a factor `e^-2` in the lower probability; incoming arrows can add leaves, but cannot promote the designated leaves. A deterministic lower bound for setup is `q=e^-1 (1−2/e)(1−e^-1)(3/4)e^-2>0`.

Use independent lower birth–death populations from the two leaves. For one population the extinction-by-age probability is `(1−z)/(2−z)` with `z=e^-u/2` (here `z=exp(−u/2)`); eventual extinction has probability `1/2`. The surviving-at-age-`u` population is geometric on the positive integers with parameter `z/(2−z)`. Hence the defective late extinction probability is `z/[2(2−z)]` and the probability of a positive count below `K` is at most `Kz`. No independence from the exterior is asserted. For a failed attempt the duration `D` is the setup time or the first extinction rounded up to an integer; hence for `s≥d+1`, `P(D>s,fail | restart)≤exp(−(s−d−1)/2)`. Since the failure probability is at most `1−q/4`, integrating `r exp(rs)` against this tail gives `E[exp(rD);fail]≤1−q/8` for sufficiently small deterministic `r>0`. The failure decisions are observable at their restart times. Iterating over finite failure prefixes and summing the geometric bound gives a finite `r`-exponential moment for the setup-completion time of the nonfailing attempt. This latter time need not be a stopping time.

At a deterministic integer `t`, exclude a nonfailing setup completed after `t/2`. For each possible integer setup completion `s≤t/2`, its occurrence is measurable at `s`; bound the small-positive-stock probability at `t` by the **unconditioned** birth–death tail above and sum over `s`. To evaluate this bound if a trial was abandoned before `t`, extend each computed lower population with independent drivers after abandonment; on the nonfailing trial no extension is used. For `K=ceil(exp(t/8))` the total error is bounded by `C exp(−ct)` for some `c>0`. No smoothing calculation uses conditioning on the genealogy of this trial. Instead select the first eligible edge measurably in the physical tree at `t`. Given that tree and edge, the probability of at least one subsequent crossing is at most `4/(K+1)` by the two directed first-send bounds. These apply to genuine leaf stock other than the receiver; since the receiver is core this requires **zero** return immigration. Thus failure of either eligibility or no-cross has probability `O(exp(−ct))`, reducing `c` if needed. A size cap `exp(2t)` adds at most `3exp(−t)` by Markov, as used by 1374.

## Fixed-cut inverse and both endpoints

On the no-cross sublaw use the exact reference weights from the smoothing note: the holding correction and the no-cross exponential cancel, leaving an embedded-path product in `[0,1]`. Conditional on embedded paths the reference proportion density is `Beta(n1,n2)`. Even after dropping the terminal weight for an upper bound it is at most `n1+n2−1`: its ratio to that number is the probability of exactly `n1−1` successes among `n1+n2−2` Bernoulli trials. Crucially the coefficients `a,b` are intrinsic core/size limits on the embedded paths, not functions of the reference gamma proportions. They are finite, nonnegative, and not both zero wherever the terminal likelihood is positive. Define arbitrary values off that set, which cannot contribute.

For `g(p)=a p^μ+b(1−p)^μ`, `−g''≥μ(1−μ)(a+b)` in the open interval. This also covers `a=0` or `b=0`. Since the derivative decreases at this speed, the portion with `|g'|≤h` has length at most `2h/[μ(1−μ)(a+b)]`. Each of the at most two complementary intervals has an inverse that is `1/h`-Lipschitz by the mean value theorem, even up to a boundary where `g'` may blow up. Thus the inverse-measure estimate holds for arbitrary Borel sets. The endpoints of the proportion interval have no beta atoms. No large-`X` cutoff is needed; for the small-`X` endpoint, `g≤a+b` permits imposing `a+b≥v` when testing `X>v`, and the discarded physical mass is at most `C v^(1/μ)` from the left-tail theorem. This audit uses that theorem as an input rather than reproving it.

The reference conditional bound with the size cap is `C exp(2t) sqrt(δ/v)` for `δ=|A|`. Substituting `t=floor(log(1/δ)/16)` and `v=δ^(1/4)` gives `δ^(1/4)` as asserted; flooring only changes constants in `exp(−ct)`. Null sets follow by outer approximation (or the fixed-cut identities and vanishing discarded mass). If `f` is the full density, for `H≥1` the set `{f>H}` has measure at most one and `H Leb{f>H}≤C (Leb{f>H})^ξ`. Thus `f` is in weak `L^(1/(1−ξ))`, and the strict strong `L^p` range follows by layer cake, using `∫f=1` below height one. The weak endpoint does not give strong integrability at equality. Since each adaptive-at-time-`t` retained sublaw is a genuine restriction of the physical law, its density is dominated by `f`. Its missing mass tends to zero, giving convergence in each of the stated strong `L^p` spaces by truncating at a fixed density height and then sending the height to infinity.

## Optional sharper parameter tradeoff

The hard size cutoff can be dropped. Conditional on the time-`t` seed the small-set bound is `C |T_t| sqrt(δ/v)`; averaging this bound costs at most `C exp(t) sqrt(δ/v)`. This needs no independence between eligibility and size. For the fixed deterministic choices `v=δ^b`, `t=floor(a log(1/δ))`, the three exponents are `ca`, `(1−b)/2−a`, and `b/μ`. Equating them works with

```
ξ = 1/(2 + μ + 2/c),     a = ξ/c,     b = μξ.
```

Here `c>0` is any established exponential loss rate for eligibility and crossing, with the size cap removed. It remains a small unspecified deterministic exponent for this proof; no optimized numerical `p` or bounded/continuous full-law density follows. Removing the size cap still gives physical sublaws dominated by the full law, and leaves all terminal weights in the conditional argument.
