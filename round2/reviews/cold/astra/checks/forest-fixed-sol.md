# Cold review: sequential fixed finite-shape intensities

**Verdict: holds as a sequential, shape-by-shape theorem.** Reviewed `open/forest-age/fixed-shapes/README.md`, using the existing physical stable-age/top-edge formula and the tagged-stock bounds in the locally finite birth environment. Both limits and the bounded continuous log-parent mark extensions follow. The singleton coefficient exists, is finite and strictly positive. This does not give a diagonal in `n,N` or a summable envelope over all shapes at the scale `N^µ`.

## Old branches, nested cuts, and the singleton

* **Finite-time old pairs are possible.** A physical chain `o–v–z–w–q` can have high `o,w,q` and bare degree-two `v,z` after any prescribed finite delay, with positive probability. Deleting `w` already disconnects `q`; the retained component is the old pair `{v,z}`. It would be wrong to identify fixed observed size with recent birth or to rule out cut-inside contributions pointwise at a finite epoch.
* **They cannot persist in the fixed-early-birth limit.** Degrees are nondecreasing integers. On a bounded-degree path a vertex eventually has a fixed neighbour set. Two adjacent vertices cannot both remain bounded, since on a directed channel with bounded source degree there is a strictly positive lower rate for gains at its target. For a vertex whose degree is unbounded, at its first hit of degree `d` a fresh leaf admits a tagged birth–death lower comparison with survival at least `1−1/d`; on survival its stock tends to infinity. At a hit of large stock the conditional gamma-minus-loss comparison excludes zero degree mass with probability tending to one, uniformly over live exteriors. This justifies the mass dichotomy without erroneously reasoning that an unbounded integer degree must have positive scaled mass. Countably many labels and edges can be put on the same event. A retained positive-mass vertex has a diverging ordinary leaf stock, as its core-neighbour count grows at most linearly in time. Every such leaf survives the cutoff. Therefore in a fixed early branch, a finite component of size `≥2` eventually disappears: either the top itself is an unbounded high/large vertex, or its bounded top has a surviving unbounded neighbour whose leaves make the component large. A deeper cut cannot remove those leaves without removing their own core.
* **Exactly which singleton counts.** A bounded top is eventually bare: a final leaf neighbour would have a rate-one send into it. Its final neighbour set is finite, includes the focal parent, and has at least one nonparent member because the top was promoted with degree two. All final neighbours have positive mass. Each contributes a strict cut gate at `t=log N+y`, namely `W_w>e^(−y)` off its equality level. The intersection takes the **minimum** in the focal parent's time origin; a later neighbour's own-clock mass needs the appropriate elapsed-time factor. Equality can be removed for almost every `y` by pathwise Fubini, even for a random list of final neighbours. Strictly positive minima need not have a unique minimizer. What is unique is the top and its deleted immediate parent for each nonfounder component; the injection into removed descendants used for one focal parent uses disjoint top-child branches. There can be **several** singleton children of that parent. The formula correctly sums them once each; it does not use the earliest child or an indicator of any success.
* **Later sends genuinely matter.** A temporary singleton can receive a late send and cease to be a singleton, then become one again after the new leaf is promoted and itself becomes a cut neighbour. An eventual bounded singleton need not have degree two or have avoided all sends since promotion. The theorem reads the *final* neighbourhood, so it accounts for these histories; the no-gain script is only a sufficient event for strict positivity. For example, after the script gives the two neighbours at least `j=32` leaves, the sum of the two first-send upper bounds and two zero-mass upper bounds is `4/(j+1)+12/j<1`. The latter use `P(Gamma(j)<j/2)≤4/j` and mean phantom loss at most one. The union bound applies under the live joint law with no independence assumption.

## Age, domination, and coefficient identification

An intact shape of fixed size `s≥2` must remain within `s−2` births of the virgin. Its intrinsic birth intensity is always `m−1/D_top≥3/2`, irrespective of exterior sends, which can only add vertices. Apply the Poisson lower clock after each promotion; when compensating a terminal mark, take its conditional expected continuation given the postpromotion state, not a future success as a predictable event. The age-tail integral is consequently uniform in the exterior. The recent intact layer has **bounded child observation age**, not bounded focal-parent promotion time. Freezing the early history at `b`, use `D_o(b)>e^(b−y)` on the early high gate to bound a fresh child's incoming hazard by `h e^(y−b)`. The ordinary size bound controls chance of a cut anywhere in the recent branch. Promotion intensity tends to one on the positive-mass high paths, using exponential growth of degree versus the rate-one bound on core neighbours; pay the early-gate discrepancy separately. This gives the product of the high-parent probability and the inert age integral, with the recent window widened only after taking the cutoff limit. The live holding formula uses the frozen *evolving* exterior, including outgoing top-to-parent sends, and a separate incoming hazard threshold. The inert holding mean at the virgin is `2/3`; the live prelimit need not have that mean.

Cut-inside tops are controlled on a different axis: by their **birth time in the focal parent's clock**, including descendants they may have promoted much later. A cut-inside top born after `b` has a removed descendant also promoted after `b`. The whole focal descendant core genealogy starts from one core and has expected promotion intensity at most `e^r` at time `r`. Each promoted vertex starts at degree two; its conditional second moment at time `t` is at most `6e^(2(t−r))`. Thus the expected number of removed descendants promoted after `b` is at most `6e^(2y−b)`. No independence of an ancestral promotion and a later high degree is needed. This handles old-looking small shapes arising from nested cuts without confusing elapsed child age and parent-clock birth time.

For fixed `s`, the left envelope for cut-inside counts is `6e^(2y)` and the right envelope is `6+2y`, obtained by splitting top births at `2y` (or `t` if earlier). For intact counts the right envelope is a constant depending on `s`. On the left, the qualifying count has uniformly bounded second moment: on each unit child-age strip use the fourth moment of the focal clock count and `P(qualifying count>0)≤E qualifying count` with the Poisson age tail, then sum the L² norms. Multiplying by the high-parent gate costs at most a factor proportional to `e^y` by Cauchy–Schwarz and the degree second moment from the virgin. These are integrable against `µe^(−µy)dy` at both ends. Thus dominated convergence is valid for each fixed rooted shape, with the same envelope across shapes of a **fixed size**, and for bounded inserted marks. There cannot be an integrable expectation bound for the scaled sum over *all sizes*: `N^µ M_N(1)∼A log N` in the established total-count law. For every individual rooted shape, reachability follows by adding vertices in an order starting from one root–child edge. Each target already has an internal neighbour that can send to it; a plant-slot send is never needed. The state visits a given size only once, which gives the finite rational hit/hold formula.

For early children, truncate at `b` and dominate their pathwise limits by the finite focal clock count. Let `b→∞`, charging late cut-inside children by the bound above. The integrable envelopes allow the subsequent `y` integration; Tonelli turns each joint gate into `∫_(−log m)^∞ µe^(−µy)dy=m^µ`. This proves existence and finiteness of the coefficient in addition to identifying it. For the parent mark the change of variable `e=y+log W_o` gives the lower end `log(W_o/m)` and the factor `W_o^µ`. The founder exception has already disappeared in the fixed-`N` top-edge representation, before scaling by `N^µ`.
