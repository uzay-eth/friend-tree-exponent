### 1. State space, infinite-volume construction, and spatial cutoff bound (§1, Equations (2)–(3))

- **Polish topology on $`\mathcal X`$.** Elements of $`\mathcal X`$ are root- and mark-preserving isomorphism classes of finite or countably infinite connected locally finite trees $`(G, o)`$ with integer leaf marks $`L_x \ge 0`$ satisfying $`D_x = L_x + c_x \ge 2`$ everywhere ($`c_x = \deg_G(x)`$, allowing $`c_o = 0, L_o \ge 2`$). Whether a radius-$`k`$ marked ball records $`(B_k(o), L|_{B_k(o)})`$ or also records $`c_z`$ on $`\partial B_k(o)`$ generates the exact same topology (the latter sits between radius $`k`$ and radius $`k+1`$ of the former). By König's infinity lemma on finite root-preserving isomorphisms between compatible balls, $`\mathcal X`$ embeds as a closed subset of a countable product of countable discrete spaces, so $`\mathcal X`$ is Polish, and finite-radius ball indicators form a clopen $`\pi`$-system determining Borel probability measures.
- **Drivers, rates, and cell domination.** When $`c_x^0 = 0`$ there are no oriented old edges; on every oriented old edge $`x \to y`$ ($`c_x^0 \ge 1`$), monotonicity of $`D_x(t) \ge c_x(t) \ge c_x^0`$ ensures $`c_x^0 / D_x(t) \in (0, 1]`$, so thinning a rate-$`(1/c_x^0)`$ Poisson process by $`c_x^0 / D_x(t)`$ yields exact rate $`1/D_x(t)`$. At any vertex $`x \in H_w(t)`$, internal core neighbours plus promotion use total clock length $`\frac{c_x(t) - c_x^0 \mathbf 1_{x=w} + L_x(t)}{D_x(t)} \le 1`$, leaving $`\frac{c_x^0 \mathbf 1_{x=w}}{D_x(t)}`$ idle. By Poisson superposition/thinning, this produces exact friend-tree core jump rates $`r_x = L_x + \sum_{y \sim x} 1/D_y`$ and $`b_x = L_x / D_x`$ with almost surely distinct jump times. On any finite set $`F`$ of old vertices, incoming boundary proposals have finite rate $`\le \sum_{y \in F} c_y^0 < \infty`$ while endogenous jumps grow at most linearly in total core-plus-leaf count, preventing explosion. Since $`b_x \le 1`$, $`|H_o(s)|`$ is pathwise dominated by a rate-1 Yule process begun at $`1`$, giving $`1 \le Q_s 1 \le e^s`$ and $`\mathbb E|H_o(s)|^2 \le 2e^{2s} - e^s \le 2e^{2s}`$ (Lemma 1.4 of `../../proofs/existence.md` (`proofs/existence.md`)).
- **Proof of the spatial cutoff bound (3).** Because newly promoted cores attach only to their promoters and inherit their cell labels, contracting each connected subtree $`H_u(s)`$ returns $`G_0`$, and the only inter-cell edges at time $`s`$ are the original old edges $`E(G_0)`$.   $`\displaystyle d_{G_s}(v, z) = d_{G_s}(v, o) + d_{G_0}(o, w) + d_{G_s}(w, z) \ge d_{G_0}(o, w),`$
  so $`B_k^{G_s}(v)`$ meets only cells $`H_w(s)`$ with $`w \in B_k^{G_0}(o)`$ (and even $`c_w(s) = c_w^0 + \deg_{H_w(s)}(w)`$ is determined once old boundary slots are retained). Comparing truncation $`R \ge k`$ with any larger truncation $`R' > R`$ using identical drivers on $`B_R(o)`$, a discrepancy in cell $`w \in B_k(o)`$ at time $`\le s`$ backtracks through strictly earlier cell-discrepancy times along old-edge proposals until exiting $`B_R(o)`$ at distance $`R + 1`$. Since $`(R + 1) - d_{G_0}(o, w) \ge R - k + 1 =: j`$, taking the last $`j`$ edges of that simple path gives a simple oriented path of length **exactly** $`j`$ ending at $`w`$ carrying an increasing $`j`$-tuple of proposals in $`(0, s]`$. By Campbell's theorem the expected tuple count on a given simple path $`(x_0 \to \dots \to x_j = w)`$ is $`\frac{s^j}{j!}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{c_{x_i}^0}`$. Applying a union bound over $`w \in B_k(o)`$ gives failure probability $`\le p = \min(1, \frac{s^j}{j!}\sum_{w \in B_k(o)} c_w^0)`$, and applying Cauchy–Schwarz separately to $`|H_o(s)|\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{fail}}`$ and $`|H_o^{[R]}(s)|\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{fail}}`$ gives $`\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{2}e^s + \sqrt{2}e^s)\|f\|_\infty = 2\sqrt{2}\,e^s\|f\|_\infty\sqrt{p}`$, confirming (3).

### 5. Full spectral radius $`r(Q_1) = e^\mu`$ (§5, Equation (8))

- **Finite-core bound (8).** On any finite core tree $`(G, o) \in \mathcal X`$, $`H_o(t)`$ partitions into $`\{o\}`$ together with the pairwise-disjoint downward branches founded at direct promotions of $`o`$ in $`(0, t]`$. Because $`A_j(u)`$, $`e^{\Lambda_j u}`$ ($`\Lambda_j > 0`$), and $`F(u + 1)`$ are all nondecreasing on $`[0, t]`$, integrating $`b_o(s) \le 1`$ over $`s \in [0, t]`$ yields (8):
  $`\displaystyle Q_t 1(G, o) \le 1 + t A_j(t)e^{\Lambda_j t}F(t + 1).`$
- **Infinite-volume extension via genuine core completions.** Given an infinite $`(G, o) \in \mathcal X`$ and radius $`R \ge 1`$, appending one new core vertex with $`L = 1`$ leaf to each unrepresented boundary core-neighbour slot of $`B_R(o)`$ produces a finite connected tree $`(G^{(R)}, o) \in \mathcal X`$ ($`D = 2`$ on every appended boundary vertex, and exact degrees $`(L_u, c_u^0)`$ preserved throughout $`B_R(o)`$).   $`\displaystyle \big|Q_t 1(G, o) - Q_t 1(G^{(R)}, o)\big| \le 4\sqrt{2}\,e^t \sqrt{\min\! \left(1, \, \frac{t^{R+1}}{(R+1)!}c_o^0\right)} \xrightarrow{R \to \infty} 0.`$
  Since (8) holds for every $`(G^{(R)}, o)`$ with right-hand side independent of $`R`$ and $`(G, o)`$, (8) holds uniformly across all $`(G, o) \in \mathcal X`$.
- **Spectral radius and uniqueness of the probability eigenvalue.** On $`C_b(\mathcal X)`$ (both real and complexified), positivity of $`Q_n`$ and $`1 \in C_b(\mathcal X)`$ give $`\|Q_n\| = \|Q_n 1\|_\infty`$, and $`Q_n = Q_1^n`$ by §1.
