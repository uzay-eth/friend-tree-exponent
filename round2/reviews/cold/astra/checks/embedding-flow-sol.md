## 1. Physical source and normalization

Let `π` be the limiting law per physical core and let `ρ=π/μ`. An occupation column is the `ρ`-mean of the relevant **labelled embeddings** rooted at the sampled core. `total=1/μ`. For a fixed pattern, under `Q_h` on the original core cell, internal arrow rates modify existing embeddings and promotions both modify old embeddings and extend each eligible existing prefix. A focal promotion also adds a new possible apex, with exactly the virgin descriptor `(2,0,())`; it cannot immediately create a larger pattern **rooted at that newborn**. The total intensity of new roots per physical core is `π(L/D)=μ`. Thus the normalized source is exactly 1 on the virgin row and zero elsewhere. Equivalently, finite local generator `G` gives `μ m_P−G_P^old=δ_(P,virgin)` at the limit. This is the unnormalized per-birth quantity; multiply bounds by `μ` to get per-core numbers. With the same true `μ` for every column, split each balance mass linearly between `LO` and `HI`; this shows feasibility, while the relaxation allows the splits to vary. Projection and objective rows use their sum. The envelope of products with the two discount endpoints is valid even for negative contrasts.

The original finite founder has no mother, and is never a planted apex. **Keep her promotions as a source** when comparing against a literal finite-tree generator of nonfounder embeddings. For example, the three-vertex star has no represented core but promotes a leaf at total rate 1; that is exactly its virgin source. Her own capped rooted pattern terms and the capped terms involving a founder mother are negligible per core: whenever the founder degree is at most the cap she has at most `cap` children, with bounded embedding multiplicity. For tests reading an exterior founder sender at larger degree, either the overflow bound holds or one can use the bounded-depth count estimate (`O(1+t)` expected direct core children, since her promotion rate is at most one). No founder correction is charged as a stationary source. A formal weak-limit route is to take expectations of finite generator identities and normalize by mean core count: the bounded local embedding and capped-incidence scores pass to stationarity by the oriented LLN and uniform integrability; alternatively differentiate the bounded cell scores using the `π Q_h=e^(μh)π` identity. At fixed cap each existing embedding's relevant events have bounded rates, and births per existing match have bounded multiplicity.

## 2. External ports and orbit conventions

For an embedding with one designated role and `r` outside child slots, extend the **labelled template** by one named child at that role. A physical extended map is in bijection with a base map and the choice of one of those outside children, not with an unordered copy of the enlarged crown. Sorting the extended descriptor only reindexes the roles. Consequently each represented donor class enters the projection exactly once with its weight from the extended labelled mass, even when the new child is identical to an existing arm. For the mother use a new apex having the original pattern as its one specified child. Again each extension represents one `(base map, actual mother)`; between that count and the child-oriented port there is no additional arity factor. If `J_d` counts represented donors of degree `d`, the common port `F` satisfies

```
Σ J_d/d  ≤ F ≤  Σ J_d/d + (r*m − Σ J_d)/(cap+1).
```

The mother has `r=1`; an unshared port uses `0≤F≤r*m/2`. These are necessary projection constraints. They do not impose full projectivity of the entire joint law of unseen donors or agreement of every higher-order port. The same `F` must be used for the physical degree derivative and for the D18 reward coefficient on that role. Independent receive endpoints per row would not check this.

**Finite exception with exact witness.** Grow the four-vertex path from `T3` by promoting one founder leaf. The founder has degree 2 and the new core is a virgin. For the virgin mother port `m=1, F=1/2, J_d=0`: the founder is physically the donor but cannot be the apex of the planted extension. The upper projection is violated by `1/4` at cap 3 and `3/10` at cap 4. The lower projection remains true. The oracle catches 105 / 215 finite upper residuals, all bounded by the actual founder-donor excess. To use this as a finite-tree identity, add the explicit founder surplus `Σ_maps 1{mother=founder, D_founder≤cap}(1/D_founder−1/(cap+1))` on the upper RHS. In the stationary model this term vanishes, so no matrix change is needed if the claim and any finite regression account for the boundary. Artificially replacing the finite founder's actual send by an overflow endpoint in a balance check would be wrong.

**Orbit versus physical source.** For a fixed census-decorated template and physical image the number of its maps is `|Aut_decorated|`; it is not the undecorated topology automorphism order. Each representative of a pointed orbit has the same integrated port statistic, by relabelling maps. That statistic is per representative, not their sum. Accordingly the balance loops over **all** roles, accumulating the repeated columns when needed. A virgin-content birth term is also run once per role: deleting a specified target virgin gives the correct source template for this map even if other target virgins are identical. In an `S3` with three equal newborn censuses an image contributes six maps, and there are three possible new labelled arm slots in the reverse balance, not one. In a mixed repeated `S3` class the stabilizer is two; for three different labels it is one. For the physical reward each image is paid once, so divide raw base and role slopes by the **decorated** stabilizer, then add all slopes on their pointed column, including duplicate orbit roles. `Yb` has oriented structurally different arms; the long and short branches cannot be swapped. Two eligible bent images in a larger host remain two physical rows.

The oracle applies literal event arrows `sender→recipient` at `1/D_sender`, adding an ordinary leaf at the recipient and rebuilding the actual core set. It independently enumerates connected physical image subsets, permutes indices to find stabilizers and pointed orbits, and compares the full generator increment with the emitted sparse balance row. It reconstructs the affine D18 charge on each image directly from the original table values: gain arrows on the image, promotions of each distinct ordinary leaf, activation of the new embedding at that birth, routed exterior sends at their center, and the incoming actual-minus-center differences for each connected tip deletion. No `Extract.row`, solver balance, or solver orbit function is used for the expected value. The target side imports the solver and its row extractor; only the binary layout/table indices are shared across both sides. It compares each witness separately and a nonunit rational contrast on the **same** physical ports. Cap 4 has 46 nonzero repeated-census `S3` source comparisons; `S3` is impossible at cap 3 in the planted model. This is a test of actual role-affine charges, not mock column weights.

## 3. Global rows, repair, and use of the result

The single-core sums satisfy: total retained mass at most `total`; total retained `k` at most `total` by orientation (one downward edge per nonfounder on average); retained promotion mass at most 1; overflow promotion mass at most overflow mass. The tail envelope follows from the terminal-age hitting formula: for each degree `d`, total gain is at most `d−1/2` for a nonfounder, since its mother is a core. Conditional on attaining `d`, the next waiting time is bounded below in law by that exponential clock; success discount to `cap+1` is at most the product in `global_rows`, with the low discount outward. Lastly, `π[(1+k)/D]=1−μ` from `L=D−1−k`. On omitted rows `D≥cap+1`, bound their reciprocal contribution by `(1+k)/(cap+1)` and use stationary full `π(1+k)=2`. Dividing by `μ` and rearranging yields exactly `omitted_reciprocal≤1`. These are stationary rows, not arbitrary raw finite-tree pointwise inequalities.

For a fixed unmarked rooted topology of size `s`, select at most `cap−1` children per required labelled edge; there are at most `(cap−1)^(s−1)` maps per physical apex. Summing **over all** decorated census columns and both discount splits still counts each map once. Therefore the topology mass cap is `(cap−1)^(s−1)/LO`, including extension-only types. In a port group there are at most `s` representative roles, each with at most `cap−1` child donors of reciprocal at most one half, or one mother donor. The implemented `s*cap/2` factor safely caps either group; it is loose for mother. Total is a separate group. If `r_i=c_i−A_iᵀy−B_iᵀz` and the rounded upper multipliers satisfy `z≤0` for minimization, then for the group with physical bounded sum `U_g`, `Σ r_i x_i ≥−U_g max(0,max_i(−r_i))`. Subtracting this once per group yields a valid lower bound for the **physical occupation** even if the matrix relaxation alone does not enforce those group caps. Negating the objective then reversing the certified bound is correct for the upper side. In particular, inspecting only the residual of a representative decorated census, rather than all columns in its group, would not suffice. The companion checks actual grouping on finite counts and exact residual envelope corners, without certifying any floated multiplier set.

**Use of `--unpaid`.** The two geometric exclusions in this flag are valid against the size-12 and size-20 reserves (degree seam is automatic at supported `cap≤16`). The flag does not remove overlaps with the present-time P4 and Yb masks already paid in the freeze. At cap 4, take degrees `(4,4,4,4)` and arities `(1,2,1,0)` for `P4`, or `(3,0,1,0)` for `Yb`. Both have one exterior child and minimum size 14; the first fits the published root P4 band, the second fits the paid Yb mask, and the flag selects both. To get an increment over `e911a1c`, carve out existing paid present-time supports (including the complementary P4 portions), or replace the matching prior payment with one under identical accounting. A contrast is not itself a pair of positive reserve payments. At caps 3 and 4 the broad paid `S3` apex condition `k≥4` cannot be met, so that particular `S3` overlap is absent here.
