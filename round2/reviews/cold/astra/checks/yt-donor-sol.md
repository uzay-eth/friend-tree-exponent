## Births, clocks, and support

- A full mark is a physical apex, a selected trunk, its **unordered** pair of distinct tips, and one exterior incidence. The trunk must be promoted before either tip; selecting the older tip first gives exactly one chain of prefixes for a chosen pair. Each existing prefix survives a promotion, and the crown and side-donor extensions at that event are separate alternatives. The same physical newborn cannot fill both roles in one mark. The labelled fixture checked all 100 admitted histories of the specified trunk, tips, possible extra donor and other apex child, including births before, between and after tip registration. Exchange of completed tips also exchanges any tagged attachment and the two tip slopes; equal census is not a second occurrence. No tip swap is used before completion.
- At its actual promotion the selected side donor has `(D,k)=(2,0)`, not the census of a virgin resampled at crown completion. Its receiver is its physical parent. The donor gains at `L+1/D_receiver` plus its actual outside-child reciprocal sum in `[0,k/2]`; its promotion rate is `L/D`. The fixed donor-to-receiver `1/D` acts in both the receive successor and the donor reward. After crossing from 3 to 4, use `[0,1/4]`, preserving the attained geometry; real degree and arity cannot fall or reset. The same slot cannot also be charged as an unknown donor. Untagged traffic is bounded adaptively; no independence of physical pressures is assumed.
- Omit the founder and all founder-mother apex births before applying the virgin mother-bin argument. The number of those apex births by time `t` has expectation at most `t`, since founder promotions occur at rate at most one. All paid absolute rewards per such apex are bounded by the fixed crown degree and arity caps, including donor-incidence multiplicity. Their stationary contribution vanishes, without conditioning on future hubhood. For a nonfounder degree-two mother with a promoted child, she had to be virgin before that promotion. Before that birth the promotion hazard is `1/2` and the total hazard leaving the virgin state is at least `3/2` (gain from her leaf at rate 1, plus the promotion). The physical birth-Palm mass of this bin is at most `(1/2)/(LOW+3/2)` by discounting its one possible birth. After promotion, if she remains degree two, both neighbours are cores; her gain is `1/D_apex` plus her own parent's send in `[0,1/2]`. On crossing, or for a mother born into the 3+ class, her send remains in `[0,1/3]`. The solver mixes the endpoint bounds with the **same allowable range** of birth-bin mass, choosing adverse endpoints, then multiplies nonnegative value bounds by the respective stable-age endpoints. This does not require knowing the disintegrated exterior law of either class.
- The complete planted apex fringe contains the four tagged cores, their exact ordinary leaf stocks, and `m=Σk−3` disjoint extra child subtrees. The lower counts are `(size,cores)=(Σ(D−k)+2m,4+m)`. A registered side child of census `(d,k)` contributes at least `d−k+2k=d+k` vertices and `1+k` cores in its own branch, so adds `d+k−2` vertices and `k` cores to those bounds. Use the attained minimum degree and arity at overflow, not the latest unobserved values; no maternal vertex contributes.
