# Cold audit: component marks, real ages, and siblings

**Verdict: holds in the stated modes, conditional on the imported local limit and birth-Palm identity.** The continuous stable-age lift has genuine total variation in `(centred parent age, normalised child age, entire tree)` even for the `N`-dependent tree. The sibling assertion at the level of the limiting intensity is justified by coupling to the *whole* preassigned proposal process, not by posterior independence under the physical parent. Exact degree-ratio TV to continuous Pareto and quenched empirical real-age TV are impossible. I also give a core-degree tail argument below that supplies the additional empirical uniform integrability requested by the sibling statement, using the existing mean-core upper and a.s. amplitude results.

## 1. Physical and stable-age targets

At fixed cutoff, each counted component other than a possible founder component is indexed by its top core, whose original degree is `≤N`, with a parent of original degree `>N`. The boundary test also detects any internal deletions; the top never belongs to another counted edge. A fixed finite observed type, with parent degree, is a countable local cylinder after checking original degrees around its finite boundary. The total count is obtained by the bounded indicator at the top, so countable-type convergence plus mass convergence gives graph-only TV. The possible missing founder affects this law by `O(1/M_n)` where `M_n` counts components; its actual parent mark is not defined.

In the continuous lift, at prospective parent age `t=L+y`, `L=log N`, retain only promotions after `b=√L`. Replace `D(t)>N` by the history-at-`b` event `E_b(y)`. A bounded joint test of `y`, child age `t−r`, and the entire endpoint can be inserted **before** compensating the birth at `r`: for each retained birth the age argument is deterministic given `t,r`, the early mark is already measurable, and the future internal arm drivers are fresh. Conditional on the history at `r`, the rate of the first downward input separating the inert branch is `≤e^(y−b)` on `E_b(y)`. Upward sends change the parent but do not change that source arm. The branch size mean is `≤(5/2)e^(t−r)`, because its gain rate is `m−1/D_top+1/D_parent≤m+1/2`; if ordinary branch size is at most `N`, every original degree within it is at most `N` even counting the top slot. These give a coupling on the **entire finite endpoint tree**, not merely a fixed-radius statistic.

The resulting product intensity uses Lebesgue age on `[0,t−b]`, not an arbitrary age coupling. Replacing this interval by `[0,L]` in the integral costs at most `|t−b−L|/L` in the dual norm over tests bounded by one: on the common interval use exactly the **same age and the same kernel**. This is why TV survives an `N`-dependent conditional `P^0` kernel; merely proving close ages and comparing their trees at shifted times would not suffice. Similarly, the limiting substitution in the parent factor only changes its mixing weights, not the `y` supplied to the test. The high event replacement uses Cauchy–Schwarz with promotion-count moments; the integrated threshold argument requires no density of `W`. Domination at both `y` tails follows from `E D_t²≤6e^(2t)` and rate-one proposal count moments. Transforming `y+log w` on the positive-amplitude event gives weight `w^μ` and mass exactly `A=E W^μ`. This answers the normalization question in the stated sequential limits.

### Singularities and what exactly converges

- At fixed `N`, the exact `D_parent/N` lies in the countable set `{j/N:j>N}`. A continuous Pareto law assigns that set zero; probability-law TV distance is **one**. Retaining the ordinary tree does not reduce that lower bound. Substituting `log R_N≈y+log W` is for bounded Lipschitz tests in this coordinate, not arbitrary joint indicators.
- The quenched empirical law of continuous ages on any one finite physical tree has finite support, even when the birth clocks themselves came from continuous time. Its TV distance to the diffuse age lift is **one**, using that support as the measurable test set. If ages are taken from deterministic integer labels (or logarithms of size ratios), their union over finite sizes is countable even before averaging. Neither obstacle applies to the *defined* continuous stable-age lift. This does not rule out empirical weak age convergence; it is not needed for the assertion audited here.
- Multiple components sampled independently with replacement have the exact conditional product of the **normalised** empirical measure. To get the limiting product at fixed `N`, use the deterministic limit and the bounded product-TV inequality; no samplewise physical branch independence is needed. If the outer tree is random, averaging conditional products is not the product of averaged marginals at finite size. Draws without replacement differ by at most `m(m−1)/(2M_n)` for fixed sample count `m`. Identity sharing is a separate issue: the exact two-draw collision probability is `Σ_x k_x²/M_n²`, where `k_x` counts surviving children and the founder convention is handled separately. Since `k_x≤c_x`, `π c=2` by end-directed transport and empirical means are `2(C_n−1)/C_n`, the ordered degree tails are uniformly integrable at order one; hence `max_x c_x=o(C_n)`. The collision probability is `o(1)` at fixed `N` with `d_N>0`. In fact the available `max c=O(log n)` estimate also suffices. None of these estimates is uniform for a growing observed cutoff.
- The latent infinite-future amplitude `W` is not an early mark. Replacing it by an early approximation only pays a Lipschitz error (localize away from zero when taking a logarithm). No TV theorem for that joint mark follows. A function of the tree such as log-volume is not a new independent mark; weak convergence to shared age is consistent with this.

## 2. Why the sibling proof really works

Split the parent's rate-one vertex clock into a unit proposal process for promotions, accepted with predictable `b_r`, and separate core-to-core channels with the original rates. This reconstructs the physical generator: successful promotion has total rate `L_r/D_r`; the conditional target is uniform among current leaves. After the early time, assign fresh independent internal inert drivers to *every* proposal, including rejects. At an accepted proposal the drivers can be used in the physical branch by fresh strong-Markov clocks at promotion; upward outputs may feed the parent and alter **later** acceptance. A child's drivers can be indexed without looking at whether future proposals are accepted. In particular the replica law is independent of the proposal times, although physical accepted births conditioned jointly on their histories need not be.

Here is the critical uniform comparison. On `E_b(y)` let `B` be the union of (a) a rejected proposal, (b) a first later downward input separating any accepted arm, (c) a cutoff loss in any accepted branch. Summing the conditional intensity/branch probabilities gives `P(E_b∩B)≤O((1+L)^2e^(A_0−b))` on a bounded window. Integrating this bound only on `E_b` is enough. Both whole factorial sums are bounded by the same proposal-count power; Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the difference *as a measure* by its `2m` moment times the square root of the bad-event probability. Exclude earlier children by the factorial estimates for disjoint proposal intervals. For the replica process, now **remove** the mask of no error; do not assert that the independent marks remain independent given successful physical coupling. Conditional on the early history its unmasked factorial mean is the product of the independent marked Lebesgue kernels. This yields independent inert drawings conditional on one shared `y`, and then under the parent intensity one shared `(W*,E)` in the asserted mixed modes. The left-tail bound still uses `e^y` times the square root of a proposal moment, and the right tail is a polynomial times `e^(−μy)`; normalizing by `L^m` is correct for every fixed `m`. Ordered tuples have factorial weight, not an extra factor `m!`.

An exact directed-arrow fixture gives each of two symmetric arms positive conditional probability `5/22` to gain in the next physical jump conditional on no parent gain, but the joint probability is zero. This specifically uses fixed jump-count conditioning. Independently, even for autonomous arms with already drawn degrees `2/3`, conditioning on a short upward-output likelihood proportional to `c+1/D_a+1/D_b` gives a weight matrix with determinant `−1/36`. Thus conditioning on an endogenous output can break independence even if the driver prior is a product. Neither example attacks the fresh-proposal proof, which replaces rather than factors the conditioned paths.

## 3. Finite-n tuple normalisation and a way to close the moment gap

On one tree, counting edges gives parent probabilities proportional to `k_x`, counting ordered distinct sibling tuples gives probabilities proportional to `(k_x)_m`, and sampling a uniform eligible parent followed by a child gives equal parent weights. Independent parent draws also do not define the sibling law. For surviving counts `(0,1,3)`, the large parent has probability `3/4` in the component law, `1/2` among eligible parents, and one in the ordered-pair law. Similarly mixing finite trees after normalizing each separately need not agree with pooling their raw counts. The empirical tuple assertion cannot be inferred just from `π(c^m)<∞` and bounded local convergence. If one samples after a deterministic cutoff on parent core degree, local convergence gives normalization immediately, and removing that cutoff *afterward* gives the intensity in the marks note.

**Empirical moment extension, using a.s. growth inputs.** For the uncapped graph-only tuple counts use `C(t)~X' e^(μt)` a.s. with `X'>0`, `E C(t)≤K e^(μt)`, and `max_x c_x(t)=O(t)` a.s. Core amplitude (`fringe/almost-sure.md`) · Mean upper (`fringe/renewal.md`) · Maximal core degree (`fringe/hub-spectrum.md`).

Let `F_a(T)=Σ_{x core} e^(a c_x)`. Its conditional drift is at most `(e^a−1)F_a + e^a·C` because a promotion increments exactly the promoter's core degree and creates one new core of degree one. Other jumps do not change `F_a`. Fix an integer `M≥1`, and retain the compensated martingale jumps on slab `[j,j+1)` only while the **pre-jump** maximum core degree is `≤M(j+1)` (use this predictable indicator also for the compensator). A retained squared jump of `F_a` is at most `K_a e^(2aM(j+1))`; expected total promotion rate is `O(e^(μt))`. Choose deterministic `a=a(M)>0` with `2aM<μ` and `e^a−1<μ`. The discounted retained martingale has finite total expected quadratic variation since `Σ_j exp[(2aM−μ)j]<∞`; hence it converges a.s. On paths with `max c≤M(j+1)` eventually it agrees with the full discounted martingale up to a fixed additive constant after some finite random time. Taking the countable union over `M` covers a.s. all paths.

Put `G(t)=e^(−μt)F_a(T_t)`. Its drift is bounded above by `−δ G(t)+e^a e^(−μt)C(t)` for `δ=μ−(e^a−1)>0`. Variation of constants gives an upper bound by the decaying initial term, a bounded convolution of the source, and `∫₀ᵗ e^(−δ(t−r))dM_r` for the full discounted martingale `M`. Integration by parts bounds that last term in absolute value by `2 sup_r |M_r|`. The source is bounded along the path by a.s. amplitude convergence. Thus `sup_t e^(−μt)F_a(T_t)<∞` a.s., with a path-dependent positive `a`; dividing by `C(t)e^(−μt)→X'>0` proves a.s. empirical uniform integrability of **every fixed polynomial** in core degree. The estimate uses promotion intensity, not total physical birth intensity. The founder is a finite initial term and obeys the same promotion rule.

The resulting UI makes the fixed-`N` graph-only tuple empirical law converge in TV, by truncating counts at the parent, proving each finite cylinder and the mass, then removing the truncation. For fixed `m`, the positive tuple mass for sufficiently large fixed `N` follows from the stable-age asymptotic. This does not supply empirical TV in real ages or justify interchanging `n` and `N`. The additional proof here is analytic; the finite replay does not verify the assumptions of its mean and a.s. amplitude inputs.
