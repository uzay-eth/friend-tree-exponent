## Conditioning and the first loss

1. **Pin the date and the stock.** Given the whole colour word, the probability of choosing the blue mark on blue selection `j` is `1/(b+j)`, even conditional on earlier avoidance. Hence `F` is independent of the colour word (and has the same kernel for every beta parameter). Condition on `θ`, `F=f`, and the physical past just before that blue choice. There are `R=1+S` red good leaves of the mark; `S` includes the red string immediately preceding blue choice `f`, so there are `f+1` geometric counts. A mark send hits red with probability `R/D`; on a hit each individual red leaf has probability `1/D` before conditioning on the hit. A miss can be augmented with an independent uniform dummy. For each possible leaf the mixture is exactly `1/D+(1−R/D)/R=1/R`, even at `R=D`. On a miss the deletion is only from the **lower bound**: the tree and future drivers are unchanged. On a hit only the selected leaf is initially damaged. In both cases all other red families start good.
2. **Expose only the genealogy needed for the charge.** Each red step picks a uniform red sender; the family is inherited from that sender, regardless of physical recipient. Conditional on the exposed past and the selected founder the sequence of charged/uncharged classes has the binary Pólya law from `(1,R−1)`. Finite word probabilities give the beta mixture and iid class indicators given `W`; after a class draw the within-class sender remains uniform. The leaf selection uses no future target or blue driver. Thus taking `W` as a latent parameter is safe even after a hit, which affects the starting tree but not the future colour/family/arrow driver kernels. The actual *initial lost-family* moments conditional on the tree are those of the charged family times `R/D`; the proof uses the conservatively charged share throughout. For `R=1`, the charged share is exactly one even on a miss and the lower payoff is zero.
3. **Condition on the family share before bounding residual loss.** The continuous density for `S=s≥1` is `s(1−w)^(s−1)`, so normalized posterior `S−1` is negative binomial with `f+2` geometric summands and parameter `z=θ(1−w)`. The future strings before blue choice `j>f` contribute `j−f` independent geometric counts of uncharged reds after charged reds are erased, with survival `z/(1−θw)≥z`. Hence the stock is at least `1+NB(j+2,z)` in stochastic order, whose exact mean reciprocal is `(1−z)[1−(1−z)^(j+1)]/[z(j+1)]`. To bound damage one may first fix the full future colour/class **schedule**, not future within-class sender identities or targets. Subsequent blue mark hazard stays `1/(b+j)`. A good uncharged sender reproduces a good leaf; a bad one cannot increase the expected bad fraction, since senders within that class are uniform. Charged-red steps cannot spoil an uncharged good leaf: only the blue mark can do that. Multiply expected deficit by the uncharged share `z`; bounded convergence for the stock fraction and Fatou give the conditional limit bound. This uses no product-of-expectations assumption between bad fractions and stock. Markov is applied to **subsequent** loss at each `(θ,f,w)`, with failure event `Z≤x`, so the strict payoff uses the threshold `z−x>0` and is set to zero at equality. The midpoint log integral is an upper bound because `1/[v(b+v)]` is convex on `v>0`; replacing `j+1` by `j` has the same direction.
4. **Check the cap atom before separating the variables.** PGF averaging gives scaled survival `(1+a/b)^−(f+1)` strictly below the cap `bθ/(1−θ)`. The clipped tail mass is `(1−θ)^(f+1)` and becomes the atom at that cap, always above the integration cutoff `t=b(θ−x)/(1−θ)` for `θ>x≥1/2`. Replacing the clipped variable by the unclipped auxiliary variable therefore preserves the payoff, *not* the full physical law. Only after this replacement does its law no longer depend on `θ` at fixed `f`, allowing the independent exponential lower comparison for `t`.
