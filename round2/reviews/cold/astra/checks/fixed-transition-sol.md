## Signed placement, all targets and the external sender

At old size `n=v−1` write `d_u` for prebirth degree, `r_u=Σ_(w~u)1/d_w`, and `h` for the physical future tail from a common final state. If first target is `u` and second is a distinct old `w`, the second rate is `r_w−1{u~w}/[d_u(d_u+1)]`: the new leaf sends only to `u`, and changing `u`'s degree changes its outgoing reciprocal to **every** old neighbour. The sign is old endpoint minus late endpoint. The two orders therefore contribute `(r_w/[d_w(d_w+1)]−r_u/[d_u(d_u+1)])H(u,w)` on an unordered edge, where `H` is the difference of final leaf tests. This includes core–core and leaf–core edges, with no independent pair approximation. Off-edge rates cancel after reversal; repeated old targets cancel by exchangeability, regardless of their exact second rate.

If the second jump hits the first newborn, its sole possible sender is the external parent `u`, now of degree **`d_u+1`**, and its exact coefficient is `r_u/(d_u+1)`. Its final middle has one fresh leaf and a parent of degree `d_u+1`, so its initial red mass is `1+1/(d_u+1)`. The tip starts with red mass `1/2`, because the middle is degree two, regardless of `d_u`. Each old-edge endpoint is a leaf, with red `1/(d_parent+1)≤1/2`. These red masses are the *actual* reciprocal attachment rates in the common final tree; a later send from the tag to the old parent remains an outside arrow. In the leading sector `d_u=1`, the coefficient is `r_u/2`, and the old exterior seen by the middle is precisely the old exterior seen by the one-birth tip: replace the prebirth leaf by the ported degree-two parent. The extra initial leaf at the middle is stock, not an initial inert branch.

For unsigned control before applying endpoint tests, the absolute oriented-edge coefficient sum at `u` is `r_u/(d_u+1)` and the chain coefficient is the same. Charging its own rows plus its leaf neighbours to each old core gives at most three: at most two for the core and at most `L_o/d_o≤1` for its leaves. Counting both endpoint probabilities gives at most six. This works for arbitrary high degrees. Normalization after the two jumps is exactly `n(n+1)`; the tower property does not compare tails from different final environments when pairing the two old-target orders.

## One moving-tilt argument for the tip and middle

Use seed size `m` and initial red `a∈{1/2,3/2}`, `b=m−a`. At reference parameter `θ`, with `u=1−θ`, let independent `G_f` be the geometric number of tag wins before outside jump `f`. At outside block start put `R_f=a+Σ_(h<f)G_h`, `B_f=b+f`, `Δ_f=R_f−r_f`. The reference has iid Bernoulli choices and the *physical conditional* outside-arrow law with denominator `B_f+Δ_f`. The exact block factors are `(1+Δ_f/B_f)∏_(j<G_f)(1−Δ_f/(R_f+j))`; the same finite-prefix upper and dominated-submeasure lower sandwiches as the interior proof apply at outside stopping indices. These stops are finite under both laws: physical outside hazard before the next one is at least `b/j`, whose accumulated sum diverges on an endless perfect block. Red gains preserve `Δ_f`, outside gains never decrease it. All tag-origin arrows, including the ones selecting the old parent, fit under a Bernoulli envelope `J_f` of independent parameters `1/(b+h)`, drawn with fresh uniforms. In particular `Δ_f≤1+J_f` and `EΔ_f≤2Σ_(h<f)1/(b+h)` for both leading seeds (old parent first increases at rate at most two while unchanged, and costs at most `1/2`; tag clock weight one). Early changes before `εm` outside draws cost `O(ε)` even given the whole red schedule. There is no need for a probability estimate relative to the physical endpoint probability here: these are under the conditional reference law, and finite-prefix likelihoods have bounded moments there.

The following block estimates work for **all** sequences `mθ→∞`, allowing `θ→1` as well as `θ→0`. ```


On each dyadic block telescope the reciprocal increments after bounding the weight by its endpoint; summing gives `O_ε((1+H)^2/(mθ))`, using `u≤1`. Blue errors are `O_ε((1+H)^2/m)`. The red main term is `−ΣΔ_f log(R_(f+1)/R_f)`. Replace the logarithm by `log(1+1/f)+e_(f+1)−e_f`; summation by parts uses the monotone variation of `Δ`, leaving summable dyadic errors `O_ε((1+H)^2/√(mθ))` (decay `2^(−k/8)`). ## Rare tail prefactor and signed error, including endpoints

With `y=mx≥1`, substitute `t=x+(1−x)z/m` in the Beta integral. Uniformly over `0<a≤3/2`, the remaining integrand `(1+(1−x)z/y)^(a−1)(1−z/m)^(m−a−1)` is bounded by `(1+z)e^(−z/2)` and bounded below on `0≤z≤1` for large `m`. Gamma ratios give `Q_(m,a)≤C a y^(a−1)(1−x)^(m−a)` down to `a→0`; at either leading `a` this is an equivalent with coefficient `1/Γ(a)` as `y→∞`. This controls rates even when the raw weighted sum to all large-degree parents is of order the entire size: the **prebirth** target rate `r_u` is charged with `a=1/(d_u+1)`, so the product is at most one per core. A prebirth star gives core target probability `(n−1)/n`, which shows why this reciprocal cannot be dropped. Nonleaf-parent births are therefore down by `(vx)^(-1/6)` relative to the leading single-birth scale. For the *signed* sum use a different bound: the nonleading middle has `a≤4/3`, everything else `a≤1/2`, and the absolute endpoint charge is at most six per old core. The same-size beta quotient is bounded by `C (mx)^(-1/6)` (also uniformly as `x↑1`, since the extra `(1−x)` power can only help). This is an error on the rare signed scale itself, not an `o(p_v)` error obtained by subtraction. An initial rare trigger near `s=0` is dealt with by the reference early-change probability and its finite-window UI, not by neglecting paths on their raw physical probability scale.

For the leading weights, local convergence and the bounded score allow averaging via the deterministic-size empirical law and core-count UI. In both sums the common quantity is `S_v=EΣ_cores (L_o/d_o)g(old side)`, with `S_v/v^μ→E[X]π((L/d)g)>0`. In the middle score the extra factor `1/2` comes exactly from the external parent sender; any normalization using `d_u` instead would fail already at an old leaf. The two Beta densities at sizes `v,v+1` share their blue parameter and satisfy `f_(v+1,3/2)(t)=2vt f_(v,1/2)(t)`. The substitution above shows `E(Θ−x | Θ>x)=O((1−x)/v)`, so the beta survival quotient is `2vx(1+O(1/(vx)))`, uniformly even near one. Hence the two leading physical terms are

```
p_v(x)        = Q_(v,1/2)(x)       (S_v+o(v^μ))/(v−1),
p_v−p_(v+1)   = Q_(v+1,3/2)(x)     (S_v+o(v^μ))/(2(v−1)v).
```

Dividing proves the gap ratio tends to one along every sequence `vx→∞`. For upper-edge gluing alone, the published upper-edge relative adjacent quotient plus `x→1` also suffices (it keeps the factor `1−x`, even for superexponentially small `1−x`). For bounded `vx`, apply the compact birth-scale signed result at `y=(v−1)x`; it includes the hub probability at `x=0` by the right-continuity squeeze, and has positive infimum on every finite compact. If there were arbitrarily late failures anywhere in `[0,1)`, take their sequence: `vx` either has a bounded subsequence or has a subsequence tending to infinity. Both are impossible. Equivalently one can split a convergent threshold subsequence into the zero, interior, and one limits and use the corresponding published results. At `x=1` strictness is impossible and is excluded. This does not resolve early labels, identify a numerical cutoff, or give a uniform relative approximation on the bounded birth scale by the formula `x p_v`.
