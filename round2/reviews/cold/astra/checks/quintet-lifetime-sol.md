## Charge, real clock, and seams

The archived replay accepts `ASTRA_QUINTET_AUDIT` with the same layout as the producer (`occupation-quintet/lifetime/README.md#evidence-and-replay`). It replaces the historical inode restriction with a full SHA-256 check of the selected snapshot; size, exact headers, receipt digest and unchanged-metadata checks still apply.

Write `c` for core degree including the mother on a nonfounder, and `L=D−c`. An incoming arrow from a core sender has rate equal to **that sender's** reciprocal, so the real gain rate is `L+ρ`, `0≤ρ≤c/2`. Promotion changes `c` at fixed `D`, with rate `L/D`. In ticks, its charge includes `f(D,c+1)−f(D,c)+f(2,1)+g((D,c+1),(2,1))`. This pays the newborn edge value once, on the singleton, with the actual post-promotion degree and arity. There is no bud term when `L=0` (no read of illegal `c+1`).

For centered rows (`D<180, 1≤c≤26`) subtract the exact outgoing transfer `(ρ−c r1/2^30) Δf` from the uncapped physical singleton drift. Each incident edge gets its one-neighbour contribution in the accepted allocation. The charged row then uses the center, but its physical Bellman gain and holding still use `L+ρ`. For every other row there is no such transfer and the slope is the actual signed `Δf`. The `c=0` census is a legitimate founder row but not a Palm newborn; the payment starts only at `(D,c)=(2,1)`. Both signs match the independent directed rate/transfer identities at zero, an interior receive and the upper corner. The checker detected replacing a noncentered receive by a frozen center, including bare arity overflow `(27,27)`. At bare states there can still be gains from core neighbours, despite zero stock and zero promotion. The audit covered `D=179/180`, exact arity overflow below 180, and bare cases on both sides of `1999/2000`.

At degree 1999 the old potential and bud use tabular singleton values, while the **gain** reads the analytic singleton at 2000 with unchanged arity. For the newborn edge at `D≥180`, interpolate between infinity and the tail knot at `min(c+1,27)` with weight `180/D`; do not clamp the actual `c` in `L`, `ρ`, or the analytic singleton. With `x=1/D`, `q=1−cx`, write analytic `f=A+Bq+Mq²+Eqx`. A gain replaces `(x,q)` by `(x/(1+x),(q+x)/(1+x))`; promotion replaces `q` by `q−x`. Computing the increment directly in powers and cancelling the factor `x` from the gain numerator gives a polynomial `P=(1+x)^2 F` at each receive endpoint. The checker reconstructed every exported census row and all 13 fixed-arity stock bands, including the `c=0` comparison, with exact Bernstein bounds. The bands are finite in arity; the all-stock ceiling below has a separate proof for higher arities.

## Lower lifetime and all-stock upper comparison

Use `F=cap_f+inward_row`, with `cap_f=0/−1053`. The original cap is in the *opposite* sign convention and implies `F≥0`. The apparently more favorable upper analytic cap `−6509` is not substituted into this global account. Within the retained census the cross-multiplied Bellman inequality is affine in `ρ`; using the same variable in reward, gain continuation and holding proves it for every adapted physical environment from its two corners. Round both reward coefficients down for the lower price (up for the upper ceiling): `ρ≥0` makes this operation valid even for a negative slope. The value is then rounded in the same direction on the fractional tick grid `10^-12`. Promotions proceed in increasing `c` at fixed `D`, and gains in increasing `D`, so descending sweeps are valid. The code checked both integer quotients and selected exact unrounded residuals across the noncentered, bare and degree-2000 joins. A move out of the arity mask still costs its holding rate; its continuation is zero, and the degree exit keeps the old arity and takes the separately proved suffix.

For all `d≥D_exit`, hold `c` fixed and use both exact polynomials on `[0,1/D_exit]`. As `P(0)≥0`, for `x>0`, `P(x)/x ≥ (P(x)−P(0))/x+D_exit·P(0)`. Its Bernstein minimum, clipped at zero using nonnegativity of `F` and divided by `(1+1/D_exit)^2`, gives `F≥K_c/d`. For the upper comparison use `P(0)` and the nonnegative Bernstein maximum of `(P(x)−P(0))/x` as separate upper coefficients; the positive denominator can only lower their sum. Both receive corners bound the row throughout the interval.

For a value `h_c/d`, the gain drift is exactly `−(L+ρ)h_c/[d(d+1)]`. Since `d−c≤L+ρ≤d`, its relative loss lies between `(D_exit−c)/(D_exit+1)` and 1. The lower recurrence uses 1; the upper recurrence uses the smaller loss and adds a constant value component, with no gain cost for that component. The two promotion corners are `1−c/D_exit` and 1; checking them for each component proves the claimed sub-/supersolutions for all degrees, not just sampled ones. One downward/upward rounding at the seam retains the needed inequality. Values are bounded; discounted stopped Dynkin then has a vanishing time boundary.

The missing upper reward at `c>64` is bounded separately. It takes at least 64 promotions after nonfounder birth, and actual promotions are a thinning of a rate-one proposal clock. At a common exponential age of rate `μ`, the proposal count has geometric tail `(1+μ)^−64`. The original verifier bounds finite `f,g` ticks by `16S` and centers by `1/2`; I independently checked analytic `f∈[0,2S]` using the pinned coefficients. At `d<2000` the gain contribution is at most `1999·32S` even at the analytic seam. The absolute bud contribution is at most `64S` and the trial term at most `16S`. For analytic gains, `|∂q f|≤64S`, `|∂x f|≤16S`, `|Δq|≤1/(d+1)`, and `|Δx|=1/[d(d+1)]`, so the gain contribution is at most `80S`. Adding the cap in either case leaves a uniform bound `65000S`. Therefore `65000S(1+LO)^−64` covers the omitted arities, **only in the ceiling**. The finite-degree cut alone would not establish an upper control.

## Stable age, sign switching, and exact results

The physical age law averages the actual nonfounder newborn process with factor `μ` and discount `μ`. Since `F≥0`, a lower price uses the exact original upper endpoint as discount and multiplies by the exact original lower endpoint; the ceiling does the reverse. The founder gets no birth credit but remains within the original capped residual and potential comparisons. Equivalently for the lower payment, normalize the finite-tree discounted birth sum by the mean core resolvent: the normalized birth count tends to `μ`, and bounded reward allows taking the discount down to `μ` uniformly in the actual birth environment. This settles the use of an adapted live mother, without replacing it by an independent or fixed receive clock.

The original signed residual caps aggregate to `err=19186/19783` ticks per core: `cap_f+max(0,cap_g)+higher_per_core` is positive in each witness. The nonfounder reserve is paid once. Normalized integration by parts in the original potential gives `sign·(μ−λ)·mean_potential ≥ reward_floor−err`. The limiting initial term vanishes by the physical mean input, and the comparison bounds carry through normalized integrals. With negative net use `m_min`, and with nonnegative net use `m_max`, since the quotient is minimized over the positive comparison interval. With zero reserve the replay recovered both original exact endpoints using `m_min`; both new lower prices have positive net and switch to `m_max`.
