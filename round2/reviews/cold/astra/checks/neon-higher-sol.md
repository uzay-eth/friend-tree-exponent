## Drift ownership (distinct from potential allocation)

I traced the original GMP verifier (`attacks/neon1/round2/cert/cert_b3f_verify_gmp.c`), [motif definitions and transfers](../occupation-neon/check/neon-proof-pin.md), and the higher extractor (`occupation-neon/higher/extract.py`). On each labelled connected embedding keep `−λq`, ordinary gains at its vertices, and existing-slot promotion increments. A new motif with at least two vertices has a unique old owner: delete the newborn tip, leaving its promoter in the connected old set. The new singleton is instead owned by the promoter's `f`. The gain rate from a core is its **sender's** inverse degree. When routed, replace that rate in the smaller row by the appropriate receive-direction center; the larger row adds exactly the displaced increment. Unsupported transfers stay at the actual rate in the smaller row. This leads to the following per-row ledger (letters refer to vertices, not distinct descriptors):

| Charged row | Receives from smaller rows | Owns at a promotion |
|---|---|---|
| `hV(a,b,c)` | delete `a` or `c`, leaving `g` with receive vertex `b` | `Yb` at `a` (new sibling is the short branch); `Yt` at `b`; `P4` at `c` |
| `hC(a;b,c)` | delete either child, leaving `g` and receiver `a` | `S3` at `a`; `Yb` at either child (that child is now the long branch) |
| `P4(a,b,c,d)` | delete `a` or `d`, leaving `hV` at the adjacent receiver | none |
| `Yt(a,b;c,d)` | delete `a` leaving `hC`; delete either fork tip leaving `hV`; receiver `b` for all three | none |
| `Yb(a;w,b,c)` | delete `w` leaving `hV` with receiver `a`; delete `c` leaving `hC` with receiver `b` | none |
| `S3(a;b,c,d)` | delete each distinct child leaving `hC` with receiver `a` | none |

In `hV`, an external parent at the top or an external child at the bottom routes to `P4`, an external child at the top to `Yb`, and a second child at the middle to `Yt`. In `hC`, an external parent of the apex routes to `Yt`, another child of the apex to `S3`, and a child of either tip to `Yb`. All require the target's actual domain, not merely that the motif topology exists. The GMP newborn lookup tests the **post-promotion** descriptor; the old portion of the row tests old support, and gain or promotion that exits support gives zero successor weight. `Yb` short and long branches must not be symmetrized, while repeated physical siblings in the symmetric motifs still give separate deleted incidences. In the signed row, an unrouted donor with coefficient `d` has floor `min(0,d/2)`; replacing this floor with `d/D_source` for known donors improves the same row, not a second payment. The top's unrouted parent send is the same `r` across its top-owned rows. If routed, the correction belongs to an enlarged ancestor-owned row and is not included in this focal sum. These assignments correspond to the six interior GMP blocks at 1375–1435, 1528–1584, 1642–1684, 1729–1774, 1826–1868, and 1912–1956.

The disjoint decomposition is by **charged row**, not by arrow or birth event. The same event may affect several rows; a transferred increment is subtracted once and added once. The verified `f` price contains its own newborn and centered terms, not its displaced credit as a second term; a `g` price is not included in this combined numerator. The six other families are counted once at their top core. A size-12 cut only changes which of those rows are retained; any suffix program on `size>12` must still be checked for overlap with other suffix programs or `g` credit. Above 12 the Bellman reward and continuation vanish; the generic charge extractor can still return a nonzero row there. No claim of validity for the interior formula at arbitrary size is needed: when `n≤12`, the parent slot makes `D_root≤n`, interior vertex degree is at most `n−1`, and gains stay strictly below 18. Narrower motif cutoffs remain active.

## Occupation and composition

The projected complete descendant fringe has exactly one unmodelled incoming arrow, from its parent; ordinary outward arrows to the exterior do not alter the projection but may change the future parent rate. The projected rate is therefore adaptable, not independent. In the Bellman inequality the same `r∈[0,1/2]` appears in `a+br` and in the root-gain transition. After multiplication by the positive denominator the inequality is affine, so both endpoint checks suffice even after downward rounding. On all monitored births the initial fringe is the same virgin; stable-age discount `μ≤HIGH` and nonnegative reward justify using `LOW·V(J)`. One can alternatively perform the birth compensation under finite-tree Laplace occupation and take `s↓μ`, as in the accepted first-body audit. Dropping the founder and the nonpaid row families is justified by B's global signed nonnegativity, not by the small fringe; no convergence of a signed unbounded generator under the stationary law is required.

The denominator pays **potential**, with a different allocation. `f` owns itself, and `g`, `hV`, `P4` and `Yb` go to the bottom. For a fixed bottom of `Yb` its parent and grandparent are fixed; there are at most `k_grandparent−1` eligible short siblings. Multiplication by this number must occur **before** taking the maximum over supported grandparent descriptors. `hC`, `Yt`, `S3` go to the branching vertex with exact pair or triple multiplicities; in `Yt` this is the middle vertex, not the top used in the higher-order drift. The unique mandatory bottom edge may have a negative ceiling; optional bottom slots include zero. Grouping mandatory pair/triple occurrences does not require zero at supported apex descriptors, but the unsupported infinity control for `hC` contributes zero. Actual tail slots are nonnegative Bernstein convex combinations. The bottom maxima may be combined at each fixed control (no joint attainment required); then the allocated vertex's weights can be used exactly for the deficit or replaced by maxima for the all-degree cap.

For $`L>L_F`$ the `f` value is exactly the clamped one, and the degree-dependent grouped remainder lies under the same tail ceiling; bounded inner apex families cannot switch back on. For $`k>K_F`$ the vertex score is the overflow constant, the Bernstein arity is saturated, and no apex charge remains. For fixed parent flag the un-recharged cap in that overflow row is thus independent of the true arity. The recharge $`t(k-h)`$ with $`t\le0`$ is largest at $`k=K_F+1`$ in that class; using that representative would be invalid for $`t>0`$. Across the actual physical tree $`\sum(k-h)=0`$, including the founder. Consequently $`\Phi\le MC-\sum_{v\ne o}\text{deficit}_v`$ after omitting all out-of-cut deficits, with all retained deficits nonnegative. The exact starter census and frequency discount argument apply here too. The residual nineteen-label prefix is an alternative lower bound on the **same** deficit; its normalization follows from $`\ell JG_\mu b=\ell K_\mu\mathbf1=\ell\mathbf1`$ and $`\nu(b)=\mu`$. Taking a maximum is valid; adding is not. I reproduced the ceiling from the selected physical-census recharge on each side, without sweeping all 51 recharges or replaying the weaker residual prefix.

For composition let $`\epsilon=+1`$ for lower and $`-1`$ for upper, with original trial $`\lambda`$. If the earlier first-body calculation proved a numerator credit at least $`v_f`$ and used denominator ceiling $`D_f`$, its displayed outward endpoint $`e_f`$ gives the conservative credit $`|e_f-\lambda|D_f\le v_f`$. This conclusion uses the documented derivation, not a claim that an arbitrary previous exponent bracket yields a numerator. Add the cap-12 credit to that conservative amount and divide by **one** common improved denominator. Applying a new rate correction as though $`e_f`$ were itself a new certified pointwise trial would be invalid. Here the exact fractions and final outward rounding give the composition in the verdict. The checker also reproduces the denominator note's *older-numerator-only* endpoints `[0.565674816769, 0.565773546690]`.
