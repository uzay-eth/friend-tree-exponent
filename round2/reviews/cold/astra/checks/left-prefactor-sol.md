# Cold audit — the fixed-size left-tail prefactor

**Verdict: accept.** The prefactor proof (`open/limit-law/prefactor/README.md`), blob `4937b7130c0d3881fea7c22d1b71d62760301222`, proves `P(X≤x) ~ 2 E[Y_∞^(−1/μ)] x^(1/μ)` for the genuine embedded-chain limit, and the claimed conditional departure-size law. No counterexample to the unconditioned large-star limit, uniform inverse-moment estimate, or random size normalization was found. The proof uses the previously reviewed [degree-sensitive gradient and stopped passages](left-tail-sol.md), the spatial mean estimate (`fringe/spatial-limit.md#1-start-in-one-old-cell`) and the inert limit (`open/forest-age/inert/README.md`). This audit checks their use; it does not recertify the fringe renewal kernel.

## 1. Entry clock and unbiased departure mixing

At size `j`, a star survives the next selection with probability `(j−1)/j` and leaves at size `j+1` with probability `1/j`. Hence `P(K=k)=2/[(k−2)(k−1)]` starting from the size-three path, which is the star of size three. On the first departure the promoted vertex is distinct from the hub; the leaves are exchanged by automorphisms, so the full embedded stopping history gives the same unlabelled seed `S_k` for every exit at size `k`. Its hub degree is `k−2` because promoting an old leaf preserves that degree. At every `k` the continuation is restarted with actual size `k`, not with the size of its promoted branch or a new counter starting at zero.

There is no independence of physical departure time and exit size. In fact conditional on `K=k` the time since size three has transform `∏_(j=3)^(k−1) j/(j+r)` at exponent `r`: the independent clock at deterministic size `j` is `Exp(j)`, until the embedded winner produces the specified departure. At `r=1` the conditional transforms for `k=4,5` are `3/4,3/5`. Alternatively the hub's initial rate-one ring is independent of its leaves' Yule driver, but the number of leaves attained at that ring is time-dependent. The submitted mixture uses the exact embedded law and never detaches these two entry variables.

## 2. Live returns, ideal marginals, and uniform amplitude transfer

An exact decomposition of physical arrows gives these three effects: a hub-to-leaf arrow promotes that leaf and makes a virgin; hub-to-core sends add to the chosen branch and may break its coupling; a branch-to-hub send adds a hub leaf and is a null choice only *inside* its own branch. All ordinary interior degrees, including the branch root's live parent edge and the corresponding ideal inert slot, agree before failure. A coupling can draw a fresh independent ideal branch stream at **every** ring, even on rejection; share it physically only when a real virgin is created and agreement still holds. The ideal stream is not chosen by success, its incumbent streams never condition on future success, and the common hub clock continues to be rate one. After failure the marginals can continue with independent fresh future drivers. This is not an assertion that the physical branches are mutually independent conditional on past or future accepted births.

**Concrete success-bias attack.** In the exact two-proposal experiment of the checker, a degree-two virgin's tick either promotes its leaf or sends to the hub, each with probability `1/2`. If the latter occurs, the hub degree goes from `d` to `d+1` while its core-neighbour count stays one; otherwise it remains `d` with that same count. At the next hub proposal, conditioning on agreement makes the return probability greater than `1/2` by `1/[2(2d²−1)]`. This counterexample is to the success-only law, not to the submission's unconditional coupling. The physical rejection rate also grows with successive hub promotions if there are no gains, so even a constant per-ring rejection approximation is not exact. The valid bound integrates `(1+P_(u−))/(k−2)` until first failure and uses `E P_u=u`, yielding `(t+t²/2)/(k−2)` without weighting the ideal law by survival.

For the infinite-time transfer there are three independent error estimates. First, the degree-sensitive squared jump bound (`open/limit-law/left/README.md#2-conditional-variance-and-a-uniform-negative-moment-start`) gives `E|Y_k−M_t^k|²≤C e^(−μt)`: the deterministic-time integrand is bounded by `e^(−2μu) const·E C_u`, and the spatial mean gives `E C_u≤const·2e^(μu)` uniformly in `k`. Paying the total Yule vertex rate instead would lose the required uniformity. Second, the bounded-time forecast at horizon `h` differs in `L¹` from `M_t^k` by `const·e^(−(μ−σ)h)` using the uniform free mean remainder from the physical state at `t` and the same core estimate. This is a forecast comparison, not a pointwise bound between the eigenfunction and the current core count. Third, at fixed `t,h` the two coupled marginal forecasts differ in expectation by at most the normalized count disagreement at time `t+h`. To see this, condition both random counts on the **same** paired filtration at `t` and apply conditional Jensen. At every current state, including after a coupling failure, each marginal's projected rate is its ordinary (physical or ideal) rate. The past success record belongs to that filtration, but the future drivers do not. Thus the forecasts in this argument are the actual Markov forecasts. The two-founder Yule second-moment bound and failure estimate make this third error vanish as `k→∞` without any bound on ordinary leaves.

For the ideal limit, its initial arm converges in `L¹` and Campbell's formula bounds the normalized contribution of ages born after `R` by `const·e^(−μR)` uniformly in the observation time; the same bound holds for the limit series. For the pre-`R` arms dominated convergence under the arrival intensity gives `L¹` convergence to their discounted independent amplitudes. This proves the ideal `L¹` limit, finiteness and positivity. Conditional on the ideal history at `t`, its forecast tends to `E[Y_∞|F_t^ideal]` in `L¹` as `h→∞` by contraction. The latter tends to `Y_∞` by the upward theorem. Given any 1-Lipschitz test, use the coupled forecast inequality at `t,h` plus all the above errors; the bounds do not depend on the test. The order is `k→∞` first, then `h→∞`, then `t→∞`. In particular no claim is being made that a bounded-time coupling stays valid until all branches reach their limits.

## 3. Size gate, uniform inverse moments, and random total size

Take the large-core threshold `K₀` for inverse moment order `2/μ` *before* choosing a waiting-time exponent `r>1`. At the earliest two-core seed `S_4` (the path), the initial promotion rate is exactly one; using a uniform instantaneous gap after departure is false. The correct first gate waits only for the total size to reach `K₀+3`; each of the at most finitely many waits has rate at least four. At the size gate, if `C<K₀`, there are at least three leaves and the claimed `1+δ` gap holds until each next promotion (including across all nonpromotion jumps). In fact one may use the stronger `7/6`: if there are three core endpoints they pay `3/2`; with two endpoints the core is a path, and a third leaf at an endpoint pays a total of at least `7/6`, while an interior leaf pays at least `4/3`. For a conditionally lower-bounded intensity `λ`, the survival function to the next promotion is at most `e^(−λt)` from the current whole history, so the MGF bound at `r<λ` is `λ/(λ−r)`. Take the product by conditioning **at actual intermediate stopping times** (pad with zero waits if the core gate is reached early). It is valid even when the waiting times and gate shapes are dependent. After both gates, use the uniform large-core moment at the full stopped history and the shift factor `e^(r·delay)`. This yields the claimed uniform moment of order `r/μ` for `Y_k`, with `1<r<1+δ≤7/6<2`. The large-core input itself iterates stopped lower-level passages of the eigenmartingale, not repeated tests of the same eventual limit; the degree-sensitive bound controls its conditional variance and the uniform jump bound prevents skipping a level.

Starting anew from a free tree of size `k`, the total rate is `m` at size `m`, independent of the particular shape. Conditional selection of the winning directed arrow and the holding time is independent. The entire size driver consequently gives a `Gamma(k,1)` limit `W_k` independent of the size-embedded `X_k`. This would not work with the inert slot rates for a branch; those rates do not enter the free size normalization. In the **same** process, `Y_k=(W_k/k)^μ D_k`, `D_k=k^μ X_k`. No independence of `W_k` from `Y_k` holds or is needed. `W_k/k→1` in probability by its variance `1/k`; by Slutsky, the large-star weak limit is the same after dividing by that scale. For the inverse moment, use Tonelli with the independence of `W_k` and `D_k`. The gamma factor has expectation `k^r Γ(k−r)/Γ(k)`, finite as `k≥4>r` and at least one by Jensen. This transfers the uniform bound in the correct direction to `D_k`, without any inverse smoothing or deconvolution. A fresh independent size draw paired with a measured `Y_k` would instead give a wrong inverse moment even in a two-point toy example.

The bound of order `r/μ>α=1/μ` makes `D_k^(−α)` uniformly integrable and implies finiteness of the analogous limit moment by truncation/Portmanteau; positivity of the ideal amplitude excludes a singularity at zero on its law. The exact adversarial control below additionally shows that a **bounded critical inverse moment** plus weak convergence need not give the prefactor. The stronger estimate is genuinely used here; it is not a consequence of bounded-time coupling.

## 4. Summation and prefactor

Put `h=x^α` and make the exact departure sum into a step integral with strips `[kh,(k+1)h)`. The strip density has multiplier `2/[h²(k−2)(k−1)]`, which tends to `2/u²`. Its CDF factor is `F_k((kh)^μ)` and tends to the claimed `F_∞(u^μ)` off a countable set by squeeze at continuity points. No atomlessness claim is needed. On each strip `kh≤u≤(5/4)kh` for `k≥4`, and the uniform inverse moment of order `r/μ` bounds the CDF factor by `min(1,M(kh)^r)`. Thus a single integrable envelope `const·u^(−2) min(1,u^r)` applies, both with and without a bounded continuous multiplier in `kh`. In the limit, Tonelli integrates `u^(−2)` from `Y_∞^α` to infinity, giving `E Y_∞^(−α)`; the coefficient two comes entirely from the star departure weights. The same step argument identifies the conditional departure-size limit without interchanging a small-tail conditional law with a finite-`k` postdeparture limit. The Poisson Laplace representation follows by independence of the initial arm, independently marked Poisson arrivals, and the change of variables `v=q e^(−μs)`; integrability at zero follows from `E V<∞`.

It checks physical source-arrow maps, the unbiased ideal mixing requirement and recurrent hub rejections; tests the distinct entry-time transforms and gated moment factors; and gives an exact normalization mutant (`16/7` versus `1` for the erroneously redrawn inverse moment). At `h=1/M` with `M=j²` the rare atom starts paying at `k=j`, and the exact scaled mixture has an extra asymptotic contribution `M/[(j−1)(j−2)]→1`. Thus the weak-limit prediction `2` is wrong for this family: the actual scaled left tail along that sequence tends to `3`.
