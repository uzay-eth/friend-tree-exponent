## Renewal and Tauberian failure attempts

- The old child has a core parent slot, and its only omitted physical input is the parent's directed gain. Cancelling the copied child term against the child's death leaves exactly this gain difference; the centre has no parent slot and supplies positive immigration. A promotion is not itself a gain of the promoter's degree. The tilted path weights do not move on leaf gains. The independent arithmetic scans **5,047** `(D,c)` rows: the largest normalized drift is `313/870` at `(3,1)`, with margin `1/4350` below `9/25`. For `D≥100`, `1/16 < (9/25)(7/10)` closes the entire tail. Stopped Yule moments, together with the positive weight lower bound, are enough for the backward/convolution argument; it does not require a strong operator derivative on the weighted supremum space.
- For positivity of the newborn kernel, charging every isolated mark `1/j` is enough even without a mass at 0: a nonzero continuous nonnegative function cannot be supported only at the accumulation point. Compactness is in mark, by the original-particle survival factor `exp(−at)` and uniform equicontinuity. At the real crossing the original particle supplies positive forcing and the centre supplies positive immigration, so the singularity cannot cancel for any cumulative reward. Strict positivity of the difference of residues comes from positive occupation at **exact degree k**, not from attempting a Tauberian theorem on that nonmonotone count.
- I tried the lattice failure explicitly: `S(x)=2^floor(x)` is positive and increasing, has a simple pole at its real abscissa, but its normalized function oscillates. Its transform is `(1−exp(−z))/(z(1−2exp(−z)))`, with genuine poles at `log 2+2π i m`. The standalone excludes this by using the no-intrinsic-event newborn's absolutely continuous birth-time component at mark `1/2`, not merely positivity of reproduction. At any fixed nonzero frequency, equality in the modulus comparison would force a varying exponential phase to be constant on an interval; full support of the positive left eigenmeasure is what makes the comparison equality pointwise. At this point the compact Fredholm argument excludes eigenvalue 1, not merely an eigenvalue of modulus one. Local analytic extension at **every finite frequency** gives local pseudofunction boundary behavior, which is all the invoked Debruyne–Vindas Wiener–Ikehara form needs. No bound uniform in frequency, uniform pole-free strip, or assertion about the resolvent at infinity is required. Explicitly, after compact frequency localization the analytic boundary values are locally uniform limits and define an integrable compactly supported function; Riemann–Lebesgue applies. The nondecreasing time statistic is the one-sided Tauberian hypothesis.

## Local law, age, and flux

- For the infinite-volume construction, only old-edge proposals carry information between cells. After loop erasure and keeping the **last** `R−k+1` steps, an influence path from outside the old `R`-ball to any cell in the old `k`-ball has a witness of exactly the length used in the bound. Reversing simple paths gives the factor `c_w^0`; no degree bound on the full old tree is implicit. Boundary degrees need an extra radius or retained slots. Both the path event shared across truncations and the Yule second moment are needed: one does not get continuity of an unbounded cell sum from an agreement probability alone. Countable fixed-horizon stabilization gives the physical transition mechanism; alternatively the Feller semigroup identity can be proved on finite completions and then passed to the limit, as in the source review.
- The two-sided time tilt uses `(log F₃(t))/t→μ` to force `w(0)/Z→0` for fixed positive tilt; it does not assume a nonzero sharp coefficient in this step. Birth-time moment estimates are uniform in the newborn exterior and use a rate-one dominating promotion count, not a stationary local law. The endpoint reversal controls the size-biased bad event via `c`, not `D`. This matters because the proof never obtains a uniform first moment of `D` and the eventual degree tail need not even have one. For a fully formal path construction it suffices to state that cell drivers, with fresh future Poisson increments, furnish the same predictable intensities and Markov kernel at local stopping times. All tests used in age and flux can then be obtained by finite-cutoff compensation on bounded cylinders plus localization and the cell moment estimate.
- In the renewal identity use `S_a`, which tracks the **same** born root; using `Q_a` would count later descendants several times. The old-root contribution disappears after scaling because it is bounded, not because its own environment grows slowly. The newborn age is independently exponential only in the mixture representation, and the newborn retains its live promoter. Supremum domination by the normalized Yule process is valid at *random* hitting times `T_k`; deterministic-time uniform integrability alone would not justify that passage. The first and second moments from two give the bound `24` for the Yule maximal second moment. The positivity event uses disjoint source clocks at the newborn and its tagged leaf; afterwards the conditional leaf inequality follows via the integrating factor and finite-horizon integrability. For a quantitative sanity check it gives `Eβ W≥2e^(−2)(1−2/e)>0`; no independence from the infinite exterior is needed. `A` is finite, positive and attached to the identified degree marginal even if other local observables depend on the subsequence.
- At level `k+1≥3`, newly promoted vertices contribute no crossing. Gain at a level-`k` vertex is `k−c+ρ`, with incoming `ρ=Σ 1/D_sender`. The active rate is bounded by `k`, so the cell formula can be integrated at any fixed positive time, with no derivative interchange at infinite degree. The exact asymmetric eight-vertex fixture gives crossing rates `4/3,7/4,10/3` at levels `k=2,3,4`; deleting `−c` gives `7/3,15/4,13/3`. From the cumulative tail the flux inequality first yields a polynomial lower bound on `p_k`; only **then** can one divide the exponential core-degree exceptional-set probability by `p_k`. For example, splitting at `c=(3+μ)log(k)/θ` even gives `E[c|D=k]=O(log k)`, so conditional crossing rate divided by `k` is `1+O(log k/k)`. The standalone's fixed-ε sandwich is sufficient and avoids needing this refinement. A positive rational law in the new fixture has cumulative constant 2 but distinct even/odd pointwise constants `5/2` and `3/2`, confirming that tail differencing is not a substitute for flux.
