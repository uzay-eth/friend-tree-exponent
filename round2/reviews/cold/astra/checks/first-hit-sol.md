## Hazard domination on physical trees

Use the labelled common/private parity coupling from the cited parity proof. A paired site's smaller-side neighbours are exactly the common ones (plus the inert slot, if present); the larger copy has `k` additional private neighbours. A private branch can never reconnect, and the infected common set stays connected because a clean vertex can first acquire a private leaf only by a surplus arrow from an infected common neighbour. At its infection that neighbour is unique, and the gap is one. At the initial insertion there is no infected common neighbour. Thus `(1,1)` covers both starts, even when the trees have already grown before the conditioning time. This uses reachability under the parity coupling, not just equalities of degrees in arbitrary pairs.

For a fixed clean outward target with an existing common edge, only the source's rates enter the difference: `1/B−1/(B+k)`. The total surplus on *all* real common outgoing arrows is at most `B` times that difference, i.e. `k/(B+k)`; this includes later-formed neighbours and repeats as an upper bound. Only arrows into clean neighbours increment `i`, and the tagged arrow is separated as an absorbing channel. In contrast, a repeated contact into an already infected neighbour can increase that target's gap and later cause extra returns. Those returns must be retained in `k` growth: each infected common neighbour supplies at most one per unit time of surplus, and each private neighbour at most one. Other arrows either preserve these counts or only advance `B`; no replacement of the real root clock by a suppressed or fixed-rate clock is needed.

One rigorous construction uses independent marked Poisson measures for the three classes (gap increment, non-tag infection increment, first tag) and a residual channel for the other physical arrows, with a conditional choice of labelled successor when a class fires. At each pre-jump state the class intensity is bounded by the appropriate rate at `(K,I)` because `B≥d` and the three upper rates are monotone in their count arguments. Threshold thinning then preserves coordinate order while both are alive, and a physical tag would force a dominating tag at the same clock if no dominating tag has occurred yet. The comparison tag can occur earlier and then stop, which gives the claimed ordering of the first-hit transforms, including infinite times. Rate bounds apply to predictable, history-dependent actual inputs; no independence of the physical counts or of successive first hits is used. Both the physical and dominating systems are nonexplosive by their size/linear-rate bounds.

## Why `b` agrees; which arrows the cut removes

Until the top first becomes infected, both top degrees and neighbour sets coincide. Equal top degrees **alone** would not suffice: an unmatched promotion of a common structural leaf would change `b` without adding a new neighbour to the top. In the actual coupling that leaf has only the common root as an incoming source, and its root arrow is synchronized while the root is clean. Old core neighbours never become leaves. The initial discrepancy is at an old core, so it cannot infect a root structural leaf at time zero. The checker deliberately makes the common-leaf promotion one-sided and breaks equality at identical root degree in 200 checks.

The initial path of cores stays common. Reconnection is impossible, so each next first infection on the path comes from its predecessor, or there is no root infection. At a nonseed infection the source may already have one infected neighbour but not two; the initial-degree factors remain valid because degrees never decrease. Conditional iteration of the first-hit transform therefore yields the product, without a product-of-independent-times assumption. After the root infection, `|b_A−b_B|≤1` for the remainder, giving `1/s` times the discounted hitting probability.

For the frontier, lift the modified process to full trees by suppressing only each arrow **from a real core child at depth `h+1` into its parent at depth `h`**; hidden activity can be kept in this lift and need not be simulated in the projected integral. At the boundary keep the real child slots and whether they are core or leaf. A leaf must still send to the boundary at rate one and may become a core by a boundary-to-child arrow. That promotion preserves the boundary degree and its status changes the future cut. An ordinary inward parent send from above remains, as do the other real arrows needed by the projected process. If using rate-one clocks, a suppressed selection is null and does not reallocate probability to retained targets. Our fixture gives omitted directed mass `1/3`, then `5/6` after promoting an old frontier leaf; the initial retained inward gain is `1+1/4`. It rejects cutting all below-boundary sources and cutting the outward promotion instead.

My checker reconstructs all finite-degree values via complementary loss, using a flat reverse-topological grid and integer down-rounding, and compares a small exact forward visit sum with the backward first-hit result.
