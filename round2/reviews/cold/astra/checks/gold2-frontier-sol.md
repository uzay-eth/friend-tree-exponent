## Singleton source and original cap

Use one reward on `f`: `R_f=cap_f−P_f`, where `P_f` is the original signed outward row at the original nominal `λ`. Centering is part of this original charge; the test potential's generator must still use actual physical gains. A promotion keeps the mother's degree, increases her physical arity, and starts the daughter at `(2,1)`. Thus for a cut-24 occurrence at `(D,k)` with `k=c−1`, the birth factor is the true column coordinate `b`, while the root mark is `(D,k+2)` at the promotion. A pooled root must bound every actual mark represented by its cell. In particular the degree sentinel is not a real degree, and marks in its arity-saturated cell must use a root valid for the entire degree tail.

A root value `V` in ticks per newborn enters the stationary expression as `π(bV)`. Since `η(b)=1`, a particular η-average of `bV` is an average per birth and must be converted to π units once. It is not added to the old conditioned singleton floor, and the producer's external H3/FY floors, already per core, do not acquire a birth factor. Subtract `E=cap_f+max(0,cap_g)+H` once; in this accounting the original singleton cap appears inside `R_f` and cancels that same cap in `E`. A separately certified reduced correction or lifetime price on `R_f` cannot also be paid.

For positive inward trial displacement `a=d(s−λ)`, let `U` be the upper host comparison of the same original stationary potential and `M_global` the original global upper comparison. With `h` the original local higher-cap allocation, the signed trial lower bound has constant `external−E−a M_global` and per-core lifted objective `π(bV+H−h+a(M_global−U))`. Reconstruct the same `h` and use the cap saving once, not as an additional physical H3 reward. A newly proposed lower H3 value on the old 2417 support replaces the matched lower value only after a separate audit. Distinct extra floors need explicit charge ownership and pairwise exclusion on actual physical masks.

## Restricted trial and sparse verification scope

An original global prior gives a uniform bound. Under an adverse hypothesis for a stronger trial, use `[LO,s]` on lower or `[s,HI]` on upper. Any lifetime test specialized to that interval must use the appropriate discount bound for the **same** hypothesis. When checking a restricted trial, a strictly positive margin rules out the adverse hypothesis; dividing that margin by a denominator to claim an even stronger unconditional endpoint is not justified. One-sided transfer of a lifetime floor is valid if the discount and birth-normalizer assumptions used to certify it are weaker than the final adverse hypothesis. Sparse signed samples are a check of the physical reader and selected rows, not a substitute for a universal bound on unpaid or omitted states. A conditional interval must name the inherited certificates and scope of any unreplayed singleton solver.

## Lower compact live sibling and upstream (separate revision)

Write the original outward row as `P=A+∑b_iρ_i`, and set `B` to its all-port adverse upper envelope. For each distinct exterior neighbor of the mother, let `z_j=max(0,b/2)−b/D_j≥0`, where `b` is the original mother slope. Untracked ports are still adverse, so `P≤B−∑z_j`; the sum may contain the unique upstream neighbor and each other child individually. If `C(z)=[z−B]_+`, the incremental function `C(z)−C(0)` is superadditive for `z≥0`: for `B≤0` it is exactly `z`; for `B>0` it is `[z−B]_+`. The revision omits the box and replaces the old threshold floor; the `D_mother=80..100` seam is disjoint from the compact `19..79` mask.
