## 1. Degree-sensitive free gradient

Reconstruct the free coupling (`open/limit-law/README.md`) from directed arrows: a common source sends into each common target at the minimum of the reciprocal source degrees, and the excess goes to the smaller-degree source's copy. This is the parity-favoured copy of the target. An arrow from a private branch back to its attachment point can only grow an already-infected vertex. Degrees do not decrease, the common graph stays a tree, and the infected set stays connected. A clean vertex's first infection has exactly one private leaf and exactly one infected common neighbour. A genuine leaf target already has that same parity rule; the first infection can change its core indicator immediately. Use the established uniform jump bound for `d=1` and enlarge the resulting constant to cover it.

At the original seed with common degree `m(t)>=d`, private count `k(t)>=1`, the **aggregate** surplus on its common edges is `m(t)(1/m(t)-1/(m(t)+k(t))) = k(t)/(m(t)+k(t))`. It includes the new paired neighbours. Its private-edge sending rate on the larger copy is the same fraction; private-account first promotions are a subset of those sends. Before the first common surplus `τ`, the killed private count has growth drift at most `k` (only private neighbours can add to it; all common neighbours have equal degrees). Localization, followed by Yule domination, gives `E[k(t) 1_{τ>t}]<=exp(t)`. Both discounted charges before `τ` are therefore bounded by

```text
I_d = ∫ exp(-μt) min(1,exp(t)/d) dt
    = d^(-μ)(1/μ+1/(1-μ)) - 1/((1-μ)d).
```

For the surplus this is the compensator of the first send; for private accounts it pays the first promotions *before* that send. After `τ`, the remaining seed private promotion charge is at most `exp(-μτ)/μ` in expectation. Counting promotions when they actually occur avoids charging an already large private population as though it were one newborn at `τ`.

**Attack: a post-time-zero common branch may already have many cores at its first mismatch.** Do not cut common branches at the initial seed. Leave each paired seed neighbour eligible until its own first infection. At that time the branch is clean except for the new private leaf at its root: every other route from the infected set would have to cross that same edge. Preserve its entire existing common outward graph in the recursion, and cut only future ordinary attachments at the infected root (including its incoming private leaf at the cut). Repeat for every noninitial infection. Clean uncut portions cancel in the two core counts; branches cut at a retained vertex cannot reconnect and cannot produce an unowned mismatch except through their own already-infected anchor. In particular any infection brought on through a common edge *born after a noninitial anchor's infection* stays in that edge's paired accounts, paid on each side from its first promotion; it is not a retained child. This is the point where applying the `.84` contraction to all new edges would be false. The delayed rule is only at the seed, where all first outward infections cost at most one per unit time regardless of degree. At each nonseed anchor, every eligible child edge already existed at the anchor's infection, exactly the condition of the old-edge certificate (`influence/check/README.md`). Cut-account promotions occur at their actual first promotion times and the free spatial bound applies in the full active exterior; old clean core descendants are never priced as a one-core cell at a mismatch.

The expected `μ`-discounted nonseed retained mass is bounded by `(1+1/μ) I_d/(1-.84)`: one possible infection at `τ`, then seed intensity at most one, followed by the certified contraction for nonseed descendants. At discount `.525` the seed costs at most `1/.525`, regardless of the delayed cuts, so the retained singleton discrepancy at horizon `t`, after normalization, is `O(exp(-[μ-.525]t))`. Each nonseed retained anchor pays at most `2/μ` in discounted branch promotions (one unit intensity per side); the conditional spatial bound multiplies this by `C_Q`. The seed private accounts pay at most `C_Q(1+1/μ)I_d`. Passing to the normalized means therefore gives the advertised uniform `D d^(-μ)` gradient. This argument needs the conditional contraction and the free spatial estimate, not independence of adjacent branches.

## 2. Second moment and downward passages

The intensity to a leaf is the reciprocal degree of its only neighbour, and their sum, from any seed with a core, is the live promotion rate `b=Σ_core L/D <= C`. At a core the incoming rate is at most its degree, so each core contributes at most `D² d^(1-2μ) <= D²` to the squared jump rate (here `D` denotes the gradient constant, `d` the vertex degree). Thus the rate is at most `2D² C`, including huge one-core stars. Localize the discounted eigenmartingale at finite size; the bracket bound and `E C_t <= C_Q C exp(μt)` give a uniform integrated second moment `2D² C_Q C/μ`. Remove localization by nonexplosion and the square-integrable bracket; the martingale closes in `L²`. At a stopping time the same future variance bound applies in the restarted clock, or with its square multiplied by `exp(-2μσ)` in the original clock.

For `C>=K`, use `Φ>=aC`, and on descending levels `aK 2^(-j)` take the first entrance at/below each level (or the infimum of strict entrance times). At a finite passage take the right-continuous value: on a continuous crossing it equals the level; on a jump the loss is at most `D exp(-μσ)` and `M_σ>=aK exp(-μσ)`. If the level is `l`, then `l <= (1+D/(aK))M_σ`; `K>=4D/a` gives even `M_σ>=4l/5`. No jump skips the next level and the next passage needs a decrease of at least `M_σ/3` using the looser `3l/4`. The strong Markov property and the conditional maximal inequality applied to the increment martingale give a bound `9V/(a²K)` (the author's `16V/(a²K)` is safe), also valid with the starting level `aK`. This is a statement about a future excursion at an actual stopping time, not independent failures concerning the same terminal limit. Multiplying these bounds over levels strictly above `y` gives the uniform power bound up to a finite `K`-dependent prefactor. Choosing the power strictly above the desired negative-moment order is necessary. This step proves any prescribed finite order only on seeds above a suitably large threshold, not at arbitrary one- or two-core seeds.

## 3. Attacks on the time from the star

From `T₃`, the centre's identity never changes before the first promotion, and its next rate-one initiation necessarily promotes. So `τ_*~Exp(1)`. Before that point the leaf stock is a Yule process from two, driven separately from the centre clock; its expected value conditional on departure at `t` is `2 exp(t)`. Thus even the exit size is not independent of `τ_*`. In the jump chain the sole promotion source of a size-`j` star is its centre; the survival product is `∏(j-1)/j=2/(n-1)` and the pre-departure size has mass `2/[j(j-1)]`. Following a promotion of a size-`j` star the instantaneous rate is exactly `(j-2)/(j-1)+1/2`. At `j=3` this is one. Even arbitrarily large physical paths have rate exactly one, because all interior cores are bare except the two endpoints, each with one leaf. This disproves a strict uniform gap based just on having left the star, or based just on being large without controlling `C`. These shapes can arise via positive-probability finite path histories.

With `2<=C<K` and size `>=K+2`, there are at least three leaves. Every endpoint of the core tree must carry at least one leaf. With three core endpoints the rate is at least `3/2`. Otherwise the core tree is a path. If one endpoint has two leaves the total is at least `7/6`. If neither does, at least one interior core carries a leaf and has core degree exactly two, giving total at least `4/3`. Thus the submitted size gate `K+3` is safe, and its `1+min(1/6,1/K)` can in fact be replaced by `7/6`. A path with its third leaf appended to an endpoint core attains that bound. A path at size `K+1` has `C=K-1` and rate one, so the sharper gate `K+2` cannot be reduced in general.

The additional wait after `τ_*` to hit both gates is controlled first by the finitely many holding times for the size gate, with rates `n>=4`, then by at most `K-2` further promotions. The conditional survival of each latter wait is at most `exp(-7t/6)`: the killed indicator has drift at most `-7/6` times its survival indicator until that next promotion, even with changing shape. A uniform conditional `r`-moment of the exponential of the wait is therefore bounded by `(7/6)/(7/6-r)` per promotion, by tail integration. Iterate at stopping times, padding with zero waits if necessary. For example `r=13/12` works for every `K` using the sharpened gap. There is no change of measure here and no conditioning on the future waiting times. Rates exactly one before reaching the size gate are harmless: the time spent before that finite size is reached has an exponential moment for `r<4`, even on runs staying bare. These bounds cover all stock/shape histories, not just a typical star exit.

Restart at the two-gate time `σ` and set `Y=exp(-μ(σ-τ_*)) Z_{S_σ}`. Tower at `σ` gives a uniform conditional bound for `E[Y^(-r/μ)|F_{τ_*}]` by multiplying the uniform restarted negative moment by the conditional exponential moment of the delay. Choose `K` for negative-moment order `2/μ` first; `r<7/6<2` suffices. Neither the exit state nor this future factor is independent of `τ_*`. Conditional Markov gives `min(1,M_r x^(r/μ) exp(r τ_*))`; integration against the actual exponential marginal split at `log(1/x)/μ` is at most `(1+M_r/(r-1)) x^(1/μ)`. No critical exponential moment of `τ_*` is taken, and this integral has no extra logarithm because `r>1`.

## 4. Fixed-size law and the endpoint

The `T₃` time origin gives `Z=W_size^μ X` with the Claim 6 normalization `Φ=d₂Ψ`. `W_size` depends only on the size holding times and is independent of the jump chain and `X`; it need not be independent of `Z`. On `W_size<=B`, `X<=x` implies `Z<=B^μ x`, giving the fixed-size upper bound after division by the fixed positive probability `P(W_size<=B)`. For the lower bound the exact finite star history at size `n` has probability `2/(n-1)` and conditional `E[X|T_n]` equals the gamma ratio `Γ(n)/Γ(n+μ)` times `Φ(T_n)=O(1)` there. Choose `n` of order `x^(-1/μ)` with the mean at most `x/2`, and apply conditional Markov. This does not require that the process eventually leave the star independently of its size.

The upper bound tends to zero and directly excludes an atom at zero (which is nevertheless in the support). Integrating `p x^(-p-1) P(X<=x)` near zero gives finite negative moment exactly for `0<p<1/μ`, with logarithmic divergence at equality. Using `<` in this identity instead of `<=` is immaterial under integration. Divergence at equality is not evidence of an endpoint atom. Clock smoothing of the continuous-time variable does not imply atomlessness of the fixed-size law.
