## Birth window: which successful paths get paid

The timeout applies to a finite real-time retry algorithm, not to all eventual hubs. At positive tracked stock at least one leaf supplies a rate-one birth channel; actual tracked loss and other tag gains can be counted as decisions. At a last real loss the newly promoted neighbour is the actual degree-two sender. While its degree is at most 31, until the tag gains or the sender steps up, it has the specified send channel at rate at least `1/31`. Degree increments count and the race stops at 32; a different tag gain also ends the race successfully. The parallel leaf has at most one preliminary race of the same type. The serial old-neighbour bonus has instead one preliminary race with the initial tagged-leaf birth channel at rate one, even if the old neighbour is very large. Keeping the original continuations after the first decision realizes the whole lower endpoint, including the serial bonus, rather than paying the baseline and bonus as two separately timed promises.

Entering as a leaf or a degree-two serial middle, the successful path gains at most 19 times before degree 20. Initial tracked stock is at most one for that middle; thereafter new tracks come from gains, and each loss consumes one. The conservative upper count `19+20+20·32+1=680<700` therefore covers positive-stock stages, initial and retry returns, and the serial preliminary race. It does not count unmonitored exterior rings. Conditional on the past, each decision wait has survivor bounded by `exp(−t/31)` and hence exponential moment at `1/64` at most `64/33<2`. Iteration over the padded sequence bounds the cost of successful paths late at `2^16` by `<2^-324`. No independence of decision waits, or conditioning on eventual success, is used.

At the degree-20 stop the terminal comparison can be realized by the union of survival events of `l≤20` linear families with rates `1,1/20`. These are restart families, not the actual limiting degree mass. For birth rate one and death rate `q`, conditional on being positive at age `t`, the count is geometric on `{1,2,…}` with parameter `(1−q)e^(-(1−q)t)/(1−q e^(-(1−q)t))≤2e^(-(1−q)t)` at both rates used here. Survival implies a positive count at age 128; hence survival with fewer than `D=2^32` particles is bounded by the unconditional probability of positive count below `D`. Across 20 families the error is at most `40D·2^-121`. On physical stock at least `D` **at that age**, apply the remaining-mass comparison `Gamma(stock,1)−Exp(1)` conditional on the physical past; two marginal bounds cost `8/D`. Intersecting with survival does not require any conditional gamma law given survival. A successful remaining mass larger than `D/4` gives true starting-clock mass larger than `(D/4)e^(-(2^16+128))`. The founder-centre case uses two families at death rate `1/2`, no retry time, and shortage at most `4D·2^-64`.

The real size amplitude `S` at post-placement size `m` has gamma shape `m` and `W=SZ` on the same trajectory. Its moment at `1/2` gives `P(S>32m)≤(2e^-16)^m<2^-45` for `m≥3`. No independence of `S` from retries, stock or `W` is needed. With `S≤32m` and `mx≤2^-131400`, the mass threshold is bounded by `2^(5-131400)`, strictly below `2^(30-2(2^16+128))` using `e<4`, and likewise below the founder-centre floor. Thus the strict tail event `Z>x` is obtained even at the displayed inclusive endpoint. The union of the timeout, size, stock and mass errors is below `eta=2^-27` per affected lower endpoint; tip uppers already bound the positive tail. These inequalities apply in every displayed physical exterior, conditioning on the past at each restart, without freezing a future exterior trajectory.

## After direct and suppressed payments

## Hub-ranking bases and telescope

The literal signed seed / star fixtures return a score divided by total serial weight; multiply back before comparing with `c`. The resulting rational numerators exceed `0.18940948`, `0.20181908`, `0.22577249` for degree 1 (seed), 2 and 3 respectively. For a larger star the first-send barrier is `1/(j+1)` with defect `1−D/(j+2)≤0`; on a paired edge the adverse centre's post-placement parent has `j=d−1` other leaves and degree `d+1`, so its upper `1/d` is below `L(2)>1/4`. All old-leaf debits vanish, the centre credit is nonnegative, and the leaves alone give `G(2)/2=189409485129/(2·10^12)>c`. The seed edge gives `(3,4)`, not `(2,3)`. The latter is the exchangeable-leaf comparison at size three, exact lower `127/2440`, and seed symmetry gives the exact tie between 1 and 2.

For fixed `2≤m≤i−1` and `i<j`, each adjacent inequality at the old size `v−1` for `v=i,…,j−1` can be projected onto the **same** earlier `F_m`. Its denominator is `(v−1)v`, giving `c(1/(i−1)−1/(j−1))`; no across-label independence is needed. Nonnegativity of the later hub probability allows `j→∞` without assuming a separate convergence law, and linearity then supplies the harmonic conditional expected count. For an a.s. finite stopping **size** `τ≥2`, apply the fixed-size result on each `{τ=m}` with deterministic offsets `i=m+r`, `j=m+s`, `1≤r<s`: that event lies in `F_m`, and the stopping-history formula follows by partition. This is conditioning on history through the stopping size, not just on a marginal law of `τ` or on future hub outcomes.
