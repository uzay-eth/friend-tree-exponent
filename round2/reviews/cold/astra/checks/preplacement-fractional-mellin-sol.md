## Harmonic boundary and physical comparison

For `H(z)=Σ φ(j)z^j`, the birth generating term is `H'−H/z` and the unit-loss term is `zH` (using `φ(0)=0`). The discounted diagonal is `zH'+(1+p)H`. Thus the ODE in the proof is exact; its proposed `H=e^(z−1)z(1−z)^(−1−p)` satisfies it by logarithmic differentiation. The positive-integer recurrence is valid down to `j=1`, where it reads `φ(2)=(2+p)φ(1)`. In the coefficient convolution the old summands increase with stock and a new positive term enters; each product `P_p(j)=∏_(i=1)^(j−1)(1+p/i)` is increasing in `p`. The Gamma asymptotic of `P_p` and termwise limit `P_p(j−k)/P_p(j)→1` at fixed `k`, dominated by the factorial weights, give asymptotic coefficient one relative to `P_p(j)`.

Tracked leaves produce fresh leaves through their own rings and are lost only at the focal ring, at rate `j/d≤1`. Increasingness therefore makes the exact unit-loss harmonic identity a subsolution for the true stock. At zero, movement can only improve the zero test. Total-size Yule domination gives uniform integrability at fixed and increasing horizons; for stopped comparisons one can use Doob's maximal inequality on the normalized total-size Yule martingale at an order greater than `max(1,p)`. Since stock is at most degree, the discounted stock test has limit superior at most `W^p/Γ(1+p)`; convergence of stock itself is unnecessary. This boundary applies at **any** physical degree and any tracked subset, including the full stock, with no restriction relating the degree to `p`.

The zero-stock return uses the **actual newly promoted** degree-two neighbour, not a generic core. While the focal degree is `d`, the neighbour's growth rate is at most `k−1+1/d`, and its return hazard is `1/k`. The backwards return row decreases in `k`, because its formula mixes `1/(1+pk)` with the smaller next value. A different focal gain also triggers the same fresh-leaf lower state, so an earlier gain cannot worsen the return test. For `v_j=V_p(d,j)` and `g_j=V_p(d+1,j+1)`, start with `(1+p)v_0≤g_1`. For subsequent columns, `v_{j−2}≤v_{j−1}` in the preceding recurrence gives `g_{j−1}≥(1+p/(j−1))v_{j−1}`. The increasing next row yields `g_j≥(1+p/j)v_{j−1}`, exactly the criterion for `v_j≥v_{j−1}`, and also sufficient for `v_j≤g_j`. This proves the physical gain seam at every degree, with no sign interpolation and no `p≥1` assumption.

**At `p↓0`**, only the terminal polynomial is evaluated at zero in the first box; discounts in every lower denominator are still evaluated at its positive right endpoint. In the polynomial extension `φ_0(j)=e^(−1)Σ_(k<j)1/k!` for `j≥1`, with value zero at zero. The physical limiting moment, if expressed at the endpoint by dominated convergence, is `P(W>0)` and is not to be interpreted as `E[W^0]=1`. The lower value from the all-zero algebraic recurrence is an optional continuous limit of the finite test, not an input to the signed bound.
