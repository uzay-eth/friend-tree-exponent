## Fixed-cut law

* A vertex with `k` external slots and physical degree `D` loses exactly `k/D` of its unit outgoing intensity to the cross directions. Each internal arrow has rate `1/D` even after deleting its other incidences. In an ordinary connected side with at least two vertices, every vertex has an internal neighbour and the genuine total is positive. A singleton side with only slots has zero genuine rate and is outside this construction. Product reference waits of rate `n` and normalized genuine arrow choice assign the same arrow likelihood ratio `λ/n` at every completed jump. On the entire interval, including each unfinished wait, physical no-cross survival uses total intensity `λ+d=n`. Hence there is no unfinished-wait factor in the *combined* likelihood. Multiple slots at one anchor cause no independence problem: they are different directed hazards, not separately conditioned uniform choices.
* Finite-horizon likelihood products decrease to `L∈[0,1]`. For any fixed finite-horizon cylinder, use the larger terminal horizons and bounded convergence before extending to the full path sigma-field. The fixed-cut law is a subprobability; it need not put full mass on `L>0` under the reference. For finite slots, `Σ d_i(n)/n<∞` is equivalent to `L>0` by the logarithm test (only finitely many early factors need separate treatment and they are positive). Given the embedded shapes, the centered weighted sums of exponential holds converge almost surely by the summable variance `Σ (d_i(n)/n)^2`; hence the same finiteness criterion holds for integrated reciprocals. Altering finitely many holds cannot change it.
* At a deterministic time or physical stopping time choose a finite cut with a history-measurable rule and use the physical future kernel. If the cut was chosen using future information, only a submeasure obtained by further masking is justified; in particular the distribution of holdings after such selection need not remain independent. A simple control is a mask that tests whether the first wait on one side exceeds the other: retaining only that event no longer leaves unchanged product exponentials even if the original no-cross weight is shape-only.
* The Fubini/dilation step for existence of individual core coefficients is valid with no multislotted-core theorem. On `L>0`, the reference shares the physical full limit for almost every clock vector. Convert to the sum of individually nonnegative normalized counts using the positive total Yule limit. Fix shapes and all future waits beyond the first on each side and choose two valid first waits in the full-measure fibre. Subtract the two sums and solve the asymptotic delay relation with contraction `e^(−µh)<1`: bounded limsup and liminf must agree. At the size epochs this gives convergence of the original embedded coefficient sequence (not just a submesh), and at least one coefficient is positive. Since these coefficients and `L` depend only on shapes, the fixed-cut normalized Gamma proportions retain their Dirichlet law.

## Density thresholds and exhaustion

For all positive coefficients there is one critical point by strict concavity; the tangent Hessian is strictly negative definite there. The critical patch density is proportional to `(max−x)^((m−3)/2)` times a continuous factor with positive limiting value, so boundedness starts at three sides and continuity at four. In particular three positive sides generally have a jump at the maximum. On boundary patches choose a small coordinate and compensate with a coordinate bounded away from zero; the derivative is positive and bounded away from zero there. Along each such coordinate line it is monotone by concavity. The Dirichlet density vanishes at the endpoints/physical faces because `n_i≥2`; regular patch densities extend continuously by zero with a bounded transverse dominating integrand. With zero coefficients combine the zero masses; varying a positive coordinate against the combined zero mass gives a global positive derivative lower bound `µa_i` and the same endpoint argument. No coefficient-uniform constants are implied.

For the fixed number of anchors, the finite staged physical script has uniform positive probability, and the independent embedded lower leaf families have uniformly positive survival probability. On joint survival the cross rates are integrable, so there are finitely many actual sends (use stopped compensators, not independence conditional on survival). Upward convergence of conditional probabilities gives almost-sure existence. At the later integer cut time each side still has at least two vertices because the designated internal edges persist even if a designated leaf has promoted. Taking a countable cover, truncating conditional density bounds and allocating to the first successful retained cut gives bounded-density **submeasures** increasing in covered mass. The ownership masks only decrease the component sublaws. This implies TV approximation; the original argument supplies no integrability gain merely from that cover. The symmetric large-parameter example is a genuine obstruction to uniform bounds over all Dirichlet inputs, not necessarily a physical concentration event.

The new development uses adapted integer-time cuts. Restart a two-stock setup at detectable failure; its defective duration has an exponential moment strictly below one. At a deterministic time the chance of no eligible high-stock edge and the chance of a future cross at the chosen edge are both exponentially small. With total size localized and the existing left-tail estimate paying small amplitudes, the uniform Beta/Jacobian estimate gives a power-law bound on **all Borel sets of small measure**, which is strong enough for the layer-cake `L^p` argument. An interval bound alone would not justify that step. This does not claim continuity or boundedness for the full density.
