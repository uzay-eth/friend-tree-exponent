## Signs and simultaneous host inequalities

With `s_u=r_u/[d_u(d_u+1)]`, a direct edge `u,v` of the old core tree costs at most the maximum of the two directed terms, not their sum; only the side with larger `s` can be adverse. On that side `s_v≥1/[d_u d_v(d_v+1)]`, because the genuine old edge contributes `1/d_u` to `r_v` even if `v` is bare. Thus each direct edge spends at most one baseline `b` plus both directed excess reservations; an unspent excess is fine. The pendant debit has already been subtracted inside `C`, with the explicit final-state leaf stock (`l−1` if the opposite recipient was an old leaf, `l` on core–core placements). Repeat targets cancel by exchangeability, nonadjacent old targets cancel in reverse order, and the serial sector is assigned to the old attachment recipient using its **post-first-birth** reciprocal rate. There are no hidden costs for the deleted ordinary leaves.

A high internal host of degree `d≥10`, core degree `c`, and stock `l=d−c` pays direct vouchers `F=.00356` and chain bills `D=.00625` **on disjoint actual ports**. For `k` direct and `j` chain ports, receive lies between `k/4+j/2` and `c/2`, where receive is the sum of reciprocal degrees of actual old core neighbours. In the uniform host residual `C+a(c−2)−b−c(sU−b)_+−kF−jD`, the dependence on receive is concave, and the two endpoints of its receive interval are affine in `(k,j)`. Testing the count simplex corners therefore covers every simultaneous mixture, rather than charging independently optimized receive to each port. The all-chain corner has identical upper and lower receive, giving five distinct tests. A high terminal with one port can afford either bill from the stocked terminal bound; it cannot be asked for both on that port. At low retained hosts reserve the greater of the directed excess and chain bill for every old degree-two slot: that degree can mean a direct terminal or a suppressed neighbour. A low degree-three/four recipient gets a direct high voucher only when its actual old neighbour is high, which excludes a chain port. Verification retains the voucher only for the profiles that need it; granting one on unused direct recipients can only add slack, and the high budget pays them too.

For `d≥113`, `sU≤1/[2(d+1)]<b` pays all excesses. At the adverse low-stock corner set `z=l/d≤1/2` and use `C≥z[g·113/114+G(2)/2−.611/2]`, with negative bracket, and `c−2≥109/2`. Paying all direct bills leaves at least the half-bracket plus `(a−F)109/2−2F−b>0`; the no-payment corner is weaker. For stock `l>d/2`, the pendant loss is paid by the leaf serial piece; `C≥g·113/228`, leaving at least `C−2F−b>0` after using `a>F` and `c≥2`. At the all-chain receive `c/2`, the old-leaf serial piece pays either signed pendant loss (for `l≤3`, use `G(2)>2/114`; for `l≥4`, the `s>s_l` loss is zero). Again `C≥g·113/228`, leaving `C−2D−b>0` since `a>D`. These are separate tail checks from the high-end **chain exposure** `.012`: the latter holds at actual stock for `10≤d≤41` by exact arithmetic and for `d≥42` by `1/[2(d+1)]≤1/86`. Dropping stock from this first-send bound would not justify the printed chain budget.

## Every suppressed chain and the root-free ledger

Delete only bare degree-two cores (total and core degree two). A maximal path of these has two **distinct** retained ends, since the finite original core is a connected tree and has at least two terminals whenever it has at least two vertices. Contracting all such paths yields a connected simple tree: a loop or parallel pair would expand to an old cycle. All deleted cores lie in exactly one path, and the degree of each retained core in the reduced tree equals its old **core** degree, with the same port multiplicity. The retained vertex charges still use original old neighbours, including the first bare neighbour on a chain. For a pure core path both old core terminals survive, even if their total degree is two. With exactly two old cores there is just one direct edge, no interior sites. With one core apply the star certificate; with none (in the model) the old tree is the size-two edge.

For a path of one bare core, both endpoint debits read the same internal coefficient `(x+y)/6`. At length two the separate end coefficients are `(1/2+x)/6,(1/2+y)/6`, and their common edge is charged once. At length three the middle coefficient is `1/6`; each added site beyond that has the same coefficient as its equal interior neighbour, adds positive serial credit and introduces no new debit. Each endpoint pays its own bill, even when they have equal degree or the same type. A low endpoint keeps exact stocks and both directions of the paired debit; test extrema of its coefficient with the chain receive fixed at `1/2`. High endpoints use the actual-degree favourable tip lower through degree 30, and thereafter replace it by zero and use the closed reciprocal interval `[0,1/31]`. With fixed slopes, chain debit minus the bare serial credits is convex in the ranged coefficients/reciprocals (maximums of affine terms and absolute differences, minus a linear term); hence the checker endpoints cover them jointly, including the length-one shared coefficient. The printed chain maxima below baseline after both bills cover all three pair types. The earlier special terminal payments in the first half of `edge-dual` are **alternatives**, not extra credits in the final ledger.

Let there be `K'` retained cores. Their residuals after reserved excesses and chain bills are nonnegative. Direct transfers have equal debit and credit on the **same** reduced direct edge, so cancel. Each chain already includes the removed sites' credits before charging its one baseline plus its bills. The retained core sum `Σ(c−2)=−2`, and the edge count `K'−1`, yield exactly `b+2a`. This is root-free: the identity in terms of branch counts is `#terminals=2+Σ_(c≥3)(c−2)` on the reduced tree, unchanged by removing degree-two vertices. It applies to a two-vertex reduced tree and makes no implicit assumption of branching or of a large core count. The old size-two edge gives `p₃>p₄` by the finite-clock pullback; the centre/leaf founder bound gives `p₂−p₃≥127/2440`. Taking expectations at each fixed old size gives strictness, with no missing labels or passage to a limit in label.

## Verification and planted error

The pinned `edge-dual/check.py` replay passed, including 6,180 mixed-stock profiles at both receive endpoints, 12,424 uniform corners, simultaneous host transfers, and all low-end and chain checks. The smallest low terminal and bare-three residuals are about `0.000005478682` and `0.000020000000`; I would not replace these tests by decimal arithmetic. The independent checker imports only the pinned scalar `G,U,L`/base inputs, creates actual ordinary trees (including post-attachment stocks), and implements contraction and the signed budgets separately from the writer's profile search. A planted dropped-chain-end mutation fails an exact high–high two-bare-site interval fixture: its certified upper cost is strictly above `b+D` but strictly below `b+2D`. This tests the budget proof, not a negative physical gap. The original pre-transfer negative rows are not physical counterexamples either.
