# Cold audit: compact birth-scale order and fixed-threshold rate

**Verdict: both claims hold**, using the physical core local limit (`fringe/spatial-limit.md`), core-count uniform integrability, and the [one- and two-birth score identities](adjacent-age-sol.md). The reviewed proof (`open/age-order/scaled/README.md`) needs no shape-uniform endpoint concentration, independence of physical degree and size masses, or tail comparison between neighboring labels at a fixed positive threshold. Below are the checks most sensitive to those distinctions.

## Positive endpoint atomlessness, conditional on the initial tree

Fix the locally finite marked input, including the exact endpoint placement. With degree `D`, leaf stock `L` and number `c=D−L` of core neighbours, the degree gain rate is `r=L+Σ_core neighbours 1/D_j`. Thus `0≤D−r≤c`. Every unit increase of `c` comes from a focal initiation targeting an adjacent leaf; an initially marked endpoint of degree one causes no exception to this bound. Give the focal vertex a rate-one proposal clock for these initiations. Thus `c(t)` is bounded by its initial value plus the clock count and is `O(1+t)` almost surely. The normalized degree is a nonnegative supermartingale. Expected absolute drift after `h` is at most `(c(0)+h+1)e^(-h)` and expected martingale bracket there is at most `D(0)e^(-h)`, from `E D(t)≤e^t D(0)`. These give an `L¹` tail bound uniform on a fixed initial marked cylinder, not uniform over all possible degrees.

For any deterministic `w>0` and integer `t`, let `z=e^t w`. Conditional on the history to `t`, use gamma domination and the phantom-tag lower coupling (`fringe/hub-spectrum.md#2-a-vertexs-limiting-mass-as-seen-at-time-t`). They are local: a tag rings at rate one, and it can disappear only on a focal initiation. At late times on `{W=w}`, the focal vertex is a core with `L≥1`; no lower gamma law for an initial leaf is needed. The two comparisons need not use the same gamma variable or share a coupling. If `L≥1`, then for `a>0`

```
P(W=w | F_t) = P(e^t W≤z | F_t) − P(e^t W<z | F_t)
             ≤ F_Gamma(L)(z+a) + exp(−a) − F_Gamma(D)(z)
             ≤ (a+c) sup f_Gamma(L) + exp(−a).
```

The upper bound on the first CDF is a union bound, and the lower bound on the second CDF uses the degree upper comparison. Only for computing the marginal `Gamma(D)` CDF do we introduce an independent `Gamma(c)` increment (zero when `c=0`). On `{W=w}`, `L~we^t` since `e^(-t)c(t)→0`, so taking `a=t` forces the conditional atom estimate to zero by the density maximum bound `O(L^(-1/2))`. The conditional probabilities instead converge to `1_{W=w}` by upward convergence: `W` is measurable on the full path, even for infinite input. This contradiction excludes the positive atom for **each fixed input**, and then also for mixtures of post-insertion inputs. The argument permits an atom at zero. It proves the needed statement about `W`, and does not get atomlessness of a ratio by dividing a gamma marginal by a random variable.

## Passing to a compact of thresholds

The old-cell cutoff gives convergence of finite-time marked histories after each finite insertion, and the preceding `L¹` estimate gives weak convergence of `W` along a locally converging sequence of post-insertion inputs. For finite inputs, start the future clock at the insertion epoch of size `m`. The full size mass is `S~Gamma(m,1)` conditionally on that input, so `mZ=(m/S)W`, with `m/S→1` in probability uniformly over finite shapes. For a converging sequence of rooted inputs and positive thresholds, fix a small ratio tolerance, squeeze the endpoint event between two ordinary `W` thresholds with the exceptional probability of failure of the ratio tolerance, then let the tolerance go to zero. No product of marginal probabilities is used. At zero, use continuity of hubhood for both endpoint types from the adjacent audit (a first-hit lower test above the initial degree, and the upper stock charge on current neighbours). For `y_n→0`, the moving event is bounded above by that hub test and below eventually by every fixed positive threshold test. Let the latter threshold decrease to zero on the limiting graph. This also covers the finite-size ratio multiplier.

Conditional rooted convergence applies to each placement along a converging rooted sequence: its list and coefficients are eventually fixed by a finite marked ball. The number of placements need not have a uniform bound. Instead the one-birth per-core score is bounded by `7` at every threshold (subevent of hubhood), and the absolute two-birth score is bounded by `3` before cancellation. With the almost-sure empirical core-law convergence one can use a rooted Skorokhod coupling pathwise for each realized empirical sequence, then multiply by `C_n/n^mu` and use uniform integrability. The factors converting `y_n/n` to the two post-insertion thresholds are `(n+1)/n` and `(n+2)/n` respectively. The common adjacent denominator is `n(n+1)`. Thus along every `y_n→y≥0` the two scaled tails have the stated single and signed limits `A(y),B(y)`. This entails compact uniform convergence: take a bad sequence, extract a converging subsequence of thresholds, and also use continuity of the limiting scores; pointwise convergence alone would not suffice for the signed score.

In the telescope from `n` to `floor(rn)`, keep the physical threshold `y/n` fixed. The summand at old size `j` has birth-scale argument `jy/n`; multiplying the telescope by `n^(1−mu)` gives weights `(j/n)^(mu−2)/n`. This yields the identity in the reviewed proof. At `y=0` it gives `B(0)=(1−mu)A(0)`. At all other `y`, monotonicity of `A` bounds the left side below by `(1−r^(mu−1))A(y)`, yielding `B(y)≥(1−mu)A(y)` as `r↓1`. Since the initiating core's leaf-target flux is `mu>0`, there is positive stationary weight for an insertion from which a finite local script promotes the marked leaf and grows an arbitrarily large stock. For example, the new leaf can first gain by its parent's directed choice, then a tagged leaf can grow the stock while the mark's promotion proposals are silent. All finitely many required races have positive probability on each admissible initial placement; unrelated infinite exterior births do not have to be silenced. From sufficiently large stock, gamma concentration and the phantom loss bound give positive probability of `W>y`. Thus `A(K)>0` and the signed limit is bounded strictly away from zero on each `[0,K]`. This proves the eventual strict uniform ordering, including `y=0`. Differentiability away from zero follows by differentiating the telescope's continuous-integrand right side with respect to `r` and choosing a starting `y` below the argument of differentiation.

## Fixed fraction: cut without a posterior independence assumption

Condition on an arbitrary birth tree at size `v`, with marked newborn `m` and parent `u`. At the next jump let `m` ring (probability `1/v`): it must attach a new leaf to `u`. Then let `u` ring and choose `m` (joint probability `1/((v+1)D_u)≥1/(v+1)^2`, with updated degree). The mark side has exactly two vertices; the exterior has `v` and a designated leaf at `u`, regardless of other branches. Restart at completion. The two physical cut endpoints now remain cores. Retain the slot on each side: their missing arrows have rates `a(t)=1/D_m(t)` and `b(t)=1/D_u(t)`, and a side with `N` actual vertices has genuine rate `N−1/D_boundary`. Deleting the slot would change these rates and can promote the designated leaves at the wrong intensity.

Under the independent inert-side law, use independent missing-arrow drivers, thinning by the realized sending degree until the first accepted crossing. Consequently the physical sublaw with no future cross is the inert product measure times `exp(−∫(a+b))`. This follows first up to a finite horizon and then by monotone convergence of the no-cross events. It is not the law obtained by independently conditioning the two sides on a posterior event. The factor on the good paths below is bounded from below by a positive constant independent of `v` and of the birth tree. The actual total normalized size on that event is `S_mark+S_ext`, and the fraction is `W_mark/(S_mark+S_ext)`. Both numerator and denominator use the same restart time, so neither setup waiting time contributes a bias or cost beyond the setup jump probabilities.

**Exterior.** Embed the exterior in the proposed Yule size `N_+`: every proposal increments `N_+`; a uniform slot among `N_+` then chooses a physical initiator or a dummy, and the retained boundary slot causes a rejection. Thus `N≤N_+` pathwise. The proposal waiting clocks are exogenous to the embedded slot and neighbour draws; conditional on the joint history the terminal Laplace factor is `h(t,N_+)=(1+theta e^(-t))^(-N_+)`. Tilting by `e^(-theta S_+) / E e^(-theta S_+)` multiplies the rate of **every** proposal by `q(t)=h(t,N_++1)/h(t,N_+)` and leaves its mark law alone. It is essential here to tilt dummy proposals as well; the physical marginal has its ordinary inert intensities in clock `Q(t)=∫_0^t q`. The tilted `S_+` is gamma of shape `v` and rate `1+theta`. This is a law of the auxiliary upper size, not the physical exterior size. In the `Q` clock, the designated leaf embeds a common rate-`(1,1/2)` birth–death family `J`, because the physical promotion hazard per tracked leaf is at most `1/2`. Its discounted count `e^(-u/2)J(u)` is an `L²` bounded martingale with mean one; its limit has positive probability of being positive, and there `∫du/(1+J(u))<∞`. Since `q≥s=(1+theta)^(-1)`, a fixed finite integral quantile divided by `s` gives a common `M_theta` and positive success probability for `∫dt/D_u≤M_theta`, for every exterior. Union bound with gamma concentration on `|S_+/v−s|≤epsilon` retains a fixed positive tilted probability; the integral and gamma band need not be independent. On the band the inverse likelihood is at least `exp[−v(I(s)+theta epsilon)]`, where `I(s)=s−1−log s`, and the actual `S_ext≤(s+epsilon)v`.

**Marked side.** In the inert law, silence an ordinary rate-one focal proposal clock to time `h`; the leaf count is then a one-ancestor Yule process independent of that silence. The clock may include inert null proposals. For `k_v=ceil((1+eta)ze^h v)`, this gives cost exactly `e^(-h)(1−e^(-h))^(k_v−1)` for the silence and stock event. Conditional on any star of stock `k≥k_v`, the future lower gamma-minus-loss bound and Yule upper size bound give, for any sufficiently small fixed `delta>0`, simultaneously

```
W_mark ≥ e^(-h)(1−delta) k,
S_mark ≤ e^(-h)(1+delta)(k+1)
```

with probability `1−o(1)` uniformly for `k≥k_v` (union bound and gamma concentration). Choose `delta` with `(1−delta)(1+eta)>1` and `(1+delta)/(1−delta)<1+eta`, giving `W_mark≥zv` and `S_mark≤(1+eta)W_mark` for all sufficiently large `v`, even if the star overshoots `k_v`. The stock envelope on the same conditional future has failure probability `O(1/k)` and bounds the reciprocal integral after `h` by `2/k`; intersect by another union bound. Before `h` the integral is at most `h`. This establishes the joint event on the marked side without coupling its endpoint gamma to an independent physical size. Its log cost per `v` is at most `(1+eta)ze^h[-log(1−e^(-h))]`, with the multiplier of `(1+eta)z` tending to one as `h→∞`.

**Ratio and uniformity.** Now use the product only for the good events in the inert joint law, and multiply by at least `exp(−h−1−M_theta)` for the genuine no-cross restriction. On that restriction the actual fraction is at least `z/[(1+eta)z+s+epsilon]`, which can be made strictly greater than `x`. Fix `s∈(0,1)` and `z>xs/(1−x)` first, take `eta,epsilon>0` sufficiently small, and send `v→∞` with all auxiliary parameters fixed. Then take `h→∞`, `eta,epsilon→0`, and `z↓xs/(1−x)`. The polynomial setup and all fixed multiplicative probabilities have zero log cost per `v`. Choosing `s=1−x` makes the cost `I(s)+xs/(1−x)=−log(1−x)`, the minimum by strict convexity. The degree-one Pólya comparison gives the matching uniform upper bound `(1−x)^(v−1)` conditional on any birth tree. All lower-bound constants for a fixed `x` were uniform in that tree. For a compact interval inside `(0,1)`, use a finite threshold grid, take the maximum of its finitely many size cutoffs, and use monotonicity of each conditional tail and uniform continuity of `log(1−x)`. This proves compact uniformity even over the birth trees. The log rate leaves room for errors much larger than the difference between adjacent labels at a fixed fraction.

```text
5912 endpoint drift controls; 872 exact two-jump cuts; 5232 proposal kernels; 10464 tilted kernels; 30 strict ratio controls
```

The script expands labelled initiating arrows on all increasing trees through size seven. It checks degree and core-neighbour drifts including leaf endpoints, the two-jump setup probability, slot-preserving inert rates, rejection rates when the upper clock has extra slots, exact cancellation in the Laplace tilt, and strict ratio feasibility with overshoot. It also compares a first combined holding interval against the different result obtained by multiplying separate first-race survival probabilities. The infinite-time atom, rate and compactness arguments are analytic; the replay checks their finite bookkeeping.
