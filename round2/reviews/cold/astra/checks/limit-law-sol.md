## 1. True marginals and the first infection of a genuine leaf

Root both copies at the target of the added leaf, without a stub. At a common edge the synchronized rate is the minimum of the two `1/D_source` rates, and the surplus belongs to the smaller-degree **source** copy. This is the larger-degree **target** side by parity. A private source can reach only its own vertices or the common anchor where its side is already favoured. A paired source reaching a private target grows only that target's private component. Common births preserve the parity convention, so induction gives one favoured side at each common site, with every extra neighbour in that copy. The old parent of a genuine leaf initially has identical degrees in the two copies: it is not a second seeded discrepancy. The root has degrees `(2,1)` in `(larger,smaller)`, so its core indicator is already different; the private leaf itself is the initial account, not a second retained core. Total joint rate is bounded by the sum of the marginal sizes, and jumps of this sum are at most two, giving nonexplosion by linear pure-birth domination.

Let the paired filtration record realized arrows and accounting tags, not any future clock values. While a given copy's tree is unchanged, each projected oriented-arrow intensity is the physical rate for that tree, even after arbitrarily many jumps in the other copy. The compensator of its next jump, conditional on a paired stopping history, therefore has constant total hazard equal to the current size until that jump, with mark probabilities proportional to the physical rates. Restart at each projected jump. This proves the conditional marginal Markov law at paired stopping times, stronger than equality of the two projected laws at time zero. Neither conditioning on eventual infection nor conditioning on future survival of a branch is used.

For a freshly infected nonseed vertex, connectedness of the infected set in the common tree means it has just one already-infected common neighbour, the infector, and degree gap one. With lower degree `d`, there are at most `d−1` eligible children (zero if `d=1`). To cross-check the conditional input in the [influence audit](../influence/check/README.md), set its time to zero and let `I(t)` count infected common neighbours, including any born later. Infections across fresh neighbouring edges must come from the vertex itself, at total surplus rate at most one, so `E I(t) ≤ 1+t`. The degree gap gains from excess returns by those neighbours (rate at most `I(t)`) and from private neighbours (rate at most the gap). The resulting conditional drift bound and initial gap one give `E gap(t) ≤ 3e^t−t−2`. Lower degree is nondecreasing and Jensen bounds the expected individual excess by `B(t)/(d(d+B(t)))`, `B(t)=3e^t−t−2`. Also `E D_high(t) ≤ (d+1)e^t`, since its gain rate is at most its degree, hence the other envelope `1/d−e^(−t)/(d+1)`. These estimates use all returns, after localization by size and removal of that stop by finite-time domination. Only the *output* to be counted is restricted to outward edges already present at infection. At the initial seed there is no incoming edge; bound its entire discounted row by `1/s` using the total surplus rate one, even at degree one. The rational replay gives a nonseed row below `.97` at `.525` and below `.84` at `.565`; monotonicity extends the latter to `μ≥.565`. No contraction across all future-born edges follows from this calculation.

## 2. Coverage: partition at infection time, not at time zero

Maintain three disjoint classes in each copy: clean, unowned paired sites; retained infected singletons; and vertices in disjoint accounts. At the first infection of a retained vertex keep its current common outward edges eligible, including branches grown while it was clean. Create a fresh account for the arriving private leaf, and for **every** subsequent direct attachment there. If the attachment is synchronized, create an account on each side; equal at birth does not mean equal forever. Every event whose target is already owned puts its newborn in the same account. A clean unowned target can be infected only on a surplus arrow from a common neighbour. That neighbour cannot be in an account: the only exit from an account goes to its already infected anchor. The infector is therefore a retained singleton, and its edge to the target must have existed before its own infection (otherwise the target would have been born owned). This proves coverage by induction over events. Uninfected unowned sites cancel, retained singleton differences cost at most one, and the remaining signed differences are exactly the core counts of the accounts. There is no bound here on the number of paired births at an infected anchor. An account can contain arbitrarily many later infected sites; none is made into another retained singleton.

## 3. Spatial payment in live exterior and uniform gradient

For a fixed account, its first promotion time `β` is a stopping time. Until then it is a single leaf, and its first promotion requires its anchor to choose that leaf. Per copy and retained anchor those disjoint first promotions have total predictable intensity at most one. At `β`, removing the account's unique boundary edge cuts off a side with exactly one core, the promoted vertex. The anchor has degree at least two: the branch root was a new attachment to an already present ordinary vertex of degree at least one. Reset core-cell labels at `β` in that marginal alone, giving every current core its own label. No future edge can cross the cut other than its original boundary edge. Cross-edge arrows can add leaves but cannot promote the opposite endpoint, which is already core. Every promotion on either side inherits its promoter's label, so the cores on the branch side at `β+u` are exactly the cell of its initial core. The marginal law from the full paired history at `β` stays physical; use the full then-current environment in `Q_u1`, not just the branch shape. This also applies on `{β≤t}` with `u=t−β`, known at `β`. There is no independence assumption between the cell, its exterior, and sibling accounts.

The specific input is `spatial-limit.md` §1, (2)–(3) (`fringe/spatial-limit.md#1-start-in-one-old-cell`) (blob `4210a37782cdfe1f1b66ddab705e780438a6dfbc`), only for `f=1` and finite free environments. In its renewal proof, only *new* children of the old root enter the copy/immigration sum; the old neighbours contribute to its real degree and remain active. With `d=3/20`, `q=9/25`, the immigration score against `W` is at most `1/d+2e^(qu)`, uniformly over those old neighbours. The weighted renewal estimate gives `P_v1≤K e^(μv)W`; integrating the bound with `q<μ` and adding the original core gives `Q_u1≤[1+K(1/(dμ)+2/(μ−q))]e^(μu)`. This use is upstream of the new gradient: it does not use the limiting empirical cell distribution or its proof. Muting the exterior is not an identity, even to second order (control below).

For each retained anchor at `τ`, conditional expected branch count at a deterministic terminal `t≥τ` is at most `(2C_Q/μ)e^(μ(t−τ))` summed over the two copies. Indeed, sum `C_Q e^(μ(t−β))` over the two first-promotion point processes and compensate from `τ`. This conditions on the post-promotion state for the cell and then uses predictable pre-event intensities for its entry charge. Both accounting choices are set at stopping times. Steps/sums may first be restricted to finite event counts, then extended by nonnegativity. For `s=.525`, the retained discounted mass is bounded by `D_s=1+(1/s)/(1−.97)`: conditional expectation at each retained parent first infection gives a geometric bound by generation. The same argument at `s=μ` gives `D_μ≤1+(1/μ)/(1−.84)`. The normalized singleton cost at `t` is bounded by `D_s e^(−(μ−s)t)`, which vanishes even at `μ=.565`. The normalized account cost is bounded by `2C_Q D_μ/μ` uniformly in `t`. Taking the two means' limits now gives `|Φ(T⁺)−Φ(T)|≤2C_Q D_μ/μ`, independently of the location, leaf stock, exterior and seed size. The edge limit follows by integrating the first rate-two wait against the normalized bounded mean from `T₃` (the core count before it is zero); the integral of `2e^(−(2+μ)u)` gives the claimed factor. No exchange of a supremum with a pointwise limit is needed for the uniform finite-time estimate.

## 4. Embedded tails and support

Use Claim 6 §§4 and 6 (`standalone/claim-6.md`) (blob `bb6bb84eb5cab035bf0806ebba82a3747fd6ec0c`): `Φ≤KC`, `C≤K(1+Φ)`, and `Y_n=Γ(n)Φ(T_n)/Γ(n+μ)=E[X|F_n]`. One step of the martingale is `γ_(n+1)` times a one-leaf difference minus its conditional mean. It is deterministically bounded by `A n^(−μ)`, uniformly over successors and past histories. Conditional Hoeffding at finite horizons followed by conditional Fatou for the exponential moment of the `L²` limit gives each one-sided limit bound `exp(−t²/(2A² Σ_{j≥n}j^(−2μ)))`, including a closed threshold. Orthogonality of martingale differences in the same conditional law gives the variance bound by `V n^(1−2μ)`. Equivalently these arguments hold separately on each finite positive-probability prefix. There is no concentration statement made under a future scripted-growth event, and no conditioning on the independent size clock to manipulate the eigenmartingale.

- **Upper tail.** With `p=1/(1−μ)` and deterministic `Y_n≤B n^(1−μ)`, choose `n=floor((x/(2B))^p)`. Then `x−Y_n≥x/2` and `x² n^(2μ−1)≥c x^p` for large `x`. Apply the conditional bound and average.
- **Lower tail on the right.** Under the degree-three cap, `N₂=j−2L+2`, `N₃=L−2`. Targeting a leaf when `L≥j/4` costs probability at least `1/12`, because each leaf has a source of degree at most three. Otherwise target any degree-two vertex: it has two incoming directions, giving total probability at least `2N₂/(3j)>1/3`. These are bounds under every present capped tree. Multiplying conditional lower bounds through the *deterministic* size `n` costs at most exponential in `n`. The cap gives `C≥(n−2)/2`, hence `Y_n≥b₀n^(1−μ)` for all sufficiently large `n`, since the reverse comparison only loses an additive constant. Take `n=ceil((2x/b₀)^p)`; condition on the prefix (not on all later trees being capped). Uniform variance gives probability at least `1/2` of `X≥x` there. The resulting lower bound is `exp(−C x^p)` after changing constants. The two tail estimates imply the double-log exponent and all positive moments. Applying the tail integral to `X^p` gives finite exponential moments at small positive rates and infinite ones at large rates, without identifying the threshold.
- **Reachability and full support.** On a size-`N` star, `Y_N=O(N^(−μ))`. A prescribed finite run of attachments at the tip of one arm has positive probability, and each step adds one core. Along the infinite *list of reachable prefixes* the means diverge, by the reverse comparison. For a ball of radius `ε` about a fixed `x>0`, take `N` large enough that `Y_N<x`, `A N^(−μ)<ε/2` and `V N^(1−2μ)<ε²/8`. At the first crossing, whose index is deterministic on this selected list, the mean lands inside `ε/2`. The conditional variance bound gives probability at least `1/2` of landing inside the ball, on a prefix of positive probability. This works without monotonicity of `Y` along the list. Closure gives zero and Claim 6 excludes negative support; no positive probability of following an infinite prescribed path is asserted. The size-`n` star event has probability `2/(n−1)` (one-step factors `(j−1)/j`); taking `n` of order `ε^(−1/μ)` with `Y_n≤ε/2` gives the stated lower bound near zero by conditional Markov. This gives no matching upper estimate there.

## Exact controls

`limit_law_sol.py` uses only standard-library rational arithmetic and no writer imports. The paired enumerator checks marginal arrows and the partition on short histories from a genuine leaf of a tree with a core. The deterministic scripts target places where simpler proofs fail:

- **Swapped marginal:** at the initial genuine leaf, rates of its arrow to its old parent are `(1/2,1)` across the copies; assigning the surplus to the wrong side gives `(1,1/2)` before any later conditioning.
- **Older branch and recurrent new-birth class:** a synchronized birth at the still-clean parent makes a new leaf which is older than its parent's infection. It must be retained when infected (rate `1/12`). Two different synchronized births *after* that infection are instead separate paired accounts; the surplus can promote their larger-side copies at `1/20` and `1/30`. After one internal promotion, omitting this recurring class misses three cores on that finite path. Counting all newly infected paired vertices as retained would double-pay those accounts.
- **Live exterior:** on the post-promotion seed with a degree-three anchor and a degree-two branch root, the actual incoming rate is `1/3`, drops to `1/4` after another branch arrives at the anchor, and must not be frozen or muted. Tracking reset labels under all short arrow prefixes agrees with the cut component's core set. Muting only the incoming arrow leaves the first derivative of the mean at `1/2` but changes its second derivative by `1/18` (from the additional leaf raising the next promotion rate from `1/2` to `2/3`). An equal initial drift is not a live-cell comparison.
- **Reachability:** exact adaptive degree-cap histories and delayed-star arm extensions check nonzero weights and the core-count constraints. A deterministic mean-preserving replacement of each near-target conditional law by a mixture of `0` and `2` preserves all chosen means near `1` but misses its neighbourhood; its variances are bounded below by `1015/1024`. Conversely zero conditional variance at two visited endpoint states still misses the neighbourhood if the interpolating prefix is removed. These are controls for the logic, not substitutes for the physical variance or jump bounds.

The certificates and exact checks are finite or numerical-envelope controls. The partition, conditional projection, martingale concentration, and limiting conclusions are analytic.
