## Physical size tail and arithmetic limitation

Use the positive chronological expansion of the physical trigger (`finite/gauge/physical-trigger.md#4-positive-chronological-series-and-uniform-error`), with diagonal `s+a+n−1/D_o`. On the planted star with root degree `d`, displayed size is `d` (the exterior parent is omitted), mark is `1/2`, and there are `d−1` leaf senders. Its all-leaf-gain path therefore has coefficient `(d−1)/(s+d+1/2−1/d)` per jump. At degree `m≥20` the positive retained direct-production arrow has rate `(m−1)/m` and points to `J_(1/20)`; ending there pays `(1/2)/(s+31/20)`. This is an offspring lineage term of the positive expansion, not a physical jump that deletes the old star or a replacement by a different process. It is valid irrespective of other available arrows. If `m>N`, the path was already killed at size `N+1`, so its contribution cannot reappear in the finite resolve after the reset. These are distinct omitted summands indexed by `m`.

Dropping `−1/d` enlarges each denominator. The resulting star product is `Γ(m−1)Γ(s+5/2)/Γ(m+s+1/2)`, with a strictly positive uniform lower by a constant times `m^(−s−3/2)` on the compact discount interval. The reset adds a factor at least a constant times `1/m`, and the terminal reward is bounded away from zero. Summing over `m>N` by the integral test proves the stated lower exponent. The explicit fallback follows at `s≤1`: the star gains are at least `(d−1)/(d+2)`, the reset at least `(m−1)/(m(m+2))` and the reward exceeds `1/6`. Their product is `1/[m²(m+1)(m+2)]`. On `N+1…2N` it dominates `(m+2)^−4`; integration gives `[(N+3)^−3−(2N+3)^−3]/3≥1/[16(N+1)^3]` for every `N≥20`. Thus an exponential upper error in **size** is impossible; an exponential upper bound in number of newborn generations concerns a different truncation.

At a hypothetical reduced rational root `r`, the finite positive deficit is `1−F_N(r)>0`, so clearing its actual reduced denominator `D_N` leaves a positive integer. The published tail upper applies throughout this enclosure (`L>0.56564`), and immediately yields the stated necessary lower bound on `D_N`. A sufficiently small denominator on an unbounded subsequence would exclude that trial, but no such physical denominator result is established. The physical tail lower works in the opposite direction for high-growth denominators: if `D_N/(N+1)^(r+3/2)→∞` then the product of reduced denominator and positive deficit cannot become small. Neither raw determinant growth nor prime appearances in the expansion at infinity measures the reduced denominator at evaluation.

## Complex line and countermodel

At the physical crossing the positive Laplace measure tilted by `μ` has mass one. Its initial contribution has mass `1/[2(μ+2)]`; the triangle inequality splitting this part proves the stated gap. In fact every chronological term contains the same first denominator `μ+2+iy`, including the zero-transition reward. Removing that denominator and taking absolute values of the remaining holding factors bounds their sum by its real value, giving the stronger shared-factor bound. The analytic window to the left gives a finite second moment of the tilted time measure, and its initial continuous contribution forces strictly positive variance. Thus the modulus gap is quadratic near zero; the critical-line pole is isolated and simple with positive residue, without any arithmetic consequence for its location.

In the rational-root model, the negative-real simple poles have nonzero residues, and the residues of the first model are summable (`O(n^−2)`); the shared-hold version keeps the same nonzero poles away from the initial one. Both are positive Laplace transforms and have a simple crossing at the prescribed rational. On the critical line the strict gap away from zero, uniform large-imaginary decay, and a local simple zero permit a crossing strip. At `r` the truncated deficit is exactly `c/(N+1)^2` in both versions. With `c=A/B` reduced, cancellation is exactly `gcd(A,(N+1)^2)`, giving quadratic reduced-denominator growth and a positive cleared numerator. This is an obstruction to drawing an irrationality conclusion from the indicated positivity, complex-line and order-of-tail information alone; it does not match the whole physical generator.
