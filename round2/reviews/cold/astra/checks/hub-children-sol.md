Independent audit of `../fringe/hub-children.md` for the physical friend tree and the silent planted process. Countable simultaneous claims are intersections of vertexwise probability-one events in a well-defined process; this is not an existence claim for arbitrary infinite initial states. The external mean-asymptotic and ranked-spectrum results used by the note are inputs to this audit.

- **Mass and dichotomy.** A gain changes `(D,L)` to `(D+1,L+1)`; a promotion changes it to `(D,L-1)`. Degree is nondecreasing and its gain intensity is at most `D`. The stopped integrating-factor argument gives the nonnegative supermartingale and the gamma-minus-exponential bound is valid at leaf-count hitting times: migrated tags continue independent Yule families, and an immigrant family at every focal ring dominates the migrated families. No independence between the two limits is required. In the birth–death embedding an unmatched proposal removes only a tag, not a leaf; a matched proposal uses the same physical focal-to-tag channel, not a second promotion. Individual births and tag losses are at rates `1` and `1/d`; other directions can be split separately. On the infinite-degree event every degree threshold is hit. Bounding the probability of infinite degree and bounded leaves at each such threshold by `1/d` gives zero, without conditioning on infinite degree. In the silent newborn the initial leaf supplies the same comparison at `d=2`. A finite-degree core can retain no leaf permanently because that leaf rings at rate one.
- **Factorial bound.** The exact gain and promotion ratios in the note give relative drift at most `L[(D+1)/(2D)-1]+(L/D)[(D+1)/2-1]=0`. Localization and conditional Fatou apply despite unbounded `V`. The degree bound uses only the event where the eventual value is `A(D∞)`, not convergence of `V` in mean. Summing the promotion-time bounds uses the predictability of being promoted by size `n` at the corresponding stopping time; the founder has `V=4/9`. Stirling and the dyadic summability and monotonicity arguments give (2).
- **Silent lower event.** The star races give `2/d` then `(d+1)^(-(d-1))`. Suppress the child-to-root channels only after the star reaches `d`. On the successful root race, its degree stays `d`; one can construct the race with independent clocks and feed each promoted branch its independent outward clock of rate `1/d`. Branch age is measured from its promotion, so integrating at random birth times adds no factor. Independently reinserting the omitted channels with their path intensities gives conditional probability at least `theta` per child on the prescribed survival comparison, since `D_child >= B`. The linear birth–death martingale has a positive limit on survival (the generating function gives an atom `1/2` at zero, precisely the extinction probability). Thus the reciprocal integral is finite there and `theta>0`. This proves both the exact-degree lower bound and the common logarithmic order. A mark for a hub may be assigned degree zero for the extreme-value argument.
- **Poisson allocation.** The mark at a ring is independent of the past, including the decision to spend that ring on a leaf. The child's effect on its parent *after* promotion need not be independent of the assigned trajectory. Splitting directed arrows makes the rootward channel nonmutating for the branch and allows the parent-to-child send to interrupt it. The total intensity of outgoing core choices is integrable on the hub event by the linear clock bound on `c` and exponential degree growth; localized compensation gives only finitely many such choices. This removes finitely many missed marks and finitely many changed entire trajectories. Push-forward of `dt × law(W^0)` under `w exp(-t)` gives the stated intensity; the `≥` convention agrees almost everywhere with `>`. Integrability above a positive threshold follows from the first moment, and the leading logarithmic coefficient from the tail at zero. Poisson count and mark laws hold before restricting to the hub event. Their probability-one versions survive restriction, finite edits, and the common random scaling. The maxima among dyadic promoted-child prefixes give the stated order; finite edits and the ratio of consecutive dyadic normalizers tending to one preserve it. This fixed-hub argument alone does not give a population exponent.
