# Cold audit: eventual adjacent hub ordering

**Verdict: holds**, using the physical core local limit, its core-count uniform integrability, and the pointwise late-birth asymptotic (`open/age-order/late/README.md`). There is no required sign on an individual state, edge, or newborn chain. A further comparison below gives strict ordering on the `1/n` threshold scale at regular limiting thresholds, and on a whole interval abutting zero. It does not settle fixed positive thresholds.

## Exact identity and normalization

Write `r_u=Σ_(z~u)1/D_z` on the prebirth graph, and `H(u,v)` for the difference of the two newborn endpoint probabilities in the ordered two-birth extension `u,v`. The endpoint is the same label-neutral event for both vertices. Given an old first target `u`, the second rate at distinct old `v` is `r_v−δ_u 1_(u~v)`, where `δ_u=1/[D_u(D_u+1)]`; the new vertex has target rate `1/(D_u+1)`. Repeated targets make the newborns twins even though the weight of that order need not be `r_u²`. For distinct old targets, the two final graphs are related by swapping the newborn labels, so `H(v,u)=−H(u,v)`. The uncancelled numerator for one old edge is `(r_vδ_v−r_uδ_u)H(u,v)`; for a chain with first target `u`, it is `r_u H(u,new)/(D_u+1)`. The common denominator is `n(n+1)`.

Define `G` by charging the oriented sum `−Σ_(v~u)r_uδ_u H(u,v)` and the chain term to old `u`, then moving each *old* leaf's charge to its old core parent. For a core, the absolute oriented and chain charges together are at most `2r_u/(D_u+1)≤2`. For a leaf of `o`, `D_u=1`, `r_u=1/D_o`; the two charges together are at most `1/D_o`. Hence `|G(Gold,o)|≤2+L_o/D_o≤3`, requiring neither a degree moment nor a bound on the number of possible orientations. Every coefficient and every possible endpoint insertion of an individual rooted score is determined by a finite neighborhood (leaf types can be aggregated). This charging is specific to the *old* core/leaf split, not to the core membership after two births.

## Continuity for both newborn endpoint types

In any locally finite initial core, define hubhood by unbounded degree. For a finite physical state this equals `Z>0`; the same argument identifies it with positive continuous-time mass in the locally finite process. Indeed at the first hit of degree `d` from below there is a fresh leaf. Its attached tracked population dominates a supercritical birth–death family of individual rates `1,1/d`. Survival forces leaf stock to diverge and has probability `1−1/d`. It also implies positive mass a.s.: at stock first hitting `k`, the gamma-minus-exponential comparison from hub-spectrum (`fringe/hub-spectrum.md`) gives conditional probability of zero mass tending to zero as `k→∞`. The local limit of `e^(−t)D(t)` exists by the nonnegative supermartingale property. Conversely bounded degree implies zero mass. Unbounded degree without diverging leaf stock has probability zero by the same first-hit argument. In particular, choose `d>2` when the marked endpoint begins as the degree-two chain middle: a hit used in the lower bound is a **new** degree gain. No leaf-at-start assumption is needed.

For the lower finite-history test, multiply the probability of hitting `d` by time `h` by `1−1/d`. For the upper test, at time `h` sum (cap at one) over current neighbours the stock-based bound `b(k)` on that neighbour making its first subsequent arrow to the endpoint, using its `k` other leaves and the stock envelope (`open/age-order/README.md`). Any future hub must have another gain, and its first such gain must come from an existing neighbour. On a nonhub path the focal degree is eventually constant, and each of its finite set of permanent neighbours is an unbounded-degree hub: if both endpoints had finite degree, both would eventually be constant and a directed channel of eventually fixed positive rate would contradict that. Thus the upper charge tends to zero on nonhub paths. Its expectation cannot undershoot the true probability by the Markov property, and any excess is bounded by its expectation on nonhub paths. Bounded convergence, and sending `h` then `d` to infinity in the lower tests, squeeze the probability. Finite-time histories of the designated cells and their current neighbours have continuous bounded tests by the old-cell graphical cutoff (`operator.md`) after the finite insertion (for a fixed `h`, restrict the old cells to a large ball first). This proves continuity at each input, for leaf and middle alike, hence for the bounded per-core score.

The empirical-core limit and uniform integrability of `C_n/n^μ` give `n^(2−μ)(p_(n+1)−p_(n+2))→b=E[X]π(G)`. Because `μ<1`, the tail series of the differences is absolutely convergent, and `p_n→0`. Summing the asymptotic with a uniform tail `o(m^(μ−2))` bound (also when `b=0`) yields `n^(1−μ)p_n→b/(1−μ)`. Comparing with the strictly positive late-birth coefficient `c` gives `b=(1−μ)c>0`. This proves eventual strict adjacent hub ordering with no effective starting label. In contrast the first-order equivalent `p_n∼c n^(μ−1)` alone gives no sign for adjacent differences.

## Positive tails: a birth-scale extension

For each fixed post-insertion input let `W=lim_h e^(−h)D(h)` for the marked endpoint, with time measured from insertion. Its law is locally weakly continuous, even without knowing atomlessness. To see this write the martingale decomposition for `e^(−t)D(t)`. Its drift in absolute value is at most `e^(−t)c(t)`, where `c(t)` counts core neighbours; `c(t)` is bounded by its initial value plus the endpoint's own initiations and at most one additional unit (an initially leaf neighbour newly becomes core only on an endpoint initiation). Its martingale has expected quadratic variation after `h` at most `D(0)e^(−h)` since the unit degree gain rate is at most `D(t)` and `ED(t)≤e^t D(0)`. Thus the expected tail after `h` is bounded by `√D(0)e^(−h/2)+(c(0)+h+2)e^(−h)`, uniformly within an initial marked cylinder. Finite-history continuity and this estimate give the weak continuity. For original finite inputs of size `m`, `mZ=(m/S)W` with the Yule size limit `S∼Gamma(m,1)`. Here `S/m→1` in probability uniformly in the input; no independence of `W,S` is used.

Let `A(y)` be `EX` times the limiting single-birth score with endpoint `W>y`, and `B(y)` be `EX` times the limiting adjacent score with the same endpoint event. Call a positive `y` regular if neither the single-birth positive measure nor the total absolute two-birth coefficient measure assigns mass to endpoint `W=y`, after integrating over `π`. For the single-birth leaf multiplicity, restrict to positive masses: the measure is finite by the uniform per-core hub bound in late (`open/age-order/late/README.md`). The two-birth coefficient measure is finite by the charge bound (count both endpoints). Thus all but countably many positive `y` are regular. Finite-time approximation or bounded a.e.-continuous test convergence yields, for `y_n→y` regular,

```
n^(1−μ) Pr(Z_(n+1)>y_n/n) → A(y)
n^(2−μ) [Pr(Z_(n+1)>y_n/n)−Pr(Z_(n+2)>y_n/n)] → B(y).
```

For detail on the varying inputs: on convergent rooted states, each of the finitely many placements at that root converges locally, hence so does the `W` law. The `m/S` estimate and a threshold sandwich give convergence whenever that placement law has no atom at `y`. The bounded scores, rooted weak convergence (equivalently, use a Skorokhod representation on the marked-tree space), and core-count UI then give the displayed limits. The single-birth score bound uses `W>y⊂hub`, and the two-birth score bound applies to every identical threshold on the two endpoints. For `y_n→0`, squeeze each endpoint between its hub event and a fixed small positive regular threshold, using hub continuity and monotone convergence as the fixed threshold decreases to zero. Hence the scaled two-birth difference tends to `B(0)=(1−μ)c>0` along **every** nonnegative sequence `y_n→0`. Also `B(y)→B(0)` along positive thresholds decreasing to zero by dominated convergence. These sandwich arguments imply that for some `ε>0`, all large `n` have strict ordering at every `0≤x≤ε/n`: otherwise take a sequence of bad scaled thresholds tending to zero by using `ε=1/j` and arbitrarily large bad sizes.

There is also a sign at each regular `y>0`. Telescope from size `n` to `⌊rn⌋` keeping the common threshold **`y/n`** in every summand. The displayed limits, dominated Riemann integration (the normalized score bound is uniform), and regularity of `ry` give

```
A(y) − r^(μ−1) A(ry) = ∫_1^r s^(μ−2) B(sy) ds.
```

Pointwise convergence for almost every integration point follows using `m/n→s` and regularity of `sy`. The left side is at least `(1−r^(μ−1))A(y)` since `A` is nonincreasing. Divide by `r−1` and send `r↓1` through regular endpoints; continuity of `B` at a regular `y` gives `B(y)≥(1−μ)A(y)>0`. To check positivity of `A(y)` at any finite `y`, use the positive stationary leaf-target initiation flux `π(L/D)=μ`. After such a placement the marked tip has positive probability of promotion and then of growing an arbitrarily large stock by a prescribed finite script within a bounded time. The gamma-minus-exponential comparison at the end of the script gives positive probability of any prescribed finite lower mass. Thus the typical birth-scale positive tails are eventually strictly ordered at regular thresholds. For fixed unscaled `x>0` the threshold inside the local mass instead goes to infinity. The bounded-score limit at fixed `x` then has both leading coefficients zero and provides no sign; the first-order hub law cannot remedy that loss.

## Independent exact replay

`taskset -c 13 nice -n 10 timeout 12s python3 -uB adjacent-age-sol.py`

```
128 exact state/endpoint comparisons; nonzero directed-edge terms in 72, nonzero chain terms in 128
```

The direct side enumerates both births by independently reconstructing initiating-arrow rates on enlarged labelled trees. The comparison side assembles directed edge charges and promoted-middle chain terms from the old graph. Endpoints are exact finite backward harmonics: hit degree `2`, `3`, or `4` by absolute size `n+4`, and terminal degree fraction above `1/3` at the same size. The run includes all increasing labelled trees of sizes 3–5; it evaluates the horizon beyond the two-birth split, including degree-two initial middles. This checks the cancellation and finite normalization, not analytic convergence.
