# Independent audit: fine diagonal and the strong-mode obstruction

The diagonal note (`open/forest-age/fixed-shapes/diagonal/README.md`) concerns a **fine** count, normalized by `(n/N)^μ`, and original-degree deletion. The frozen sequential law (`open/forest-age/fixed-shapes/README.md`) identifies the constant but takes `n→∞` first. Neon-2's simultaneous macroscopic scale has an additional `log N`; a vanishing relative macroscopic error need not be negligible for a single shape.

**New obstruction:** there is a deterministic sublinear cutoff sequence for which the positive fine pair limit fails almost surely. The divergence of `n/N` in this example can be as slow as any prescribed divergent upper envelope (eventually). Thus the probability conclusion at arbitrary slow divergence cannot in general be strengthened to an almost-sure one. This says nothing against the stated almost-sure small-power cutoff range.

**Audit scope:** the old-singleton constant and the stopping-time transfer are consistent with the sequential constant, using the diagonal note's cohort and tail estimates. The independent argument below tests the strong-mode boundary; it does not rederive the full cohort concentration theorem.

## 1. Eventual stability at a fixed proportional cutoff

Choose `c∈(0,1)` outside the countable union of positive atom sets of all `Z_v`, and use `N=floor(cn)`. Almost surely the removed labels eventually equal the finite set `H_c={v: Z_v>c}`. To justify this beyond pointwise convergence, for each label `v≥3` couple its degree to the two-colour urn starting from one red at size `v`, using the degree upper attachment rate. The urn proportion is a bounded martingale with `Beta(1,v−1)` limit. Its terminal second moment is `2/[v(v+1)]`; the nonnegative submartingale maximal bound for its square gives

```
P(sup_(n≥v) D_v(n)/n ≥ c/2) ≤ 8/[c² v(v+1)].
```

These per-label comparisons need not be coupled independently. Summability implies a.s. that only finitely many labels can ever exceed `c/2`. On those labels use convergence and exclude threshold equality. This works also at every sufficiently late physical time, with the proportional cutoff evaluated at the current ordinary size.

## 2. Fixed proportional cutoffs have infinitely many empty pair censuses

Root the tree at its founder and orient by inheritance. Once `H_c` has stabilized, any retained component containing an original core is either a descendant branch intact below its top, or one of finitely many exceptional components. For the latter, either the founder is in it or its top is the child of a high vertex and its branch contains a high descendant. All possible such tops are on the finite paths to `H_c`, apart from the single founder top. For each such fixed top, the component defined by removing the fixed set `H_c` is increasing in ordinary size once all removed labels exist. An eventual component of exactly two vertices containing a core is impossible. If it contained two permanently retained adjacent vertices but had finite size, their original degrees would stabilize (their only other possible neighbours are in finite `H_c`), contradicting the permanent positive arrow rate across their edge. Hence none of these finitely many components is a pair at all sufficiently late epochs. The same argument handles `H_c=∅`.

An intact pair containing a nonfounder original core must be a virgin branch (top plus its first newborn leaf), created by an actual promotion by a member of `H_c`. It ceases to be a pair no later than the first ring of that new leaf: as long as the branch is a pair, this ring appends another leaf at the top. Other genuine changes can only remove it from the pair census earlier. At any fixed finite start time the already present finitely many virgin branches all leave that census in finite time, under the same clock test.

For the recurrence argument avoid conditioning on `H_c`. Take a deterministic `k` and start at size `k`; attach independent dummy `Exp(1)` lifetime marks to **all** rings of vertices with labels at most `k`. At a genuine promotion use the mark for the first wait of its newly added leaf; future clocks are fresh after that wait. No such leaf belongs to the first `k`. The independent marked Poisson queues therefore dominate virgin census candidates arising from these sources, for every later realization, whether or not those vertices are high. The finite superposition is an immigration–death queue with Poisson rate `k` and exponential lifetimes. It has infinitely many empty intervals of length one a.s.: extend the Poisson inputs to negative time to dominate by its stationary version, whose empty-unit event has probability `exp(−2k)>0` and recurs by ergodicity of the independently marked Poisson process. Every such sufficiently late interval contains at least one actual size epoch, since the physical exponential holding gaps tend to zero a.s. Intersect over `k`, and on the realized path choose `k` covering `H_c`. Therefore the physical pair count at `floor(cn)` equals zero at infinitely many deterministic integer indices `n` (the indices of zeros themselves are random). No claim that the first `k` rings are independent of future high membership is needed.

## 3. A deterministic slow diagonal that fails a.s.

Pick a decreasing deterministic sequence `c_j↓0` of regular thresholds from §1. For each `j`, given any deterministic start `b_j`, the probability of having no zero pair census at cutoff `floor(c_j n)` anywhere in `[b_j,B]` tends to zero as `B→∞`. Choose deterministic successive block ends so that this failure probability is at most `2^-j`. On block `j` set `N(n)=floor(c_j n)`; starts can be arranged arbitrarily large by extending the previous block (do not discard its already chosen search window). In particular impose `c_j b_j≥j` and any prescribed eventual divergent upper bound on `n/N(n)`, by waiting until the upper envelope exceeds `2/c_j`. The resulting deterministic `N(n)` has `N→∞`, `N/n→0`. Borel–Cantelli gives at least one zero pair census in each sufficiently late block almost surely. The proposed limit for the pair is strictly positive a.s., namely `2AX/3`. Hence a.s. convergence fails on this diagonal. No rate on the recursion of block ends is required. This construction does not dispute convergence in probability at each deterministic observation index.

## 4. Cold check of the probability proof and physical singleton readout

The scale split in the diagonal note is important: in the high-cutoff regime a fixed positive power of `n` permits the large-degree concentration, whereas it cannot be applied uniformly down to slowly growing `N`. Arbitrarily slow **`n/N`** divergence in the high-cutoff regime only requires conditional Poisson variance divided by the square of the number of proxy parents. It does not supply a summable failure bound. The small-cutoff cylinder argument instead uses a fixed-radius score capped by the maximal core degree and the exponentially integrable limiting core degree. These are distinct concentration mechanisms.

For the old singleton, the correct stopped test is a finite snapshot of a bounded-degree candidate and **all** its currently recorded neighbours with large stocks, including the parent. The first-send barrier pays `2q/(j+1)` for any later gain when the candidate has degree at most `q` and the neighbours each have at least `j` other leaves. This also rules out a future unrecorded neighbour on the successful bracket. The all-future compensated degree remainder in the diagonal note is appropriate: conditional on snapshot degree `d`, the size-one jump bracket for its normalized degree is at most `d`, and the integrated drift is at most current core degree plus a constant, before multiplying by the snapshot's time-unit factor. Thus the bound applies at a random physical size epoch without conditioning on a future high set. Localize `Y` in finitely many deterministic clock brackets and keep the cutoff comparisons at both ends. Integration over the offsets removes equality in the limiting mass test. On a bounded top with final neighbour masses `W_j` in the parent's time units, the old event is then the threshold on `min_j W_j`, not just on the parent; the shifted-exponential mark is exactly the sequential one. The argument still needs the finite-history cohort concentration and the cutoff/tail estimates explicitly cited in the diagonal note. There is no claim of an independent proof of those imported quantitative local limits here.

### Low-order physical checks

With an inert slot at the top the virgin genuine rate is `3/2`; its next addition hits the root with rate one and its leaf with rate `1/2`. Thus the pair occupation is `2/3`, the root-centred three-vertex star occupation is `(2/3)/(3−1/3)=1/4`, and the rooted three-vertex path occupation is `(1/3)/(3−1/2)=2/15`. With a live parent, the incoming send to the virgin is an additional genuine event. Until the next branch change its rate is `1/D_parent`, with that degree evolving even when the branch is frozen: the holding mean is bracketed by `1/(3/2+1/D_parent(0))` and `2/3`, rather than always equalling `2/3`.

An actual singleton plant is a five-vertex path whose middle core has two core neighbours, each with one private leaf. Give each neighbour `k` leaves by repeated births from its leaves, while prohibiting sends into the middle; any finite prescribed prefix has positive probability. Each source then has `k` other leaves for its outgoing first-send barrier. The probability of **any** future gain of the middle is at most `4/(k+1)` by a union bound. Its two neighbours are unbounded with probability at least `1−2·2^-k` by the tagged stock lower processes and a union bound; do not multiply the no-send and growth probabilities. Consequently the probability of a permanently bare middle with two positive-mass neighbours is strictly positive (already certified at `k=5`). At a finite stopping size, if just one of those neighbours is still at or below the degree cutoff, that neighbour and its private leaf stock invalidate the old-singleton read. The finite degree mask fixture explicitly checks both directions of this distinction.
