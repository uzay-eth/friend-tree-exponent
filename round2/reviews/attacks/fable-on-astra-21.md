## 2. Why a negative pointwise margin with a stationary reward bounds mu (derived by the lead)

What is certified pointwise, on every finite tree, is eps (L - s) P >= m (C - 1) + Q, with eps = +1 for the lower and -1 for the upper side, P the corrected potential (comparable to the core count C), Q >= 0 a sum of rewards attached to specified configurations, and m < 0. Put F(t) = E P(T_t), M(t) = E C(T_t). Dynkin's formula on [t, t + a], divided by M(t):

    eps (F(t+a) - F(t))/M(t) >= integral_0^a [eps s F(t+u) + m (M(t+u) - 1) + E Q(T_(t+u))]/M(t) du.

With F/M -> pi P, E Q/M -> pi Q and M(t+u)/M(t) -> e^(mu u) (the normalised mean limits), both sides carry the factor (e^(mu a) - 1)/mu and one gets eps (mu - s) pi P >= m + pi Q. If N = m + R > 0 for a certified lower bound R <= pi Q, and 0 < pi P <= B, then eps (mu - s) >= N/B: mu >= s + N/B on the lower side, mu <= s - N/B on the upper. This is exactly "trial point plus or minus paid over denominator". It is an inequality between averages; no pointwise sign is needed, and the endpoint being proved is not assumed. What it requires: that the three normalised limits exist (Astra's mean-level local limit, claim 10, and the sharp mean, claims 2 and 3), that R is a true lower bound for the stationary frequency-weighted reward (the occupation prices computed from the exact stable-age law of small fringes), that B is a true upper bound for pi P, and that no reward is counted twice across the three blocks and the earlier releases. Those four points are the ones audited only inside the GPT family.
