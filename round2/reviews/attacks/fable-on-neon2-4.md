## 2. Why every prime occurs, and why that excludes algebraicity (checked by the lead for U)

*Mechanism.* Every rate is an integer or a reciprocal of a degree, and b is a number of leaves over a degree. Starting from J (degree 2), a vertex of degree p cannot exist before p - 2 events, and after exactly p - 2 events only on one path: the top receives a leaf from one of its own leaves each time. That path has integer weight 1 * 2 * ... * (p - 2), ends at the star whose top has degree p, and contributes (p - 2)! (p - 1)/p to u_(p-1)(J) through b = (p - 1)/p. Every other term of u_(p-1)(J) involves only degrees smaller than p. So the coefficient of order p - 1 is (p - 1)!/p plus a p-integral number: valuation exactly -1, for every prime p >= 5 (for p = 2, 3 the count starts differently). This agrees with the four cases computed above and with the same observation in my child's earlier report (there for primes above 20 and with the order counted from 0).

*From denominators to transcendence.* U_s(J) has the asymptotic expansion sum u_n s^-n as s -> +infinity, with rational u_n (finite path sums; the remainder after n terms is O(s^-(n+1)) because the resolvent of the bounded-rate prefix is O(1/s)). If U were algebraic over C(s), substituting the expansion into the polynomial relation would give a formal relation (uniqueness of asymptotic expansions along the ray; convergence of the series is not needed), the relation can be taken with rational coefficients because the u_n are rational (linear algebra over Q), and then the recursion for the coefficients of a power series satisfying a polynomial equation over Z[z] produces only denominators dividing a power of one fixed integer (the elementary content of Eisenstein's theorem). Infinitely many primes in the denominators contradict this. Correct.
