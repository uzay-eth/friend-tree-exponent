## 1. The exact split (derived by the lead)

Fix a tree T_0 of size n and an edge e = {u, v} whose removal leaves two sides A (with u) and B (with v), each with at least two vertices. In discrete time a step picks a uniform vertex x of the N current vertices and a uniform neighbour of x. Give each side its *planted chain*: the side evolves by the same rule among its own vertices, the edge e counting in the degree of its endpoint as a slot; a step in which the endpoint rings and picks the slot is a *kill*. Take three independent objects: the planted chain of A (an infinite sequence of marks, killed or not), the planted chain of B, and a Polya urn started from (|A|, |B|) which says on which side the next ring happens (probability N_A/N). Until the first kill, the physical tree is a function of the three, with the right law: P(ring in A) = N_A/N, and given the side the ringing vertex is uniform on that side and its choice uniform among its neighbours. A kill is exactly a ring along e. So

    {no ring along e after time n} = {the chain of A is never killed} and {the chain of B is never killed},

because the urn gives each side infinitely many steps. This event does not involve the urn. Conditionally on it: the urn is still a Polya urn independent of the two mark sequences, so N_A/N -> U with U ~ Beta(|A|, |B|); the two mark sequences are independent of each other, each with the law of its planted chain conditioned on surviving for ever. In continuous time the same statement reads: each side has its own Yule clock (every ring on a surviving side adds a vertex to that side), independent of its marks; e^-t N_A(t) -> Gamma(|A|). What is conditioned is the embedded mark sequence only. This is the claimant's "exact rate cancellation", and it is correct. Two cautions that the proof respects and that a reader must not relax: (i) the event must be defined by the marks alone; a cut selected by a time criterion ("the first global birth is on side A") or by the hub status of the endpoints changes the Beta law (my child's exact example: sizes (2, 3), E U = 2/5 becomes 1/2); (ii) the half-limits are those of the *survival-conditioned planted* chains, not of free trees, and they are not claimed to have the law of X.

If C_A(j)/j^mu -> a and C_B(k)/k^mu -> b along the sides' own step counts, then on this event X = a U^mu + b (1 - U)^mu (cores on the two sides add up; the endpoint of a side of size at least two is counted consistently because the slot keeps its degree).

## 2. The half-limits exist and are positive (derived by the lead)

In continuous time let H(t) = e^(-mu t) C_A(t) for the surviving planted chain of A run with its own clock, and fix everything except the first holding time w of A. Claim 6 gives, for almost every w, convergence of e^(-mu t) [C_A^w(t) + C_B(t)], where C_A^w(t) = C_A^0(t - w). Taking two such values w_1 < w_2 = w_1 + d and subtracting, H(t) - q H(t - d) -> l with q = e^(-mu d) < 1, and H is bounded (it is at most the convergent total). Then limsup |H - l/(1 - q)| <= q limsup |H - l/(1 - q)|, so H converges; dividing by the Gamma mass to the power mu gives the limit a along the side's own steps. Positivity: gluing two independent copies of the same side by an edge (a reachable tree, with positive survival probability) would give X = 0 with positive probability if a had an atom at 0, against claim 6. Both steps are correct.

## 3. Absolute continuity, and the density (lead's route for part 2)

*Cover.* Almost surely there are a size n and an edge e between two cores with no ring along e after n: a hub has hub children (Addario-Berry et al.; also this lead's claim 2(e), proved differently), and along an edge between two hubs the rings have total rate 1/D_u + 1/D_v, integrable because both degrees grow exponentially; a counting process with finite compensator has finitely many points. The events F(n, T_0, e) are countably many.

*Part 1.* On F(n, T_0, e), conditionally on (a, b) with a, b > 0, X = g(U), g(u) = a u^mu + b (1 - u)^mu, with U Beta-distributed and independent of (a, b). g is strictly concave with one critical point, so the image of a Lebesgue-null set under either monotone branch is null and g(U) has a density. A countable union of events on each of which the conditional law of X is absolutely continuous gives an absolutely continuous law. This is a different mechanism from the one behind this lead's claim 2(e) (free independent proxies after a cut at a hub-hub edge with large leaf stocks, and a coupling error that tends to 0): the claimant's split is exact, with no error term, at the price of survival-conditioned half-laws.

*Part 2, by a route that needs no planted estimate.* Fix x > 0 and 0 < l < x < h. On every piece of the cover, min(a, b) <= X <= 2^(1 - mu) max(a, b). Since P(X < l) > 0 and P(X > 2^(1 - mu) h) > 0 (claim 6), some planted side S_1 that occurs in the cover has P(a < l | survival) > 0, and some planted side S_2 has P(b > h | survival) > 0. Join the roots of S_1 and S_2 by an edge: this tree is reached with positive probability, both sides survive with positive probability, and on that event (a, b) has the product law, which charges {a < l} x {b > h}. For such (a, b), g decreases on its second branch from a maximum >= b > h to g(1) = a < l, so x is a regular value inside that branch, uniformly for (a, b) in a small neighbourhood of a point of the support; the Beta density is bounded below on the compact piece of the branch and |g'| is bounded above there. Hence the law of X dominates c_x times Lebesgue measure on a neighbourhood I_x of x, with c_x > 0. A density that is locally bounded below in this sense has a lower semicontinuous version that is positive everywhere and bounded below on compact sets: take the lower semicontinuous version p_0 given by the disjoint pieces (sums and mixtures of branch densities continued by 0, lower semicontinuous by Fatou) and replace it by max(p_0, sup_j c_j 1_{I_j}) over a countable subcover, which changes p_0 on a null set only. This argument was found by my child and checked by me; it uses the same two inputs as part 1 plus the two tail facts of claim 6, and none of the inert-parent estimates.

The claimant's own route to part 2 proves more (the support of *every* surviving planted half-law is all of the half-line) and needs more: the inert-parent mean and its identification with the count limit, a uniform one-leaf gradient of the eigenfunction, and a lower bound for the amplitude along a long arm. My child audited each of those lines and supplied three missing details (the reciprocal-stock bound 14/(3K) for the kill probability from leaf stock K; the amplitude along the arm through the positive immigration formula; the normalisation needed if the gradient is taken from Neon-2's claim 3 instead of the inert theorem). None is a gap in the conclusion.

## 4. Findings

* `REPORT.md` of the claimant, the sentence that weighting the inert law by its no-kill probability "preserves its null sets": true in the direction used (inert-null implies surviving-null), false as an equivalence. Counterexample (child): from the inert newborn, promote the only leaf, grow a large leaf stock at the new core child; with positive probability the child never sends a leaf to the root, the root stays bare of degree two for ever, and the weight of the path is 0.
* The board text names "`inert` section 2" as the only extra input of part 2; section 2 gives the stock bound only; the submitted proof uses sections 1 to 3. (With the route of section 3 above no such input is needed.)
* Negative control (d) of `check_density.py` selects on a condition that is identically false, so it tests nothing about a misallocation of the directed rates; the child's own asymmetric control does (it rejects an incoming rate substituted for the kill rate).
* The claim's own caveat stands: no continuity, no local upper bound, no smoothness of the full density; the Dirichlet multi-cut smoothing is valid for each fixed cut and localisation and is not summable over the cover as written.
