## 2. Where the blind proofs differ from the claimant's

1. *Variance weight.* Claimant: the effect of one leaf on a conditional mean is at most 3 e^(2u) (coupling with "dirty" cores), so the variance bound carries the total number of vertices n(T) and the argument needs 2 mu > 1 (true: mu >= 113/200). R6P: a different coupling gives e^(2u), and a refinement: before the first failed paired ring all unmatched vertices are extra leaves at the attachment vertex x, the failure intensity is at most L/(d_x + 1), hence the influence of a leaf at a vertex of degree d >= k is at most e^(2u) (2u + 1 - e^(-u))/(d + 1). Summing squared influences against the attachment rates gives a bound proportional to the core count C(T), not to n(T). I checked this derivation line by line (drift of the number of unmatched vertices <= 2B because every unmatched component has at most one edge to the paired tree; first two Yule moments; q_x/(d+1)^2 <= 1). Consequence: R6P's proof does not need mu > 1/2. Both arguments are valid; the claim does not depend on the refinement.
2. *Positivity of the limit.* Claimant: L^2 bound for the martingale e^(-mu t) Psi(T_t) (recursion with horizon log t), conditional Cauchy-Schwarz and Levy's 0-1 law, using C <= C_3 (1 + Psi) and the almost-sure exponent of round 1. R6P: exponential L^1 rate of convergence of e^(-mu t) C(T_t), so that a zero limit would force C(T_t) <= e^((mu - a) t), contradicting the almost-sure exponent of round 1. Both use the round-1 almost-sure exponent; R6P does not need the lower comparison L6.
3. *Tails.* Small values: stars, in both. Large values: claimant uses paths and the exact identity E[X | T_m] = d_2 Gamma(m)/Gamma(m + mu) Psi(T_m) with Psi(path) >= m/(2 C_3); R6P uses a hub carrying b three-vertex branches. Both valid; R6P's E X_T = Psi(T)/E Z^mu is the same identity (E Z^mu = Gamma(m + mu)/Gamma(m) for the Gamma(m) limit of the Yule size).
4. *Horizon.* Claimant: conditioning horizon of order log t; R6P: proportional to t. Either works.
5. *Strip.* Claimant: sigma exists by compactness (finitely many zeros of det(I - K(z)) in a compact rectangle, none on Re z = mu except mu). R6A: an explicit sigma. After the repair of section 4 its certified width is mu - sigma of order 10^-354: explicit and useless as a number; the claim needs existence only. My Monte Carlo scan of the kernel at complex arguments (my FACT F21) saw no further eigenvalue near 1 on its grid (Re s from 0.30 to mu, |Im s| <= 30), which suggests a gap of order 0.1 or more; that is numerical and partly outside the half-plane Re s > 51/100 where the kernel is proved to exist.

## 3. The three steps the owner named as weakest

**(a) Uniform planted-state error.** Statement: |P_t f(S) - e^(mu t) H(S) d(f)| <= C e^(sigma t) W(S) for every marked planted fringe S and every score |f| <= W, and then |E_T N_{>=k}(T_t) - e^(mu t) Psi(T) d_k| <= C_1 C(T) e^(sigma t) for every finite tree T. Chain: P_t f = R_t f + (direct births of the residual family, each a newborn of one of 19 labels); the newborn asymptotics is a statement about a 19 x 19 matrix, hence uniform; the three error terms are bounded by e^(rho t) W(S), by int_0^t e^(rho u) e^(sigma (t-u)) du W(S) and by the tail of the integral defining H, and all three use only R_t W <= e^(rho t) W, i.e. the weight inequality L1. Uniformity in the state therefore comes from the exact inequality L1 and from nothing else; unbounded degrees enter only through W, and W(S) <= (number of cores of S)/d. From fringes to trees: every core x contributes to the fringes of its ancestors with the path factor z <= 10/13 per step, so sum_v W(S_v) <= C_0 C(T) with C_0 = 2/(d (1 - 10/13)); this is where a long bare path would break a cruder count, and it does not. I re-derived each of these inequalities. The founder adds at most 1 to each count; the cap of the marks at 1/20 does not cap the degrees that are counted.

**(b) Contour estimate.** Needs: (i) k(t) absolutely continuous with |k'| <= 42 e^(rho t)/d, from the bound |R B_0 g| <= 42 W on the jump intensity of a bounded score (checked: gains of the top cost at most 1 + 38, promotions 1, death 1/2, copies (13/20)^2/(7/10) W); hence K(z) = O(1/|z|); (ii) no zero of det(I - K(z)) on Re z = mu except mu: if K(mu + i tau) v = v then |v| is the Perron vector and equality in the triangle inequality is needed in column 2, where every row has the density k_{i2}(t) >= (1/2) e^(-(1/i + 3/2) t) (a newborn that has had no event still has degree 2 and promotes its leaf at rate 1/2); impossible for tau != 0; (iii) simple pole from the Perron structure; (iv) inversion applied to K^2 (I - K)^(-1) = O(|z|^-2), not to the full transform. All four are in the claimant's text in compressed form; (ii) is the step where the blind proof erred (section 4) and the claimant did not.

**(c) Second-moment and supermartingale steps.** Claimant's recursion E(Z*_{j+1})^2 <= (1 + C_9 (j+2)^-2) E(Z*_j)^2 + C_10 [(j+2)^(4/eta) e^(-(2 mu - 1) j) n(T) + (j+2)^-2 e^(-2 mu j)] with horizon h = log(j+2)/eta: re-derived (the factor (j+2)^-2 is e^(-2 eta h); the first additive term is e^(4h) E n(T_j) e^(-2 mu (j+1)) and is summable exactly because 2 mu > 1; the lower comparison turns C^2 into 1 + Psi^2). Conditional positivity bound and Levy's law: re-derived. The stopped supermartingale of claim 7 is treated in `fable-on-astra-7b.md` with an exact test.
