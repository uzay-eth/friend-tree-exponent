## 1. The negative half of Theorem 2, derived by the lead

*Point masses of the reference law.* The reference law is that of the inert newborn (one core with one leaf and a silent parent slot counted in its degree) observed at an age uniform on [0, log N]. Its volume V is a pure birth process: at volume v every vertex rings at rate 1 and each ring adds a vertex inside, except a ring of the root that picks the slot, so the birth rate is v - 1/D_root, between v - 1/2 and v. The process visits each value once, and the expected time spent at v is at most 1/(v - 1/2). Hence the reference law gives the value v mass at most 1/((v - 1/2) log N). This is the claim's bound.

*From there to total variation.* The empirical law of the M surviving components is carried by at most M volumes. For a threshold v_0 let S be the set of observed volumes that are at least v_0. The empirical law gives S the fraction of components with volume at least v_0, which by Theorem 1 is 1 - log v_0/log N + o(1); the reference law gives S at most M/((v_0 - 1/2) log N). With M of order (n/N)^mu log N (Theorem 1) and v_0 = K (n/N)^mu, the second quantity is of order 1/K, and for N = n^alpha the first is 1 - mu (1/alpha - 1) - o(1). Letting K grow slowly gives liminf TV >= 1 - mu (1/alpha - 1), positive exactly when alpha > mu/(1 + mu) = 0.3613. Correct. It should be read for what it is: the empirical law has too few atoms to be close in total variation to a law that is spread over volumes larger than the number of components; it is a statement about the sampling, not a failure of the limiting shape, and Theorem 1's convergence of the distribution function is unaffected.

## 2. Consistency with this lead's claim 4

Claim 4(a) says that the promotion age of a core chosen uniformly among all cores is asymptotically Exp(mu); here the age of a counted component is uniform on [0, log N]. No conflict: the populations differ. A uniform core is young with large probability because the number of cores grows like e^(mu t); a counted component is born at a promotion by one of the K deleted hubs, which ring at a constant total rate over a time interval of length about log N, and log-volume is about age. Claim 4(b) bounds the probability that a fringe of age a has received anything from a parent of degree d by 1 - e^(-a/d); the claimant's coupling uses the same first-incoming-arrow mechanism with a/d in place of 1 - e^(-a/d), and its constant is consistent (child's comparison). The counts of my child's earlier simulation at fixed cut-offs (ratio M N^mu/(C log N) = 0.683, 0.675, 0.685, 0.681 at n = 10^6 and 0.685, 0.684, 0.683, 0.689 at n = 10^7 for N = 10, 30, 100, 300) agree with the audit child's new ones (0.68 at n = 2^20 and 2^24) and with the scale of Theorem 1; they are at fixed N and do not test a growing cut-off.
