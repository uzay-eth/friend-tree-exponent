## Physical clock, conditional expectation, concentration

Each vertex rings at rate one and chooses a live neighbor uniformly; a new leaf is attached at that **receiver**. At size `n` the minimum holding time is `Exp(n)`, independent of the ringing label and its target mark. Iterating the conditional transition law separates the complete size-clock sequence from the discrete tree sequence. The Yule size started at `m` is the sum of `m` unit families, so its normalized limit is `Gamma(m,1)`; the gamma shape is 2 for the edge, not 3 just because the first post-edge shape is deterministic. The limit identity follows directly by multiplying `C/N^μ` by `(e^(−t)N)^μ`. For a restarted deterministic tree, with `F=d_2 Ψ`, the Gamma moment cancels the ratio in (L2), giving `E_T Z_T=F(T)`. Restart at time `t` identifies `M_t=e^(−μt)F(T_t)` as the terminal conditional expectation; convergence to `Z_T` follows because that limit is measurable in the full path.

For the finite-terminal-count martingale, the total rate into leaf receivers is the total promotion rate `Σ L_v/D_v≤C`; these jumps use the unrefined (L4) influence. Into a core receiver, the rate is at most `D_v`, which cancels the squared refined denominator since `D_v/(D_v+1)^2≤1`. Thus the raw squared-jump rate at remaining horizon `s` is bounded by `C e^(4s)[1+(2s+1)^2]` even if total size is arbitrarily large at fixed core count. The bound applies to the whole pre-jump state, including the jump that crosses the stopping barrier. The deterministic conditional mean between jumps has finite variation (first-jump convolution at each fixed finite tree), so no continuous martingale variation is omitted.

At scale `r≥C(T)`, on no previous failure the martingale stays below `(d_2 c''+η)r`; the lower comparison converts this to `C_j≤Dr e^(μj)` at block start. Core growth is Yule-dominated because only promotions increase `C`, one at a time at total rate at most `C`. A geometric-population exponential moment bounds the one-block crossing probability of `Hr e^(μj)` by `exp(−r e^(μj))` for a fixed sufficiently large `H`. The block martingale conditions on one **fixed terminal time** `j+1+s_j`, where `s_j=s_0+δj`; it is not the rolling conditional mean. On the good block, (L3) costs at most `2KHr e^(−(μ−σ)s_j)` at the two endpoints. Here `a=2−μ`, `δ=μ/(8a)` and `β=min(μ/4,(μ−σ)δ/2)`. The inverse normalized Freedman exponent has two terms: the bracket term decays (up to a quadratic polynomial) at `μ−2aδ−2β≥μ/4`, and the bounded-jump term at `μ−aδ−β≥5μ/8`. The horizon start `s_0` is chosen after fixing `H` and `η`, uniformly in both `T` and `r`. The conditional bound for a first bad block is summable as `A exp(−br)`; no interblock independence is used. Summing the good tolerances gives a total error at most `ηr`. This proves (b) and the high-deviation version used in (c).

For the upper bound in (c), the exponential tail for `Z` and independent Gamma multiplier give `E X^k≤A B^k k!/Γ(2+μk)`. Its log is at most `(1−μ)k log k+O(k)`, so choose the Markov moment of order `x^(1/(1−μ))` with a small fixed coefficient. For the matching bound, at binary frontier size `k` the tree has `4k−1` vertices. Each of the `j` remaining frontier leaves can be promoted by its parent with probability at least `1/(18k)` per discrete step: the parent's degree stays at most three during those promotions. Then each of the `j` unserved new tips has a distinct ringing leaf, giving probability at least `j/(8k)` to add a leaf to some such tip. The two factorials cancel the potential `k log k` costs; doubling gives a total probability `exp(−O(m))`. From the resulting positive core density, (b) and `P(W_m≤2m)→1` intersect with probability at least `1/2` for large `m`. Use a union bound here: `Z_T` and `W_m` are dependent. The restart yields `X≥b m^(1−μ)`, and adjacent available sizes have a bounded ratio.

## Uniform inverse moments and the leading left constant

At `C≥2` the core graph is a tree with two distinct tips. Each has at least one leaf. Until any promotion no leaves are lost. Use one marked ring clock at each tip and one tagged leaf clock at each tip. The four sources are distinct. A tip's guaranteed promotion threshold is `1/2` before the tagged leaf first rings and `2/3` thereafter. The auxiliary no-flag survival is `(6/5)e^(−2t/3)−(1/5)e^(−3t/2)` for one tip; squaring gives the claimed `(36/25)e^(−4t/3)` envelope for two. It is an envelope with a prefactor, **not** an instantaneous lower bound `4/3` on the true total hazard.

To get a uniform *subexponential* hitting-time moment, the two-tip estimate alone is insufficient. For an independent check of the additional ingredient, let `ℓ` be the number of core tips. If `ℓ≥C/12`, use disjoint tip clocks, each with a first-half positive chance to witness its promotion threshold. Otherwise the non-degree-two vertices touch at most `4ℓ−6` edges (degree sum in a tree); hence a linear number of edges join two core-degree-two vertices. Match disjoint pairs along those paths. Mark a helper ring ≤`1/3` in the first half, and a base ring ≤`1/3` in the second half. On no promotion, a helper with leaves would itself promote; without leaves it has two core neighbors and can rank the base first. The base then has at least one leaf, so its mark forces promotion. These independent per-site flags are defined without conditioning their laws on no promotion. Thus for each fixed positive `t`, the supremum over core size `c` of `P(wait>t)` tends to zero. The two-tip envelope dominates the exponential-moment integral for every `r<4/3`; the sup moments `B_c(r)` tend to 1. By the strong Markov property, the hitting-time moment of level `k` is bounded by `∏_{c=2}^{k−1}B_c(r)=exp(o(k))`, uniformly also for seeds already above the level.

At level `k` the chance that the future normalized amplitude misses a fixed linear lower threshold is at most `A e^(−bk)`, by (b) and (L2). Let `K` be the first successful level in these **future** tests. It need not be a stopping time: only the hitting time `H_k` is used for restarting, and `P(K=k)` is bounded by failure at the preceding level. Hölder with `q>1` and `pqμ<4/3` pays `e^(pμH_k)` on `K=k`; the exponential smallness beats the subexponential moment. This gives `sup_{C≥2} E Z_T^(−p)<∞` for `0<p<4/(3μ)`. For the star-exit tree at size `m+1`, `Z=(W_{m+1}/m)^μ V_m` with independent multiplier. Jensen bounds the inverse multiplier moment uniformly below, so the same uniform inverse-moment estimate holds for `V_m`, including an exponent strictly above `1/μ`.

The infinite-hub description is a reduced Markov process, **not** a uniform choice among infinitely many leaves. An ideal hub clock adds a fresh tip, away vertices follow the ordinary process and sends to the hub have no effect on the recorded state. The total rate of changes is bounded by a Yule size for the recorded vertices. A real hub starts at degree `m−1`; its first ring selecting a core has conditional rate at most `C/(m−1)`. Up to that time the reduced paths couple exactly; the Yule bound supplies `2(e^t−1)/(m−1)` at fixed horizon. Repeat the coupling from a matching state at time `t` for the fixed look-ahead `s`, bounding the conditional core expectation error by Cauchy–Schwarz and the core Yule second moment. This proves convergence of the fixed-time conditional look-ahead observable. To pass to the amplitude, (L3) gives a uniform bound on `E e^(−μt)C_t` and (b) gives a small relative conditional error at time `t` when `C_t` is large. The two-tip bound makes `C_t→∞` uniformly in probability by bounding fixed level hitting times. Take small relative tolerance, then large fixed `t,s`; the (L3) look-ahead bias is `O(e^(−(μ−σ)s))` in mean. Tightness plus uniqueness of subsequential limits proves the weak limit, with positivity by the uniform inverse moment. The Gamma ratio tending to one transfers it to `V_m`. There is no claim that each real finite-hub amplitude couples to a common infinite-hub amplitude for all times.

The last star-size law is exactly `2/[m(m−1)]`, `m≥3`, and after that exit the shape is deterministic up to isomorphism. In the mixture set `d=ε^(1/μ)` and `y=md`. Away from 0 and infinity the normalized sum is a bounded mesh integral with integrand converging at the continuity points of the limiting CDF. Atoms of `V` would contribute only countably many integration discontinuities; no density input or uniform convergence of CDFs is needed.

## No-cross coupling, support and variance

From a core carrying a leaf, the tracked leaf count dominates the linear birth–death process with individual rates `1,1/2`; extra arrivals can be ignored and removal is at most `L/2`. The normalized lower process is bounded in `L²` and has positive survival amplitude with a fixed positive probability. Conditional event martingales, first at deterministic times and then along the promotion times of each fixed vertex, give a hub and a hub child almost surely. The mother's core degree is at most initial core degree plus her ring count, so eventually her leaf fraction tends to one and there are infinitely many promotions. This uses conditional lower bounds at each trial, not independence among candidate children. The selected edge maximizing its smaller endpoint leaf stock therefore has both stocks tending to infinity.

Conditional on the cut history, construct **independent free paths** and give the full tree extra rejection marks at the endpoint rings. Before mismatch, each actual cross choice has rate `1/(D_free+1)`; other choices have the right uniform marginal. With `L` initial leaves, a linear number of independent lower birth–death families sustain a fixed positive multiple of `e^(s/2)` for all future `s`, except with probability exponentially small in `L`. The hazard integral is stopped at the first violation of the resulting degree curve: it is not taken under the law conditioned on future survival or on no cross. This bounds total mismatch probability by `O(1/L_1+1/L_2)`. The **unconditioned future proxy given the cut history** has independent discrete component amplitudes and Beta split independent of those amplitudes. The proxy equals the actual amplitude on successful coupling, but its law *given success* can be biased. For fixed positive amplitudes the Beta pushforward under `a u^μ+b(1−u)^μ` is absolutely continuous, using its two regular branches and the null singleton of the maximum. For each null set the actual probability is bounded by the mean mismatch plus the probability of an unusable cut, which tend to zero. A first-edge decomposition would instead have instantaneous side rate `N_1−1/D_a+1/D_b`, and has no such exact product law.

For full support, any finite shape is reachable, and moving a deepest leaf to a fixed root monotonically lowers the total root distance until the tree is a star. Compare successive relocation states by two additions to the same size-`m−1` seed. Optimizing the two (L3) error bounds and one (L4) bound gives order `C^(a/(a+g))`, with `g=μ−σ` and the harmless `1+C` convention. At the first crossing of a target conditional mean `x>0` from star to path, the lower comparison and bounded change in cores give `C=O_x(m^μ)` in all three states. The conditional-mean mesh width is then `O_x(m^(−μg/(a+g)))`; the upper comparison forces `C` of order at least `m^μ` on the chosen crossing states. Concentration and the Gamma ratio make the restarted law tend to a point at `x` along these states. A sufficiently large *finite* crossing state with positive reach probability gives every prescribed neighborhood positive mass. Closure and (L1) supply the boundary `0` and exclude negative points.

Finally the L² martingale is `Q_m=E(X|T_m)=d_2 Γ(m)/Γ(m+μ) Ψ(T_m)`. The right-tail bound supplies L² of `X`; alternatively the lower comparison and moment controls justify the limiting mean. In detail, the pathwise inequality `C(T_m)≤(Ψ(T_m)+1)/c'` and the uniformly L²-bounded martingale `Q_m` make `C(T_m)/m^μ` uniformly integrable, yielding `E X` as the mean-asymptotic coefficient. Orthogonality gives the variance by conditioning **the next** `Ψ` on `T_m`; at size two the next shape is deterministic, so the displayed sum begins at `m=3`. The continuous-time first wait need not have zero variance; confusing it with the deterministic discrete first shape would drop the edge clock contribution.
