# Tracking the return of the last selected leaf at finite size

For an old tree of size `b−1`, the older of the next two births has strictly greater conditional probability of a limiting share above `x` whenever `b≥20`, `0<x<1`, and `(b+1)x≥13/10`. The four closed boxes checked here cover the interval from `13/10` to `23/10`. The preceding finite-size result applies at every larger threshold. Since `b+1≥21`, the early paired transport receipts then give unconditional strict order for every adjacent pair from label 2 onward throughout `[13/210,1)`. The two founders have equal laws. ## Three states for the physical lower bound

Give every discrete birth at current size $s$ an auxiliary activation with conditional probability $R/s$, $R=18$. These activations mark physical births. At any reached state, take a disjoint set of physical transitions of the required rates, thin rates with excess, and use remaining physical births with auxiliary randomness to supply the required synthetic transitions. Finally fill the activation probability to $R/s$ with holds. The sum of selected rates fits within $R$, and $R\le m=b+1$ at the initial completion. Consequently the conditional probability of each activation outcome is its listed rate divided by $R$, independently of the size at activation.

* In a positive-stock state $(d,j)$ the physical tag degree is at least $d$. Keep $j$ specified neighbouring leaves, with another permanent neighbour outside the subset. Their sends create leaves at the tag with total rate $j$. The tag removes a specified leaf from this subset at rate at most $j/d$. Supplement this loss to exactly $j/d$. On gain use $(d+1,j+1)$, on loss use $(d,j-1)$. On the last loss keep track of the discarded neighbour. It has degree two after a physical promotion and degree one after a supplementary loss.
* At zero stock, with physical tag degree at least $d$ and the discarded neighbour's degree at most $k$, thin sends from that neighbour to rate $1/k$. They create a new leaf at the tag and give $(d+1,1)$. The discarded neighbour gets a new leaf at rate at most $k-1+1/d$ because one of its contacts is the tag. Include every such growth and supplement to this upper rate, then increase $k$ by one. The two transitions are disjoint. Other tag gains can be ignored. A favourable pending leaf with physical parent degree $k$ starts the same process with $d=1$.
* The serial middle also has its old host. Until leaving this initial state it has tagged stock one, physical degree at least two and an old host of degree at most $k$. Use a gain at rate $1+1/k$ from the leaf and the old host, a supplemented loss of rate $1/2$, and old-host growth supplemented to $k-1/2$. At gain use $(3,2)$, at loss the zero-stock state with $(d,k)=(2,2)$, and at old-host growth continue with the incremented bound. At the host cutoff use the ordinary positive-stock state $(2,1)$.

Write $H_{s,j}$ for the exact Poisson–beta lower payoff on the specified leaves, with $H_{s,0}=0$, and $\Delta H_j=H_{s+1,j}-H_{s+1,j-1}$. It is harmonic for the gain and loss rates $j$ and $1$. In the three cases just given, the positive contributions at the next activation respectively equal $(1-j/d)\Delta H_j/R$, $(1/k)\Delta H_1/R$, and $[(1/2)\Delta H_1+(1/k)\Delta H_2]/R$. This follows by summing the one-step harmonic discrepancy at each pre-activation size and using the activation hazard $R/s$. Leaving a tracked set of states, or reaching the fixed count of 110 activations, ends the extra credits. The physical continuation always supplies the corresponding $H$. Taking the larger of the serial construction with and without its host is valid separately on each starting tree. [Sharp Poisson payoff](../../../hub-ranking/poisson-tail/README.md)

## Integrating the physical clock

At activation number $a$, the inverse threshold multiplier has logarithm distributed as the sum of $a$ independent rate-$R$ exponentials. This is the exact stopped beta product at the starting size. Its survival at $s$ is $e^{-Rs}\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}(Rs)^i/i!$. Subtraction of a right upper bound from a left lower bound gives a rational lower bound on the mass of each integration cell. The checker uses cells $e^s=(129/128)^i$. For the logarithmic step it uses the positive series $2\sum_{j\ge0}[(2\cdot128+1)^{-(2j+1)}]/(2j+1)$ with a geometric remainder. Powers $e^{-Rs}$ have rational values at the mesh endpoints. The initial-size binomial lower rectangles and their normalization by $(1-x)^{m-1}$ are the same as in the beta-product proof. Each term retained by the backward finite computation is nonnegative. Degree 14 and return-neighbour degree 16 are cutoffs for extra source only. [Exact occupation and integration rows](check.py)

## Spending the physical credits

Write $M$ for the serial bound, $H_k$ for its improvement with an old host of degree $k$, $F_k$ for the favourable return lower bound, and $U_k$ for the locked adverse upper. Set $H_k=M$ and $F_k=0$ where the finite records are absent. For a core of degree $d$, core-arity $c$, ordinary leaves $l=d-c$ and core-neighbour reciprocal sum $r$, put $s=(l+r)/(d(d+1))$ and $t=1/(2d)$. Its serial credit after the old-leaf debits is at least
\[
 ds(H_{d+1}-U_2)+lt(H_2-U_2)
 -l(t-s)_+(U_2-F_{d+1})_+
 -l(s-t)_+(U_{d+1}-F_2)_+ .
\]
The two signs of the old-edge coefficient attach the adverse price to one endpoint and the favourable return price to the other. Bare degree-two core chains use serial price $H_3$. Every old tree here has at least 19 vertices, so an old tree with at most one core is a star. The inherited star check tests the displayed net credit directly. For unbounded degrees with $l>d/2$ the high-star bound also follows from $s\ge t$ and the positive surplus $M-U_2-z$, with $z=17/(16\cdot32y)$ and $y$ the lower edge of the box. The two unbounded-degree core bounds from the preceding proof use $H_2-U_2$ for the leaf serial gains, retaining $M-U_2$ at the centre. All retained-site and contracted-chain budgets use the original tree degree identity. [Exact signed ledger](ledger.py)
