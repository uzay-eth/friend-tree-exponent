## Alternating embedding, including residual marginals

Hold the chosen common matching fixed for one joint step. Orient both copies from the focal roots. At an even pair, all neighbours of the B vertex are matched: children by the injection and the parent by the matching above (no parent for either root). Their matched A partners are odd and have degrees no larger than their B partners, so the sum of their reciprocal degrees is at least the full B attachment rate. Extra A neighbours add nonnegative rates. At an odd pair the same reasoning with letters reversed gives the opposite inequality. These are comparisons of **attachment** rates `r(w)`, obtained by summing incoming arrows; they are not comparisons of the rate-one initiating clocks.

On each matched pair put common rate `min(r_A,r_B)`. In each copy, sum its residual rates, including all unmatched vertices. That sum is `n−Σ min(r_A,r_B)` in both copies since each of the `n` senders contributes a total unit rate. If the residual sum is `R>0`, put rate `r_A^res(u)r_B^res(v)/R` on independent residual target pairs. The row and column sums, after adding the common part and dividing by `n`, are exactly the physical target probabilities. If `R=0` omit this term. Common births extend the matching to both newborns. Residual attachments in A are at even matched targets or outside the matched subtree, and in B at odd matched targets or outside; in every case leaving the newborn unmatched is legal. This also works when residual updates change a neighbour's future reciprocal, since all comparisons are recomputed at the next step. Induct on the common size; the even roots are ordered in each coupled time slice, and the physical limiting fractions retain that order. The equal-size hypothesis matters for the embedded chain: equal **unnormalized** residual total is not available by this argument when the denominators differ. Coupling two rooted copies does not order the vertices simultaneously in the same realized labelled tree.

For (b), match the chosen leaf child of the first root to the second root's only child. The first member of that odd pair has no descendants to inject. This proves domination of **every leaf** of the finite tree by any vertex with a leaf neighbour, and in particular the asserted parent–leaf order. The initial equal laws follow from exchanging labels 1 and 2.

Independent replay from this folder, `taskset -c 13 nice -n 10 timeout 12s python3 -Bu check.py`, exhausts small labelled history pairs and computes rates by summing arrows. It checked **404** comparable rooted pairs, **2,832** positive joint target pairs, and **192** zero-residual cases, plus **120** exact append hazards. Neither transition code nor witness code is imported from Fable.

## Strictness and near one

On positive transport mass the earlier root is a core and the later root is a leaf. The earlier root's unmatched child gives strictly positive residual rate, and the later root's parent has a strict residual contribution `1−1/D_A(root)>0` across the matched root edge. Under the product residual rule, force finitely many births at precisely these two targets; matching and strict residual support persist through the script. Then the earlier root has many leaves while the later root is a leaf of a stocked parent. For `0<x<1`, the first has conditional mean limiting share close to one (leaf-stock submartingale), the second has mean at most the reciprocal of common size (degree supermartingale). A union bound after the finite forcing gives positive mass of `Z_A>x≥Z_B`. At `x=0` replace the latter mean bound by the vanishing first-promotion hazard bound using other leaves; the endpoint audit checks its supermartingale. The ordered coupling makes the reverse event impossible. This proves strictness for each threshold separately, not a gap uniform in `x`. It applies to any certified pair with positive core-to-leaf mass, including the later single-program pair if that certificate is used; the pinned (d) itself states `u≤19`.

For (e), the attachment deficit is `D_v−r_v=Σ_(w~v)(1−1/D_w)` and is nondecreasing. At a fixed restart size `n0`, shifted degree is thus dominated by a fractional Pólya urn with red mass `r_v` and white mass `n0−r_v`. The common-uniform coupling preserves order because the red-count difference is an integer for the fixed restart comparison. At birth, the parent is at least degree two, so using shift `1/2` uniformly gives `Beta(1/2,v−1/2)`. For the matching unconditional lower bound, birth on an old leaf has positive mass, so the parent starts at degree two. Under the all-append script from that history it stays degree two and the rates at the marked vertex are exactly `j−v+1/2`, not just the leaf-stock contribution. The product of their probabilities to size `m` is of order `m^(−(v−1/2))`; the leaf-stock submartingale then gives conditional mean deficit `O(v/m)`, turning `m≍1/ε` into a positive conditional continuation probability. This is a fixed-label exponent, not a sharp constant or an asymptotic in the birth label at fixed threshold. More generally the restart exponent is `n0−r_v`, so the best annealed exponent is not imposed on every birth tree. Founders are exchangeable and have exponent one.

[Astra's prior near-one window](../../open/age-order/preplacement-transfer/positive/README.md) is a distinct analytic comparison, with explicit sufficient `ε` and an earlier-label lower bound uniform over its size-`v` birth tree by forcing immediate promotion. That bound uses the weaker `ε^v` lower power. Fable improves the unconditional exponent to `v−1/2` by retaining the old parent contribution on a positive-probability birth sector. Neither near-one result furnishes dominance for all labels at a fixed intermediate threshold.
