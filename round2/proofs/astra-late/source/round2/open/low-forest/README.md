# Q5: the bounded-degree forest seen from a core

Addario-Berry, Briend, Devroye, Donderwinkel, Kerriou and Lugosi, [*Random friend trees*, arXiv:2403.20185v1, §7, item (5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.20185v1#S7), ask for the law of the forest obtained by retaining vertices of degree at most $`N`$, and whether its components resemble young friend trees.

**Result, using Claim 10.** For every fixed integer `N≥2`, the empirical law of a component seen from a uniform surviving core converges almost surely in total variation on **finite** rooted component types. Nonisolated component frequencies divided by `C_n` converge in total variation as finite measures. The limiting core radius has an explicit exponential bound, and the component volume a polynomial bound. The estimate holds for every fixed finite cutoff. The bounds are not uniform as `N→∞`; in the successive limit `n→∞` then `N→∞`, the uniform-core component diverges in size and converges locally to the full one-ended decorated limit.

## 1. Degree rule and the three sampling laws

Degrees in the test `≤N` are **original** degrees, before any deletion. In the marked core tree keep cores with `2≤D≤N` and the edges between them; restore all original leaf neighbours of retained cores. The other original leaves are isolated, and deleted cores are not counted as isolated. A retained core can itself become an isolated component if it has no leaves and all its core neighbours are deleted. All physical boundary edges of a component lead to a core of degree `>N`. When building the law under the limit `π`, orient cores toward the unique infinite end (the founder direction in the finite genealogical description). These directions are not needed for the unrooted degree cut. The boundary may be recorded as stubs with the original degrees of their external endpoints; it is a reporting rule, not a silent boundary dynamic.

* **Uniform ordinary vertex.** The conditional probability of lying in a nonisolated component is at most `(N+1)C_n/n→0`. Sampling only surviving ordinary vertices has the same trivial limit, since at least `n−C_n` vertices survive.
* **Uniform core / uniform surviving core.** Under `π` perform the degree cut at the root, taking a cemetery state if it is deleted. For a uniform surviving core, condition on `2≤D_o≤N`; its limiting mass `p_N` is positive (already degree 2 has positive mass). These are core-size-biased component laws. If conditioning on being in a nonisolated component, delete the isolated-core outcomes and renormalize.
* **Count each component once.** For an unrooted type `F` with a surviving core, the limiting frequency per `C_n` is

  ```
  f_N(F) = E_π[ 1{root survives, U_N(o) has type F} / k_N(o) ],
  ```

  where `U_N` is the decorated component and `k_N` its number of surviving original cores. The same formula works for types with recorded original kinds/degrees/stubs. For unmarked shapes forget the decorations **after** weighting by `1/k`. Equivalent: count at the unique highest surviving core (its parent is deleted; the founder is a one-count exception). If all original singleton leaves are included, the singleton frequency divided by `C_n` diverges; the formula concerns components with cores. The total mass on core-containing components is `π(D_o≤N, D_parent>N)`. It is positive: in the stable-age formula a newborn has positive probability to be born from a promoter already beyond `N`, and to stay virgin for a short time. A uniform core-containing component has law `f_N/Σ_F f_N(F)`; restrict to nonisolated shapes if those are the components of interest.

For any finite rooted type with `r` ordinary vertices, its event is determined by exploration out through the component and its original boundary (radius at most `r+1` in the marked core topology, enough to read full degrees). Claim 10 gives convergence on these cylinders, including with exact finite stubs. Once finiteness under `π` is proved, their probabilities sum to one (including cemetery); pointwise convergence on this countable alphabet yields total variation. Weighting by `1/k≤1` preserves finite-measure total variation. Top count and inverse-size count agree on every finite tree apart from the founder convention; under `π` equivalence also follows by mass transport. For exact finite frequencies one can use Claim 10's `π(f)=ν(F_{j,f})` on a ball of radius `j`, with `ν` the positive residual occupation mixture of the nineteen newborn labels. This is an explicit renewal expression for each finite frequency, not an independently evaluated shape table.

For example the isolated-core intensity is exactly `π(2≤D_o≤N, L_o=0, every core neighbour has degree>N)`. The intensity of two-vertex components is the sum of `π(2≤D_o≤N, L_o=1, every core neighbour has degree>N)` and half the probability that the root has a single surviving core neighbour, both endpoints have zero leaves, and every other core neighbour of either endpoint is high. The `1/2` affects only the two-core term. At `N=2` all shapes are paths: the same ordinary path can come from a run of degree-two cores with zero, one, or two original leaves at its endpoints, subject to the boundary rule. It need not be a whole two-vertex (or longer) descendant fringe.

## 2. Backward window: a quantitative radius bound for all fixed N

Write `R_N` for the maximum core distance in the surviving component of the root, `−∞` on deletion. The **spatial eigenmeasure** identity `π Q_t=e^(μt) π` lets us construct the law at time zero from a time `−t` under `π`: evolve physical cells for time `t`, sum the observation over the descendants of the one old root label, and multiply by `e^(−μt)`. No independently reweighted ancestors and no independence of cells are asserted. Conditional on the starting tree, the old label's cell size `Y_t` satisfies `E Y_t²≤2 e^(2t)` by rate-one promotion domination. The old core tree itself has unchanged edges, and each new core lies in the cell of the old vertex at which its newly grown branch was founded.

1. **Long path through recent edges.** A simple path from a focal cell member goes up within its cell, possibly traverses a segment of the old tree, then possibly goes down through a recent branch. If it stays wholly within its cell it has at most two recent segments, before and after its turning point. A recent segment of length `j` guarantees that some vertex in its cell has depth at least `j` from the old anchor. Iterated promotion compensation bounds the probability of that event by `t^j/j!`, uniformly in the full old environment (there must be at least one individual at exactly depth `j`).

2. **Many old edges.** A path of `j` old edges in a bounded-degree component is contained in the old low-degree graph. While all its vertices remain low, the directed sends along its `j` edges are accepted at total rate at least `2j/N`. Each adds one to the sum of degrees on the path. Initially each of its `j+1` vertices has degree at least 2, so at most `(j+1)(N−2)` such sends can occur before a degree exceeds `N`. Other gains only help. A common thinning driver gives the uniform survival bound

   ```
   P(path stays low) ≤ P(Poisson(2jt/N) ≤ (j+1)(N−2)).
   ```

   The argument works for a fixed path in the full old environment. Stop thinning on the first high vertex; if the path remains low, all the base proposals ran for time `t`. For `t≥4N²`, the threshold is at most half the mean, so the Poisson bound is at most `exp(−jt/(4N))`. This uses the elementary lower-tail Chernoff `P(Pois(u)≤u/2)≤exp(−u/8)`. There are at most `N^j` rooted old low paths of length `j` (restrict to old vertices with initial degree `≤N`). By Cauchy–Schwarz, multiplying their survival indicator by the focal-cell size costs at most `√2 e^t` times the square root of this union bound. All future root weighting is thus paid even when the path and cell interact.

For explicit constants use a backward window of length `t=k/(16N)` and take `r=k/3` for `k` a multiple of 3. Every simple length-`k` path from a focal member has either ≥`r` old edges, or a recent segment of length ≥`r`. In the second case, when there is no old segment of length ≥`r`, the long recent segment lies either in the focal cell or in a cell whose old anchor is within distance `<r` in the original low graph from the focal old root. This set has at most `2 N^r` cells, including the focal cell (include it separately if its old anchor was already high). Conditional union plus Cauchy–Schwarz gives a single recent charge. For the old segment use at most `N^r` paths of length `r` starting at the focal old root. We get the explicit bound

```
π(R_N ≥ k) ≤ min(1,
  2 e^((1−μ)t) sqrt( N^r exp(−rt/(4N)) )
  + 2 e^((1−μ)t) sqrt( 2 N^r t^r/r! ) ),
  k=3r, t=k/(16N), t≥4N².
```

For the second term use `N t/r=3/16`, so `N^r t^r/r!≤(9/16)^r`, making it ≤`3 exp(.435 t)(3/4)^r` for `μ≥.565`. For the first term require `t≥8N log N` (already implied by `t≥4N²`); then it is at most `2 exp(.435t−rt/(16N))`. In particular the exponent for the recent term is less than `−.08k` for `N≥2`, and the old term is at most `2 exp(−.08k)` once `r≥(16N)(.435+1.28N)`. Hence the bound is at most `5 exp(−.08k)` for multiples of three `k≥64N³`. Passing to all integer `k` uses monotonicity. The number of component vertices is at most `(N+1)(1+N+…+N^k)` when its core radius is `≤k`. Thus its size has a fixed-`N` polynomial tail (for instance exponent `.08/log N`), conditional on survival with the extra factor `1/p_N`. No all-`N` uniformity or finite mean volume is claimed. The same limits and bounds apply to limiting empirical tail fractions for each fixed radius or size, not to a simultaneous largest-component bound as `n,N` grow.

## 3. A precise young-tree test, and the large-threshold limit

Take the physical stable-age birth law `β` from [degree-law](../../../../../astra/source/round2/degree-law.md). Condition at core birth on a parent of degree `d>N` and observe the focal newborn at a deterministic young age `a↓0`. Equivalently, under the independent exponential stable-age mixture, first condition on age `≤ε` and then let `ε↓0`. Condition on the component having exactly four ordinary vertices. For `N≥4`, when there have been just two births in the focal downward branch, all its vertices survive (the focal root has at most original degree 4 and its parent remains high). Other histories contributing to the four-vertex event after more local births require at least three branch births and do not affect the leading coefficient. The branch count rate is at most `n+1/2` at size `n`, uniformly in the exterior. For the leading coefficients use the two successive directed birth rates at age zero, allowing for the changed internal degree after the first birth. Exterior changes affecting the finite parent path have probability tending to zero and do not alter the order-`a²` term; the leading products may be justified by conditional hazards or dominated convergence with the branch birth-rate bound. The coefficients share the factor `a²/2`.

Write `u=1+1/d`. From the two-vertex planted fringe the first addition is to the top at rate `u`, or to its only leaf at rate `1/2`. In the first case the star-completing birth is to the top at rate `u+1` and the path-completing birth to either leaf at total rate `2/3`. In the second case the star-completing birth is to the middle vertex at rate `3/2`, and the path-completing birth to either endpoint at total rate `u`. Thus the limiting conditional star probability is

```
 [u(u+1)+3/4] / [u(u+1)+3/4+7u/6].
```

For example at `d=20` it is `1161/1651`, and as `d→∞` it is `33/47≈.70213`. The ordinary RFT on four vertices starts its fourth birth from a three-vertex path with no parent slot; its star probability is `2/3`. The displayed ratio is strictly larger for every `u≥1` (the difference has numerator of the sign of `u²−4u/3+3/4>0`). The comparison survives mixing over `d>N` under any newborn environment distribution, since the two positive leading coefficients mix before division. Consequently a literal **ordinary RFT conditional on its size** fails this early-age, high-parent topology test even without stubs. This does not reject an age-mixed planted law with physical boundary rates, or establish the unconditioned `|U_N|=4` star probability under the core-size-biased or component-counted law. It also matters that a threshold cut can hide some descendant cores of an old root rather than sampling a whole young fringe.

Finally, take `n→∞` at each fixed `N`, then `N→∞`. In a coupled `π` sample each fixed ball has finite maximum ordinary degree, so it is eventually wholly retained and joined to the root. The component increases to the entire infinite decorated tree. Therefore uniform-core and uniform-surviving-core sizes escape to infinity in this order of limits. For component counting per `C_n` the total core-containing density is at most `π`-mean number of low children of a high root, bounded by `π[c_o 1_{D_o>N}]`. This tends to zero by integrability of core degree, or at rate `O(N^(−μ) log N)` by taking a logarithmic core-degree cutoff and using its exponential moment and the degree tail. That vanishing intensity alone says nothing about the size of a uniform component when counts are renormalized by this vanishing intensity. The large-`N` law under that normalization, joint `N=N(n)` extremal sizes, and an effective fine shape table are open here.
