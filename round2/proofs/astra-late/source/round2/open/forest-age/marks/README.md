# Parent overshoot and child age under component counting

Use the [forest-age sampling rule](../README.md): choose a core-containing component uniformly, root it at its highest surviving core, and record its deleted parent's degree. Take `n→∞` at fixed cutoff `N`, then `N→∞`. The following statement for ages uses the continuous-time stable-age lift, with a vertex's age measured **since promotion to core**, not since its first appearance as a leaf. The graph-only projection is a statement about the almost-sure fixed-`N` empirical limit from Claim 10; the age lift is defined by the physical birth law `β` and the stable-age integral below. It does not require a separate age-marked empirical convergence theorem.

Put `L=log N`, and `A=E_β W^μ`, where `W=lim e^(−t)D(t)` for a potential parent begun from its core birth in its live environment. Then the limiting marks can be generated by

```
W*  ~ w^μ Law_β(W)(dw)/A,
E   ~ Exp(μ),                   independent of W*,
V   ~ Uniform[0,1],             independent of (W*, E),
Y*  = E − log W*,              centered parent age,
R*  = exp(E),                   relative parent degree,
T*  = T⁰(L V),                 entire inert virgin tree, with independent drivers.
```

The tree omits the inert slot. The unnormalized count coefficient is **`A`**, with scale `N^(−μ) log N`; no extra `1/μ` or second factor of `W` appears. In particular `P(R*>r)=r^(−μ)` for `r≥1`, independently of the normalized child's age. The high parent is sampled through a component edge, so at finite `N` a parent is weighted by its number of surviving core children. The factor `log N` is this multiplicity to leading order, rather than a degree-proportional bias `D`. This agrees with the marginal age-law count and with the degree-tail coefficient of a uniform core. At the level of graph observations the joint weak limit of `(D_parent/N, log|T|/log N)` is the independent product Pareto–Uniform law.

## 1. Precise modes of convergence

In the stable-age lift let `t` be the parent's core age at observation, `τ` the parent's age at the sampled child's promotion, and `F_N` the physical top-rooted component. Write `V_N=(t−τ)/L`, `Y_N=t−L`, and keep `W` as a latent mark of the entire parent trajectory extending into the future. Then:

* The law of **`(Y_N,V_N,F_N)`** is within `o(1)` in total variation of `(Y*,V,T*)`. Here the `N`-dependent distribution of the entire tree is intentional. This allows arbitrary bounded joint tests, even tests depending on `N`.
* With the latent `W` and exact lattice mark `R_N=D_parent/N`, the approximation holds for bounded tests uniformly 1-Lipschitz in `(W,log R_N)` and measurable in the other coordinates. Replacing `log R_N` by `Y_N+log W` on `W>0` incurs `o(1)` in this class (give the latter an arbitrary value on `W=0`). Total variation to a continuous Pareto law is impossible: for each `N` the exact degree ratio is supported on a fixed countable lattice. Full joint TV with the latent amplitude `W` alone is not established either.
* `log|F_N|/L−V_N→0` in probability. The resulting joint log-volume limit is weak. In the target, log-volume is a function of the tree and `V` determines the tree's observation time; one should not replace these coordinates by independent uniforms in total variation. The unscaled trees grow and the statement uses an approximating family, not a fixed limiting law on finite tree types.

Merely replacing `W` by a convergent early estimate does not justify arbitrary joint indicators of `W` and the child tree. Section 3 gives the mixed Lipschitz/TV statement used here.

## 2. Early measurable marks, ages, and the exact change of variables

With the notation and compensators of [the forest-age proof](../README.md), initially replace the high event by `E_b(y)={W_b>e^(−y)}`, where `b=√L`, `W_b=e^(−b)D_b`, and integrate on `|y|≤A_0` for fixed `A_0`. One may also multiply by any bounded test of the early history and `y`, or insert it as an argument of the child test. The same arrow and count estimates apply uniformly. At each retained birth, the early-history mark belongs to the history being conditioned on. The endpoint comparison thus factors through the deterministic child kernel `P^0_u` at age `u=t−τ`. Compensating the birth clock, replacing its intensity by one, and adjusting the age interval gives the following law in full total variation on bounded windows:

```
μ e^(−μy) dy · E_β[1{W_b>e^(−y)} δ_(early marks)]
            × [dv P^0_(Lv)(J,dT)],     0≤v≤1.                 (1)
```

The absolute, not relative, bounded-window error is at most a constant (depending on `A_0`) times

```
(b+A_0)/L + (L+1)e^(A_0−b)
 + ∫_(−A_0)^A_0 P_β(H_N(y) △ E_b(y))^(1/2) dy,               (2)
```

where `H_N(y)={D_(L+y)>N}`. The same estimates as for the unmarked law bound both outer tails, by integrable multiples of `e^((1−μ)y)` on the left and `(2+y)e^(−μy)` on the right, uniformly for bounded marked tests. This is also an error decomposition usable for finite-threshold diagnostics.

There is a quantitative bound on the integrated indicator error without any density assumption on `W`. Write `W_r=e^(−r)D_r`. Its drift is `−e^(−r)(c_r−ρ_r)` with `ρ_r=Σ_core-neighbours 1/D`; its martingale jump is `e^(−r)` at a degree gain. Because `E D_r≤2e^r` and `E c_r≤1+r`, martingale isometry and Doob's inequality give

```
E sup_(r≥b) |W_r−W_b| ≤ 2√2 e^(−b/2)+(b+2)e^(−b) = η_b.
```

For a given path and `L−A_0≥b`, if the early and final indicators differ then `e^(−y)` lies within `sup_(r≥b)|W_r−W_b|` of `W_b`. On `|y|≤A_0` this threshold interval has `y`-length at most `2e^A_0 sup|W_r−W_b|`, since the derivative of the inverse map has magnitude at most `e^A_0`. Therefore the last integral in (2) is at most `2√(A_0 e^A_0 η_b)` by Cauchy–Schwarz. This argument uses a common parent path for all `y` and applies as well when the final indicator is replaced by `1{W>e^(−y)}`. In particular the unnormalized approximation can be bounded explicitly by integrating on a finite window and adding the displayed tails; these bounds are conservative for moderate cutoffs and no sharp finite-`N` fit is claimed.

After passage to the limit the parent measure is `μ e^(−μy) 1{w>e^(−y)} dy Law_β(W)(dw)`. At `w>0`, set `e=y+log w`. Its indicator becomes `e>0`, and `e^(−μy)=w^μ e^(−μe)`. Integration gives unnormalized mass `A` and normalized law `W* ⊗ Exp(μ)` in `(w,e)`. The relation `Y*=E−log W*` shows that centered parent age is **not** generally independent of overshoot. For the child, exact-age tests use the uniform interval from (1); ordinary size at inert age `Lv` has log-size `/L` tending to `v`, via the two linear pure-birth comparisons. This also proves the volume statement for any fixed number of children handled below.

## 3. Decoupling the exact latent amplitude

For bounded Lipschitz tests in `W` (or in `log W` after restricting to `W≥η>0`), replace `W` by `W_b` in the physical integral. On bounded `y` windows the error tends to zero by `W_b→W` and the bounded second moment of `B_t/L`. For `W_b` on the target side, couple it to `W` on the same unconditioned `β` trajectory, multiply by the event indicator, and bound the event difference as in (2). This proves the joint assertion with weak `W` coordinate, and exact TV in `(Y_N,V_N,F_N)` after forgetting `W`. A proof of full joint TV including the latent `W` would require a separate decoupling estimate, for example smoothing of the conditional law of the parent's infinite-time amplitude given child observations. Almost-sure convergence of the early mark does not supply such an estimate.

To handle the exact overshoot, one can work directly with `log(D_t/N)=y+log W_t`. On any bounded `y` interval the physical high event implies `W_t≥e^(−A_0)`. With probability tending to one there, `W_b` is also bounded below by a strictly positive number on the relevant event. The log difference then vanishes in probability, and Hölder with focal clock moments gives a uniform error on bounded tests Lipschitz in log-degree. The exceptional set `W=0` carries vanishing physical mark mass by the same event replacement. After dropping the bounded-window restriction, the stated weak parent law follows with the uniform outer-tail bounds. No exact amplitude continuity assumption is imposed in conditioning on the high event: only a.e. `y` is needed.

## 4. Several samples and deliberate sibling sampling

There are two different experiments.

**Independent component draws.** At fixed `N`, independent draws with replacement from the same finite observed tree have conditional law equal to the product of its empirical law. That deterministic limiting marginal (graph marks included) is obtained from the bounded top-root observation under Claim 10, with countable-type truncation and the parent degree in a finite boundary ball. Without replacement differs by at most the repeated-index probability, tending to zero for any fixed sample count because the number of components is asymptotic to `d_N C_n` with `d_N>0`. Shared physical parent identities also disappear at fixed `N`: the probability of a shared parent is bounded by `max_v c_v/(# counted components)`. Almost surely `max_v c_v=o(C_n)`, since the empirical core-degree law converges locally, empirical mean is `2(C_n−1)/C_n`, and `π c_o=2`. The last identity follows directly from the one-ended orientation: every core sends one unit of mass to its unique parent, so the mean number of children is one. Convergence of means plus local convergence gives uniform integrability of empirical degrees; in particular `max_v c_v/C_n→0`. Product laws in the second limit therefore have independent Pareto marks and independent inert observations for each sampled component, in the modes stated above. This is not a law for all components jointly or for `N=N(n)`.

**Ordered tuples of distinct siblings.** Instead count each ordered `m`-tuple of distinct surviving child components of one high parent, for fixed `m≥2`, and normalize by the total tuple count. Such tuples share **one** parent overshoot and amplitude; they do not draw their parent marks independently. The tuple intensity per core is asymptotic to `A N^(−μ) L^m`; the target children at independently uniform ages have independent inert drivers. In particular counts of available tuples are falling factorials, not powers with replacement; no `m!` appears for ordered tuples.

One must not factor the law of physical sibling trees conditional on all their actual birth times. Here is a coupling to justify the tuple assertion. Give the focal parent a rate-one **promotion proposal clock**, independent of the child drivers; accept proposals with an independent uniform at probability `b_r=1−c_r/D_r`. Its core-to-core arrows can instead run on separate predictable channels. After `b` assign an independent inert virgin replica to every proposal, even a rejected one; on an accepted proposal use the assigned drivers jointly in the real branch and the replica until the first parent input. These drivers are fresh and the proposal times are independent of all replica drivers. On `E_b`, expected rejected proposals after `b` are at most `e^(y−b)(t+t²/2)`. Expected failed branch matches or further degree-cut losses are at most `t(t+5/2)e^(y−b)`, by the conditional bounds at births. On a bounded window the probability of **any** such error is `O((L+1)^2 e^(A_0−b))`. By Cauchy–Schwarz the expected discrepancy of the tuple totals on this event, divided by `L^m`, is `o(1)` using the `2m`th moment of the proposal count. Births before `b` contribute at most `O_m((b+1)/L)` on bounded windows, by independent proposal increments and factorial moments. Likewise the high-event replacement costs a bounded moment times its symmetric-difference probability to the one-half power. For the remaining proposal process, conditional on the history at `b` it is fresh Poisson, so its ordered factorial expectation with independent replicas is the product of the Lebesgue age kernels on `[0,t−b]`; the proof uses actual replica independence, not posterior physical independence. The Poisson `2m`th moment divided by `L^(2m)` is bounded by a polynomial in `2+y` on the right, and by a constant on the left. The same `√P(H_N(y))≤√6 e^y` gives uniform left domination. Adjust each interval to `[0,L]` at cost `O_m((b+A_0)/L)` on bounded windows, and apply the same parent change of variables. Joint exact-degree lattice and weak amplitude qualifications remain as before.

The tuple claim here is for the limiting marked intensity under `π`. To identify it with finite-`n` empirical sampling requires the extra uniform integrability of `c^m` for the *empirical* core degree. A finite moment under `π` alone would not prove that. Alternatively count marked tuples at their parent with a deterministic truncation on parent core degree and pass to the limit through that truncation before removing it. This gives the same intensity under `π` without an empirical moment assertion.

## 5. Independent quantitative validation

The suggested forward audit uses the ordinary labelled arrow law from `T₂` (sample a uniform vertex, then a uniform actual neighbour). For each fixed threshold, count top edges directly and again by high parent, recording `D_parent/N`, component size and the number of child components per high parent. Use independent **whole trajectories** as replicates and, for deliberately grouped data, put all siblings in the same cluster. At several fixed cutoffs use multiple late observation sizes before any across-threshold fit. Compare:

1. `d_N N^μ/log N` against an independently estimated degree-tail coefficient (or `E_β W_b^μ` from a correctly birth-Palm sampled parent population, with a separate finite-time error study). Plot the ratio `d_N / [π(D>N) log N]`, predicted to tend to one without fitting `A`.
2. For `r≥1, x∈[0,1]`, compare the joint rectangle frequencies `P(R_N>r, log|F_N|/L≤x)` to `r^(−μ)x`. The lattice-aware diagnostic uses `D_parent>rN` exactly, rather than binning a purported continuous observation. With timestamped growth also compare child age directly to log-volume. Compare conditional component shape tests to a separate inert simulator at the same random ages; never infer uniform tree shape conditional on size from the age law.
3. Count ordered sibling pairs by actual parent and compare their normalized count to `A N^(−μ)L²`, the shared overshoot to Pareto, and their two age rectangles to `xy`. Test the no-input coupling directly by logging the first parent→child arrow after each late promotion, and the raw branch-size cut on the same physical histories. These are separate diagnostics for the terms in (2), not confidence intervals for the omitted asymptotic bias.
