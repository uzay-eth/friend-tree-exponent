# Fixed-shape counts on the simultaneous cutoff scale

Neon-2 Claim 5 (`round2/attacks/neon2-forest/primary/README.md`, historical reference) addresses the stronger macroscopic component-count and log-volume CDF scope, on the scale `(n/N)^mu log N`. Its error budgets at that scale do not give asymptotics for a fixed shape. Here we obtain the fine-scale empirical limit **in probability throughout `N→∞`, `N/n→0`**, with parent marks, and an explicit smaller power range for almost-sure convergence. All counts use original degrees and original cores on the finite graph.

Let `K_n=(n/N)^mu`. Write `L_R` for the finite measure of the log relative parent degree given by the coefficients in the [sequential fixed-shape law](../README.md), before dividing by its mass. Thus for the pair `L_R=(2A/3) Exp(mu)`, and for a singleton it is the live lifetime measure involving the smallest mass among all final neighbours of the bounded core. Let `B_{n,N}(R,f)` be the count of components of rooted shape `R`, weighted by a bounded continuous test `f(log(D_parent/N))`. Give the one possible founder component any bounded weight. For every deterministic sequence in the stated range,

```math
K_n^{-1} B_{n,N}(R,f)\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\ X L_R(f). \qquad\text{(1)}
```

This also gives weak convergence in probability of the normalized parent-mark law among occurrences of `R`. For fixed positive relative degree thresholds the corresponding rectangle counts converge as well: both limiting mark measures are atomless (the singleton law is a mixture of shifted exponentials). The proof does not give simultaneous a.s. convergence for cutoffs arbitrarily close to `n`.

## 1. A finite-history cohort lemma

Start the actual clock at `T3`, put `Y=lim e^-t |T_t|` and `H=lim e^-mu t C(T_t)=Y^mu X`. A core's age starts at **promotion**, not at its ordinary birth. In a deterministic promotion window `(a+l,a+d]`, observe each promoted root through a fixed elapsed horizon `b`. Consider a bounded local read of these histories, possibly carrying the shifted promotion time `z∈[l,d]`. Assume its conditional history score is locally continuous for almost every `z`, or can be squeezed by such scores with convergence of the limiting integrals. Then the normalized count has limit in probability

```
e^(-mu a) sum of reads  →  H ∫_l^d mu e^(mu z) E_beta[read at shift z] dz.     (2)
```

Here and below the finite-history reads needed for (2) can be capped in a finite local ball and in the number of descendants and neighbours tested. To justify the lemma, use the predictable insertion forecast, at each old core charging promotion at rate `b_o=L_o/D_o`, followed by the marked physical future of the newly promoted tip. The conditional expectation of the remaining forecast integral equals the conditional terminal count of promotions strictly after the current time. Subtract the two backward forecasts, exactly as in the [quenched birth-mark proof](../../../age-order/spectrum/quenched/README.md). The common Markov history can include the marks already read. A degree/cap-bounded read through a fixed number of levels has a finite jump bound `J e^{2u}` for its conditional mean at remaining time `u`: in the dirty-core coupling, matching cores agree on their promotion stamps; charge a dirty core to every contributing read that touches it in either copy. Within capped balls the number of such centers is bounded by the finite cap and radius. An incident old leaf that later promotes dirties its former parent's pair if its mark was affected. To apply this to history reads, freeze each read at its stipulated endpoint and retain that value; the dirty set is increasing and the same charging works at the read times. Alternate implementation: inspect a finite local initial neighbourhood and the finite subsequent graphical history; kill the read if the capped space-time observation exceeds its cap. A first difference of admitted reads then occurs at one of the capped reads that meets a dirty core. Finite exit supports can use the extra level needed for boundary degrees. The difference of the two backward martingale jumps is bounded by twice the one-read bound; the elapsed interval is fixed. Its variance is `O(e^a)` by stopped jump isometry and `E|T_s|=3e^s`, so `e^-mu a` kills it as `2mu>1`. The forecast integral converges by the shifted Claim 10 LLN, dominated by the normalized core count, and `π B=mu β` for the promotion kernel `B`.

A useful equivalent version avoids history coupling for the reads actually used below: use a **common deterministic** endpoint and include promotion time as a mark of each newly promoted core. The focal root, its early children and their immediate neighbours and leaf stocks form a fixed-radius endpoint read. In particular the early-child list is recognizable from core promotion stamps; no stamps of cores present before the restarted experiment are needed. Cap the focal number of incident cores (or the whole local ball) when bounding a score by a constant; a capped contributing read in either copy can be charged backwards to at most a fixed number of roots per dirty core. Remove caps at fixed endpoints: for each focal promotion the omitted read weight is bounded by its number of early offspring. This count has a uniform Poisson tail, so truncate it first. For the remaining bounded weight, the expected omitted mass per promotion tends to zero under the limiting newborn law as local caps grow, by finite-time local finiteness and the shifted Claim 10 LLN applied to the capped indicators and to the total promotion rate. Normalized expectations in that application converge by the bounded core-count second moment. Old-cell cutoff gives local continuity of the finite histories, with degree equalities read as discrete marks. Use continuous margins around any real mass threshold; the final margins close by integration in `y`, not by a no-atom assertion about a proxy at a fixed read. In particular (2) does not interchange a growing observation horizon with Claim 10.

## 2. High parents and recent intact branches

First observe at deterministic time `t`, use a deterministic cutoff `N`, and write `a=t−log N`. Both `a` and `L=log N` tend to infinity along the sequences below. In a fixed recent window `[t-h,t]`, identify the high parent set by the degrees at `t-h`, multiplied by `e^h`. For fixed `h` and all but a vanishing fraction on the scale `e^mu a`, high sets at any fixed nearby threshold are sandwiched by multiplying the proxy threshold by `1±ε`. Here is a way to pay **only on that scale**, even if `N` grows slowly. For cores of degree above `N e^-h/2` at `t-h`, their number is `O_path(e^mu a)` by [Claim 11](../../../../../../../astra/source/round2/fringe/hub-spectrum.md). Its conditional gamma/Yule error for large core degree is handled by deleting the cores with too many core neighbours: in the high-cutoff argument below take `N≥n^δ` for any fixed `δ>0`, so `max c=O(t)=o(N)` and the ordinary union bound over all vertices suffices. Cores below half the proxy and vertices born later cannot cross, by the same finite-horizon Yule upper tail, with total failure `O(e^t e^-cN)`. The intermediate degree spectrum counts the proxy sets at each relative threshold, including their thin difference bands. Equivalently use `D(t-h)e^h/N` as the mark in the ideal sample and bracket its tails by `1±ε`. Lower cutoffs are covered separately in §5.

Over `[t-h,t]`, the proxy parents have disjoint newly promoted branches, with independent ideal inert virgins attached to their rate-one proposal clocks as in Neon-2. The conditional mean number of sends from these sources into existing cores is at most `O(h t/N)` per proxy parent on `max c≤C t`, stopping at the first violation. Each such send spoils at most one proposal and one already promoted branch. No new core in these branches can be high with probability tending to one, by the finite-horizon Yule tail. The ideal count within the window has conditional Poisson law, with mean equal to the number of candidate parents times `∫_0^h Pr(T^0_u=R)du`. Concentration in probability only needs the candidate count to diverge; no claim of concentration conditional on the true **future** high set is made. It follows for positive parent-mark rectangles that the recent contribution, divided by `e^mu a`, has the limit `HA` times the incomplete occupation integral and the Pareto parent-mark test. The same comparison on a bounded observation interval applies to a pair of deterministic endpoints: use marks resident in `R` throughout the interval for a lower bound, and all marks visiting `R` during it for an upper bound. The loss from the first condition at interval width `ε` costs at most `( |R|−1/D_root) ε I(R)` per parent, and the second adds at most `ε` per parent times the probability of ever hitting `R`. These bounds use size increasing after each genuine jump.

The part of the intact count with branch age `>h` is negligible in probability as `h→∞`. A uniformly useful annealed proof, also used for all other exceptional counts below, is in §3. Intact contributions to a singleton are impossible.

## 3. Tail bounds at the scale of the number of parents

The original free growth has `E C(T_s)≤C e^mu s`; the core promotion intensity is at most `C(T_s)`. Hence expectations of sums over focal parent promotions at time `s` can be bounded by `C e^mu s ds` times a **uniform conditional** bound from a newly promoted core. The founder is a separate initial root with initial degree two, so the same bounds for its trajectory apply at `s=0` without an intensity integral. Write `y=a−s`, the focal parent's age offset. On `|y|≤D` the focal parents are in a fixed cohort as in (2). Per focal parent use exactly the [sequential estimates](../README.md): intact counts older than `h` have expectation bounded by `∫_h^∞ b_R(u)du`; cut-inside counts with top promoted after focal age `b` have expectation at most `6e^{2y-b}`. The latter chooses a distinct high descendant in each contributing branch, so it works for singleton and pair alike. On `y>D` use `O_R(1+y)` for all counts (split removed descendants at focal age `2y`, and clock bound the earlier tops); on `y<−D` use `O_R(e^y)` (intact second-moment bound times the high-parent probability; for cut-inside use `6e^{2y}`). The constants are uniform over the live exterior. Integrate against `C e^-mu y dy` after division by `e^mu a`. Thus first enlarging `D`, then `h,b`, bounds discarded counts in normalized expectation, tending to zero in that order. Founder-parent contributions have normalized mean `O_R((1+a)e^-mu a)`, and a founder-top component costs at most one. For endpoint intervals of width `ε≤1`, the same bounds apply to sup counts with cutoffs checked at the two endpoints and a shape of size at most `|R|` at some intermediate epoch: charge high descendants at the right endpoint and, for intact branches born by the left endpoint, bound age there and count size at most `|R|` there. Add births within the interval using the high-parent-bound at the right endpoint (the same Cauchy/clock bound). Constants may be enlarged uniformly for width at most one.

## 4. Inherited cuts, including old singletons

Keep the focal cohort `|y|≤D` and only tops promoted by focal age `b`. Read at a common deterministic time `a+D+B`, with `B>b+2D`. At each such top record its current neighbour set and proxy masses `e^{-(a+D+B)}D_w(a+D+B)` for the top, its parent and its nonparent neighbours. Use units `e^{-a}` for thresholds. It suffices to consider **two-sided finite proxies**, rather than to infer boundedness from a small degree at this read.

For precision, the following squeeze bounds the entire old-cut count and its parent mark; it also allows observation at any time in a fixed interval of width ε. Fix an integer `q≥2`, and call a read vertex *large* if its current degree exceeds `q`. At the common read, for finitely many early tops consider these tests:

* The following explicit stopped tests avoid paying for retrospective selections or an unseen neighbour. At the read call a top **frozen candidate** if its degree is `≤q` and each current neighbour has at least `j` other leaves. The probability of any subsequent first gain of such a top is at most `2q/(j+1)` by the first-send barrier. A recorded neighbour with at least `j` leaves starts a dominated Yule-minus-loss comparison; its limiting mass and late normalized degrees can be read, within a prescribed tolerance in `e^{-a}` units, using the martingale remainder, uniformly when the number of read vertices and their read degrees are capped. In particular the probability that a recorded neighbour remains low with at most `|R|` leaves at observation tends to zero up to an error going to zero with `j`: compare to an independent tracked birth–death system of `j` initial families with death rate `1/2` (the comparison families, not the physical neighbours, are independent). If all neighbours are high and the top makes no gain, the surviving top is exactly a singleton.
* As the other certified class use **growing candidates**: tops having at least `j` leaves at the read. The same stock comparison excludes a fixed-size physical component, except for an error tending to zero with `j` at late observations. Everything not in these two classes is counted as an error by its finite read, with no attempt to decide its future. Under `β`, for the finitely many early tops, bounded ones eventually stabilize with degree `≤q` or have eventual degree `>q`; all their final neighbours have diverging stock. Unbounded ones have diverging stock themselves. First let the read horizon `B→∞` at fixed `q,j`, then `q→∞` and choose `j≫q` in the iterated estimates (equivalently, for each `q` take `j` large *before* choosing `B`). The expectation of the undecided early-top count tends to zero in these successive tolerances, by the Poisson bound and the physical dichotomy. This uses only stationarity for the **finite** tests (2), not an assumed uniform rate of the dichotomy.

Here is more detail on mass readout, where the order of the tolerances matters. At the common read `s_*=a+D+B`, the expected remaining supremum error of the normalized degree, in `e^{-a}` units, is bounded by

```
e^(-(D+B)) [2 sqrt(D_w(s_*)) + c_w(s_*) + 2].             (3)
```

This is Doob's inequality for the compensated degree gains and the integral of the core-neighbour drift, with the remaining clock restarted at `s_*`. It includes future finite-time reads as well as the terminal mass. For the early-top neighbours needing this test, cap the number of focal children and neighbour indices first, and cap the times at which they were added if necessary. Under `β` the final list of neighbours of a bounded top is finite and appears by a finite time, so the discarded bounded-top mass can be made arbitrarily small in the limiting finite tests. For each recorded vertex at a time before a **fixed** offset from `a`, cap its degree and core degree at that recording time; the finite-window conditional tree score and Poisson clock bound remove this cap in the same order. Conditional Yule and Poisson bounds from those bounded states make the expectation of (3) tend to zero with `B`. Alternatively use (3) truncated at one, summed over all candidates: under `β` it goes to zero along each stabilized finite list, by the almost-sure finite mass limit and the linear core-degree bound, and its number is at most `q` times the early-top count. That truncated bounding score is a finite read, so (2) gives its expected normalized error by uniform integrability on the finite window. For this UI, cap early offspring at `k`, then the census is bounded by `k C(T_{a+d})` and normalized core counts are `L²` bounded; the dropped focal clock excess has mean `O(e^mu a E[(Pois(b)−k)_+])` by compensation at promotion. No replacement by independent masses is needed.

Now apply (2) at each finite read, use the conditional first-send, stock and mass bounds, and squeeze all high-neighbour inequalities by fixed margins. In the limit the snapshot contribution is exactly the singleton test of [sequential §4](../README.md): the top is bounded and its parent and **every** final nonparent neighbour have mass exceeding `e^{-y}` in the focal parent's time origin; for upper mark tails also enforce the corresponding parent mass threshold. Threshold equalities vanish after integrating `y` over its compact window. The difference in log parent degree is controlled on the high-parent event by the normalized mass tolerance; for weak convergence it suffices to compare positive parent threshold tails. Growing candidates contribute zero for all fixed shapes; a frozen candidate with a surviving neighbour of diverging stock cannot contribute a pair or larger fixed shape. Sending the tail cutoffs of §3 to infinity identifies exactly `H L_R` at the deterministic clock scale. The argument thus deals with old components without subtracting an `o(K log N)` error from the macroscopic count.

### Passing to deterministic original sizes

Bracket `Y` by countably many deterministic multiplicative intervals of log width ε, so that the size epoch `τ_n` lies in the corresponding deterministic time interval about `log n−log Y` eventually, except on a set of probability tending to zero. The recent count uses the residence/visit sandwich above. The old cut count uses the same read and the all-future version of (3), checking high inequalities at both ends (or multiplying the proxy mass cutoffs by `e^{±ε}`); the tail estimate of §3 already allows this interval. Localize first to finitely many brackets with arbitrarily high probability, and let ε decrease. No monotonicity of the fixed-shape count or independence of `Y` is used. Since `e^mu(t−log N)/(n/N)^mu` differs by at most `e^{±mu ε}` from `Y^-mu`, this gives `Y^-mu H L_R=X L_R` in (1).

## 5. Explicit almost-sure power range and completion of (1)

Here one can dispense with old/young classification. At a high parent use the fixed downward cylinder that counts low child components equal to `R`, reading their entire boundary to determine the equality. Any matching component of `s` ordinary vertices lies within `s` core levels; a breadth-first probe with a stop at `s+1` ordinary vertices gives the same cylinder even on rejected inputs. Include a test of the original parent degree, and cap the parent's **core** degree at `B_0 log n` so the score norm is `O(log n)`. At finite size this cap excludes nothing eventually a.s. after intersecting deterministic choices of `B_0` (use `max c=O(log n)` or its exponential-moment proof). Under `π` the removed mass is at most `π[c 1_{c>B_0 log n}]`, which is polynomially small with arbitrarily large exponent as `B_0` grows, by the exponential core moment. The founder contributes at most `1+c_o=O(log n)` if excluded from the cylinder estimate. By the [stable deterministic-size cylinder estimate](../../diagonal/README.md#1-uniform-physical-cylinder-estimate-at-deterministic-sizes), the normalized error per `C_n` is

```
O_path(log n · n^(-eta/100) + log n · n^(-(mu−11/20))) + O(n^-2),
eta=2^(-348),
```

plus the negligible founder error. This estimate covers all integer `N≤n` and polynomially many threshold tests by union, with radius fixed by `R`. In particular `N≤n^(eta/300)` makes the error `o(N^-mu)`. Combine this almost-sure conclusion with §§2–4 on `N>n^(eta/300)` (a fixed positive power, sufficient for the recent Yule union bounds). The split is deterministic and covers every sequence in (1). The explicit a.s. range can use the simultaneously checked rational parent-tail thresholds; weak convergence follows by the atomless limiting law. This small exponent uses the published conservative gap bound, not an estimate of the best possible a.s. regime.

## 6. Samples and the necessary macroscopic obstruction

In the macroscopic count regime of Neon-2, dividing (1) by the component count gives frequency `L_R(1)/(A log N)` in probability. In particular the pair frequency is asymptotic to `2/(3 log N)`, and the singleton frequency has positive constant `L_bullet(1)/A` on the same scale. Let `m` be the number of independent uniform component draws **with replacement**, conditionally on the finite tree. For a fixed shape the hit probability is `1−(1−p_R)^m`. Thus `m=o(log N)` misses it with probability tending to one, `m/log N→λ∈(0,∞)` gives hit probability `1−exp(−λ L_R(1)/A)`, and `m≫log N` sees it with high probability and estimates its frequency to vanishing relative sampling error. The same scale applies without replacement: when `m=O(log N)`, the hypergeometric no-hit product has the same limit since the population is of order `K_n log N` and `K_n→∞` (use the product directly, not a repeated-index coupling requiring `m²` below population size). These statements concern the sampling error conditional on the actual empirical law.

At `N/n→c∈(0,1)`, the deterministic count scale `K_n` stays bounded. In particular a nondegenerate continuous law of parent marks cannot be obtained as a weak-in-probability limit of the empirical probability measures of a fixed shape with bounded count in probability: with high probability at most a fixed number of atoms is available, and measures with at most that many atoms are weakly closed. The proposed finite-intensity limit itself would imply such tight counts and a strictly positive atomless mark law, giving a contradiction. This obstructs a general extension past the sublinear cutoff regime and does not preclude separate random discrete limits there. It does not decide a.s. convergence for all sublinear cutoffs.

There is also a scalar obstruction without a full-support theorem: if (1) claimed a positive limit at **every** `c∈(0,1)`, then `c^-mu X L_R(1)` would have to be integer-valued for every fixed `c`. Choose two constants with an irrational ratio of their `-mu` powers. The two integer assertions would force `X L_R(1)=0` on their common probability-one event, a contradiction. With the stronger full-support input for `X`, the obstruction applies at each individual `c`.
