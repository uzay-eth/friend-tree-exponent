# The core-size limit has a density positive almost everywhere

Start the undirected random friend tree from one edge. Let $`C_n`$ be its number of vertices of degree at least two. For the limit $`X=\lim_n C_n/n^\mu`$ in Claim 6, the law of $`X`$ is **equivalent to Lebesgue measure on $`(0,\infty)`$**. In particular it admits a density which is positive almost everywhere there. The argument gives no formula or smoothness bound for that density.

The proof uses the following three inputs. From Claim 6 the limit exists and is finite and strictly positive almost surely, with positive probability of being arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large. From the inert-seed theorem, if a core edge is replaced by one inert parent slot on each side, each finite half with at least two ordinary vertices has positive finite size and core limits $`A=\lim_t e^{-t}N(t)`$ and $`B=\lim_t e^{-\mu t}C(t)`$. The root's physical degree includes its slot. For the size limit explicitly, at size $`m`$ the genuine holding rate is $`m-1/D_o`$. In an exponential race the sum of the holding-time corrections to rate $`m`$ has finite mean since it is bounded by $`\sum_{m\ge2}1/m^2`$. The resulting size hitting times minus $`\log m`$ converge to a finite real number. Finally $`0<\mu<1`$. ## Some edge receives only finitely many crossing arrows

Use independent rate-one initiator clocks, with directed intensity $`1/D_x`$ from $`x`$ to each neighbour. After size three, choose a core $`x`$ at an endpoint of the core tree, with any fixed tie rule. It has at most one core neighbour and at least one leaf. Require silence of $`x`$ for one time unit and at least two rings of one specified adjacent leaf during that unit. Only $`x`$ could promote this leaf, so those rings give $`x`$ two more leaves. At the next ring of $`x`$, its chance of selecting a leaf is at least $`3/4`$. The selected leaf becomes an adjacent core $`y`$ with a fresh leaf. The original core retains at least two leaves. The conditional probability of this finite preparation is at least $`q=(3/4)e^{-1}(1-2/e)>0`$ from every initial state with a core.

At each of $`x,y`$ select one adjacent leaf and follow a family of such leaves. A tracked leaf rings at rate one, adds a new leaf to its anchor, and brings the new leaf into the family. Give every member an independent death proposal of rate $`1/2`$. At every proposal retire the member from the tracked family. Promote it in the physical tree with probability $`2/D_{\rm anchor}`$. Supply the remaining physical directed channels at their actual rates. Every tracked physical promotion thereby has rate $`1/D_{\rm anchor}`$. The two lower family counts are independent linear birth–death processes, with per-member rates $`1`$ and $`1/2`$. For a family started from one, its count times $`e^{-t/2}`$ is a nonnegative martingale with mean one and second moment at most four, from the martingale isometry. Its limit is positive with probability at least $`1/4`$. With conditional probability at least $`1/16`$ both families have positive limits. On this event both endpoints have finite future integrals of their reciprocal degrees.

Let $`E`$ be the event that some adjacent cores have both reciprocal integrals finite. At every integer time after size three the argument gives $`\Pr(E\mid{\cal F}_j)\ge q/16`$, uniformly in the realized state. The event is measurable from the full path. Lévy's upward theorem gives $`\Pr(E)=1`$. For each fixed pair of labels the crossing count, when the edge exists, has compensator $`\int (D_x^{-1}+D_y^{-1})\,dt`$. Stop the count when this integral reaches an integer and use its bounded stopped expectation. This gives finite count whenever its compensator is finite. There are countably many labelled edges. Almost surely there is consequently a core edge and a **deterministic integer** in the countable list of times after which it has no further crossings and both reciprocal integrals are finite. No assertion that its last crossing is a stopping time is needed.

## Vary the holding time of one half

Fix a finite state, a particular core edge and an integer cut time in that countable list. Replace the edge by its two slots and start the independent inert halves. Let their product path law be $`Q`$. Internal arrows have the correct physical intensities. Give each direction across the cut an additional independent candidate clock, accepting a candidate with probability $`1/D_{\rm root}`$ on the sending side. The construction agrees with the original tree up to the first accepted crossing. Thus the physical subprobability measure on two paths with no future crossing has density
```math
 w=\exp\!\left[-\int_0^\infty
 \big(D_1(t)^{-1}+D_2(t)^{-1}\big)\,dt\right]
```
relative to $`Q`$. Conditioning on no crossing can bias the paths. Domination by $`Q`$ suffices for absolute continuity, and $`w>0`$ on pairs for which both reciprocal integrals are finite.

Under $`Q`$, condition on the whole second path, the first half's first genuine successor, and its subsequent path with time measured from that jump. The first waiting time $`T`$ has an exponential law of rate $`m-1/D_o>0`$, independent of this information. Denote the restarted first half's limits by $`A,B`$ and the second half's limits by $`C,D`$, in order of size then core count. All four are strictly positive. On a path with no crossing the two sizes add, and the core counts add with the slot degrees included. The normalized combined count is the following function of $`r=e^{-T}`$:
```math
 f(r)=\frac{B r^\mu+D}{(A r+C)^\mu},\qquad
 f'(r)=\frac{\mu(BCr^{\mu-1}-AD)}{(Ar+C)^{\mu+1}}.
```
The variable $`r`$ has a density on $`(0,1)`$. Its image under $`f`$ also has a density, since the numerator of $`f'`$ has at most one zero and the inverse is locally Lipschitz on each regular branch. The preimage of any Lebesgue-null set therefore has zero conditional probability. This holds on integrating under $`Q`$ and then restricting to the sublaw with weight $`w`$. The countable cover by cut states, times and edges proves absolute continuity in the edge-seeded tree. Its embedded limit is the same ratio because each genuine free-tree event adds one ordinary vertex.

## Every set of positive Lebesgue measure has positive probability

Call an inert path good if its root has finite reciprocal integral. Its own normalized limit is the ratio of its core amplitude to its size amplitude raised to $`\mu`$. Both goodness and this ratio persist on deleting its first waiting time and restarting at the successor.

Fix $`0<l<h`$. The edge-seeded tree has positive probability of $`X<l`$, and positive probability of $`X>2^{1-\mu}h`$. For a pair of halves with intrinsic ratios $`x_1,x_2`$, the combined ratio is $`p^\mu x_1+(1-p)^\mu x_2`$, where $`0<p<1`$ is the limiting size share of the first half. It lies between $`\min(x_1,x_2)`$ and $`2^{1-\mu}\max(x_1,x_2)`$. Apply the preceding countable cover on each of the two positive-probability events, retaining goodness of both endpoints. Some deterministic planted seed has positive probability of a good path with intrinsic ratio below $`l`$, and another has positive probability of a good path with intrinsic ratio above $`h`$. This follows by domination by the two-half product law and then projecting onto the indicated half.

Join those two roots with a real edge. This finite unlabelled state is reachable with positive probability from the initial edge. In reverse order of deleting leaves, each required attachment can be made by a neighbouring initiator at positive rate. Start independent planted paths at that state. Conditional on their successors and relative future paths, the two initial waiting times $`T_1,T_2`$ are independent exponentials of positive rates. There is positive probability for a pair of good continuations having the two specified ratios. On such continuations, $`r=\exp(T_2-T_1)`$ has a strictly positive density on $`(0,\infty)`$. The same formula for $`f`$ now connects the two intrinsic ratios as $`r`$ ranges from zero to infinity. Each positive-Lebesgue-measure subset of $`(l,h)`$ is hit with positive probability by change of variables on its regular branches. Both waits are finite, so $`w>0`$ on these good continuations for every pair of waits. Weighting by $`w`$ and by the probability of reaching the joined seed preserves that positive probability. Exhausting $`(0,\infty)`$ proves the assertion.
