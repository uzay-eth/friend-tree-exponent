# Exact multi-side Dirichlet splitting and its smoothing scope

This extends the **Neon-2 Claim 1** [two-side density argument](../density/README.md). On every fixed finite cut of the kind below, the **physical no-further-crossing sublaw** is an exact mixture of Dirichlet images, with a shape-only terminal weight. Three or more sides give a bounded density after conditioning on the embedded component paths (where the terminal weight is positive); four or more give a continuous one. Arbitrarily many permanent cuts exist almost surely. The conditional density bounds are not uniform over the paths or the cut. In particular this proof does not upgrade the full law of `X` from the absolute continuity established by Neon-2 to a locally bounded density or an `L^p` estimate.

## 1. The holding weights cancel exactly

At a fixed deterministic time or physical jump stopping time, fix the finite tree and any set of `m−1` edges with core endpoints. Removing them gives `m` components. In each component retain every deleted incidence as a degree slot at its endpoint. Use no incoming sends from slots. For component `i`, at ordinary size `n`, let `d_i(n)` be the sum of its outgoing cross rates, **one `1/D` per slot**, even when several slots belong to one vertex. Then its genuine jump rate is `λ_i(n)=n−d_i(n)>0`, provided it has at least two ordinary vertices. Below we only need a path of cut edges with a private leaf at each endpoint, so these conditions hold. All genuine jumps add exactly one ordinary vertex. Define reference `Q` by independent component embedded chains with transition law given by their normalized genuine arrow rates, and independent holding times of rate **`n`** on each component, independent also of the embedded chains.

Fix a deterministic elapsed horizon `t`. Relative to `Q`, changing the independent waits to the inert physical rates gives the weight

```
∏_(completed jumps by t) [λ_i(n)/n] · exp( ∫₀ᵗ Σ_i d_i(N_i(s)) ds ).
```

The exponential includes the unfinished wait of every component. Given the inert paths, reinstating the suppressed crossings with independent marked Poisson proposals and requiring none by `t` gives exactly the inverse exponential. Thus the physical no-cross sublaw at `t` has the remaining product over completed jumps. Do not simulate additional independent events for a slot and condition them on the same normalized choice: the independent candidates are a rate-splitting construction with total true intensity restored. This argument works also at multi-slot vertices, since the killed rate is the sum of the directed slot rates, and genuine transitions keep the entire degree.

The product decreases to

```
L = ∏_i ∏_(n≥n_i) [1−d_i(n)/n].
```

The identity at infinity holds on the whole path sigma field: for an event observable by a fixed finite horizon, apply the finite formula at every larger horizon and pass by bounded convergence; then use a monotone-class argument. All components have infinitely many reference jumps. `L` depends only on the embedded shapes (and factors across components); any other terminal event must still be included as an additional indicator. The conditional law for an arbitrary path-dependent selection of cuts cannot drop that indicator. In particular we use domination when eliminating such selections, not an assertion of conditional independence under them.

## 2. Exact Dirichlet image, including the core limits

On the physical no-cross sublaw the planted core counts sum to the free core count, because all slot degrees are retained. Ordinary sizes also sum. Under `Q`, let `G_i=lim e⁻ᵗ N_i(t)`, so that independently `G_i∼Gamma(n_i,1)`, and independently of the embedded paths. The proportions `P_i=G_i/ΣG_j` have `Dirichlet(n_1,…,n_m)` law. This is a statement for the deterministic cut sublaw as well, since `L` alone changes only the shapes.

One can obtain all the needed finite component coefficients without assuming a multi-slot version of the one-slot positive-limit theorem. The original free `X` exists and is finite and positive almost surely from each reachable cut state. Its exceptional set remains null under the no-cross sublaw and therefore under `Q` on `L>0`. Put `a_i(n)=C_i(n)/n^μ` for the embedded shape sequence. At almost every sequence with `L>0`, for almost every independent set of clocks, the physical ratio has a finite limit. For a generic deterministic time tending to infinity each nonnegative summand is bounded by the sum, so the sequence `a_i(n)` is bounded (every size is attained and all relative size proportions converge to positive values).

To show convergence rather than just boundedness, vary the **first** hold on each side. Fix the embedded chains and all subsequent reference waits, and write `b_i(t)=e^(−μt)C_i(t)` for the normalized core count of component `i` measured from its first jump. By Fubini, on `L>0` the weighted sum `Σ e^(−μs_i)b_i(t−s_i)` converges for Lebesgue almost every positive vector of first waits `s_i` (the denominator has its positive limit). Fix all other waits in a full-measure fibre. Subtract the two convergent sums for two distinct valid first waits `s<s'` of component `i`. Put `f(t)=e^(−μs)b_i(t−s)` and `h=s'−s>0`. Then `f(t)−e^(−μh) f(t−h)` has a limit. Boundedness of `f` follows from the nonnegative sum for the first vector. If the limit of the difference is `d`, taking limsup and liminf in `f(t)=e^(−μh)f(t−h)+d+o(1)` gives the same value `d/(1−e^(−μh))` for both. Consequently each `b_i` has a limit. Evaluating along the size epochs implies `a_i(n)→a_i` depending on shapes alone. These coefficients are finite nonnegative; at least one is positive since the physical `X>0` on the weighted sublaw. We do not need strict positivity of every multi-slot coefficient.

The exact ratio is therefore

```math
 X=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i P_i^\mu .
```

There are no additional holding-time weights in this formula. For fast-edge restrictions used below, finite integrated reciprocals are invariant under changing the first waits and, on the embedded chain, can equivalently be tested by convergence of `Σ_n d_i(n)/n` (conditional reference variance of its weighted clock sum is summable, since the slot count is fixed). In any case fixed-cut densities can be used as upper bounds even with a remaining mask.

## 3. Bounded and continuous conditional densities

If all `a_i>0`, the map on the simplex interior is strictly concave, with unique critical point (maximum) at `p_i∝a_i^(1/(1−μ))`. Close to the face `p_i=0`, some coordinate `p_j≥1/m`; then the tangent derivative in direction `e_i−e_j` tends to `+∞`. For fixed coefficients choose a small collar with a finite partition of unity and a tangent direction of derivative bounded away from zero on each patch. Use coordinates with `p_i` the varying coordinate and integrate over the other `m−2` coordinates. On such a patch the density is bounded by a constant times the maximum Dirichlet density: the restriction to a line is strictly concave and the portion with positive derivative is one-to-one. For continuity, the Dirichlet density vanishes at each face (`n_i≥2`), so extend the patch integrand by zero and use dominated convergence in the transverse coordinates. A smooth partition may be chosen on the closed simplex, supported in the designated derivative regions, with charts obtained by eliminating `p_j`. The remaining regular interior patches use the same change of variables and compact supports. At the maximum the negative Hessian on the tangent space is definite; the Morse coordinates reduce the pushforward to spherical integration with radial factor `(max−x)^((m−3)/2)`. The local density contribution is bounded for `m≥3` and continuous (vanishing at the maximum) for `m≥4`.

If some coefficients are zero, combine their Dirichlet masses into a single coordinate of parameter `n_0≥2`. There is no critical point in the interior: with the zero coordinate eliminated, all derivatives in positive coordinates are positive. For the boundary with the zero coordinate small, the same coordinate argument can be applied globally by holding all but one positive coordinate fixed and trading it against the zero coordinate. The derivative in that direction is always at least `μ a_i>0`. Dirichlet vanishing gives continuity even at the line endpoints. This proves the conditional assertions including degenerate patterns of zeros. They require at least one positive coefficient, as proved above.

## 4. Arbitrarily large permanent cutsets and the summation obstruction

For any fixed number `m`, start from an endpoint core (a lone core is allowed). It has a leaf. In a bounded number of stages grow a path of `m` consecutive anchors, each with a retained private leaf. At each stage silence the current anchor for one unit while its designated leaf rings at least twice; then require its first ring in the next unit to select a leaf. The active anchor has at most one core neighbour at the start of this stage and until its first ring; exterior sends cannot remove its leaves or increase its core-neighbour count. The promotion leaves spare leaves and creates the next anchor with a private leaf. Previously used anchors may continue to ring, so to guarantee their retained private leaves until the path is complete, silence them for the remaining bounded duration. These simultaneous requirements have probability bounded below by a positive constant depending only on `m`, uniformly over the input tree. They can be imposed in fixed length stages through the promotion and with postpromotion silence until the next stage; the memoryless clock property gives the same uniform positive cost. At the end embed disjoint birth–death stocks from one leaf per anchor, with independent lower rates `1,1/2` as in Neon-2. With a further uniform positive probability they all survive and grow exponentially. Both directions on every path edge then have finite compensator and only finitely many actual sends. The path existence event is measurable; at each integer time after size three its conditional probability is bounded below by the same fixed constant. Upward convergence of conditional probabilities gives probability one. Intersect over `m`. The cuts can share vertices (as these path cuts do); to obtain vertex-disjoint cut edges take alternate edges of a sufficiently long such path. The retained private leaves ensure at least two ordinary vertices per resulting component at every later time if they remain leaves; alternatively the same fact holds at the eventual cut time since each anchor was connected to a private non-cut neighbour at the setup, and edges are never removed.

This yields countably many deterministic cut states/times/edge lists whose no-cross events cover the law. However, even **one** fixed cut has unbounded conditional smoothing constants on the unrestricted coefficient/seed domain. For a concrete sharp concentration obstruction set `n_i=N` and `a_i=a>0`. Then `P` concentrates at `p_*=(1/m,…,1/m)` with `E||P−p_*||²=(1−1/m)/(mN+1)`. Taylor expansion near `p_*` has no linear tangent term. Thus a fixed positive fraction of the mass lies within `O_{m,a}(1/N)` of the same maximum `a m^(1−μ)`. Any bound on the conditional density must grow at least linearly in `N`; by Hölder its `L^p` norm grows at least as `N^(1−1/p)` for `p>1`. This is a Dirichlet-domain obstruction, not a claim that such amplitudes occur with a prescribed probability in the real selected cut law. Even a perfect local bound at each fixed coefficient and cut therefore gives no summable control without estimates on their mixing law, including possible masks. A countable cover suffices for null sets, not for these integrability gains. Quantitative control of the actual cut-size law together with the coefficient distribution, or an argument that bypasses last-cut selection, remains needed for stronger global density regularity.

One unconditional consequence of the conditional bounds is an **exhaustion by bounded-density sublaws**: truncate the countable cover to finitely many cuts and at each cut retain only coefficient paths for which the conditional supremum is at most a finite `H`. These restrictions exhaust full mass as the finite list and `H` increase. To avoid double counting, allocate each realization to its first eligible cut on the finite list; the extra indicators only diminish the corresponding sublaws. Their total density is bounded by the sum of the finitely many bounds. This approximation is in total variation but with no power-law relation between its discarded mass and its density bound; it does not give full-law `L^p` for any `p>1`.
