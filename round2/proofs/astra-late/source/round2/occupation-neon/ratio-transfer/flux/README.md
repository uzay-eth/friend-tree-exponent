# Incident flux and a quadratic hinge: exact missing-information test

**The incident-pair and sterile observations do not determine a usable variance bound on the complete paired reward.** The replay finds opposite signs for one actual D18 `S3` row averaged over two mixtures of reachable crowns. The mixtures agree exactly on uniform census, uniform sterile census, oriented edge count, normalized incident arrow, and even the two-arm census. A separate stationary example shows why one-step pairing also cannot supply the variance decay of a semigroup corrector. No exponent endpoint is transferred here.

## Two clocks, with exact normalization

Write `π` for uniform-core stationary occupation, `ρ(x)=Σ_(y~x core) 1/D_y`, and `b=L/D`. For any nonnegative test `f(y,x)` of a directed physical core send, define

```
 I(f) = π[Σ_(y~x core) f(y,x)/D_y] / (1−μ).
```

This is a probability law. The normalizer is **exactly** `πρ=π((k+1)/D)=1−μ`, by the [audited two-endpoint transport](../../flow/README.md) and birth balance. The target marginal is `ρ π/(1−μ)`; given the target the donor is chosen according to the true reciprocal rates. The donor marginal is `(1−b)π/(1−μ)`. A uniform core followed by some choice of incoming donor is not this law. Nor is sampling a uniform core edge and then dropping the reciprocal factor.

Let `r(x)` be a complete aligned reward, e.g. the remaining paired `δ−−θδ+` after deleting exactly identified, disjoint paid charges. On `ρ>0` one can recover its uniform contribution by `π[r 1_(ρ>0)]=(1−μ) I(r/ρ)`. For a class `C`, the exact rate-weighted mass is `(1−μ)I(C)` whereas its uniform mass is `(1−μ)I(1_C/ρ)` on the positive-rate part. If `ρ≥η>0` on `C` the former gives a uniform upper bound divided by `η`; otherwise it does not. In particular `k=0` (no core children, called *sterile* here) still leaves an incoming parent arrow at rate `1/D_parent`. A sterile uniform observation is not automatically a zero-incident observation. To partition the two sampling schemes use a disjoint class split, and carry the uniform contribution directly on the class where the reciprocal weight is too costly.

The descriptions above are for the exact stationary flux. The finite replay uses its finite analogue with the actual founder flag: divide raw arrow mass by `π((k+h)/D)`, without replacing a finite founder by a stationary nonfounder. This enables a countermodel for the retained projections without pretending to construct a second stationary law of the physical growing tree.

## What a corrected quadratic certificate would need

The tracked newborn law carries the entire live environment. For its generator `G` and any bounded local test `V` satisfying discounted Dynkin, `π[(μ−G)V]=μ E_birth V`. This gives an exact mean-zero corrector `GV−μV+μ E_birth V`, including its **birth term**. Equivalently one may use the stationary semigroup of a correctly normalized occupation lifting, if stationarity and the entry source of that lifting are proved. With either valid construction write `Y=r+corrector`. Then `πY=πr` and

```
 πr ≤ π(Y_+) ≤ (m + sqrt(M2))/2,
     m ≥ πY,   M2 ≥ π(Y²).
```

For an all-rational certificate, use `z_+≤(z+a)^2/(4a)` for any `a>0`, pointwise. The upper price is `(M2+2a m+a²)/(4a)`. Apply the same formula to `−Y` for a lower bound. The mean is signed; the squared term must be computed on the same corrected reward, not on a set of independently extreme continuations. A split into incident and sterile classes can carry separate first and second moments, but needs the actual return masses and the second moment with the appropriate reciprocal weight. For example `π[r² 1_C]=(1−μ)I(r² 1_C/ρ)`, not `(1−μ)I(r² 1_C)`.

A block version replaces `Y` by `H⁻¹∫_0^H r(X_t)dt` for a proved *stationary* observation process. The exact variance is `2/H² ∫_0^H (H−t) Cov(r(X_0),r(X_t))dt` in continuous time. A stationary space transport of a sampled donor and target does not bound that time covariance. A killed/sterile return incurs an exit or restart reward; if that term is signed it must be included before squaring. A Chernoff alternative needs an actual bound on the tilted complete-reward semigroup, which includes the same missing correlations; exponentiating separate row means does not give it.

For scale, the preceding [averaged calculation](../averaged/README.md) needs residual positive contrast below `2.9319160e−6` for a `1e−5` upper move at `θ=1.2415`. With a corrected mean bound `m=0`, the quadratic route would need a complete complementary second moment below roughly `3.44e−11` in squared charge units per core. Neither the published sum budget nor an unweighted rarity of a host yields that weighted squared bound.

## Exact spatial countermodel using a pinned `S3` row

Use a degree-three founder with two leaves and one degree-six host. The host has two leaves, its parent, and three named sterile children; each has degree two or three and no core children. All configurations are reachable by legal attachments from `T3`. Let bit 1 denote degree three. Compare uniform mixtures over the four even-parity and the four odd-parity triples. Every named bit is fair and each two-bit marginal is uniform. Thus no census that observes only the host and at most two children, or samples a single directed incident donor and target with the true rates, separates these mixtures. The replay verifies equality of the complete declared projection tables, including the finite raw incident mass `9/20` in both. It does **not** assert equality of an observation that carries the full crown, the count of degree-three arms, or complete coupled path histories.

Use the reviewed [all-eight charge extractor](../../fringe/README.md), `θ=1.2415`, and the actual `1/3` outside donor. The exact contrast depends only on the number of degree-three arms. Its four values are about `−5.246496e−6`, `+4.634775e−6`, `−8.068517e−7`, `+1.640815e−5`. The resulting conditional moments per eligible host are:

| Mixture | Signed mean | Positive hinge mean | Raw second moment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Even | `−1.916762827e−6` | `0` | `7.369687872e−12` |
| Odd | `+7.578118643e−6` | `7.578118643e−6` | `8.341769223e−11` |

Thus even the **sign** of the complete row mean is missing, and the upper second moment cannot be inferred from sterile rates or pair counts. Conditional hinges must be multiplied by the actual class load before entering a global ledger. For these particular finite fixtures the per-core gap is `1.898976294e−6` at unit fixture mass (five cores, one eligible host); embedding a mixture at mass `ε` in a common background scales that gap by `ε`. This is a projection countermodel rather than a claim that these fixtures have that mass under physical `π`, or that two different physical stationary tree laws satisfy the full occupation identities. It identifies at least one missing three-arm statistic; restoring that statistic for this row is not a certificate for the other seven families.

## Time countermodel

Consider three stationary discrete-time sign observations: (i) a uniform phase on periodic `−−++`, (ii) independent fair signs, (iii) a uniform phase alternating one frozen fair sign and a fresh independent sign. In all three the single-time law is fair and the adjacent ordered-pair law gives exactly `1/4` to each outcome. For an even block of length `2n` divisible by four the variances of the **sum** are respectively `0`, `2n`, and `n²+n`. In the third construction the frozen sign is independent of the entire fresh-sign sequence, and a random phase makes the process stationary. At length sixteen the replay gives `0,16,72`. Consequently a stationary pair transport or a one-step return experiment by itself cannot justify variance decay, even when the one-step matches exactly. The physical signed-spine route needs either a direct joint moment of the omitted corrected reward, or a proved multistep contraction/return estimate for that reward with its causal context intact.
