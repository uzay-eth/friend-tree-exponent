# Genuine 19-type birth-Palm return and D18 row obstruction

**Result.** A physical 19×19 direct-birth minorant and a coherent 19×19 degree-occupation minorant exist. The birth constraint improves both sides of a signed D18 first-body contrast through degree 18. It does not control the omitted charges at EDGE precision. There is also a quantitative obstruction to an **exact, environment-independent** 19-type reduction: two reachable births of the same type give different full-lifetime return entries and different original D18 charges. No new exponent endpoint follows.

## The physical equations

Type `i = min(D_mother_at_birth,20)`, with `i=20` meaning every actual degree at least 20. For a core in its live birth environment, let `X_t` be its physical history, `b=(D−k−1)/D` its direct promotion rate, and `m(D)=min(D,20)`. Define `T_μ(i,j)` by averaging the following integral **over the actual conditional birth environment**:

```
∫₀∞ exp(−μt) b(X_t) 1{m(D(X_t))=j} dt.
```

The promotion preserves the parent's degree and increments its core-child count. Its child takes type `m(D)` from that parent and starts at `(D,k)=(2,0)`. Birth compensation (`π(b)=μ`) and the independent stable age give `β T_μ=β`, `β1=1` for the genuine birth-type distribution `β`. Normalized age occupation of a score is `μ` times the corresponding discounted integral under this same birth law. This is a stationary identity at `μ`; a replacement discount does not produce an independently evaluable eigenvalue test. The conditional live birth environment is part of the definition of `T_μ`.

The [stationary P4 calculation](../../../../../../reviews/cold/astra/checks/stationary-p4-sol.md) gives the direct-birth construction at 12 classes. The replay here uses all 19 classes and two versions of the lower comparison:

* **Coherent minorants `A,O`.** For a source type `i`, a core with `L=D−k−1` has gain in `[L,H]`, where `H=L+k/2+1/i` holds throughout its life by monotonicity of maternal degree. Keep the true promotion rate `b`, keep gain arrows at rate `L`, and stop at the first other gain or when degree exits 34. A clock with extra killing fills the gain hazard to `H`. This can be implemented by marking physical arrows and adding predictable killing at the difference, so it defines one lower suboccupation for every actual birth environment. Use discount `.565775` for the birth matrix `A`, and multiply the same integrated age measure by `.565673` for `O`. Degree `34` is a stopping frontier, not a cap on any reciprocal or reward denominator. `O` counts the discounted occupation of current degree classes, not the births of those classes. The exact check conserves discounted probability between age observation, extra killing, and true gain exits. All entries of `A,O` are positive. Row masses of `O` lie in `[0.464203318351,0.686061165294]`; the remaining mass is not replaced by a normalized surviving law.
* **Sharper entrywise minorant `B`.** Solve each genuine birth column by a finite discounted Bellman inequality, keeping all ordinary promotion arrows and using affine gain corners. The reward is zero after the irreversible degree exit; no claim about the post-exit true birth column is required for this positive lower bound. This dominates `A` entrywise; the different columns need not arise from a common policy. Its row sums lie in `[0.612617488630,0.690948538892]`. The unassigned stationary birth mass is `1−βB1`, between `0.30905` and `0.38739`. The actual `T_μ` need not be row-stochastic, and this unassigned mass is not a lower bound on physical exit mass or on signed continuation error. In both constructions strict row positivity follows already from a positive path of consecutive leaf gains to the target class followed by positive time in its childless row (and, for births, its positive promotion intensity).

For any signed vector of conditional lower prices `f`, `β≥βB` gives `β f ≥ min_i[f_i−v_i+(Bv)_i]` for any `v≥0`. The upper price uses the lower price of the negated score. Prices of the truncated signed score integrate over **all ages**; once degree exits its mask, the score is identically zero. Within the mask the same actual pressure enters the exact affine row and the gain rate. The Bellman checks use both corners of pressure and both endpoints of discount in the normalized running source `s f`; the residual is affine in either variable when the other is fixed. This comparison allows history-dependent pressure. No lower birth entry or killed occupation mass is applied to a negative reward as if it were exact.

## Exact obstruction to row preservation

Start from the edge `0–1`. Send `1→0` to create founder 0, then `0→1` to promote core 1 and attach its first leaf 3. Send `3→1` to attach leaf 4 and repeat to grow the degree of mother 1 to either 20 or 1000. Now `1→4` creates the genuine newborn, with permanent type 20 in both cases. Each arrow is legal, with strictly positive rate. The conditional gain at the virgin differs by `1/20−1/1000`, even though its state, birth type, and promotion rate are identical. The difference survives full occupation in the direct type-2 birth entry:

* Type-2 births from this core can occur only before its first gain or promotion. In the virgin the constant part of its departure hazard is `3/2`; the extra hazard is the live maternal reciprocal. If `S(t)` is the probability the core is still virgin, then `S'=−(3/2)S−E[1_virgin/D_mother]`. Integration gives the full type-2 entry as `1/[2(s+3/2)]` less `1/[2(s+3/2)]` times the discounted extra-hazard integral.
* The degree-20 mother's own gain hazard is at most 20 until her first gain, including when hidden events alter her stock. Thus the extra-hazard integral is at least `(1/20)/(s+3/2+1/20+20)` in that environment. It is at most `(1/1000)/(s+3/2)` in the other, by monotonicity of her degree and the constant virgin departure hazard. This gives the strict uniform row gap `>0.000430038` over `[.565673,.565775]`. A uniform point value for this entry, based only on the birth mark, has error greater than half that gap. The claim is about the conditional row before mixing, not the error in the unknown stationary conditional mean.
* Even without a cap, the birth degree alone does not determine the time-resolved return. At maternal degree 3 either promote leaf 3 before leaf 4, or leave 3 a leaf. Both newborns have exactly the same maternal degree. Their initial virgin loss `h=3/2+1/3` agrees but the mother's initial gain hazards differ by `1/2`. The second derivative of virgin survival is `h²` minus the drift of `h` from non-departure jumps, and only maternal gain changes `h` by `1/4−1/3`. The survival derivatives therefore differ by `1/24` at age zero. This distinguishes the time densities without asserting a signed integral gap at an individual discount for the exact-degree pair.
* Continue either history by sending the virgin's first leaf to it until `(D,k)=(18,0)`. The two original D18 first-body rows at this same census differ in absolute value by `22883/97200000000000000` and `4753/12150000000000000` per occurrence. The two occurrences use the same pinned affine coefficients, but different true reciprocals. Both also have the same true induced type for their next promotion. A type-only birth credit at this row, `b v_18`, is identical in the two histories and cannot remove the difference. The fixture checks the physical arrows and reads the signed corners of the SHA-pinned first-body table; it neither freezes a rate nor rounds a physical degree.

This rules out substitution of a single exact row for the live environments, including substitution that also remembers the first-body census at this fixture. It does not rule out averaging with the true conditional law, joint edge constraints, or a correction that knows the live donor state.

The mask is an original summand, with the newborn edge assigned once to the promoting parent in its post-promotion state. It is not fresh post-EDGE slack. To get the last row, write `π(δ₋−tδ₊) = paid + omitted₋ − t omitted₊`, with both omitted masses nonnegative by the pinned GMP charge decomposition. Subtract the respective certified paid floors (`2.195265070137`, `1.768483194382` micro-units per core, rounded down) from the total ceilings `(edge_hi−a) πP_upper` and `(b−edge_lo) πQ_upper` to form the two omitted upper budgets. This use of the frozen bracket and the stationary denominator ceilings is a scale comparison, not a new endpoint proof. The wide interval is a bound on this particular method, not a lower bound on the achievable error. A full quotient needs the common product `r=(πδ₋/πδ₊)(πQ/πP)`, so that `μ=(a+b r)/(1+r)`. The [frozen EDGE interval](../interpolate/edge/FREEZE.md) maps to `r∈[0.051337057689,21.733756269951]`; a positive masked contrast is not a sign for this product. Near the numerical center `.56572365`, a width of `1e−8` requires odds width about `3.9e−4`, or direct control of the centered score at scale `1e−8·π(P+tQ)`.
