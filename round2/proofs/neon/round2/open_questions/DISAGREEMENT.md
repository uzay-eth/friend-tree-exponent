## Channels, marks, and the local bound

For a core with $`L`$ leaves, full degree $`D`$, and $`b`$ inert slots, the leaf-gain rate is $`a=L+\sum_{v\text{ real core neighbour}}1/D_v=D-b-\sum_v(1-1/D_v)`$. The promotion rate is $`L/D\le1`$; it removes one leaf and gains a core neighbour without changing $`D`$. Couple gains and promotions separately at paired cores: a shared clock at the minimum rate and a one-sided clock at the difference give the correct marginals. Coupled promotions pair the two newborn cores; no event simultaneously causes a promotion and a neighbour gain. Unpaired cores and both copies of charged branches keep their true dynamics. Since each copy's population is dominated by a rate-one Yule process from its initial size, the joint chain is nonexplosive.

Orient the common core tree away from the initial infected core or connected set. When a core first becomes infected, freeze its current (eligible) common outward neighbours. Every later child born at that core, including each side of a paired promotion, starts a charged family excluded from the eligible exploration. A clean eligible vertex has the same leaf count, degree and core neighbours on both sides, and at most one infected common neighbour, its predecessor. This holds inductively: clean gains and clean promotions are coupled unless the predecessor sends an unmatched gain. An excluded branch attaches only at its infected anchor and cannot bypass an edge in the common tree. At an ordinary infection the gain is unmatched, so the new infected pair has a degree gap of one. All already-present common children except the predecessor qualify as outward candidates. This includes children created while the pair was still clean.

At an ordinary infection let $`d\ge2`$ be the smaller degree at infection. Track $`Z=|D^+-D^-|`$, $`R`$ the number of union promotions since infection, and $`B`$ the number of frozen outward neighbours infected from this vertex. Initially $`(Z,R,B)=(1,0,0)`$. In $`a=D-b-\sum_v h(D_v)`$, $`h(x)=1-1/x\in[1/2,1)`$ on cores. Frozen clean neighbours and unchanged inert slots cancel. The predecessor and the $`B`$ infected outward neighbours each contribute an absolute difference at most $`1/2`$. Each union promotion adds either one unpaired child (contribution less than one) or a pair (difference at most $`1/2`$). Thus, including all return influences,

```math
|a^+-a^-|\le Z+R+(1+B)/2.
```

At an inert root with an extra leaf and no predecessor this still holds, and its stub ensures at most $`d-1`$ outward candidates, as does the predecessor for ordinary infections. A specified still-clean neighbour has unmatched gain rate exactly $`|1/D^+-1/D^-|\le Z/[d(d+Z)]`$ by monotonicity of both degrees, regardless of their order. Thus new outward infections have total rate at most $`(d-1)Z/[d(d+Z)]\le1`$. Union promotions have rate at most one, the larger marginal promotion rate. The sum $`Q=Z+R+B`$ has unit upward jumps at total rate at most $`Z+R+(1+B)/2+2\le Q+5/2`$. A common Poisson measure for the upward channels couples it below the process $`Y`$ starting at 1 with birth rate $`k+5/2`$ at $`k`$: at equal counts the dominating process accepts every upward proposal of $`Q`$, and downward jumps of $`Q`$ preserve the order. This comparison is conditional on the joint infection history.

## Discounted reproduction: exact numbers and the infinite tail

Compensating the first-outward-infection indicators and using monotonicity of $`z/(d+z)`$ bounds the expected discounted offspring mass by

```math
m_d(s)=\tfrac{d-1}{d}\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\mathbb E[Y(t)/(d+Y(t))]dt.
```

It decreases in $`s`$, so use $`s_0=11/20`$. The independent holding time at state $`k`$ has rate $`k+5/2`$; the mean discounted occupation $`w_k`$ is the Laplace transform of the entrance time divided by $`k+5/2+s_0`$. This gives $`w_1=20/81`$ and $`w_{k+1}/w_k=(20k+50)/(20k+81)`$. The process is nonexplosive (it is bounded above by a Yule process with a suitably larger initial count and time rate), hence the occupation sum is $`1/s_0`$. Using $`k/(d+k)=1-d/(d+k)`$ and dropping positive terms in the subtracted sum gives the upper bound

```math
\frac{d-1}{d}\left(\frac{20}{11}-d\sum_{k=1}^{16}\frac{w_k}{d+k}\right).
```

Exact fraction arithmetic for $`2\le d\le63`$ gives its maximum at $`d=6`$, $`1669075573443141901662377825/1772128093509182310390306279<19/20`$. The [checker](check_disagreement.py) regenerates all 62 comparisons. For $`d\ge64`$, $`\mathbb E Y(t)=(7/2)e^t-5/2`$ by the linear drift (finite first moments by Yule domination); Jensen for $`z\mapsto\min(1,z)`$ bounds the expectation in the integral by $`\min(1,7e^t/(2d))`$. Replace $`s_0`$ by $`1/2`$ and split at $`\log(2d/7)`$; the integral is $`4\sqrt{7/(2d)}-7/d\le\sqrt{7/8}<15/16<19/20`$. Thus $`q=19/20`$ works over the whole domain.

## Charging and the free-tree initial conditions

An ordinary infection pays at most $`q`$ expected discounted offspring relative to its own infection time. A seed infection with arbitrary degree gap has at most $`d`$ frozen outward candidates and per-edge intensity at most $`1/d`$, so pays at most $`1/s`$; this also works for each of two adjacent seed infections. Once below the seed set, successive conditioning and summation over generations yield expected total discounted infection mass at most $`2(1+1/[s(1-q)])`$; for Long's single inert seed with the unit discrepancy, the bound is $`1/(1-q)`$. These calculations use no independence. They may first be done up to a finite population and infection generation, and extended by monotone convergence.

Each common eligible core contributes equally to the counts. Every remaining core is in a unique charged family in its own marginal, rooted at a child created by an infected core after its infection (or an initial unpaired child that is already a newborn). Future feedback remains present. At each infected core the union-promotion rate is at most one; each such event charges at most two families. The future marginal law conditional on the joint birth state is the physical law; its discounted family mean is at most the tagged newborn bound $`W(s)`$ from the renewal estimate, also when an exterior is retained. Hence conditional on an infection at time $`\tau`$ the expected discounted future charges there are at most $`2W(s)e^{-s\tau}/s`$. Summing gives the bound quoted in the report, with an extra $`W(s)`$ for the possible initial newborn. For the inert unit discrepancy the finite-horizon form is $`2W_T(s)/[s(1-q)]\le40W_T(s)/s`$. Finite-horizon $`W_T(s)\le\int_0^T e^{(1-s)t}dt`$ follows because each cell member promotes at rate at most one. If estimating an inert cell by free cells instead of using the direct renewal argument, replace each of its $`b`$ stubs by a genuine degree-$`R`$ parent. The total inward rate across these edges is at most $`b/R`$ until the first mismatch. Cauchy–Schwarz and the second-moment bound of a rate-one core Yule process give an error $`O_{s,T}(\sqrt{bT/R})`$ for any fixed finite profile. Neither continuation asserts that arbitrary predictable input equals a physical exterior.

**Scope of the rest of Long's section.** The stated hybrid estimate follows conditionally on Long's separate hybrid growth input. After crossing degree $`M`$, the predictable extra rate into the cut component's root is at most $`1/(M+1)`$. For the inert finite-horizon value $`f_r`$, the backward equation is $`\partial_r f_r=C+(\mathcal L^0-s)f_r`$, $`f_0=0`$. The time-dependent Dynkin identity for $`e^{-st}f_{T-t}`$ after crossing therefore gives the inert continuation plus the expected integral of the true extra rate times the root-leaf increment of $`f_{T-t}`$. This identity localizes at finite population; on a bounded time interval the physical Yule moments bound the polynomial size terms in the stopped identity. Integrating the uniform finite-horizon gradient gives $`W_T(s)\le\int_0^T e^{-st}A_M(t)dt+40W_T(s)/[s^2(M+1)]`$, where $`A_M`$ bounds the hybrid's mean. At $`M\ge132`$ and $`s\ge11/20`$, the factor is at most $`16000/16093<1`$. If $`s`$ is above the hybrid growth exponent, first absorb at finite horizon and then use monotone convergence to obtain $`W(s)<\infty`$. Finally monotonicity gives $`F_x(t)\le s e^{st}W(s)`$ by bounding the resolvent integral below over $`[t,\infty)`$, so the supremum newborn exponent is at most $`s`$. Taking $`s`$ strictly between the hybrid exponent (and $`11/20`$) and an alleged larger uniform exponent gives Long's contradiction. We neither invoke nor reprove the separate hybrid input.
