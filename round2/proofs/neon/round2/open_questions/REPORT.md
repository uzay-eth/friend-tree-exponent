## PROVED

### 1. When a fixed label becomes a hub

Write $`D=D_u(n)`$, $`L=L_u(n)`$ for the degree and number of leaf neighbours of $`u`$, and $`\rho=\sum_{w\sim u:D_w(n)\ge2}1/D_w(n)`$. For $`n\ge3`$ the conditional probabilities of $`L`$ increasing and decreasing by one are $`(L+\rho)/n`$ and $`L/(nD)`$, respectively. A degree increment is exactly a leaf-count increase. With $`\mathcal F_n=\sigma(T_2,\ldots,T_n)`$, we have

```math
\frac{(L_u(n)-1)_+}{n}\le \mathbb E[Z_u\mid\mathcal F_n]\le\frac{D_u(n)}n.
```

Indeed $`D_u(n)/n`$ is a bounded supermartingale since $`L+\rho\le D`$; $`(L_u(n)-1)/n`$ is a bounded submartingale since the decline probability is at most $`1/n`$. Their limits agree: $`D-L`$ is nondecreasing and starts at at most one at $`m_0=\max(3,u)`$; each unit increase has conditional probability at most $`1/n`$. Thus $`\mathbb E[D-L]=O(1+\log n)`$, which gives $`(D-L)/n\to0`$ along dyadic times by Markov and Borel–Cantelli and then at all times by monotonicity of $`D-L`$. Taking the positive part on the bound uses $`Z_u\ge0`$. This recovers the normalized-degree convergence used here from [Addario-Berry et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20185).

**Hub characterization.** On $`\{D_u(n)\to\infty\}`$, the number of changes of $`L`$ is infinite. From $`L=0`$, its next change must be an increase if it occurs; at $`L=1`$, the conditional probability of an increase given a change at the next step is at least $`1/2`$. Starting at $`L\le1`$, the conditional probability that the next change both occurs and is downward is at most $`1/2`$: sum the one-step inequality over possible first-change times, with the indicator of no prior change. Therefore the event of infinitely many changes with $`L`$ always at most one from a given time has probability zero: there must be arbitrarily many visits to one followed by a downward change, each successive such trial having conditional probability at most $`1/2`$. Taking the union over starting times shows that $`L\ge2`$ occurs infinitely often on $`\{D\to\infty\}`$.

The same first-change argument from $`L=2`$ gives infinitely many times with $`L\ge3`$ on $`\{D\to\infty\}`$: the conditional probability of a downward exit at the first change is at most $`1/3`$, and avoiding 3 with infinitely many visits to 2 forces infinitely many such exits. At a time $`N`$ with $`L\ge3`$, start a Pólya urn with one black ball and $`N-1`$ white balls, and write $`B_n`$ for its black count. Use a common uniform variable, with the first $`B_n/n`$ of the gain interval assigned to the black increment while the gap $`G=L-B`$ is at least two. Initially $`G\ge2`$. Stop at $`G=1`$. Until then the gap increases with probability at least $`G/n`$ and decreases with probability at most $`1/n`$. Consequently $`2^{-(G-1)}`$, stopped at $`G=1`$, is a nonnegative supermartingale, and the conditional failure probability is at most $`1/2`$. If no failure occurs then $`L_n/n\ge B_n/n`$, whose limit is positive almost surely by the Pólya urn beta limit. Use fresh urns after failures, starting each at the next $`L\ge3`$. The probability of $`k`$ launched trials all failing is at most $`2^{-k}`$ by conditioning at their starting times. Thus almost surely on $`D\to\infty`$ a trial succeeds. The converse follows from $`D_n/n\to Z_u`$. This works simultaneously for all labels by countability. The proof adapts the paper's urn argument for an edge to one vertex; it does not assume that a leafless vertex must have another gain.

**Every label has positive hub probability.** Force four consecutive recipients to be $`u`$ starting at $`m=\max(2,u)`$. Each prescribed transition has positive probability in every finite tree, and afterwards $`L_u\ge4`$. The conditional mean bound gives $`\mathbb P(Z_u>0)>0`$.

**Promotion does not suffice.** At the first promotion of $`u`$, force each of its leaf neighbours to be promoted (without attaching to $`u`$), then give each of its two neighbours at least seven leaf neighbours by prescribed gains. This finite sequence has positive conditional probability from every promotion history. To freeze at degree one instead, first make the sole neighbour a core if necessary and give it seven leaves without attaching to $`u`$.

Now run the chain that at each step rejects $`u`$ as recipient and renormalizes the other probabilities. In this chain $`u`$ remains bare (no leaf neighbours); writing $`\lambda=\sum_{w\sim u}1/D_w\le1`$ for either prepared case, a neighbour $`w`$ gains a leaf with probability at least $`L_w/n`$ and loses one with probability at most $`1/(n-\lambda)\le2/n`$. Couple a separate one-black Pólya urn to each $`L_w`$ until its gap first hits 3. On a gap $`G\ge4`$ the up-probability is at least $`G/n`$ and the down-probability at most $`2/n`$. The stopped $`2^{-(G-3)}`$ bounds each failure probability by $`1/8`$, since the initial gap is at least 6. The urns need not be independent: one may first sample the tree transition, then thin each gain event to its urn's required probability. By a union bound and the beta limits there is positive probability that **both** neighbours grow linearly (or that the sole neighbour does in the degree-one case).

For any finite cylinder event $`F \in \mathcal F_k`$ and $`n \ge k`$, the probability of $`F \cap \{u\text{ avoided through step }n\}`$ in the real chain equals $`\mathbb E_{\rm rej}[\mathbf 1_F \prod_{j=N}^{n-1}(1-\lambda_j/j)]`$. Letting $`n \to \infty`$ by bounded convergence shows that the two finite measures $`A \mapsto \mathbb P_{\rm real}(A \cap \{u\text{ avoided forever after }N\})`$ and $`A \mapsto \mathbb E_{\rm rej}[\mathbf 1_A \prod_{j=N}^\infty(1-\lambda_j/j)]`$ agree on all cylinder sets, hence on all of $`\mathcal F_\infty`$ by the $`\pi`$-$`\lambda`$ theorem. On the positive-probability event of linear neighbour growth the infinite product is strictly positive, since $`\sum_j\lambda_j/j<\infty`$. Thus degree one or two, as prepared, freezes with positive probability. In particular $`\mathbb P(Z_u=0\mid u\text{ is promoted})>0`$ and $`\mathbb P(Z_u>0)<1`$. Promotion has positive probability by forcing a recipient hit. The $`1/n`$ threshold estimates promotion by time $`n`$, whose probability has a strictly larger limit than the hub probability.

**Exact formula for the mean and strict comparison of 2 and 3.** For $`m=\max(2,u)`$ put $`Q_u(j)=\sum_{w\sim u}(1-1/D_w(j))`$. One-step drift and bounded convergence give the nonnegative-term series

```math
\mathbb E Z_u=\frac1m-\sum_{j=m}^\infty\frac{\mathbb E Q_u(j)}{j(j+1)}.
```

Each term is rational and computable by the finite recursion below. At $`n=8`$, the exact lower bound on $`\mathbb E Z_2`$ is $`1123736801/5225472000`$, while the exact upper bound on $`\mathbb E Z_3`$ is $`18007643/87091200`$. Their difference is $`43278221/5225472000>0`$. Label 1 has the same law as label 2 by initial-edge symmetry. The same general upper bound $`\mathbb E Z_u\le1/u`$ for $`u\ge2`$ and positivity prove that for every fixed $`u`$ the mean is strictly larger than all sufficiently late-label means.

The error of the finite-time mean is uniformly small: put $`m_0=\max(3,u)`$. At $`m_0`$, $`D-L\le1`$, and it increases precisely upon promotion of a leaf adjoining $`u`$, at probability at most $`1/j`$ in step $`j`$. The two conditional-mean brackets above consequently give

```math
0\le \mathbb E[D_u(n)/n]-\mathbb E Z_u\le \frac{2+\sum_{j=m_0}^{n-1}1/j}{n}.
```

A conditional bracket is useful for future exact or certified computations: at time $`n`$, its width is at most $`(D_u(n)-L_u(n)+1)/n`$. It is directly computable without simulating any future steps.

For the hub probability itself the characterization gives the exact nested finite-distribution formula $`\mathbb P(Z_u>0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P(D_u(n)\ge k)`$. This uses monotonicity in time of $`D_u`$ and continuity of probability for the decreasing events of ever attaining degree $`k`$. It gives no sharp error on either limit for comparing labels.

### 2. Both tails of the core limit

**Inputs and normalization.** Use Astra Claim 6 (`round2/fringe/almost-sure.md`, independently audited `HOLDS` by three model families): $`C_n/n^\mu\to X\in(0,\infty)`$ a.s., and its nineteen-mark renewal estimate (3) and free mean expansion (4). Write $`\Phi=d_2\Psi`$, so (4) at $`k=2`$ is $`\mathbb E_y C(t)=e^{\mu t}\Phi(y)+O(C(y)e^{\sigma t})`$, with $`\sigma<\mu`$. Claim 6 gives $`\Phi\le AC`$ and $`C\le K(1+\Phi)`$, so $`\Phi\ge a_0 C`$ for $`C\ge K_0`$ with some fixed integer $`K_0`$. Total promotion rate is at least one: the star centre promotes at rate one, and a core tree with at least two vertices has two endpoints, each of promotion rate at least $`1/2`$. The hitting time of $`K_0`$ cores is therefore stochastically bounded by the sum of $`K_0`$ rate-one exponentials, by the conditional waiting-time bounds (each survival probability is at most $`e^{-t}`$). The eigenfunction identity from (4), obtained by conditioning at time 1 and using the uniform error and the Yule first moment, gives $`\Phi(y)\ge e^{-\mu}a_0 K_0\,\mathbb P_y(C(1)\ge K_0)`$. This is uniformly positive and combines with the large-$`C`$ bound to give $`aC\le\Phi\le AC`$ for every seed with a core, including a uniform lower bound on all stars.

For a tagged newborn in any finite exterior, assign it one cell and let its future promotions inherit its cell. In the same marked renewal identity used in Claim 6, there are no initial nonroot fringes in this cell. Only the cell root's direct promotions and transfers to children **born into the cell** are immigration. The weighted immigration is bounded by $`1/d+2e^{q_0 t}`$ with $`d=3/20`$ and $`q_0=9/25`$: the initial cell weight is at most 2; the capped promotion inequality used in Claim 6 gives drift at most $`q_0`$ times this weight, and the weighted-child identity bounds the transfer term by the cell weight. Old exterior neighbours enter the root's physical degree and rates, but are not immigration terms. Integrating the renewal bound $`|P_t1(S)|\le K e^{\mu t}W_{\rm mark}(S)`$ against this immigration proves $`\mathbb E C_{\rm tag}(t)\le K' e^{\mu t}`$ uniformly, since $`q_0<\mu`$ (add one for the root). The same argument works with silent stubs, which count in degrees but send no input. Thus the supremum $`W(s)`$ of tagged newborn discounted rewards satisfies $`W(s)\le K'/(s-\mu)`$. In the inert newborn seed the event that its first source ring promotes within time one has positive probability, uniformly bounded below; more directly the leading cell coefficient in this seed is positive because the direct term pays a positive Perron newborn value while it still has one leaf. The same renewal convolution, with its $`O(e^{\sigma t})`$ remainder, therefore gives $`V_s^\infty\asymp(s-\mu)^{-1}`$ as $`s\downarrow\mu`$. Together with the integrated free expansion this recovers both resolvent comparisons near criticality. See also the cell derivation in Astra Claim 10 (`round2/fringe/spatial-limit.md`, §1). No critical resolvent or eigenfunction result from Long is assumed.

**Bounded jumps at the true exponent.** The canonical free eigenfunction $`H=\Phi`$ has all the following properties, for positive constants $`a,A,B,K_2`$ independent of the initial tree with a core:

```math
aC\le H\le AC,\qquad \mathcal L H=\mu H,\qquad |\Delta H|\le B,\qquad
\mathbb E[(H(T_{n+1})-H(T_n))^2\mid T_n]\le K_2 C_n/n.
```

Here $`\mathcal L`$ is the rate-one-per-source continuous-time generator. Use the aggregate coupling and independently reproduced contraction in [DISAGREEMENT.md](DISAGREEMENT.md), based on [Long's boundary gradient](https://github.com/long-mathematics/friend-tree-growth/blob/5bd25a7d506cb64a8444b86334ab531d295f6c78/sections/original/boundary_gradient.tex). To bound the change under a promotion, start with the parent infected (same full degree but one fewer leaf and one extra unpaired child), and charge the unpaired child as a newborn family at time zero. The appendix allows this initialization and also one or two initially infected adjacent common cores of arbitrary degree discrepancy. Its bound on the expected integrated absolute count discrepancy is $`(1+(4/s)(1+1/[s(1-q)]))W(s)`$ with $`q=19/20`$.

For a gain at a core whose initial full degree is $`d`$ and number of core neighbours is $`c`$, delay starting the infection exploration until the first unmatched promotion at the focal core or unmatched gain at a neighbour (time $`\sigma`$). Before $`\sigma`$, pair all common promotions, including at the focal core; all other cores remain identical, and the focal pair has the same core-neighbour count $`c(t)`$, of expected size at most $`c+t`$ when stopped at $`\sigma`$ (paired promotions there have rate at most one). The degree of both focal copies remains at least $`d`$. The mismatch intensity on the event $`t<\sigma`$ is at most $`2c(t)/d`$: each of $`c(t)`$ neighbours has unmatched gain intensity at most $`1/d`$, and the difference of promotion rates $`1-c(t)/D`$ is at most $`c(t)/d`$. Hence $`\mathbb E[e^{-s\sigma};\sigma<\infty]\le(2/d)(c/s+1/s^2)`$. Before $`\sigma`$ the counts agree. At $`\sigma`$ there is either one additional unpaired newborn or a newly infected neighbour of the focal core. The preceding arbitrary-initial-discrepancy estimate applies; it freezes all then-current common outward neighbours and charges subsequent union promotions. It follows that the gain resolvent gradient is bounded by $`K'W(s)(c+1)/d`$, uniformly in $`s\in(\mu,1]`$.

Multiply the resolvent gradient bounds by $`s-\mu`$ and take $`s\downarrow\mu`$. By the integrated uniform expansion, $`(s-\mu)V_s^{\rm free}(y)=\Phi(y)+O(C(y)(s-\mu)/(s-\sigma))`$, so the full limit exists without choosing a subsequence. In particular **this construction yields $`H=d_2\Psi`$ uniquely in this normalization**, not a uniqueness assertion for every core-comparable generator eigenfunction. The semigroup identity gives $`\mathcal LH=\mu H`$ by the finite-successor first-event formula. For the second moment, gain at $`v`$ has rate at most $`d`$, and $`d((c+1)/d)^2\le 2(c+1)`$ since $`c\le d`$ and $`d\ge2`$. Sum over cores, using $`\sum c=2(C-1)`$; sum the squared promotion jumps using total promotion rate at most $`C`$. Dividing by $`n`$ proves the displayed one-step second-moment estimate.

**Stretched-exponential upper tail.** In discrete time $`M_n=H(T_n)/g_n`$ is a martingale for $`n\ge3`$, where $`g_n=\Gamma(n+\mu)/\Gamma(n)`$ and $`g_{n+1}/g_n=1+\mu/n`$. Its increments are bounded in absolute value by $`b/g_{n+1}`$ with $`b=B+A\mu`$, since $`C_n\le n`$. Also $`g_n\asymp n^\mu`$, $`g_n/n^\mu\to1`$, and the sum of squared increment bounds is finite ($`2\mu>1`$). Hence $`M_n\to M_\infty`$ a.s. and in $`L^2`$. With $`H=d_2\Psi`$, Claim 6 identifies $`M_\infty=X`$ (the bounds $`aX\le M_\infty\le AX`$ also follow directly from core comparability). For $`x`$ large choose $`m=\lfloor c x^{1/(1-\mu)}\rfloor`$ with a sufficiently small fixed $`c>0`$, so that $`M_m\le A m/g_m\le ax/2`$ deterministically. Conditional Hoeffding's lemma for the centered bounded increments gives, for either sign and $`t>0`$,

```math
\mathbb P(\pm(M_\infty-M_m)\ge t\mid\mathcal F_m)\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\sum_{j\ge m}(b/g_{j+1})^2}\right).
```

The infinite-horizon form follows by conditional Fatou on the exponential moment and then optimization. The denominator is $`O(m^{1-2\mu})`$, so $`\mathbb P(X>x)\le\exp(-c' x^{1/(1-\mu)})`$ for $`x`$ large. Integrating the tail gives every positive moment and $`\mathbb E\exp(\theta X^{1/(1-\mu)})<\infty`$ for sufficiently small $`\theta>0`$.

**Left tail: lower bound.** A star must have stayed a star from population 3; given a star at size $`j`$, the next recipient is its centre with probability $`(j-1)/j`$. Hence $`\mathbb P(T_m\text{ is a star})=2/(m-1)`$. Given a star at $`m`$, $`\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_m]\le M_m/a\le A/(a g_m)`$. Choose $`m=\lceil K_0\varepsilon^{-1/\mu}\rceil`$ with $`K_0`$ sufficiently large and apply Markov to obtain $`\mathbb P(X<\varepsilon)\ge c''\varepsilon^{1/\mu}`$.

**A continuous-time variance bound from any seed.** Write $`S_t`$ for the continuous-time free tree from seed $`y`$ and $`J_t=e^{-\mu t}H(S_t)`$. The second-moment rate estimate (before division by $`n`$) bounds the predictable quadratic variation by $`K_2\int_0^t e^{-2\mu u}C(S_u)\,du`$. By (4) and $`H\le AC`$ its expectation is at most $`V C(y)`$ uniformly in $`t`$, for a finite $`V`$. Localization at finite population, the Yule moments on bounded time intervals, and the martingale isometry then give an $`L^2`$ limit $`J_\infty`$ with $`\mathbb E_y J_\infty=H(y)`$ and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}\nolimits _y J_\infty\le V C(y)`$. Both estimates apply after stopping by the strong Markov property. For the matching right-tail lower bound below, the discrete counterpart is equally direct: $`\mathbb E[C_j\mid\mathcal F_m]\le (A/a)C_m g_j/g_m`$ by martingale iteration, so summing the conditional variance increments gives $`\mathbb E[(M_\infty-M_m)^2\mid\mathcal F_m]\le V' C_m/g_m^2`$. In particular $`\mathbb P(M_\infty<M_m/2\mid\mathcal F_m)\le 4V'/(a^2 C_m)`$.

**Matching left-tail upper bound.** We use the star-departure and downward-passage argument of Astra Claim 17 (`round2/open/limit-law/left/README.md`), with the variance estimate just proved by the aggregate coupling. For a seed with $`C(y)\ge K`$, use successive first passages of $`J_t`$ below levels $`aK2^{-j}`$, $`j\ge1`$. At a passage at time $`t`$, the value is at least $`3/4`$ of the level if $`K\ge4B/a`$: overshoot is at most $`B e^{-\mu t}\le (B/(aK))J_t`$, and between jumps paths are continuous. The first passage is separated from $`J_0`$ by at least half its value, and every subsequent level is separated from the passage value by at least one third of that value. From a passage state the conditional future variance is at most $`V e^{-2\mu t}C(S_t)`$, at most $`V J_t^2/(a^2 K)`$. Doob's maximal inequality (or the first-passage martingale isometry) bounds the chance of the next passage by $`9V/(a^2K)`$. By conditioning at each passage, the probability of reaching level $`j`$ is at most $`[9V/(a^2K)]^j`$. Convergence forces every passage at a level strictly above the limit. Thus by choosing $`K`$ large enough we obtain a uniform negative moment of order $`2/\mu`$ for $`J_\infty`$ over seeds with at least $`K`$ cores, by integrating the resulting power bound with exponent greater than $`2/\mu`$.

From the size-three star let $`\tau`$ be the first promotion time. Until then the promotion intensity is exactly one, so $`\tau\sim\mathop{\mathrm{Exp}}\nolimits (1)`$. After departure first wait for size $`K+3`$ if necessary; the additional holding times have rates at least four and there are at most $`K+3`$ of them. If more promotions are then needed to reach $`K`$ cores, their rate is uniformly at least $`1+\delta`$, $`\delta=\min(1/6,1/K)`$: two core endpoints alone give one; three endpoints give $`3/2`$; and with exactly two endpoints the presence of at least three leaves implies that either an endpoint has two leaves (rate at least $`2/3`$ there), or an internal core has a leaf (rate at least $`1/K`$ there). There are at most $`K`$ further promotions. Conditional waiting-time tails and iteration of conditional exponential moments show that the additional time $`D`$ to reach both thresholds has $`\mathbb E[e^{rD}\mid\mathcal F_\tau]\le K_r`$ for $`1<r<1+\delta`$. No independence of waiting times from the departure state is needed.

At the final hitting state $`J_\infty=e^{-\mu(\tau+D)}J'`$, with $`J'`$ the future limit restarted there. Its conditional negative moment of order $`r/\mu<2/\mu`$ is uniformly bounded. Thus $`\mathbb P(J_\infty\le x\mid\mathcal F_\tau)\le\min(1,K'_r x^{r/\mu}e^{r\tau})`$. Integrate against the rate-one exponential law by splitting at $`\log(1/x)/\mu`$ to obtain $`O(x^{1/\mu})`$. The Yule holding-time construction gives $`n e^{-\tau_n}\to W>0`$, independent of the embedded chain (the centered holding-time series has summable variances and the harmonic sum differs from $`\log n`$ by a convergent sequence). Claim 6 identifies $`M_\infty=X`$ under the normalization $`H=d_2\Psi`$, hence along those epochs $`J_\infty=W^\mu X`$. For any fixed $`b>0`$ with $`\mathbb P(W\le b)>0`$, independence gives $`\mathbb P(X\le x)\mathbb P(W\le b)\le\mathbb P(J_\infty\le b^\mu x)`$. This proves the claimed upper bound. Together with the star lower bound it gives $`\mathbb E X^{-p}<\infty`$ exactly for $`0<p<1/\mu`$ by tail integration. The leading constant at zero is not identified.

**Matching right-tail lower bound.** Start with $`T_4`$ a path, a positive-probability event, and take its two cores as the active set. Each active core starts with one leaf neighbour and one core neighbour. For an active set of size $`k`$, prescribe four phases of $`k`$ steps each: give each active core one gain in each of the first two phases, then promote one leaf of each active core in each of the last two. In each phase the unserved core can be chosen in any order. It has a leaf for the gain, and during promotions it has degree four and a leaf available. The distinct unserved cores thus offer total next-step success probability at least $`r/(4n)`$ when $`r`$ are unserved. At the end, the $`2k`$ newly promoted cores form the next active set, each with one leaf and one core neighbour. With $`k=2,4,8,\ldots`$, the population at the start of a phase block is $`4(k-1)`$ and throughout the block is at most $`8k`$. A phase therefore costs probability at least $`k!/(32k)^k\ge (32e)^{-k}`$, by the logarithmic integral bound on $`k!`$. Multiplying over phases and generations gives population checkpoints $`m=4(k-1)`$ with $`C_m\ge k`$ at probability at least $`e^{-K_4 m}`$ for some constant. The conditional variance estimate gives $`M_\infty\ge M_m/2`$ with conditional probability at least $`1/2`$ for large $`m`$ on this event. There $`X\ge H(T_m)/(2Ag_m)\ge c_1 m^{1-\mu}`$. Choosing the first checkpoint with $`c_1 m^{1-\mu}>x`$ proves $`\mathbb P(X>x)\ge e^{-K_5 x^{1/(1-\mu)}}`$ for large $`x`$, since successive checkpoints have bounded ratios. In particular the right-tail power is sharp, and exponential moments of $`X^p`$ diverge at every positive parameter for $`p>1/(1-\mu)`$.

### 3. What Q5 asks

The paper's Q5 asks what components induced by vertices of degree at most $`N`$ look like and whether they resemble young friend trees. The full thresholded forest including isolated leaves has a trivial uniform-vertex limit for fixed $`N`$: every nontrivial component contains an original core when $`n\ge3`$, and each original leaf in such a component is adjacent to a surviving core. Hence there are at most $`(N+1)C_n`$ vertices in nontrivial components and at most $`C_n`$ excluded vertices. Since $`C_n/n\to0`$, a uniform original vertex is isolated in the forest with probability tending to one.

**Fixed-label limit and independent set.** Fix labels $`u,w`$ and an integer $`k`$. On the event that $`u,w`$ become adjacent and $`D_u`$ stays at most $`k`$, for every step $`n`$ after the edge appears the conditional probability that source $`u`$ sends to $`w`$ is $`1/(nD_u)\ge1/(kn)`$. Lévy's conditional Borel–Cantelli lemma forces infinitely many such sends on this event, so $`D_w\to\infty`$ and $`Z_w>0`$ by §1. Intersect over the countable triples $`(u,w,k)`$. The finite-degree vertices form an independent set in $`T_\infty`$ a.s. At cutoff $`N`$, a fixed label either crosses $`N`$ and is permanently deleted, or has final degree at most $`N`$. In the latter case its neighbours eventually stabilize and all cross $`N`$, so its component is eventually a singleton. Nontrivial components escape every fixed finite set of birth labels.

**Comparison with the Astra claim ledger.** Claims 6 and 7 give the random core normalization, fixed-degree frequencies, hub mass one and total-variation convergence of the attachment law. Claim 10 gives the rank-one normalized mean limit on bounded local scores and the one-ended empirical rooted-core law; for that law the ordinary descendant fringe has a size tail asymptotic to a positive constant times $`n^{-\mu}`$, and the root *core*-degree tail has successive ratio tending to $`1/(1+\mu)`$. The descendant fringe is not itself the component after a degree cutoff. Claim 11 supplies almost-sure ranked hub counts $`x^\mu\#\{v:Z_v\ge x\}\to AX`$ as $`x\downarrow0`$ and $`N_{\ge k}(n)\sim AX(n/k)^\mu`$ along every intermediate scale $`k\to\infty`$, $`k/n\to0`$, with the ledger's normalization of $`A`$. Neither of these count asymptotics identifies correlations between neighbouring retained vertices.

The later **Claim 19** answers the fixed-cutoff component question for a uniformly sampled surviving original core: almost-sure total-variation convergence to its finite component in the cut one-ended law. Counting components once is a different law obtained by biasing by the reciprocal of their surviving original-core counts, with normalization for a probability law. **Claim 20** addresses the young-tree comparison for components with a surviving original core, counted once and rooted at their highest surviving core. Taking $`n\to\infty`$ first and then $`N\to\infty`$, their density per $`C_n`$ is asymptotic to $`AN^{-\mu}\log N`$, and their rooted shape is asymptotically, in total variation, a newborn with a silent parent observed at a uniform age on $`[0,\log N]`$ (omit the exterior parent from the shape). The simultaneous $`n,N\to\infty`$ component limit remains open in the ledger. These results distinguish four sampling rules: fixed label, uniform ordinary vertex, uniform surviving core, and uniform counted component.

## DEAD ENDS / OBSTRUCTIONS

**Birth-order stochastic domination and hub probabilities.** In the reachable tree with edges $`12,13,24,35,46,57`$, labels 3 and 4 tie at degree two, but their recipient rates are respectively $`1/2+1/2=1`$ and $`1/2+1=3/2`$. Exchanging the initial endpoints and the two arms gives the reverse rate comparison at the same degree tie. A same-tree ranking is not preserved, and marginal stochastic domination would require a different coupling or comparison of the full environments. Positivity-probability ordering and strict ordering of the remaining adjacent limiting means are unresolved here. The obstruction to finite-size mean separation is quantitatively visible: at $`n=8`$ the rigorous brackets overlap for 3 vs. 4, 4 vs. 5 and 5 vs. 6.

Astra Claim 18 goes further for separated labels: for each fixed $`u`$, all sufficiently late $`v`$ have strictly smaller survival probability at every threshold $`0\le x<1`$. It also gives a reachable conditional reversal of hub probabilities; neither fact settles the unconditional comparison of arbitrary adjacent labels.

**Long's arithmetic obstructions — ten lines.** Read-only shallow clone at `5bd25a7d506cb64a8444b86334ab531d295f6c78`; see the [README](https://github.com/long-mathematics/friend-tree-growth/blob/5bd25a7d506cb64a8444b86334ab531d295f6c78/README.md) and [rational specialization audit](https://github.com/long-mathematics/friend-tree-growth/blob/5bd25a7d506cb64a8444b86334ab531d295f6c78/docs/rational_specialization_audit.md).

1. The Siegel–Shidlovskii argument applies to modified local redirection, whose CMJ characteristic equation is scalar; it does not transfer to isotropic redirection.
2. The original first-promotion transform is explicit, but $`\Phi(\mu)\ne1`$; solving the first-promotion arithmetic does not solve the critical equation.
3. That transform's positive rational telescopes occur only at positive integer discounts; its coefficient heights constrain the natural E/G-function routes.
4. The full-environment critical operator has $`r_{\rm ess}=r=1`$, and finite full-environment compressions are nilpotent; a finite determinant needs a new space or reduction.
5. Finite pressure roots approach $`\mu`$ from below, but this is not a numerator-smallness argument: even primitive reduced population numerators have minimum log size $`N\log N+O(N)`$ at $`\mu`$.
6. The fixed-generation population error does not control the outer pressure approximation; geometric iteration with exact pressure does not supply that missing evaluation error.
7. Mellin shifts retaining the unshifted term retain the critical tail; shifts deleting it give convergent transforms without an established rational critical value.
8. The exact signed renewal germ tends to one on the positive real axis, but its Taylor radius can be limited by complex zeros and rational discounts do not make the infinite-history coefficients rational.
9. Signed product martingales leave a neighbour-dependent leaf term; the identities alone also hold for a process with exponent one. The iterates $`C,\mathcal LC,\ldots`$ are linearly independent, and a negative Hankel determinant excludes the proposed positive exponential mixture.
10. The concrete remaining handle is a candidate-specific signed/Padé approximation with a provably nonzero residual smaller than the reciprocal exact denominator, or a new arithmetic operator reduction. Neither is obtained by the tail or hub results here.
