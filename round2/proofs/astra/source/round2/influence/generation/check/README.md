## Physical identity and source

Start with one ordinary core, a real leaf and a silent parent slot. The root is generation 0 and has birth mark 0; it is counted exactly once. Let `Q` denote the nonnegative old-copy operator, with rates `a_Y−p` and `p` for `Y,Y⁺` respectively. Let `L` lift a label score to a type by evaluating it at the mark generated by a promotion of that type's root, `b` multiply by the physical promotion rate, and `J` restrict to virgin types. For any test put weight `z^depth` on the summand at each physical core. An exterior parent send contributes `z^(d+1)p[f(Y⁺)−f(Y)]` where the parent depth is `d`. The plain-plus copy row contributes `z^(d+1){p[f(Y⁺)−f(Y)]+a_Yf(Y)}`. Its last term cancels the child's own `−a_Y` killing at depth `d+1`. Root killing is zero. A direct promotion puts its new summand at depth `d+1`, so it also gets one `z`. Intrinsic changes to an existing summand stay at its own depth. This proves the generator identity for the depth-weighted first moment, including for a finite projection onto depth coefficients `0,…,m` (use the truncated polynomial ring). A finite **physical** size cutoff applied to the whole tree is not an independent **particle** size cutoff on fringes.

Write `R_z=L0−a+zQ`, `K(s,z)=z J(s−R_z)^−1 bL`, and

```
F(s,z) = e_0 (I−K(s,z))^−1 J(s−R_z)^−1 (s·1).
```

The factor `s` is required: a physical core born at `τ` contributes `s∫_τ^∞ exp(−st)dt=exp(−sτ)`. Thus `[z^m]F=A_m^∞(s)`, including `A_0=1`. To justify the unbounded identity, for any fixed coefficient use finite-time stopped jump identities and Yule moments (as in Claim 2). The depth coefficients of the positive particle semigroup are nonnegative and match the physical coefficients at every finite time. Long's bound `A_m≤s^(−m)` makes the Laplace transform finite coefficientwise, and gives absolute convergence of the scalar for `|z|<s`. Cutting the positive expansion at each direct newborn transfer gives the displayed resolvent formula initially for sufficiently small `|z|`; no inversion at the critical point is used for the coefficient identity. The capped mark gives the same scalar coefficients when the *actual* reciprocal remains in `p` and cap is used only at mark assignment; the inert start still has mark 0 (do not replace it with `1/20`).

The direct physical birth-flux coefficients at positive depth are `z J(s−R_z−z bL)^−1 bL·1` at the inert start, with no factor `s`. Parent/root generator localization and one final newborn factor make this agree with the occupation coefficients, after adding the generation-zero source. The ordinary compact kernel at `z=1` instead counts its own renewal steps: `K(s,1)^m` alone is not the physical generation `m`.

## Physical-generation gap and ratio criterion

Let `W` be Claim 2's weight, `ρ=.51`. The copy-weight identity gives `QW≤.65 W`, so the residual resolvent is analytic in `z` for `|z|<1+(s−ρ)/.65`. On `|z|≤1` use domination by the positive residual; outside, a Neumann series about the modulus-one resolvent (at the same argument) gives the extra collar. The resulting kernels are compact on continuous labels by the original-particle death-rate argument, or finite rank in the capped version. Their scalar source is also analytic there. At `s=μ`, the physical-generation pencil has the Perron crossing at `z=1`; its derivative on Perron vectors is positive since each power of `z` in `K` is positive and has degree at least one.

For explicit aperiodicity, at **every** input label compare two paths to the same output mark `1/3`: (a) grow one leaf on the original virgin, then emit from degree 3 (one transfer); (b) promote at degree 2, take the plus copy of the old child, then emit from its degree 3 (two transfers). For either path each stage denominator is at most `s+4`, and path weight is at least `c=1/[6(s+4)^3]`. This also holds with the cap. Let `h>0` be the Perron eigenfunction normalized arbitrarily and set `η=c h(1/3)/max h`. On the unit circle at `z=e^(iθ)` the norm in the `h`-weighted supremum space is at most `1−2η(1−|cos(θ/2)|)`: remove two common masses of size `η` from that output column before taking the absolute sum. The estimate also holds for the inert row when it is included in the kernel. Hence there are no other poles of the scalar on `|z|=1`. Near but off criticality, the same argument applies at the positive crossing `z=r(s)` using two lower path masses multiplied by `r(s)` and `r(s)^2` respectively. No lattice claim about physical time is involved.

For the capped kernel one can avoid unknown Perron ratios in this far-arc bound: every matrix entry on labels 2–20 is at least `κ=1/[2(s+22)^19]`, by growing a virgin star directly to the desired degree then emitting. At criticality evaluate `h=Kh` at label 3 and retain the contribution of a maximizing output label; this yields `h(1/3)/max h≥κ`. Include the inert root as an output-free starting row (no return to mark 0, and its own normalized value is the weighted average from that row). For this argument take the maximum over ordinary labels: the angular inversion for the ordinary matrix and the source formula for the separate inert row suffice. This explicit margin is small and does not replace a validated local Perron-complement bound.

By compact analytic Fredholm and simplicity, there exists `R>r(s)` in the residual collar with only the positive simple pole in `|z|≤R`, for real `s` sufficiently near `μ`. Write `v(z)=J(s−R_z)^−1(s·1)` and let `ν` be the positive left Perron functional at the crossing, with `νh=1`. Then

```
C = h(0) ν(v(r)) / [r ν(K_z'(s,r)h)] > 0
|A_m − C r^(−m)| ≤ M R^(−m),
M ≥ sup_(|z|=R) |F(s,z) − C/(1−z/r)|.
```

In particular at `μ`, `A_m^∞(μ)` has a positive limit and converges exponentially in **physical generation**, assuming the underlying compact renewal theorems. This is an existence argument, not a numerical value of `R,M,C`. A usable numerical route is a rational enclosure of `K(s,z)` and its derivatives on a small disc about `r`, with Perron-complement inverse there, plus the explicit angular margin away from that disc and a validated outward collar bound. For example, if `||K_z'||_h≤D_*` on the collar, a radial displacement less than `2η(1−|cos(θ/2)|)/D_*` preserves invertibility on the far arcs at criticality. Use a certified contour resolvent bound to evaluate `M`. If `e_m=(M/C)(r/R)^m<1`, then

```
| A_(m+1)/A_m − 1/r | ≤ (1/r) (e_m+e_(m+1))/(1−e_m).
```

This states what would make the ratio a sharper lower/upper decision near the crossing than a comparison against Long's uniform `12(s+3)`. The short test does not evaluate a complex contour or resolve a near-critical trial sign.
