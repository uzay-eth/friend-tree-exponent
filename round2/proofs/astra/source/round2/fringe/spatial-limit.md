# The spatial eigenmeasure and the tree seen from a uniform core

Let $`Q_t`$ follow the core cell of a specified old vertex, as in [the spatial construction](../operator.md), on the space $`\mathcal X`$ of locally finite core trees marked by their numbers of leaves. Let $`\sigma<\mu`$ be the real number in [the finite-label renewal estimate](almost-sure.md), (3), with $`0.51<\sigma`$. There is a bounded strictly positive continuous function $`h`$ and a probability $`\pi`$ such that for every bounded radius-$`k`$ test $`f`$,
```math
 \left\|e^{-\mu t}Q_t f-h\pi(f)\right\|_\infty
   \le C 4^k e^{-(\mu-\sigma)t}\|f\|_\infty .                 \qquad\text{(1)}
```
The constants in the renewal estimate determine $`C`$. The norm ranges over finite and infinite environments. For every fixed $`f\in C_b(\mathcal X)`$ the left side tends to zero. In particular $`h`$ spans the bounded continuous eigenspace of $`Q_1`$ at $`e^\mu`$ and $`\pi`$ is its unique Borel probability eigenmeasure there. Under the original growth from an edge, the conditional law of the marked tree rooted at a uniformly chosen core converges almost surely, in the local topology, to $`\pi`$. This limiting tree has one end. Its finite descendant fringes have an explicit distribution in terms of the residual semigroup.

## 1. Start in one old cell

Use the nineteen-label process of [finite-kernel.md](finite-kernel.md). A new core attached to its promoter is a child in the oriented core tree. In an arbitrary initial environment follow the cell of the old root $`o`$. Its members other than $`o`$ were born into this cell after time zero. Their descendant fringes are finite planted trees. Old neighbours of $`o`$ retain their ordinary physical rates. Give the new members the same permanent birth labels as in the positive renewal. They satisfy the parent inequality at all times.

Write $`M_t f`$ for the mean sum of a fringe score over those new members. The only terms of the generator not allocated within this sum come from promotions of $`o`$ and its transfers to its *new* children. Precisely, with $`p_o=1/D_o`$, $`b_o=L_o/D_o`$, they are the expectations of
```math
 \mathcal I f=b_o f(J_{1/m(D_o)})+
  \sum_{Y\text{ new child at }o} ((a_Y-p_o)f(Y)+p_o f(Y^+)).
```
Thus $`M_t f=\int_0^t I_u P_{t-u} f\,du`$, where $`I_u=\mathbb E\mathcal I`$ and $`P`$ is the full fringe mean semigroup. This is the same cancellation as [renewal.md](renewal.md), equations (1)–(3). In an infinite initial tree one can also obtain the identity by the stopped jump formula. For local integrability, the number of actual vertices in the cell, including the leaf stock at $`o`$, has birth intensity at most its size plus $`c_o(0)/2`$ from the finitely many old neighbours. It has finite moments of every order on a finite time interval for each starting environment. The backward derivatives of the score before the observation time have the same polynomial bounds in the sizes of the finite fringes as in the renewal proof.

Let $`Z`$ be the sum of the usual path weights $`w_x h_0(c_x)`$ over this cell, where $`h_0(c)`$ denotes the vertex weight called $`h(c)`$ in the renewal proof. The core degree $`c_o`$ includes the old neighbours even though they are outside this sum. Only promotions by cell members affect $`Z`$. Their one-event inequality is still (4) of the renewal proof, using the clamped labels, including at the original root. We have $`Z(0)\le2`$ regardless of the environment, so $`\mathbb E Z(t)\le2e^{qt}`$ with $`q=9/25`$. Each $`W(Y^+)=W(Y)`$ and the weighted new-child sum is $`Z-h_0(c_o)`$. Hence, uniformly in the starting environment,
```math
 I_t W\le 1/d+2e^{qt},\qquad d=3/20.                            \qquad\text{(2)}
```
All newly founded individual fringes retain an ordinary parent slot. In particular the many old neighbours of a high-degree root never appear as new child summands.

Write $`H`$ and $`d(f)`$ for the right function and the positive linear functional of (3) in [almost-sure.md](almost-sure.md), with $`d_2=d(1)>0`$. The notation $`d(f)`$ is separate from the numerical weight parameter $`d`$. Insert that estimate into the cell identity, use (2) and integrate the missing leading tail from $`t`$ to infinity. Since $`q<\sigma<\mu`$ this gives
```math
 M_t f=e^{\mu t} h(G,o)\,\nu(f)+O(e^{\sigma t}\|f\|_W),
 \quad h(G,o)=d_2\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu u}I_u H\,du,
 \quad \nu(f)=d(f)/d_2.                                         \qquad\text{(3)}
```
Here $`\|f\|_W=\sup_S |f(S)|/W(S)`$ and the error constant is absolute for the fixed renewal scheme. The law $`\nu`$ is a countably additive probability on finite marked planted fringes. Indeed $`d(f)`$ is the integral of the finite residual occupation measures with the nineteen positive left Perron coefficients, as in that proof.

Taking $`f=1`$ in (3) and adding the original cell root gives uniform convergence $`e^{-\mu t}Q_t1\to h`$. Thus $`h`$ is bounded and continuous by the spatial construction and $`Q_t h=e^{\mu t}h`$ by the semigroup property. It is strictly positive at every input. If the root has a leaf, it has positive probability of promoting during a prescribed time interval. At a bare root one of its finitely many ordinary neighbours can ring and select it before any change at that neighbour, then the root can promote. This finite script has positive probability even in an infinite tree by its finite degrees and the old-edge construction. The direct term of $`I`$ at that promotion has a uniformly positive newborn $`H`$ value.

## 2. Recovering both sides of a rooted ball

Let $`Z_k(S)`$ count the core descendants of the planted root at depth exactly $`k`$, with $`Z_0=1`$. The smallest child edge factor in $`W`$ is $`1/4`$ and $`h_0\ge7/10`$, $`a_S+d\le13/20`$. Thus $`Z_k\le4^k W`$. From $`S`$, reroot at each such descendant $`x`$ and read the given un-oriented radius-$`k`$ cylinder $`f`$ there. All the marks it needs, including the number of leaves and the exterior-parent slot at the ancestor if its degree is read, are present in $`S`$. Define $`F_{k,f}(S)`$ to be the sum of these readings. The bound $`|F_{k,f}|\le4^k W\|f\|_\infty`$ follows. For $`k=0`$ this reads the actual root mark. If the chosen convention includes total degrees on the boundary of a ball, these are read from all the stored children of that boundary vertex plus its parent slot.

Sum $`F_{k,f}`$ over the new cores of an initial cell. Each member at depth greater than $`k`$ contributes exactly once, at its depth-$`k`$ ancestor. The original member and the new members at depths at most $`k`$ cost in expected absolute value at most $`\|f\|_\infty\sum_{j=0}^k t^j/j!`$, by iterating the rate-one domination for direct promotions. This sum is at most $`2^k e^{t/2}`$. Equations (2)–(3) give (1) with coefficient $`\nu(F_{k,f})`$. In particular for $`f=1`$, comparison at any single input with $`h>0`$ gives $`\nu Z_k=1`$ for all $`k`$.

The probability eigenmeasure of [operator.md](../operator.md) exists and integrates $`h`$ to one by uniform convergence for $`f=1`$. Apply it to the preceding estimate. This identifies $`\nu(F_{k,f})=\pi(f)`$, for every bounded cylinder, independently of which radius is used. If $`\widetilde\pi`$ is any other probability eigenmeasure of $`Q_1`$ at $`e^\mu`$, integer times in (1) first give $`\widetilde\pi(h)=1`$ and then equality on all the cylinders. This proves uniqueness.

For completeness take a bounded continuous $`f`$. A compact set of $`\pi`$-mass $`1-\varepsilon`$ is covered by finitely many marked-ball cylinders on each of which $`f`$ oscillates by at most $`\varepsilon`$. Refine their union into cylinders of one fixed radius, retaining values within $`\|f\|_\infty`$ and assigning zero outside. Estimate (1) applies to this bounded cylinder score and to the indicator of the complement of the union. Boundedness of $`h`$ shows that replacing $`f`$ by this score costs $`O_f(\varepsilon)`$ in the limit uniformly over the starting graphs. Thus the rank-one convergence holds for each fixed $`f\in C_b`$. Applying it to any bounded continuous eigenfunction of $`Q_1`$ at $`e^\mu`$ proves that function is $`h\pi(f)`$.

## 3. The ordinary finite trees

Root at the founder of $`T_3`$ and store the capped birth marks. For a finite planted type $`S`$ let $`E_S`$ be the event that it embeds downwards, root to root, into the observed fringe. All vertices that are cores in $`S`$ must be cores with the indicated birth marks at their images. A leaf in $`S`$ may map to any ordinary child vertex. The two events $`E_S`$ and $`E_S\cap\{|X|\ge |S|+1\}`$ are increasing during the life of every fixed physical vertex, are decidable from a fixed finite number of downward levels, and their indicator difference is $`\mathbf1_{\{X\cong S\}}`$. Descendant sizes here count ordinary vertices. At equal finite size an embedding is a rooted isomorphism. The corresponding two physical sums over noninitial cores are nondecreasing.

The proof of sample-path convergence for cumulative degree counts applies to either of these two sums. Here are the bounds needed for this extension. In the coupled trees differing by one new leaf in (6) of [almost-sure.md](almost-sure.md), birth labels and parent directions agree on the clean matched cores and on births from clean matched promoters. To compare a bounded indicator depending on at most $`r`$ downward core levels, discard every dirty vertex and every one of its first $`r`$ ancestors in each tree. On the remaining clean cores all the required descendant data coincide. Each discarded dirty core accounts for at most $`r+1`$ vertices on its path to the founder. The initial marking and infection calculation in that proof thus gives $`3(r+1)e^{2u}`$ for the one-leaf difference of conditional means at horizon $`u`$. The variance estimate follows with the square of this extra constant. The uniform finite-seed mean expansion (4) of the same proof holds for both indicators since each is bounded by $`W`$. Its leading random coefficient is the same positive eigenmartingale limit as for the total count, multiplied by $`d(f)`$. Mesh Borel–Cantelli with $`h=(\mu-\sigma)^{-1}\log(t+2)`$ and monotonicity of each sum give convergence at every physical time, hence at all discrete Yule epochs.

Taking differences, the frequencies of all individual planted fringe types among the cores converge almost surely to $`\nu(S)`$. Intersect the countably many events. Their limiting masses sum to one, which gives total-variation convergence on finite fringe types. Omitting the founder costs $`1/C_n`$.

For ancestral neighbourhoods use the probability on a finite ancestor fringe and a distinguished depth-$`k`$ vertex in it. One may enumerate the distinguished positions up to rooted marked isomorphism, retaining their multiplicities. At each fixed ancestor type the empirical mass for each distinguished position converges to its multiplicity times $`\nu(S)`$. The total limiting mass is $`\nu Z_k=1`$. The empirical mass is at most one, since every noninitial vertex has at most one depth-$`k`$ ancestor. Consequently the missing mass near the founder tends to zero and the empirical measures of distinguished finite types converge in total variation for each fixed $`k`$. Projecting to the ball about the distinguished vertex gives the claimed almost-sure local convergence. The marked, oriented ancestor distributions are consistent since they are limits of the same empirical trees. Each sampled vertex has an infinite chain of ancestors, and the fringe descending from every finite ancestor is itself finite almost surely. A ray leaving this ancestor chain at any stage would be contained in one of those finite fringes. This gives exactly one end after the orientation and birth labels are forgotten.

The convergence in (1) is in the norm of test values at the input; for each radius it is uniform over all functions of that ball. The laws of the entire finite trees need only converge in the local topology.
