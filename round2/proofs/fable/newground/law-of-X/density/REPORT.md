## 1. Inputs and time of cut

Use Astra Claim 6 for the positive finite limit from any reachable free tree, Astra's `open/limit-law/left` §3 for arbitrarily high uniform negative moments of the **continuous-time** amplitude once the starting core count is sufficiently large, Fable's `newground/law-of-X/REPORT.md` § small-tail for the two-tip waiting bound, Astra's `open/limit-law/prefactor` for `V_j ⇒ V` and the prefactor (its `k=j+1` normalization differs by a factor tending to one), and the deterministic no-cross Dirichlet identity of Astra `open/limit-law/smoothing` §§1–2 (or the same killed-chain construction of Neon-2 Claim 1). The disjoint-site combinatorial lemma used below is Fable `proofs/almost-sure-exponent.md` Lemma 1.1. The construction that follows uses it only for the disjoint drivers and low core degree.

Let `H_k` be the first time with `k` cores, starting from `T_3` (whose embedded limit equals `X`) or from `S_j`, the `j+1`-vertex double star just after departure from a star of size `j`. Consider `k` above a fixed deterministic minimum at least 3. At time `H_k+h` for a fixed integer `h` we get a history-measurable list of at most `k` candidates, each consisting of **eight** pendant branch edges to cut around a common base, with all eight branch seeds isomorphic to one fixed rooted tree with one exterior slot. Each seed has a private leaf at its root, has ordinary size `d`, and can have as many cores as desired (chosen once). The remaining component has at least two vertices. There exist `b>0, D<∞` independent of the initial state such that

```
P(no listed candidate has all eight edges uncrossed after H_k+h) ≤ D exp(−bk).   (1)
```

Here are details of the graphical construction. At `H_k` take the at least `k/6` disjoint sites of Lemma 1.1. Its A-sites have a base of core degree ≤2 and a designated real leaf; triple sites are `u,w,z` whose centre `w` has exactly `u,z` as neighbours. During a first unit silence `u` (and `z` at triples); in a triple require the first point of `w` in this unit to send to `u`, guaranteed by the first-half mark since `u,z` are silent so no third neighbour can be added at `w` before this point. Tag the resulting leaf in a triple, and use the existing designated leaf at an A-site. In the second unit keep `u` silent and require at least ten rings of that leaf. Only `u` can promote its leaves, so at the end of this unit the base has at least eleven leaves and at most two core neighbours. In a third unit require exactly eight base rings and marks ≤1/2 with leaves ranked first. Before the eighth ring there are at least four leaves and at most nine core neighbours; to guarantee the choices, use marks ≤1/4 instead (the lower leaf fraction is `4/13`). Those rings promote eight different leaves and leave at least three real leaves at the base. From promotion to the end of the third unit silence each new branch root and the new leaf attached to it. After the third unit and through the end of preparation, silence the base. In a fixed number of subsequent unit intervals, prescribe the rooted seed separately in all eight branches, with exactly one scheduled originating vertex clock ringing once per unit within each branch and the other branch clocks silent; arrange the mark in the exact interval for the desired neighbour. These finitely many choices have uniformly positive probability since there are no external births inside a branch while the base is silent. One example of a seed is a path starting at the root together with an extra leaf at the root; it is built from an edge by explicit leaf additions, each one feasible by a neighbour's ring. At the terminal time each branch root has at least one real leaf and so does the base. Branch clocks can be fresh tagged independent sources from promotion onward, and each newborn gets a fresh assigned source: this is predictable reassignment with correct conditional rates. Promoted leaves are not among another site's reserved drivers because their only neighbour was their own base. Any necessary flags are defined on the preassigned drivers, with unused drivers ignored on failure; e.g. postpromotion silence is imposed by requiring silence on the entire third unit of the preassigned new-root/new-leaf drivers. Thus the per-site flags are independent conditionally on the starting history, each with probability bounded below by a fixed positive constant. The candidate list can be recorded in the enlarged history at the prescribed terminal time. On a flag, no sends from outside can remove the reserved stocks or enter a pendant branch except via its attachment edge, which the script prevents. Different sites' eight branches are disjoint since all lie beyond leaves of distinct old bases. The remainder for a candidate contains its base and its spare leaves.

**Use a canonical, physical-history-only selector at every level.** For each base from the beginning of the block, inspect branches incident at the terminal time whose far side contains none of the cores present at the beginning. If at least eight have the prescribed rooted shape and the base has a spare real leaf, select the first eight by labels; otherwise the site has no candidate. A successful preparation guarantees that the selector finds a candidate, though it need not pick the eight used by the script. The selected branches at distinct bases are disjoint: their far sides point away from the subtree spanned by the old cores (if there is one old core there is only one site). Thus the auxiliary constructions can be performed separately for the bound at each fixed `k`, even for overlapping preparation windows; the candidates and the actual laws being partitioned do not depend on any auxiliary choices.

For each site that has a candidate, protect one distinct real leaf stock at each of its nine anchors, by the independent birth–death family construction of Astra `density/README.md` §1: tracked births at rate one per member, retirements at rate 1/2, retirement causing physical promotion with probability `2/D_anchor`. Births and retirements alone define independent lower stocks; they use distinct drivers. All tested directed edge sends can be implemented with separate unit-rate candidate Poisson clocks (accepted with `1/D_sender`), independent of these lower drivers. The remaining physical rates are nonnegative: tracked retirements pay just the directed channels to tracked leaves, candidate clocks just the tested core-edge channels, and the other physical channels are filled at their remaining rates. All tested edges are distinct across sites, including when sites are adjacent. Starting from one, a lower stock has a positive probability of staying above `a exp(t/2)` for all `t≥0` for some deterministic `a>0`: on the survival-positive-limit event its normalized infimum is positive (on every compact interval its nonzero integer count is at least one). Require this trajectory at each anchor and that each of the 16 candidate clocks has no point beneath the deterministic mark envelope `min(1,a^−1 exp(−t/2))`. This sufficient event has a constant probability `q>0`, and the tests are independent across selected sites conditional on the end-of-preparation history. The crossing intensity is indeed below the envelope when the stock bound holds. The marginal physical process still has its original rates in the enlarged filtration. The conditional probability of all candidates failing is at most `(1−q)^r` for `r` sites with candidates, and this number is at least the number of successful preparation flags. Integrate over the independent preparation flags to prove (1). There is no conditioning on the artificial success flag in a density calculation: it is used only to bound failure of the physical no-cross tests.

## 2. Moment of size at the cut, relative to starting size

Let `n_0` be the starting size and `N=|T_(H_k+h)|`. For any fixed `a<1` from `T_3`, or any `a<4/3` uniformly from `S_j`, and any `η>0`,

```
E[(N/n_0)^a] ≤ C(a,η) exp(η k).                         (2)
```

From a free tree with at least one core, total promotion intensity is at least one until the next promotion: for a star it equals one; otherwise two core tips contribute at least one half each. From ≥2 cores the stronger uniform wait survival `(36/25)exp(−4t/3)` is the cited Fable two-tip argument. For each fixed `t>0` the uniform interpromotion survival tends to zero as the number of cores grows: the independent site flags given in Fable's inverse-moment lemma force a promotion by `t` (A-site base mark ≤1/3; triple middle mark ≤1/2 in first half, then base mark ≤1/3 in second). On the no-promotion event all core degrees remain unchanged so the flags suffice. The uniform exponential moment of the wait therefore tends to one at every order strictly below the appropriate survival rate, by dominated convergence on the survival integral. Strong Markov iteration of these uniform bounds gives `E exp(r H_k) ≤ exp(o(k))` for each such `r`, uniformly in the starting states under consideration. The normalized Yule martingale has `sup_{s≥0} e^−s |T_s|/n_0` with moments of every finite order uniformly in `n_0`; apply Doob to its Gamma limit divided by `n_0` (which is the mean of `n_0` iid exponentials, so Jensen bounds its moments). Hölder with an exponential order `r>a` strictly below the survival rate now proves (2).

## 3. Clock-safe partition and moments of the small-side amplitudes

Order candidates by `k` and then by site order. Keep a candidate's no-cross event only if earlier candidates all failed. This disjoint partition is exhaustive by (1). When conditioning on the physical history through the present cut time, every earlier candidate list is known because the times `H_l+h` are increasing. For a predecessor, any crossing through the current history is already decided. All later crossings of a predecessor edge on the **current no-cross event** are either impossible (the edge was also cut now) or determined by the marked embedded arrow sequence of the current component containing that edge. The predecessor edge already exists at the current time and can never move to another component. Use marked arrows, not just unmarked tree shapes; any future vertices can therefore use local component birth labels, without merging the future clocks to learn global labels. Thus the disjointification mask depends on these sequences and the already fixed history, not on relative holding times after the present cut. In particular, mass of a retained sublaw at `k` (or any individual site at `k`) is at most the failure probability at `k−1`, `D exp(−b(k−1))`. The first level just uses mass at most one.

For a candidate, conditional on the cut history, run nine independent reference chains with genuine embedded arrows normalized to sum one, their holdings exponential at the current component size, and weights `L_i=∏(1−d_i(n)/n)` where `d_i(n)` is total outgoing slot rate at size `n`. By the cited exact no-cross law, the physical no-cross subprobability has density `∏L_i` over this reference law. The further disjointification mask depends only on the embedded marks as just proved. Under the reference law the size shares `P_i` have a Dirichlet law with eight parameters `d` and one parameter `N−8d`, independent of all embedded paths. On `∏L_i>0` the physical limit is `∑ a_i P_i^μ` with `a_i` the component limit, nonnegative; the eight small sides in fact have positive limits except on weighted null sets. For the latter and for quantitative control, glue two copies of the fixed small seed at their slots. This free tree is reachable and has large core count. Conditional on the two independent weighted embedded paths and no-crossing, a combined size ratio equals `u^μ a+(1−u)^μ a'`. If `a,a'≤ε`, this is at most `2ε`. Consequently, for the fixed small-side subprobability `ν` of its amplitude (weight `L_i`),

```
ν([0,ε])² ≤ P(X_glued ≤ 2ε) ≤ A ε^80.                  (3)
```

For the last bound choose the fixed branch seed large enough to apply Astra's large-core negative-moment lemma at order 80, then pass from the continuous to the discrete limit: `Z=W^μ X_glued` with independent `W` and therefore `E X_glued^−80 = E Z^−80/E W^−(80μ)` by Tonelli. Choose the seed size large enough that the denominator is finite (it is strictly positive). The first inequality uses exactly the product weight and is unconditional on the beta share. Hence all moments of inverse orders strictly below 40 are finite under `ν`. The half-limit from the glued no-cross law yields the same measurable limit on each side reference sequence as under the nine-side law. For `B=min(1,a_1,…,a_8)` we obtain `E_Q[(∏ L_i) B^−s | cut history]≤C_s` for `s<40`, uniformly: bound the maximum of the eight inverse powers by their sum (plus one), use product weights, and integrate independently. Choose `d` large enough also that `2d>2μ`.

## 4. Uniform kernel estimates and summability

Pair the eight equal-sized small sides. Given pair totals `T_1,…,T_4` and the remaining share, the splits within pairs are independent `Beta(d,d)` and independent of the paths. Put `P=min T_i`. If `n_*=1` for `T_3`, or `n_*=j` for `S_j`, the distribution here is for `Y=n_*^μ X_seed`. Write `λ=n_*^μ P^μ B`. For each pair the oscillatory factor has phase `g(u)=a u^μ+b(1−u)^μ`, times `n_*^μ T_i^μ`. Since `−g''≥μ(1−μ)(a+b)`, if `s` is the frequency applied to `g` then the beta characteristic factor is bounded by `min(1,C_d (|s|(a+b))^−1/2)`. Indeed split into `|g'|≤v`, of length at most `2v/[μ(1−μ)(a+b)]`, and its complement of at most two intervals, where integration by parts with the beta density and monotone `1/g'` gives `C_d/(|s|v)`. Optimize in `v`. This works even when the amplitudes are equal and the critical point is at the beta mode. Multiplying all four factors, and excluding weighted null sets with `B=0`, shows that the fixed-path, fixed-total characteristic function is at most `C min(1,|ξλ|^−2)` in modulus. Fourier inversion for an integrable characteristic function gives a nonnegative bounded continuous density on `R`; for each `0≤α<1` its supremum (α=0) or α-Hölder seminorm (α>0) is at most `C_α λ^−(1+α)`. For the seminorm integrate the characteristic function times `|ξ|^α` and use `|exp(iξx)−exp(iξy)|≤2|ξ(x−y)|^α`.

Pointwise, each such kernel vanishes below `λ`, since even the first pair contributes at least `n_*^μ T_1^μ min(a_1,a_2)` (use `u^μ+(1−u)^μ≥1`), and all other terms are nonnegative. Thus its supremum after division by `z>0` is ≤`C λ^−2`. We use this for `S_j` only. For either estimate average over the pair totals first, possible since the likelihood and mask are independent of the shares. For `s∈{1,1+1/10,2}`, the pair totals are marginally `Beta(2d,N−2d)` and

```
E[P^−μs | history] ≤ 4 Γ(2d−μs) Γ(N) / [Γ(2d) Γ(N−μs)] ≤ C_s N^(μs).
```

The first inequality uses `P^−r≤Σ T_i^−r`, and the last follows from the gamma-ratio asymptotic and a bound on the remaining finite set. After integrating totals, the random envelope for each individual candidate is at most `C (N/n_*)^(μs) B^−s`. Apply Hölder in the measure “original history followed by reference paths with weight `∏ L_i`,” omitting noncandidate histories (this is a subprobability measure). Use exponents `9/8, 16, 144/7` for `(N/n_*)^(μs)`, `B^−s`, and the disjointification indicator. Here `8/9+1/16+7/144=1`; the first factor is bounded by (2), because `(9/8)μ(1+1/10)<1` and `(9/8)2μ<4/3`. For `S_j`, `N/n_*=(N/n_0)(j+1)/j`, with the extra deterministic ratio uniformly bounded. The second factor uses (3), since `16s≤32<40`. The last factor is at most `C exp(−b(k−1)7/144)`. Formula (2) allows arbitrarily small positive exponential growth, so the bound is summable even after multiplying by the at most `k` candidates. This proves global boundedness and global `1/10`-Hölder continuity for `X` and the uniform estimates for `V_j=j^μ X_{S_j}`:

```
sup_j ||f_(V_j)||_(C^{0,1/10}(R)) < ∞,
0 ≤ f_(V_j)(z) ≤ C min(1,z)  (z>0).                     (4)
```

The conditional kernels are the continuously extended Fourier versions. Their integrated and summed versions are continuous by the summable bounds (and directly obey the seminorm and weighted bounds). Their integrals give the entire original laws by the exhaustive partition. This argument requires no control of how often the eight side amplitudes are close to one another.

## 5. Pointwise left asymptotic

The bounded, uniformly Hölder densities in (4) and the known weak convergence `V_j ⇒ V` imply locally uniform convergence `f_(V_j)→f_V` to a continuous density: Arzelà–Ascoli and diagonal subsequences give locally uniform subsequential limits, tested against compactly supported continuous functions; weak convergence identifies every such limit, and continuous versions equal almost everywhere are equal everywhere. In particular the same bound (4) applies to `f_V`. The last-star-size mixture, `P(J=j)=2/[j(j−1)]` for `j≥3`, gives a density

```
f_X(x)=Σ_(j≥3) [2/(j(j−1))] j^μ f_(V_j)(j^μ x).
```

The series converges uniformly by (4) and `μ<1`, so this is the continuous version. With `h=x^(1/μ)` and `y=jh`, dividing by `x^(1/μ−1)` turns the sum into a Riemann sum of mesh `h` with terms `2 [j/(j−1)] y^(μ−2) f_(V_j)(y^μ)`. On `y∈[a,b]` for `0<a<b<∞`, local uniform density convergence gives the limit integral. At the lower endpoint the summands before multiplication by `h` are bounded by `C y^(2μ−2)`, and at the upper by `C y^(μ−2)`; their discrete tails are uniformly bounded by constants times `a^(2μ−1)` and `b^(μ−1)` by integral comparison (using `2μ>1`). Substitution `z=y^μ` gives the limit `(2/μ)∫ z^(-1/μ) f_V(z) dz=κ/μ` by the existing prefactor formula. This proves the claimed density equivalent, with no differentiating of a CDF equivalent.

## 6. The suggested two-side and Gamma routes

For fixed positive `a,b` the beta image has a genuine inverse-square-root singularity at its maximum `M=(a^(1/(1−μ))+b^(1/(1−μ)))^(1−μ)`. More precisely the density on the left is asymptotic to `sqrt(2/|g''(u_*)|) β(u_*) (M−x)^−1/2` by the Taylor expansions of the two inverse branches. A bound involving only `|a−b|` misses this, even for `a≠b`. Averaging this part requires a shell estimate on `M`, with the curvature and beta prefactors accounted for, or extra independent directions. The marginal tails of the whole law cannot supply that shell estimate; the multicut method above bypasses it and replaces late deterministic-time misses by an exponentially small failure probability in **core count**, while the clock-induced cost has subexponential moments in that count.

For `Z=W^μ X` with independent `W∼Gamma(m,1)`, the log convolution multiplies the characteristic function by `Γ(m+iμt)/Γ(m)`. By Stirling along the vertical line this has modulus asymptotic to `sqrt(2π)|μt|^(m−1/2) exp(−πμ|t|/2)/Γ(m)`. In particular the log density of `Z` has a holomorphic extension to `|Im z|<πμ/2`, by absolutely convergent Fourier inversion on each smaller strip, regardless of any regularity of `X`. Even taking `X` constant gives such a log density. Nonvanishing identifies the law of `X` uniquely given the exact log law of `Z`; it supplies no decay estimate for the quotient after division by an exponentially small number. Thus Gamma smoothing alone fails to settle the density question.

## 8. All derivatives on compact subintervals of `(0,∞)`

Fix a nonnegative integer `ℓ`, put `s=ℓ+1`, and remake the above construction with `2r` small sides in `r` pairs, `r>2s`. To prepare `2r` leaves to promote, substitute at least `8r` leaf rings in the second unit and exactly `2r` base rings with marks ≤1/4 in the third; after the last ring the base still has a spare leaf. The same reasoning gives (1) (constants may depend on `ℓ`). Increase the fixed seed size and core count so that its inverse moments under the one-slot weight exist at least through order `4s`, by (3) with an increased exponent, and so `2d>4μs`. All the partition and independence arguments still apply. Fourier inversion gives `C^ℓ(R)` fixed-path, fixed-total kernels; the sup norm of the derivative of order `v≤ℓ` is `≤C(P^μ B)^−(v+1)`. The entire kernel, including its derivatives, vanishes above

```
M = a_R + Σ_(i=1)^r (a_(2i−1)+a_(2i)) T_i^μ,
```

where `a_R` is the remainder coefficient. Indeed the omitted factors in each term are between zero and one, so the law is supported at most at `M`; continuity gives vanishing derivatives on the open complementary ray. On `[ε,∞)` insert `1_{M≥ε}≤(M/ε)^s`. For `w=v+1≤s`, the resulting estimate is bounded by

```
C ε^−s (NP)^−μw B^−w (N^μ M)^s,
```

because `N^(μ(w−s))≤1`. Nothing is claimed here for `ε=0`.

We need uniform positive moments in the no-cross weighted law (before disjointification). We give a derivation using Astra's bounds, independent of the hypotheses of the older Fable continuous-time upper-tail proof. Write `M_t=e^−μt Φ(T_t)`, the eigenmartingale converging to the continuous-time amplitude `Z_T`. The free gradient bound, the rate-weighted squared jump bound, and core comparability (Astra `open/limit-law/README.md` and `left/README.md`) give `|ΔM_t|≤D`, `C(T_t)≤K(1+Φ(T_t))`, and predictable quadratic variation density ≤`K e^−2μt C(T_t)`. Stop at the first passage to `M_t≥R`, for `R≥max(1,2M_0)`. Before passage the last density is at most `K²(e^−2μt+R e^−μt)`, and the stopped quadratic variation is bounded by `K'(1+R)` even on an infinite horizon. The bounded-jump martingale Bernstein inequality for the stopped process (first at finite horizons and then take the union) bounds passage probability by `exp(−cR)` since passage requires an increase of at least `R/2`. Thus `P_T(Z_T>R)≤exp(−cR)` for `R≥B C(T)` with a uniform `B`, by `Φ(T)≤K C(T)` and `C(T)≥1`. Integrating gives `E Z_T^p≤C_p C(T)^p` for every fixed `p>0`.

By independence of the Gamma size clock and the gamma-ratio asymptotic (with the finitely many small sizes handled separately), `E[(N^μ X_T)^p]≤C_p C(T)^p` for each seed of size `N`. To bound the remainder amplitude under the reference law, use the exact physical no-cross law with `X_T≥P_R^μ a_R`. The shares are independent of paths, and `P_R~Beta(N−2rd,2rd)` has `E P_R^(μp)` bounded below uniformly over `N≥2rd+2` (for example from the Gamma representation and monotonicity in integer remainder size). Therefore

```
E_Q[(∏L_i)(N^μ a_R)^p | cut history] ≤ C_p C(T)^p.
```

The positive moments of each small amplitude under its fixed weight likewise are finite: glue two copies as in (3); `X_glued≥U^μ a_1` on their no-cross event, and the other side's total weight is strictly positive (the finite preparation plus lower-stock protection, or Neon-2's one-slot positivity). The beta share has a strictly positive moment independent of the paths. The beta positive-moment formula also gives `E[(NT_i)^(μp)|history]≤C` uniformly, independently of the amplitudes. By the elementary bound on the power of a finite sum, these estimates imply

```
E_Q[(∏ L_i)(N^μ M)^p | cut history] ≤ C_p (1+C(T)^p).
```

In the measure of the original history and reference paths weighted by `∏L_i`, now also including the shares, apply Hölder with four exponents all equal to four to `(NP)^−μw`, `B^−w`, `(N^μ M)^s`, and the disjointification mask. The first bound follows from the same beta inverse-moment formula, after dividing by `N^(4μw)`; the second follows from gluing; the third is polynomial in `k`, since during the fixed preparation interval core growth is dominated by the rate-one Yule process from `k` particles (promotion intensity is at most the current core count); the last is `O(exp(−bk/4))` by (1). Summing over the at most `k` choices gives convergence in `C^ℓ` uniformly on every compact subinterval of `(0,∞)`. More explicitly, dominated differentiation for the integrated conditional kernels follows from the integrable bound on each derivative locally, and the uniform derivative series then gives the derivative of the mixture. Each time we change the number or size of the branches the continuous density still gives the same law, so its continuous version is unchanged. Since `ℓ` was arbitrary, this proves `f_X∈C^∞((0,∞))`.

## 9. Sharp regularity and first derivative at zero

Use ten small sides/five pairs for the star-exit laws. Their product characteristic bound is now `C min(1,(λ|ξ|)^−5/2)`. Hence the uniform bound (4) upgrades to `sup_j ||f_(V_j)||_(C^{1,1/20}(R))<∞`: for the derivative and its seminorm integrate with weights `|ξ|` and `|ξ|^(1+1/20)`, respectively, giving scales `λ^−2` and `λ^−(2+1/20)` and apply the same computation as in §4. The parameters allow this: `16(2+1/20)<40` and `(9/8) μ(2+1/20)<4/3`. The weighted bound for `f_(V_j)(z)/z` is unchanged. The density and its derivative converge locally uniformly by Arzelà–Ascoli applied to both, the fundamental theorem of calculus, and uniqueness of the weak limit density.

Uniformly in `j`, `P(V_j>z)≤A exp(−c z)` for large `z`: by the two-core upper-tail estimate used in §8, the continuous-time limit from `S_j` has this bound. Its representation is `(W_{j+1}/j)^μ V_j` with independent Gamma factor, and `P(W_{j+1}/j≥1/2)` is bounded below by a positive constant (e.g. Paley–Zygmund using the Gamma second moment).

This yields a uniform exponential bound on `|f'_(V_j)(z)|` at infinity. Here is an elementary verification. If a nonnegative `C^1` density with derivative bound `L≥1` has value `v` at `z`, it is at least `v/2` on `[z,z+v/(2L)]`. The exponential probability tail thus bounds `v` by `A_1 exp(−c z/2)`. Next, if `q=|f'(z)|`, on `[z,z+u]` for `u=(q/(2H))^(1/α)` its derivative has the same sign and modulus at least `q/2`, where `H≥1` is a bound on its derivative's Hölder seminorm of order `α=1/20`. The absolute increment is at least `q u/2`; both endpoint values are bounded by `A_1 exp(−c z/2)`. Solving bounds `q` by `A_2 exp(−c_2 z)` uniformly. The case `q=0` is immediate. Local uniform convergence transfers these bounds to `f_V` and `f'_V`.

The derivative of the star mixture can now be taken on any compact subinterval of `(0,∞)`, since the terms with factor `j^(2μ) f'_(V_j)(j^μ x)` converge there absolutely and uniformly by exponential decay. Equivalently this is the derivative of the smooth version already proved. Dividing by `x^(1/μ−2)` again gives a Riemann sum with terms `2[j/(j−1)] y^(2μ−2) f'_(V_j)(y^μ)` at mesh `x^(1/μ)`. Near zero use the uniform derivative bound and `2μ−2>−1`; at infinity use uniform exponential decay, which dominates by an integrable decreasing function for large `y`. Local uniform derivative convergence handles the middle. After substituting `z=y^μ`, the limit integral is `(2/μ)∫ z^(1−1/μ) f'_V(z) dz`. Integration by parts gives `(2/μ)(1/μ−1)∫ z^(-1/μ) f_V(z) dz`, with vanishing boundary terms because `f_V(z)=O(z)` at zero, `1/μ<2`, and `f_V` decays exponentially at infinity. This is the asserted first-derivative asymptotic.

Finally the zero extension of `f_X` is globally Hölder of order `1/μ−1`. For `δ=|x−y|<1` use the uniform size and Lipschitz bounds of `f_(V_j)` in each term of the star mixture. Sum `C δ j^(2μ−2)` up to `δ^(−1/μ)`, and `C j^(μ−2)` thereafter, giving `C δ^(1/μ−1)` using `1/2<μ<1`. For larger distances boundedness suffices. Since `f_X(0)=0` and `κ>0`, §5 precludes any larger Hölder order at zero.

## 10. Gaps and limits

- No cross-family check of the new proof is recorded here yet. Parent notified. The analytic estimates do not depend on the simulator.
- Higher-order asymptotics of the derivatives at zero are not established. The uniform scaled star-exit proof above pays a cut-size moment for derivatives; moments beyond the first derivative do not follow from (2).
- The asymptotic supplies the known prefactor but no effective error rate at zero; the proof uses weak convergence of the star-departure seeds.
