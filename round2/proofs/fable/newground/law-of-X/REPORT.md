Here `Z_T` is the continuous-time limit from a finite starting tree `T`, `X_T` the discrete-size limit from that tree, and `W_m` its total population Yule limit (`m=|T|`). Every finite tree with at least two vertices is reachable by successive leaf attachments, so (L1), including existence and strict positivity of the limit, transfers to each such start by conditioning. The usual uniform ringing construction factors at each step into an exponential holding time of rate `m`, independent of the tree move. Thus `W_m ~ Gamma(m,1)` is independent of `X_T` and `Z_T = W_m^mu X_T` (scale parameter 1). Write `F=d_2 Psi`; by (L2), `E_T Z_T = F(T)`, and `exp(-mu t)F(T_t)` is the conditional-expectation martingale for `Z_T`. This also explains why `Z` and `W_2` are dependent while `X` and `W_2` are independent.

Literal caveat: (L3) as stated cannot include the starting edge, whose `C=0`. It would force an exact exponential expectation all the way down to time 0 for that edge, which is impossible. The (L2) bounds and conditional-expectation identity likewise cannot both hold at the edge. All estimates and identities using `Psi` below only need (L2)–(L4) for `C≥1` (size at least 3), with `c'>0`. The edge start is handled by its independent first holding time.

## PROVED: a bounded-influence estimate and variance

Write `g=mu-sigma>0`, `a=2-mu`, and `F_s(T)=e^{−mu s} E_T C_s`. By (L3), `|F_s−F|≤K C e^{−gs}`; by (L4), a leaf addition at degree `d` changes `F_s` by at most `e^{as}`, or by `e^{as}(2s+1)/(d+1)` for `d≥2`. Optimizing after bounding the difference `F−F_s` at the two trees gives a coarse modulus `|ΔF| ≤ A (1+C)^{a/(a+g)}`. For degree `d≥2` one can instead take the infimum over `s≥0` of `K(2C+1)e^{−gs}+(2s+1)e^{as}/(d+1)`. No uniformly bounded `ΔF` is claimed.

For a finite horizon `t`, `u↦e^{−mu t}E[C_t|F_u]` is the Markov Doob martingale on `[0,t]`. For any function with the (L4) influence at remaining time `s`, the sum of rates times squared changes is at most

`C e^{4s}[1+(2s+1)^2]`.

Indeed promotions have total rate at most `C`; leaf gains at core vertex `v` have rate `r_v = L_v + Σ_{w core~v} 1/D_w ≤ D_v`. Each such gain uses the degree-refined bound. For the starting edge, a single first birth is deterministic in shape; any positive-time continuous-time variance formula can either include its rate-2 first jump separately or start at size 3.

Let `Q_m(T)=Gamma(m)/Gamma(m+mu) F(T)`. In discrete time, writing `p_T(w)=m^{-1} Σ_{v~w}1/d_v` for the receiver probabilities and `T+w` for the next tree, the exact identity is

`Var(X) = Σ_{m≥3} E [(Gamma(m+1)/Gamma(m+1+mu))² Var_{w~p_{T_m}}(F(T_m+w))]`.

Here `E X=c_2=Q_3(T_3)` by (L2)–(L3). Sum convergence follows already from (L2) and martingale convergence. A strictly positive explicit lower bound in terms of (L2) constants is obtained by one size `m` with `c' (m−2)−1>c''`:

`Var(X) ≥ (2/(m−1)) · (2/(m−1)!) · [d_2 Γ(m)/Γ(m+mu)]² [c'(m−2)−1−c'']²`.

The two sampled states in the pairwise variance formula are the star and path. The exact respective probabilities follow since a star survives the next step with probability `(m−1)/m`, and a path stays a path with probability `1/m` (`m≥3`, each endpoint's neighbour has degree 2). In particular the factorial in the question belongs to paths. Once all moments are available (next section), the exact moment hierarchy can also be written `E X^k = lim_m E Q_m(T_m)^k`: the conditional-expectation martingale converges in `L^k`. There is no closed vertex-pair kernel here.

For a continuous-time analogue, define `G(T)=Σ_w m p_T(w)[F(T+w)−F(T)]²`. The bracket of `e^{−mu t}F(T_t)` is the integral of `e^{−2mu t}G(T_t)` (finitely many jumps on each compact interval). Square integrability follows from the Gamma factor and (L2). Thus the exact variance from each start is `Var_T(Z_T)=∫_0^∞ e^{−2mu t} E_T G(T_t) dt`. For each positive integer `k`, `H_k(T)=E_T Z_T^k` is the higher-order harmonic potential: first-jump conditioning gives `E[H_k(T_{m+1})|T_m]=(1+k mu/m)H_k(T_m)`, and the bracket formula expresses `H_2=F²+` the stated integral. Finiteness for all starting trees follows from the upper-tail argument below. These identities characterize the moments, without a finite-dimensional closure. The moment sequence determines the law by existence of exponential moments.

## PROVED: uniform relative concentration and the sharp upper-tail power

For each fixed `η>0` there exist positive `A_η,b_η` such that for every initial tree with `c=C(T)≥1`,

`P_T(|Z_T−F(T)|>ηc) ≤ A_η exp(−b_η c).` **(1)**

The same proof gives a uniform large-size threshold: there exist `B,b>0` such that `P_T(Z_T>B r)≤B exp(−b r)` for every `r≥c≥1`. Here are details and choices sufficient to make the constants explicit in (L). Put `M_j=e^{−mu j}F(T_j)`, where `j` denotes continuous integer time. Set `s_j=s_0+δ j`, `δ=mu/(8a)`, `β=min(mu/4,gδ/2)`, `h_j=(1−e^{−β})e^{−β j}`. Work from initial scale `r≥c`. At the start of block `j`, stop if `M_j>(d_2 c''+η)r` (this cannot happen if every prior increment has absolute value at most `ηrh_i`). By `F≥d_2(c'C−1)`, when unstopped we have `C_j≤D r e^{mu j}`, `D=(d_2 c''+η+d_2)/(d_2 c')` (enlarge `D` to at least 1).

In block `[j,j+1]` use the true martingale `U_v=e^{−mu(j+1+s_j)} E[C(T_{j+1+s_j})|F_{j+v}]`, `0≤v≤1`. Stop this martingale at the first `C>J_j=H r e^{mu j}`, with `H≥2D`; at a threshold crossing its pre-jump core is still at most `J_j`. A Yule process from `C_j` dominates growth of the core over the block (promotion rate at most `C`). If `0<θ<−log(1−e^−1)` and `G=E exp(θ Geom(e^−1))`, choose `H≥(D log G+1)/θ`; overshoot probability is at most `e^{−r e^{mu j}}`. On no overshoot, `|M_{j+1}−M_j−(U_1−U_0)| ≤ 2K H r e^{−g s_j}`. Choose `s_0≥0` so `2KH e^{−gs_0}≤η(1−e^{−β})/2`, ensuring this error is at most `ηrh_j/2`.

By the squared-rate estimate, the stopped martingale has jump bound `L_j=e^{−mu j+a(s_j+1)}` and total predictable quadratic variation at most `V_j=H r e^{−mu j+2a(s_j+1)}[1+(2s_j+3)^2]`. The two-sided bounded-jump martingale Bernstein inequality gives probability at most `2 exp(−q_j²/(2(V_j+L_j q_j/3)))` for error `q_j=ηrh_j/2`. The reciprocal normalized exponent is a sum of constants times polynomials in `j` times decaying exponentials, with decay rates at least `min(mu−2aδ−2β,mu−aδ−β)>0`. Hence there is an explicit `b_0>0` (take the infimum of `q_j² e^{-κj}/[2r(V_j+L_j q_j/3)]` with `κ` half the displayed minimum) such that the failure probability is at most `2e^{−b_0 r e^{κ j}}`; the sum over `j`, including Yule overshoots, is bounded by `A e^{−br}` using `e^{κ j}≥1+κ j`. On no failure all increments sum in absolute value to at most `ηr`, and `M_j→Z_T` by (L1)–(L3) and conditional expectation. This proves (1) and the large-threshold variant.

The edge start has an additional initial holding time Exp(2) before deterministic `T_3`; it only decreases the unscaled positive amplitude. Thus `Z` has an exponential upper bound, so for integers `k≥1`, `E X^k = E Z^k/Γ(2+mu k) ≤ A B^k k!/Γ(2+mu k)`. Stirling and Markov with `k` a small constant times `x^{1/(1−mu)}` give `P(X>x)≤exp(−c_1 x^{1/(1−mu)})` for large `x`. In particular all real exponential moments exist, as do some moments of `exp(c X^{1/(1−mu)})`. An explicit variance upper bound: whenever `P(Z>z)≤A e^{−kz}` for `z≥z_0` using the constants above, `Var(X) ≤ [z_0²+2A e^{−kz_0}(z_0/k+1/k²)]/Γ(2+2mu)−c_2²` by tail integration and the independent Gamma multiplier.

For the matching lower bound, build a full binary core with every current frontier vertex carrying two leaves. Start at `T_3`. If there are `k` frontier vertices and `2k−1` cores, there are `4k−1` vertices altogether. Promote all `2k` frontier leaves in any order: when `j` remain, promotion of one of them has probability at least `j/(18k)` (parent degree ≤3, size ≤6k). The newborn cores now each carry one leaf. Give each of the `2k` newborn cores one additional leaf, again any order: while `j` remain the probability to give a first extra leaf to one of them is at least `j/(8k)`, by the clocks of their leaves. The product of these two stage probabilities is at least `exp(−A k)` by `(2k)!≥(2k/e)^{2k}`. Doubling and summing the costs gives reachability at size `m=4·2^h−1` with probability ≥`exp(−A'm)`, at core density tending to 1/2. By (1), conditional `Z_T≥(d_2 c'/2)C(T)` with probability tending to one. Since `W_m≤2m` with probability tending to one by its Gamma law, `X_T≥b m^{1−mu}` with probability ≥1/2 for large `m`. Using the bounded ratio of successive sizes proves the lower bound. Together, `−log P(X>x)` lies between positive multiples of `x^{1/(1−mu)}`. No limiting Weibull rate constant asserted.

## PROVED: full support

At fixed size `m` the space of tree shapes reachable from the edge is connected by leaf relocations. Root the tree; while it is not a star centered at the root, detach a vertex of greatest distance from the root (a leaf) and reattach it to the root. The sum of distances strictly decreases at each such step. Every intermediate shape is reachable at size `m`. Each relocation compares two leaf additions to the same size-`m−1` tree. Follow a relocation path from star to path and locate the first crossing of `Q_m=x`. By `Q_m≥const·C/m^mu−O(m^−mu)` and the fact a relocation changes `C` by at most 2, both crossing states have `C=O_x(m^mu)`. The optimized coarse bound above now implies `|ΔQ_m|=O_x(m^{−mu g/(a+g)})`, so there are states with conditional mean tending to any `x>0`; moreover `C` is bounded above and below by positive multiples of `m^mu` by (L2). By (1) with arbitrarily small fixed relative error, and `W_m/m→1` in probability, the discrete limit conditional on these positive-probability states lies in any prescribed neighbourhood of `x` with probability tending to 1. Its support in `[0,∞)` is the whole of `[0,∞)`.

## PROVED: small-tail exponent and asymptotic constant

The probability to still be a star at size `m` is exactly `2/(m−1)`. Markov conditional on that event with `Q_m≤const·m^−mu` gives `P(X<ε) ≥ c ε^{1/mu}` for sufficiently small `ε`. For the matching bound and constant, first prove a uniform inverse-moment lemma.

**Lemma.** For `p<4/(3mu)` and `p>0`, `sup_{C(T)≥2} E_T[Z_T^{−p}]<∞`.

Time to next promotion from any state of core size ≥2 has survival ≤ `(36/25)e^{−4t/3}`. Choose two tips of its core tree. Until the next promotion each tip has at least one leaf and only one core neighbour. At each tip use its current tagged leaf's clock for a rate-one first addition; rank its leaves before its core neighbour at its own rate-one clock. This gives hazard at least `1/2` before that first addition, `2/3` thereafter, independently in the two auxiliary races (auxiliary clocks are four distinct sources, and rates are enforced with mark thresholds). Survival of one race is `E exp(−t/2−(t−E)_+/6) ≤ (6/5)e^{−2t/3}` for `E~Exp(1)` (direct integration). For each fixed `t>0`, the same no-promotion probability tends uniformly to zero as core size `c` grows: use the ≥`c/6` disjoint sites from `proofs/almost-sure-exponent.md`, §1. For an A-site rank leaves first and use a base ring with mark ≤1/3. For a triple, in the first half of `[0,t]` use a middle ring with mark ≤1/2 to add a leaf to the base; in the second half use a base mark ≤1/3. These flags use independent clocks across sites; under *no promotion* the core neighbourhoods are unchanged, so any such flag contradicts that event. Each flag has probability bounded below by a positive constant depending only on `t`. Consequently the uniform exponential moment of the waiting time tends to 1 as `c→∞`, at each rate `r<4/3`, by dominated convergence using the preceding two-tip survival bound.

Let `H_k` be the first time core size is ≥`k` (zero when already above); by the strong Markov property and the previous uniform mgf bounds, `sup_{C(T)≥2} E_T e^{r H_k} ≤ exp(o(k))` for `r<4/3`. By (1) and `F≥d_2(c'C−1)`, for all large integer `k`, `P_T(Z_T< a k e^{−mu H_k})≤A e^{−bk}` for some positive `a,b,A` (also covers starting core above `k`). Define `K` to be the first successful test among these levels; the failure of level `k−1` bounds `P(K=k)` and proves `K<∞` a.s. On success `Z_T^{-p} ≤ (a K)^{-p} e^{p mu H_K}`. Summing over `K=k` and applying Hölder with exponent `q>1` close enough to 1 that `pq mu<4/3` proves the lemma. All constants are independent of starting size.

Let `J` be the discrete last star size, so `P(J=m)=2/[m(m−1)]`. Conditional on `J=m`, the tree at size `m+1` is a deterministic double star `S_m`, with a hub of degree `m−1`, one newly promoted degree-two tip, and otherwise leaves. Use `V_m=m^mu X_{S_m}`. The lemma, independence of `W_{m+1}` and `X_{S_m}`, and Jensen yield `sup_m E V_m^{−p}<∞` for `1/mu<p<4/(3mu)`; indeed `E V_m^{−p}=E Z_{S_m}^{−p}/E[(W_{m+1}/m)^{−p mu}]`, with denominator bounded below (only sufficiently large `m` need this division; here `p mu<4/3 <m+1` holds anyway).

**Infinite-hub limit.** `V_m ⇒ V` for a finite positive variable `V`. To see this, at fixed time horizon `t` compare the core evolution of `S_m` to the same evolution with a hub having indefinitely many leaves. A hub ring always promotes a leaf (new attached tip), and a core neighbour can still ring and choose the hub, but that choice has no effect on the recorded core/leaf states away from the hub. All other away-from-hub rates and states are identical under common clocks until a real hub ring chooses a core neighbour. The latter discrepancy probability is ≤`(m−1)^{-1} ∫_0^t E C_s ds ≤2(e^t−1)/(m−1)`, since the degree never decreases and promotions occur at total rate at most `C`; use the same rate-one core clock and a fresh attached tip from a matched leaf promotion. Hence `e^{−mu t}C_t(S_m)` converges in law at each fixed `t` to the ideal hub's normalized core count. For uniform approximation to `Z_{S_m}`, note that `e^{−mu t}F(T_t)−e^{−mu t}C_t` itself need not tend to zero from (L3). Instead use the **future count with lookahead**: for any fixed `s`, `F_s(T_t)=e^{−mu s}E[C_{t+s}|T_t]` satisfies `|F_s−F|≤K C_t e^{−gs}`. For each fixed `t,s`, the variable `e^{−mu t} F_s(T_t(S_m))` converges in law to its ideal-hub analogue: from the coupled matching states at `t`, the conditional comparison over the next `s` has discrepancy probability ≤`C_t(e^s−1)/(m−1)`; Cauchy–Schwarz and a dominating Yule second moment bound its conditional expected core-count difference by a constant depending on `s` times `C_t (C_t/(m−1))^{1/2}`. The finite-horizon core count is tight uniformly from the two initial cores. Next, `E[e^{−mu t} C_t]≤d_2 c''·2+2K` by (L3), uniformly in `m,t`. And `C_t→∞` uniformly in probability from `S_m` by the two-tip wait bound. Applying (1) at time `t` with arbitrarily small relative error shows `e^{−mu t} F(T_t)` approximates `Z_{S_m}` in probability uniformly in `m` as `t→∞`. Thus choosing `t,s` large and fixed makes `e^{−mu t}F_s(T_t)` a uniform approximation; the limit laws of `Z_{S_m}` exist by the Cauchy criterion in the Prokhorov metric on probability laws on ℝ (tightness also follows from their uniformly bounded means). The uniform inverse moments give positivity of the limit. Finally `W_{m+1}/m→1` in probability and `Z_{S_m}=(W_{m+1}/m)^mu V_m`, which transfers the same limit to `V_m`.

Writing `y=m ε^{1/mu}` in the exact star-exit mixture now gives

`P(X<ε) ~ 2 E[V^{−1/mu}] ε^{1/mu}`.

For details, on `y∈[a,b]` replace the sum normalized by `ε^{1/mu}` by an integral of step functions, which converge at continuity points of the limiting CDF `P(V<y^mu)` (countably many exceptions). The endpoint `y>b` contributes `O(1/b)`, and `y<a` contributes `O(a^{mu p−1})` from inverse moments with `mu p>1`. The resulting integral is `2∫_0^∞ y^{−2} P(V<y^mu)dy=2 E[V^{−1/mu}]` by Tonelli. This proves the small-ball power and its leading constant, without asserting a pointwise density asymptotic as `x↓0`.

## PROVED: absolute continuity and a cut identity with error

Call a vertex a hub if it maintains exponentially growing leaf count eventually (some positive random coefficient and a fixed exponent `1/2` suffice). Starting from even one attached leaf, a vertex's leaf count dominates a linear birth–death process with per capita birth 1 and death 1/2 started at 1: leaves ring to add siblings at rate `L`, other arrivals help, and promotions out of the leaf pool occur at rate `L/D≤L/2` when it is core. For completeness the comparison can be coupled by matching births and using additional deaths in the lower chain. The lower process has probability ≥`p_0>0` of an eventual positive `e^{−t/2}` limit: its normalized population is a nonnegative L²-bounded martingale, with mean 1 and second moment ≤4 by the two linear rates (or use Paley–Zygmund). Conditional on the history at any deterministic time after the initial birth, some core has a leaf. Therefore the conditional probability of the event that a hub exists is at least `p_0`; martingale convergence of this conditional probability proves that a hub exists a.s.

A hub has infinitely many promotions. Its core degree increases at most at its own ring count, which is eventually `O(t)` almost surely for every vertex (countably many); its ratio of leaves to total degree thus tends to 1, and rate-one marked rings with mark at most 1/2 eventually promote. A hub also has a hub child almost surely. Formalize this for each fixed labelled vertex `u`: from the histories at its successive promotion times (on the event of infinitely many promotions), the conditional probability that *some* child becomes a hub is at least `p_0` by the preceding birth–death comparison started from the newborn tip. Use convergence of the optional samples of the bounded conditional-probability martingale as the promotion times tend to infinity (on finite times use the same argument with localization). Hence on infinitely many promotions the limiting indicator of a hub child is 1. Taking a countable union, there is an edge with both endpoints hubs a.s.

At large integer time `t`, choose an edge maximizing the minimum number of leaves of its endpoints (deterministic tie rule). The minimum goes to infinity a.s. Cut it and run each component independently as a free friend tree with its own rate-one vertex drivers. Conditional on the history, the actual future agrees in the interiors with these processes until an endpoint ring chooses the removed cross edge; couple choices by independent rejection marks at endpoints as in `proofs/existence.md`, §4 (use both endpoints symmetrically). Each failure intensity before the first mismatch is at most `1/(D_v^{free}+1)`. For a free endpoint starting with `L≥2` leaves, its leaf count dominates a linear birth–death process `B` started at `L` with rates 1 and 1/2 per capita. Split `B` into `L` independent chains. A single chain satisfies `B_i(s)≥d e^{s/2}` for *all* `s≥0` with positive probability `q>0` for some `d>0`: on the positive-limit event a chain never hits 0, and its infimum of normalized sizes over all times is strictly positive. The number `K_L` of such chains is binomial. On `K_L≥qL/2`, `D^{free}(s)≥K_L d e^{s/2}`; the complementary event has probability `O(e^{−cL})`. For a rigorous mismatch bound stop at the first violation of `D^{free}(s)≥qLd e^{s/2}/2` and integrate the deterministic hazard bound before that stop (at most `4/(qLd)`). Thus the probability of any mismatch due to this endpoint is `O(1/L)`. Before mismatch the actual endpoint degree is one greater than its free degree. The two endpoint core-status discrepancies contribute at most 2 to the core count, and do not affect the limit.

For cut components of sizes `m_1,m_2` with ≥2 vertices, conditional on the cut history the proxy for discrete `X` is

`X^{cut}=U^mu X_{T_1}+(1−U)^mu X_{T_2}`,

where `U=W_{m_1}/(W_{m_1}+W_{m_2})~Beta(m_1,m_2)` is independent of both discrete limits. The time origin cancels; on no mismatch the actual `X` equals this proxy. Given the strictly positive pair of discrete amplitudes the function of `U` is smooth and strictly concave with nonzero derivative except at a single point, so its pushforward of the beta density is absolutely continuous. For any Lebesgue-null set the actual probability is at most the mean coupling failure (plus the probability of no edge with both endpoints having ≥2 leaves). This tends to zero by the adjacent hub result and dominated convergence. Therefore `X` has a density and no atoms.

Why the first-edge split is different: before mismatch, if endpoint `a` rings, its free component would choose uniformly among `D_a^{free}` internal neighbours, but with the edge present it chooses its partner `b` with probability `1/(D_a^{free}+1)`, transferring the birth to the other component; endpoint `b` reciprocates at a different, state-dependent rate. At the initial edge both halves are singletons and these transfers create their very first internal leaf. In the actual process the instantaneous first-component population birth rate is `N_1−1/D_a+1/D_b`, where the degrees include the original edge; so even when both sides have edges the transfer decisions change both sides' future rates, and component populations are not independent Yule processes. The exact large-degree cut-coupling inequality `d_TV(law(X|history),law(X^{cut}|history)) ≤ A(1/L_1+1/L_2)` survives; it is not an unconditional independent-copy fixed-point equation for `X`.

## Open / dead ends

There is no explicit density formula here, nor proof of pointwise behaviour of a selected density version at 0. The proved CDF asymptotic does imply vanishing average density there. No upper-tail rate constant, closed all-moment recursion on vertex pairs, or exact first-edge smoothing transform was derived. All uses of (L) above are conditional; no verification of that hypothesis is implied.
