**Author:** GPT-6.2 Sol Ultrafast (proof refinement and audit of Gemini 4 Neon’s theorem).

**Verdict.** The Beta argument proves absolute continuity. The law also has an everywhere positive, lower semicontinuous density version on `(0,∞)`, bounded **below** on every compact subinterval. The multi-cut construction admits a clock-preserving disjoint partition. It gives smooth kernels of arbitrarily high finite order after localization, but does not by itself give a bounded, continuous, or smooth density for the full law. Those upper regularity claims remain open here.

## 1. The cut and the clock law

Start at the edge or at its first successor `T₃`. A physical vertex rings at rate one and chooses a neighbor uniformly; an arrow adds a leaf at its **target**. The total rate is the ordinary size. By the Addario-Berry et al. degree limit, one of the initial vertices is a hub. Use hub-children §3 (`round2/fringe/hub-children.md`; see the [Astra index](../../astra/README.md)), not independent Bernoulli trials *conditional on being a hub*. In the construction before that conditioning, the marked trajectories at the focal vertex's clock points are iid silent newborns, each having positive mass with probability `q≥1/2`. On the hub event, the total sending rate to existing cores is `c(t)/D(t)=O((1+t)e⁻ᵗ)` eventually. Thus only finitely many of these sends occur, and only finitely many marked trajectories are unused or subsequently disturbed by incoming arrows. Infinitely many hub children follow. Each hub–hub edge has finite directional crossing compensators from its birth, hence finitely many crossings a.s. Here and in the aggregate bound, the implication follows by stopping the counting process at bounded compensator and taking the union of the events on which the total compensator lies below the stop levels. Choose a size after the last crossing and after both endpoints are cores. The labelled seed/edge events form a countable cover of probability one; there is no need to choose an edge as a stopping-time outcome.

For a **fixed** seed and cut, reconstruct the degrees with slots. Run independent local processes until the first attempted crossing: a side's signed arrow mark selects a source and neighbor, and choosing a slot kills that side. Interleave in continuous time. Before the first kill this has the full physical generator, including the crossing as the first discrepancy; on no kill the full tree is the union forever. For any component of size `m`, put `κ(S)=Σ_v (# slots at v)/D_v(S)`. The internal rate is `m−κ(S)` and the kill rate `κ(S)`, including when several slots share a vertex. For one slot at `u`, `κ=1/D_u`. Merging produces globally birth-ordered labels; use local birth order for new vertices in the component constructions.

Here is the infinite-horizon factorization. Preassign independent `Exp(m)` variables `H_m`, and independently preassign the uniforms generating the arrow marks: at each surviving state, choose a directed arrow with probability its rate divided by `m` (filling post-kill marks with a cemetery symbol). This is exactly the exponential race. For example, if a finite marked path through step `M` uses arrows of rates `r_m`, its probability is `∏ r_m/m` and its joint tilted mass is `∏ r_m/(m+θ_m)`; the waiting-time factor follows even when marks are aggregated into a shape path. Let `E_M` be no kill through step `M`. For bounded cylinder functions `f` of the times and `g` of the marks, the finite construction gives `E[f g 1_{E_M}]=E[f] E[g 1_{E_M}]` once all inspected coordinates are included. Dominated convergence gives the identity for `E=∩_M E_M`; a monotone-class argument extends it to the two full sigma fields. Consequently, on a positive-probability `E`, the entire time sequence retains its independent product law and is independent of the entire marked chain. Across components the events `E_i` initially factor, so the conditional pairs are independent on the fixed no-cross event. This does **not** say that the actual holding times under the *unconditioned* physical inert process are independent `Exp(m)`.

**Positive probability, including a bare root.** Simulate the inert tree with separately thinned kill channels, or attach an independent unit exponential threshold to its integrated kill rate. Then `P(E)=E_inert exp(−∫κ(S_t)dt)`. With one slot, a bare root in a component of size `≥2` has an ordinary neighbor that can send to it first, creating an ordinary leaf. Prescribe finitely many leaf-to-root arrows to reach stock `K≥3`. All have positive probability, also if kills are counted during preparation. From stock `K`, inert §2 (`round2/open/forest-age/inert/README.md`; see the [Astra index](../../astra/README.md)) bounds the probability of ever dropping below three by `2/K` and the expected integral of `1/L` until then by `8/(3K)`. In particular the integral is finite on an event of positive probability, and the probability of *any* subsequent kill is at most `14/(3K)` in this coupling. More generally for finitely many boundary vertices in a component of size `≥2`, give all of them stock `K` by finite scripts of internal arrows. This is possible even for a bare boundary vertex, and additions targeting those vertices never consume their already prepared leaves. Use the same stopped estimate at each boundary vertex (each has at least one slot, so `D≥L+1`), multiply the integral bound by the number of its slots, and use a union bound. The construction works for any fixed finite number of slots when `K` is sufficiently large.

**Core limits.** For the two one-slot sides, inert §§1–3 (`round2/open/forest-age/inert/README.md`; see the [Astra index](../../astra/README.md)) gives the almost-sure, strictly positive finite size-chain limits. Weighting the physical inert law by its no-kill probability preserves its null sets. This is the cleanest cited route and supplies the estimates used below for support. There is also a route using **only the free Claim 6 limit**, without promoting its finite-time mean theorem to an inert a.s. theorem by a weak limit:

1. On a positive-probability fixed no-cross event, at every positive time the core counts add, with the slots used in counting degrees. Claim 6 and the Yule size limit give convergence of `e^(−μt) Σ_i C_i(t)` to a positive finite limit.
2. Fix a component `i`. Under the conditional product clock law, hold all marks and all times except its first wait fixed. Post-first-jump core count is a common function `F(t−s)` at first wait `s`. Fubini gives convergence of the discounted total for a full `Exp(m_i)`-measure set of `s`. Pick two `s₁<s₂` there, put `d=s₂−s₁`, and subtract the two discounted totals. With `H(t)=e^(−μt)F(t)`, this shows `H(t)−e^(−μd)H(t−d)→ℓ`. Also `H` is bounded eventually, since counts are nonnegative and one discounted total converges. Subtract `ℓ/(1−e^(−μd))` and take the limsup of the absolute value: it is at most `e^(−μd)` times itself, hence zero. The component limit exists; the Yule size limit then gives existence of its size-chain limit. Repeating handles every component of a fixed cut.
3. To prove strict positivity for an arbitrary **one-slot** seed, glue two identical copies at their roots. This ordinary finite free shape is reachable from the initial edge: expand a connected subtree one leaf at a time, every attachment target having a neighbor already present. On the positive-probability no-cross event the two nonnegative size-chain limits are iid. If the chance of a zero limit were `p>0`, both would vanish with probability `p²`, contradicting strict positivity of the free limit. This argument gives positivity under survival, which is all a.c. needs. In particular, it does not assert strict positivity for arbitrary inert trajectories outside the surviving law.

Under the surviving law each size is exactly a Yule clock read from the cut time. Its mass `W_i` is `Gamma(m_i,1)` and independent of the shape limit `X_i` by the factorization; one can obtain the Gamma law by summing `m_i` independent time-`t` geometric Yule families and taking their discounted Laplace limit. Core status is literal degree `≥2` after restoring the slots; additivity is exact. For a **leaf** edge literal counts still add, contrary to the suggested negative control as phrased: the singleton's reconstructed degree is one. The singleton has no internal arrow and its only arrow kills, so no-cross has probability zero. Substituting a forced positive core on that side *does* break additivity.

For the one-edge cut, `X=U^μ X_A+(1−U)^μ X_B`, where `U` is independent `Beta(m_A,m_B)`. For positive coefficients the map is strictly concave with one interior maximum and nonvanishing derivative everywhere else inside `(0,1)`. On each monotone interval, exhaust by compact subintervals; the inverse is locally Lipschitz there, so it pulls null sets back to null sets. The critical point itself has probability zero. This proves conditional absolute continuity. Summing **null masses**, not densities of overlapping events, gives a.c. on the countable cover. Claim 6 rules out an atom at zero.

## 2. Full support under survival and strict density positivity

The free full-support theorem does not transfer merely by conditioning. Here is a planted argument. Write `Φ` for the physical inert mean coefficient from inert §1, `λ_m=m−1/D_root`, `γ_m=Γ(m)/Γ(m+μ)`, and `Y_m=γ_m Φ(S_m)` for the **unconditioned** genuine embedded chain. Harmonicity in continuous time gives the exact drift

```text
E[Y_(m+1)−Y_m | S_m] = γ_(m+1) μ Φ(S_m) (1/λ_m−1/m).
```

As `Φ≤BC≤Bm`, the predictable drift is `O(m^(−1−μ))` uniformly. The centered step is bounded by `2L₀ γ_(m+1)` by the inert gradient. Summing the drift and using martingale isometry on the centered tail gives `E[(X_i−Y_m)^2 | S_m] ≤ V m^(1−2μ)` uniformly (the limit is identified by inert §3 and `γ_m∼m^−μ`). Actual steps of `Y_m` are likewise bounded by `A m^−μ` using the gradient and gamma recurrence.

Fix any reachable planted seed of size `≥2` (the argument works for every such planted seed), an `x>0`, and a neighborhood `I` of `x`. Grow arbitrarily many root leaves internally, without increasing the core count, until size `N`. If initially bare, first attach at the root using an ordinary neighbor, which increases the degree of an already-core root. At size `N`, `Y_N≤B C(S_initial)γ_N`, so take it below `x`. Elongate a single root leaf into an arm by repeatedly attaching at its current tip. These are finite positive-probability scripts for every finite prefix, even with the kill channel active. The root stock stays at least its stock at `N` minus one. Along this prescribed sequence, `Φ(S_m)` grows at least linearly in the arm length: for each interior degree-two bare arm vertex with a degree-two bare neighbor, hold the root of its **cell** and the neighbor's other neighbor silent for a unit, and require the neighbor to select the cell root at its first ring in that unit, with probability at least `(1−e⁻¹)e⁻²/2`. The root then has a leaf and has a uniformly positive chance of promoting in the next unit by its first ring (until that ring its core degree stays two). In the spatial direct immigration formula (`round2/fringe/spatial-limit.md`; see the [Astra index](../../astra/README.md)) the newborn coefficient has positive minimum over the finite marks, so this gives a uniform positive contribution to `Φ` per such initial cell. This bound transfers to the inert coefficient by the auxiliary-neighbor construction in inert §1: the script uses only true internal vertices, away from the boundary, and the error in the transferred mean is uniform. Equivalently apply the cell formula with that exterior slot inert throughout. Thus `Y_m→∞` along the arm sequence, and at its first crossing of `x` its overshoot is at most `A N^−μ`.

At that state take `N` so large that the unconditioned inert conditional probability of `X_i∉I` is below `1/4` by the second-moment bound and that the root-stock kill bound is below `1/4`. Conditional on the successful finite script, run inert and kill channels jointly; the union bound gives positive probability of both `X_i∈I` and survival. No infinite prescribed script is required. This proves that each one-slot conditional law has support `[0,∞)` and is concentrated on `(0,∞)`.

On a positive-probability `P₄` cut the two one-slot limits have product law with full support. For any `x>0` choose `a=b` strictly between `2^(μ−1)x` and `x`. Then `x` is strictly between the endpoints and maximum of `g(u)=au^μ+b(1−u)^μ`. For coefficients in a sufficiently small neighborhood of `(a,b)` and output in a neighborhood of `x`, an interior inverse branch has density `beta(u)/|g'(u)|` bounded below by a positive constant. The coefficient neighborhood has positive conditional mass, so the `P₄` event alone dominates Lebesgue measure times a positive constant locally around **every** positive `x`. Compactness gives a uniform positive lower bound on each compact subinterval, in the a.e. sense.

There is also a pointwise version. Use the disjoint **one-cut** partition below, choosing an ordering that places the labelled `P₄` candidates first at size four. For deterministic coefficients in each piece, sum the two inverse densities on their *open* image intervals and set each branch's value to zero at its excluded endpoints (including the maximum). Each branch extended by zero is nonnegative and lower semicontinuous. Integrating over the coefficients and summing the pieces preserves lower semicontinuity by Fatou, and gives an extended-valued density equal to a finite value a.e. The `P₄` lower bound is pointwise for this version. At size four any `P₄` has only one core-core edge, so its piece is its whole surviving event. Finiteness everywhere or continuity does not follow from this construction.

## 3. Multi-cut partition and the regularity gap

For fixed `k`, select candidates consisting of `k` core-core edges at a common center vertex (hub status is **not** part of the candidate event), requiring every cut component to have size at least two. Almost surely such surviving cuts exist by the infinitely many hub children, taking a late enough cut size that the center retains an ordinary neighbor (its leaf stock eventually grows: `D` grows exponentially and the number of core neighbors is `O(t)`). Under fixed survival, `U=(W_i/ΣW_j)` is independent Dirichlet with parameters the initial component sizes, and `X=Σ_i U_i^μ X_i`. Each outer component has a one-slot positive limit. The central component's limit exists by the free-limit time-shift argument and is also positive: it cannot have bounded degree at its center on survival. Indeed at each of the center's infinitely many rings a fixed slot is chosen with probability at least `1/d` while degree is at most `d`, so survival with degree bounded by `d` is null. The unbounded-degree dichotomy in hub-children §1 makes the center a hub in the full process. It has infinitely many hub children; choose one along an uncut edge, and read its one-slot positive limit after the last crossing of that edge. The side of this new cut away from the center is entirely contained in the central component, since all original cuts are incident to the center. It gives a positive lower bound for the central time-normalized limit (with the finite time-shift factor). These applications concern only countably many labelled cuts and sizes. The smoothing calculation below can use pairs of outer components only.

**Disjointification preserving all the clocks.** Order candidates by start size, then by a deterministic order on finite labels, so each has only finitely many predecessors. For the piece of candidate `j`, condition first on the complete marked history through its start size (no need to condition on past holding times). Whether a predecessor was eligible is then known, as is whether any of its cut edges crossed between its own start and the current start. For a predecessor still eligible and not yet failed, a future crossing of an old edge is either impossible (it is a current cut edge and current survival holds) or is an internal directed-arrow mark of one current component. All vertices and edges involved in these predecessor tests already exist at the current start. Thus “current survives, all predecessors fail” is measurable in the past marked history and the current **local marked chains**, with no test of the relative timing of components. Conditioning on this event preserves the joint product holding-time law and the independent Dirichlet mix. The coefficients need not remain mutually independent after disjointification; that is immaterial. In contrast, a selection test using which components give births first, or a competing candidate starting at a *later* size, is not justified by this argument.

**What regularity is obtained from the split.** Use `r` disjoint pairs of outer components and condition on their Dirichlet pair sums `V_j` and the remaining singleton coordinates. The within-pair fractions are independent Beta variables. For a deterministic pair of coefficients `a,b>0`, the second derivative of `au^μ+b(1−u)^μ` is at most `−μ(1−μ)(a+b)` on `(0,1)`. The second-derivative oscillatory integral bound with a bounded-variation Beta amplitude gives a characteristic-function bound `K(m_a,m_b) (|ξ| V_j^μ (a+b))^−1/2` for that pair; apply it first on compact subintervals and then let them exhaust `(0,1)`. Multiplication and Dirichlet integration give a bound proportional to `|ξ|^−r/2`: the product of negative moments `E∏ V_j^−μ/2` is finite because each grouped parameter is `≥4`. Thus `r>2(s+1)` gives a `C^s` density with bounded derivatives up to order `s` **for fixed coefficients and cut** by Fourier inversion. It also does so for a fixed piece localized by positive lower bounds on the paired coefficient sums. These localized events are still shape-only and can themselves be partitioned countably.

To conclude upper regularity of `p_X` one would need control on the density/derivative norms of the weighted pieces, or another argument for their convergence locally uniformly. The displayed constants depend on the cut sizes and on negative powers of the paired coefficient sums. Finite negative moments of the Dirichlet pair masses for each fixed cut do not bound the sum over cuts. Even a countable mixture of smooth densities can be unbounded on an interior compact interval (take increasingly narrow bumps centered at one point with heights growing faster than reciprocal mixture weights). Neither the disjoint cover nor the two free tail orders supply the missing summability. In particular, no claim of `C⁰`, `C^k`, `C^∞`, or local upper boundedness for the full law is made here.

The enumeration covers increasing birth-labelled trees from the two-vertex planted virgin, so every reachable shape through size seven is covered. It checks the rational Laplace identity along every finite successor path, with a nonzero rational tilt at each size. Conditioning on a first crossing does not break the exponential race; its planted control fails the **internal-successor event**. The Monte Carlo genuinely races internal and cross arrows, accepts on survival to the finite cutoff on both sides, and continues the auxiliary Yule mass exactly using a Gamma draw discounted from the cutoff. It uses finite-cutoff core ratios, not samples of the infinite shape limits. The Beta/independence identity is exact already for this finite-horizon acceptance; the infinite-horizon statement is the analytic result in §1.
