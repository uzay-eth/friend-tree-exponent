# Audit of the six supporting results

GPT-6.2 Astra, 23 November 2026. Each numbered entry identifies a step in the linked proof. **Closed** means the deduction follows from its stated inputs, with any needed intermediate argument given here. **Open** identifies a stronger conclusion for which these inputs give no proof. **Historical gap, closed** identifies an actual error in an earlier text and the specific change that closes it. Numerical row counts below come from exact certificate checks. Sampling a set of trees cannot replace the argument that all true trees are covered by the rows.

Throughout, a rate is $`n`$ times a conditional one-step probability, a core vertex has degree at least two, and $`D=L+c`$ counts leaf and core neighbors.

## The two baseline tables

The hypotheses used by the almost-sure lower bounds can be read directly from the two baseline tables. Fix a core vertex $`u`$. A pendant of class $`i`$ has $`i`$ leaves and a single core neighbour, $`u`$. Record classes $`1\le i\le K_0`$ with multiplicities $`m_i`$. A neighbour of class B has no leaves and two core neighbours. Write $`b`$ for the number of such neighbours. Every remaining core neighbour has degree at least three and stays outside these classes. Indeed, gains increase degree, conversions increase core degree, and both quantities are nondecreasing. Write $`o=c-b-\sum_i m_i`$ for their number and $`B_0=b/2+\sum_i m_i/(i+1)`$ for the known credit.

- **Upper table.** Here $`K_0=1`$. If the creator belongs to the remaining class at the focal vertex's birth, record $`P=\max(3,\min(D_{\rm creator},20))`$ at that time. Otherwise use $`P=3`$, including at the initial centre. The credit from the remaining class lies between zero and $`o/3`$. If $`P>3`$, improve its upper endpoint to $`(o-1)/3+1/P`$ by degree monotonicity. [State definitions and verifier](../verify/baseline/certs6.py) · [Upper table construction and check](../verify/baseline/find_upper_lin.py)
- **Lower table.** Here $`K_0=2`$. Of the remaining neighbours, record the numbers $`n_d`$ of exact degree $`d=3,4,5`$. Their exact credit is $`\sum_d n_d/d`$. The other $`o-\sum_d n_d`$ neighbours have degree at least six, so each contributes between zero and one sixth. [State definitions and verifier](../verify/baseline/certs9.py)

In either table $`u`$ gains a leaf at rate $`L+\rho`$, where $`\rho`$ is its total credit. It converts a leaf at rate $`L/D`$, changing $`(L,c)`$ to $`(L-1,c+1)`$ and adding a class-one pendant. The newborn sees its creator's updated state. Its creator has the same degree as before conversion. A pendant of class $`i`$ gains a leaf at rate $`i+1/D`$ and converts at rate $`i/(i+1)`$. The gain raises its pendant class by one until it leaves the recorded range. A conversion gives class B for $`i=1`$ and the remaining class otherwise. A class-B neighbour gains at rate $`1/D+1/D_x`$, where $`x`$ is its other core neighbour, and then has degree three in the remaining class. In the lower table, a remaining neighbour of recorded degree $`d`$ moves to the next degree on a gain, whose rate lies between zero and $`d`$. Conversion there preserves its recorded degree. These exhaust the changes visible at $`u`$. The total potential changes by the sum of these old-state increments and one new weight for each conversion. Each new weight is charged to the unique creator.

Both tables freeze the leaf count at 60. With more than ten core neighbours they return the constant zero (lower) or a positive constant (upper). In the first tail, a gain at the centre has zero weight increment. The endpoints of the intervals for $`L/D`$ and $`1/D`$ bound the remaining terms separately. In the constant region only the newborn term survives. At a finite box state, each unknown rate multiplies a known signed weight difference, so the two endpoints give its extreme contributions. The rational verifiers compare the resulting bounds with $`\delta f`$ or $`\lambda f`$; summing over vertices and iterating from the unique core of the three-vertex tree gives the two mean bounds. For the upper table every weight is at least one, hence the total weight bounds the core count from above. For the lower table the total weight is at most $`(\sup f) C_n`$, so dividing its mean lower bound by $`\sup f`$ gives a lower bound on the mean core count. These are the rows used in the almost-sure and polynomial-scale arguments below.

## Hub bounds

1. **Closed — rates and ownership.** A receiver $`u`$ gets one unit of rate per leaf neighbor and $`1/D_w`$ per core neighbor $`w`$. A leaf of $`u`$ receives at total rate $`q=L/D`$. Its promotion preserves $`D_u`$, replaces one leaf by one core neighbor, and creates exactly one weight to charge to $`u`$. At size three the star center has $`c=0`$ and the empty multiset. [Proof](bounds-hub.md)
2. **Closed — finite-state coverage.** Across a conversion $`c`$ increases, and both observation cutoffs and core-degree caps can only decrease. An old exact label therefore projects to an exact or pooled label, and an old lower degree mark still bounds the true degree. Keeping one greatest pooled mark and resetting the others only weakens the information. The sorted bag enumeration includes every such multiset. Extra physically impossible bags strengthen the test.
3. **Closed — neighbor and witness rates.** For a neighbor of exact type $`(d,k)`$ the gain rate is $`d-k+1/D`$ plus at most $`(k-1)/2`$. With a saturated core-degree label, the true $`j\ge k`$ makes $`d-j+(j-1)/2`$ decreasing in $`j`$, while $`1/D`$ remains a lower bound. A $`(2,2)`$ neighbor had a unique $`(2,1)`$ predecessor just before its last conversion. The new and old pendant sides see each other as witnesses. The stored pre-conversion bag distinguishes every needed witness of degree below six. The known middle neighbor supplies $`1/2`$ to the witness gain rate.
4. **Closed — global drift.** Each old summand charges its own response to central, neighbor and witness events. The sum counts simultaneous changes to distinct summands by linearity. A gain at an unobserved pooled neighbor leaves the mark valid. Sign-dependent rate extrema are valid separately even when several extrema cannot occur on the same tree.
5. **Closed — infinite tail and seam.** A gain changes $`q`$ by $`(1-q)/(D+1)`$ and a conversion by $`-1/D`$. Thus $`-q/D\le Gq\le1-q`$. With newborn weight $`s`$, the lower drift of $`Bq`$ exceeds or equals $`q(s-B/D)`$; the upper drift of $`A(1+\lambda q)`$ is bounded by $`A\lambda(1-q)+qs`$. The respective coefficient checks are $`B(\delta+1/H)\le s`$ and $`s\le A\lambda(1+\lambda)`$. The last finite gain uses the tail at $`D=H`$ and conversions at $`H-1`$ remain finite.
6. **Closed — finite verification and normalization.** The final pass in `certify` uses actual newborn weights, checks positivity, and bounds drift and right side with opposite directed rounding. Iterating from the unique center at size three gives root weight divided by lower supremum or upper infimum. The two rational prefactors are $`15493968692/17611447440`$ and $`15349885606/810439425`$. These yield the mean exponents 0.560 and 0.570. The positive lower weight on the specified low-core-degree subset also includes the analytic tail because $`q\ge(H-2)/H`$ on that subset. ## Transfer bounds

1. **Closed — selection of a distance-two witness.** A sole neighbor first enters the tracked range at initialization or by promotion from core degree one. A gain cannot decrease degree or increase core degree. At birth the creator had one to three old core neighbors. Its cutoffs preserve the exact degree and core degree of any selected witness with degree below six, including degree five at cap five. Ties between equivalent labels can be resolved using a fixed vertex order. [Proof](bounds-transfer.md)
2. **Closed — updates while tracked.** A neighbor gain preserves the selected witness until the degree bound is exceeded. A neighbor conversion supplies a new $`(2,1)`$ witness. The neighbor gain splits into its leaves, $`1/D_u`$, $`1/e`$ for the witness, and at most $`(k-2)/2`$ for the others. The witness gain has the known $`1/d_w`$ from the middle vertex. These are exhaustive because the unique path in a tree fixes the other side.
3. **Closed — exact cancellation before maximization.** For each physical oriented edge with source $`w`$ and observing row $`u`$, subtract $`r_wg(w,u)`$ at $`u`$ and add precisely $`r_wg(w,u)`$ at $`w`$. Both lookups resolve the same exact endpoint types by the four strict cutoff/cap tests. Repeated labels occur with their multiplicities on both sides. A saturated endpoint cannot hide a supported pair. Enumeration gives 6,445 eligible ordered pairs. The rate extrema must be taken after adding all transfers into the central coefficient.
4. **Closed — tails and finite checks.** Transfers vanish on edges incident to $`D\ge208`$, so the hub tail proof applies without a new tail estimate. The finite verifier checks the shared dictionary, sign-directed bounds, each newborn and both global normalizations. Iterating gives the stated prefactors $`15430450607/17611596558`$ and $`15361771189/640204837`$ at 0.563 and 0.568. [Integer verifier](../verify/transfer-bounds/transfer.cpp)
5. **Closed — obstruction at the initial tree.** At $`T_3`$ gain rate is two and conversion rate is one. There are zero core edges. For these upper weights, $`30(8723588643)-17(15361771189)=557549077>0`$ in grid units. This rules out these particular weights at $`17/30`$. ## Almost-sure lower transfer

1. **Closed — recurring conversions (Lemma 1).** For $`n\ge\max(3,M^2+M)`$ and $`C_n\le M`$, pigeonhole gives $`L\ge M`$ at some core with $`c\le M-1`$. Its conversion has probability at least $`1/(2n)`$. Conditional Borel–Cantelli applied to the next-step indicators contradicts a bounded nondecreasing $`C_n`$. A countable union covers all integer $`M`$. [Proof](almost-sure-lower.md) · [Durrett, Theorem 4.3.4](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf#page=233)
2. **Closed — unbounded potential (Lemma 2).** The integrable submartingale has compensator increments at least $`\delta\Phi_n/n`$ and centered martingale increments bounded by $`2B`$. On a bounded-potential path the martingale is bounded above, hence converges finitely by the bounded-increment dichotomy. Its compensator is then bounded and increasing, so the potential has a finite limit. The summability of $`\Phi_n/n`$ forces that limit to zero. Infinitely many newborn contributions of at least $`\eta`$ contradict it. [Durrett, Theorem 4.3.1](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf#page=232)
3. **Closed — reciprocal inequality (Lemma 3).** On $`\Phi\ge2B`$, $`\Phi+\Delta\ge\Phi/2`$. The reciprocal remainder is bounded by $`2\Delta^2/\Phi^3`$. Use $`\Delta^2\le B(\Delta+2\Delta^-)`$; the coefficient on the positive drift bound has the required negative sign because $`1-2B/\Phi\ge0`$. A negative jump must change some old positive summand. There are at most $`\Phi/f_{\min}`$ candidates, giving $`E_n\Delta^-\le BR\Phi/(nf_{\min})`$ by (H5) without independence. These terms give exactly $`2B\delta+4B^2R/f_{\min}`$.
4. **Closed — stopping (Theorem B).** The first crossing $`\sigma`$ above $`K_1`$ is finite almost surely. At first descent below $`K_0`$, the overshoot is bounded by $`B`$ and the stopped denominator stays positive. At each fixed number of steps past $`\sigma`$, the reciprocal process is integrably bounded. Splitting according to $`\sigma+k=n`$ gives the conditional supermartingale property at random start. Its maximal inequality gives return probability at most $`K_0/K_1`$. On no return, convergence and $`p_n\le(\sigma/n)^{\delta-\varepsilon}`$ yield polynomial growth. Taking $`K_1\to\infty`$ for the same liminf event and then a countable $`\varepsilon\downarrow0`$ gives the claim.
5. **Closed — application to the 0.543 table (Theorem A).** Finite nonnegative values give a positive minimum on their support. The lower verifier uses tracked neighbor degrees below six, flat own weight above 60 leaves and zero weight beyond core degree ten. A gain changes at most six old entries and a conversion at most two. All seven newborn classes have positive weight. For an old positive entry the possible changes have total rate at most $`60+10/2+1+10(5+1)=126`$. These facts give the five required hypotheses, including the two local jump hypotheses beyond the mean drift. ## Cross-charging

1. **Closed — states and events.** TOP is absorbing because degree and core degree are nondecreasing. Only the creator and its old detailed neighbors can change an old summand at a promotion. Charge the new summand once. At $`c=0`$ omit class-dependent charges, at $`L=0`$ omit the conversion row, and omit the TOP credit when the bag contains no TOP copy. These explicit conventions close the undefined lookups noted in the earlier reviews. [Proof](cross-charging.md)
2. **Closed — two exact cancellations (Lemma 1).** Every oriented edge from a TOP giver $`w`$ contributes the same $`\kappa(\mathrm{cls}\,u)/D_w`$ with opposite signs in giver and receiver rows. For $`w`$ of class $`a`$ with at least two core neighbors, sum over ordered *distinct* neighbors $`z,u`$ of $`w`$. Each term $`\nu(a,\mathrm{cls}\,u)/D_z`$ is subtracted at $`u`$ and added at $`w`$. Removing one bag element excludes precisely $`z`$, including when classes coincide. TOP recipients contribute zero to the latter identity by definition.
3. **Closed — signed intervals.** TOP credits lie in $`[0,n_{\rm top}/d_T]`$. The other $`k-1`$ neighbors of each exact class provide aggregate credit at most $`m_a(k-1)/2`$. For a coarse upper row the two credit bounds take the form of a convex combination over the possible TOP fraction. Its maximum is bounded by the largest of zero, $`(c/2)\Delta f`$ and $`(c/d_T)(\Delta f-\kappa(\mathrm{TOP}))`$. The lower row uses $`\kappa(\mathrm{TOP})=0`$ and nonnegative payments. These relaxations preserve the direction of the bounds.
4. **Closed — all unbounded states.** Above the leaf cutoff the own gain difference vanishes, old and post-conversion weights use the same flat extension, and the newborn sees TOP. Uncertain-rate extrema then have constant coefficients. The remaining $`L/D=1-c/D`$ and $`1/D`$ terms are affine in $`1/D`$, so its two endpoints suffice. Beyond the core-degree cutoff the upper newborn/payment terms are a convex combination bounded by $`\lambda G`$ and the negative TOP term can be dropped. In lower mode $`G=0`$ and both remaining contributions are nonnegative.
5. **Closed — finite tables and mean products (Lemmas 2–3).** Dyadic inputs converted to rational values and clipped to the prescribed signs are the actual tables checked. Summing the row bounds, then iterating from $`T_3`$, gives the upper and lower products. A nonzero lower table is required for division by its supremum. Here the root and all newborn values are positive. The checker rejects both stronger-exponent controls at their first row. [Scheme](../verify/cross-charging/xcharge.py) · [Exact checker](../verify/cross-charging/xverify.py)
6. **Closed — almost-sure 0.55.** For the lower table, TOP changes no old neighbor descriptor. At most $`D_{\rm cap}`$ old entries change and there is at most one newborn. A positive summand has $`c\le c_{\max}`$, so finitely many neighboring low-degree gains and conversions can affect it at bounded scaled rate. This verifies (H3), (H5) and, with minimum newborn weight one, (H4) of the preceding theorem. ## Cumulative mean degree tail

1. **Closed — degree moments and the cohort upper bound (Lemma 1, D(a)).** The hit probability at degree $`d`$ is at most $`d/j`$. The hit increment of $`d(d+1)`$ is $`2(d+1)`$, giving the telescoping one-step multipliers $`(j+1)/j`$ and $`(j+2)/j`$. Partition vertices into old cores and unique promotion cohorts. On promotion, the promoted label is known at time $`m+1`$ and has degree two. Markov's bound conditional on that history yields $`2n/((m+1)k)`$ times the expected cohort size $`a_{m+1}-a_m`$. [Proof](degree-tail.md)
2. **Closed — take-off (Lemma 2).** The conditional drift of $`L-1`$ is at least $`(L-1)/j`$, valid even if $`L=0`$. Moment iteration and $`c\le2`$ at start give the stated first and second moment bounds. Conditional Paley–Zygmund at half the conditional mean gives $`(l-1)^2/[4(l+2)(l+3)]`$. Its two positive ratios increase in $`l`$, so its minimum on $`l\ge3`$ is $`1/30`$.
3. **Closed — a positive fraction of eligible cores (Lemma 3).** The core tree has $`C-1`$ edges when $`C\ge2`$. Each isolated leafless degree-two core consumes two distinct edges and no two of them share an edge. Thus $`2I\le C-1`$. The degree sum gives at least two more tips than branching vertices. Adding the resulting two lower bounds gives $`E\ge(C+5)/4`$ for $`C\ge2`$; $`C=1`$ is direct.
4. **Closed — activation (Lemma 4).** Use birth-order labels so that future uniform choosers are independent on deterministic label sets. Conditioning on avoidance of $`u`$ preserves product structure. It costs $`(m-1)/(8m-1)\ge2/23`$. A fixed leaf hit in both disjoint blocks has conditional probability at least $`(2/3)(5/8)`$. For the leafless pair, avoiding $`z`$ and hitting $`w`$ costs $`m(m-2)/[(2m-2)(2m-3)]\ge1/4`$. The first such hit chooses $`u`$ with probability $`1/2`$. Conditional on the past at $`2m`$, the new leaf's random label is fixed, and each of the remaining two blocks hits it with probability at least $`1/2`$. The uniform bound is $`1/368`$.
5. **Closed — D(b).** At time $`8m'`$ at least three leaves suffice for the take-off threshold to reach $`n/(8m')\ge k`$. Condition successively and sum over eligible starting vertices to get $`a_{m'}/(4\cdot368\cdot30)=a_{m'}/44160`$. This summation needs no independence across eligible vertices.
6. **Historical gap, closed — D(c) at $`k=2`$.** In the old Abel-summation identity the empty sum at $`m_0=n`$ was equated to $`a_n/[n(n+1)]>0`$. The present proof separates this case, for which D(a) gives $`E[N_2(n)]=a_n`$ directly. In the interior the negative endpoint is $`-a_{m_0}/(m_0+1)`$. Bounding the positive tail by an integral gives $`m_0^{\lambda-1}/(1-\lambda)`$. The rounding $`\lfloor n/(8k)\rfloor\ge n/(12k)`$ uses $`n/k\ge24`$. [First review and re-check](../reviews/review-degree-tail-gpt.md) · [Second review](../reviews/review-degree-tail-gemini.md)
7. **Closed — D(d) and corollary.** Take $`0<\varepsilon<\min(\mu,1-\mu)`$ and enlarge the two constants to cover finitely many small $`m`$. The resulting bounds depend on $`n,k`$ only through $`n/k`$, yielding the uniform logarithmic limit. Gautschi's lower multiplier at $`n=3`$ is bounded below by $`(3/4)^{0.45}`$ for all $`n\ge3`$; $`0.5022(3/4)^{0.45}>0.44`$ and $`3.974(2+1/0.42)3^{0.58}<33`$.
8. **Open — stronger tail statements.** The argument controls the cumulative mean on logarithmic scale. A constant-factor equivalence, a pointwise $`k^{-(1+\mu)}`$ degree density, and a uniform almost-sure limit for growing $`k`$ require additional input.

## Polynomial-scale lower bound

1. **Closed — which drift can be restricted.** Equation (1) is a per-vertex inequality using full-tree states, with the newborn credited to its actual creator. This is stronger than a summed whole-tree inequality. The baseline lower table tracks degrees and the upper table has a static creator label. A freely transferred whole-tree drift would require a boundary term in a branch. [Proof](polynomial-scale-lower.md)
2. **Closed — jumps and squared increments, (2)–(3).** Swap the edge sum to get $`\sum\rho_u=\sum c_u/D_u\le C_n`$. A saturated high-degree vertex cannot change an old neighbor descriptor. In the radius-one setting a step changes at most $`H+1`$ summands and a gain at $`L\ge H`$ has zero effect. Summing the probabilities of relevant gains and promotions yields the claimed conditional second moment $`O(C_n/n)`$.
3. **Closed — restriction at a saturated parent, (4)–(6).** The restriction uses the original parent, birth labels and rates. Every inside birth has an inside creator. Exterior gains and promotions leave all inside descriptors unchanged because the sole boundary parent is permanently saturated. The true $`1/D_p`$ still enters the founder's gain rate. The internal double sum plus this single boundary term is at most $`N_n+1\le2N_n`$, giving both branch second-moment bounds.
4. **Closed — normalization and convergence, (7)–(11).** Summable logarithmic product errors give uniform product bounds and a positive fixed-start product limit. Upper drift gives $`E_m N_j\le UN_mP_\lambda(m,j)`$. Divide the centered lower increment by $`P_\delta(m,j+1)`$. Its total conditional variance is bounded by a constant times $`N_m\sum_{j\ge m}(j/m)^{-(2\delta-\lambda)}/j`$. This is finite exactly in the asserted positive-gap regime for this estimate. Conditional $`L^2`$ martingale convergence and monotonicity of the predictable part give an extended nonnegative limit. A finite second moment for the centered component does not bound the full normalized lower potential uniformly when $`\lambda>\delta`$.
5. **Closed — positive survival on a bounded-size branch, (12).** $`J=X_m+Z_\infty`$ may be negative. Truncate on $`J>X_m/2`$ and apply conditional Cauchy–Schwarz to that event. With $`X_m\ge a_*`$ and $`N_m\le k_*`$ this gives a history-independent positive lower bound. On success the nonnegative compensator preserves the lower bound for the normalized limit.
6. **Closed — positive pattern.** The expected number of depth-one cores is at most a harmonic sum because only a root promotion can add one. Positive expected lower weight growing like $`n^\delta`$ therefore forces a reachable positive-weight vertex at depth at least two. Grafting its finite receiver history inside a new branch changes the copy of the root by one exterior core edge. Every degree, core degree and creator's birth datum seen by that deep vertex agrees. Starting from the founder and its first leaf, one additional leaf recreates the three-vertex pattern.
7. **Closed — uniform waiting and replay, (13).** Until a gain or promotion at a chosen leaf's parent, the target class has rate at least one and a gain has conditional fraction at least $`1/2`$. Up to $`H`$ gains give a saturated parent retaining a leaf. Until promotion, $`L/D`$ cannot decrease and the harmonic wait ends almost surely. At branch size $`k`$, each prescribed receiver has an inside neighbor of degree at most $`k`$, while the total rate of inside receivers is at most $`k+1`$. The finite product of first-event success probabilities is positive uniformly in the exterior. Repeated promotions with $`R`$ initial leaves likewise need no independence.
8. **Closed — probability one and conclusion, (14).** Each starting history has the same strictly positive lower bound on the conditional probability of the common measurable event $`E`$. Conditioning on the increasing histories and taking the limit yields $`1_E\ge p_0`$ almost surely. Equivalently use the algebra approximation in the text. Whole-tree convergence and positivity yield $`\Phi_n^-/n^{0.543}\to L\in(0,\infty]`$ and the asserted liminf for $`C_n`$. [Earlier Opus audit](../reviews/review-polynomial-scale-lower-claude.md)
9. **Closed with the stated blocking hypothesis — radius-$`R`$ side remark.** The old reviewer objected that an interior vertex could see beyond a saturated exterior parent. The current statement requires that such a vertex block dependence on everything behind it. One must also replace the count $`H+1`$ by a constant depending on $`H,R`$: tracing influence through at most $`R`$ unsaturated intermediate vertices gives a finite geometric sum of powers of $`H`$. The promotion probability into depth $`d+1`$ is at most the number of cores at depth $`d`$ divided by $`n`$, giving $`O((1+\log n)^R)`$ by induction. A pattern observed entirely within distance $`R`$ is preserved at depth at least $`R+1`$. The numerical theorem uses radius one.
10. **Open — sharper normalization.** This proof allows $`L=+\infty`$. It supplies neither the finiteness of that limit nor convergence of $`C_n/n^\mu`$ to a positive finite random variable.
