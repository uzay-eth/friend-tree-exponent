# The sample-path exponent equals the exponent of the mean

Use the notation of [the mean-exponent proof](existence.md). We show that $`\log C_n/\log n\to\mu`$ almost surely. The lower estimate uses independent copies of one finite experiment.

## Disjoint sets of clocks

At any time with at least two core vertices, let $`A`$ count the core vertices with $`c\le2`$ and $`L\ge1`$. The other core vertices are the bare degree-two vertices B and the vertices with $`c\ge3`$. The latter number at most $`A`$ by the core-tree degree sum. Each maximal path of consecutive B vertices lies between two non-B vertices. Suppressing these paths gives a tree on the non-B vertices, so there are at most $`2A-1`$ such paths. Partition each path into disjoint triples, leaving at most two B vertices per path. If $`J`$ triples were obtained, then $`C\le 6A+3J`$. Use one site at each of the $`A`$ vertices and one at an end $`u`$ of each triple $`(u,w,z)`$ of B vertices. There are at least $`C/6`$ sites. A one-core tree supplies its centre as a site.

For an $`A`$ site reserve the clock of $`u`$ and one of its leaves. For a triple reserve the clocks of $`u,w,z`$. These sets are disjoint. The following coupling of the plantings is useful. All conditional constructions start at the same deterministic time given the entire current tree.

* In the first unit use the disjoint reserved clocks. Require silence of $`u`$ at each $`A`$ site. At a triple require silence of $`u,z`$ and that the first ring of $`w`$ occurs and chooses $`u`$ (put $`u`$ first, and request a mark below $`1/2`$). Before that ring the only two vertices which could change the degree of $`w`$ are silent. Tag its newborn leaf at $`u`$. Thus the first-stage success flags are independent with probabilities bounded below by a fixed positive number. Thin the larger probability by an independent coin if necessary.
* In the second unit use only silence of $`u`$ and at least $`M`$ rings of its tagged leaf. The tagged leaves at successful sites are distinct. Given the history, their future clocks are independent standard clocks. At a successful site $`L_u\ge M`$ and $`c_u\le2`$.
* In the third unit require exactly one ring of $`u`$ and a mark below $`M/(M+2)`$, ranking leaves first. This always promotes some leaf $`v`$ at $`u`$. Outside arrivals may change its identity but cannot invalidate promotion. Only the clock of $`u`$ could have promoted a leaf at that site; the choices of $`v`$ at distinct sites are distinct. Require that the internal clocks of the two-vertex branch stay silent to the end of the unit. Given the promotion time $`\tau`$ within this unit that probability is $`e^{-2(1-\tau)}`$. It can be thinned to $`e^{-2}`$ independently of $`\tau`$. After promotion the other sites' reserved clocks are all outside this branch.
* In the next $`k-2`$ units require the old $`u`$ clock silent and execute the prescribed growth of the rooted $`k`$-vertex seed as in the mean-exponent proof. Given the silent $`u`$, the probability of this script depends only on the prescribed rooted tree. All scripts use disjoint interiors. Taking a minimum over the finite choices of rooted seeds and thinning yields a common probability $`p=p(M,k)>0`$ for the entire preparation, independent of the initial tree.

For clarity, these lower trials can be generated by assigning independent randomness to each reserved set and to each new internal clock as it becomes needed. At the third-stage ring the chosen leaf may depend on the other histories, but its clock after that selection and the fresh child's clock can be sampled at selection, with independent increments. Equivalently use Poisson memorylessness at the ring. The site never uses a leaf belonging to a different site. Unreserved choices may evolve according to the original rule. Stages with a larger success probability can be thinned by independent uniforms, so the retained success flags have the product Bernoulli law. Once a branch is successfully prepared, only the edge to $`u`$ permits an outside source to affect it.

## Independent contributions in a block

Fix $`M,\epsilon,k`$ as in the mean-exponent proof and choose a large, fixed $`s`$. Choose $`(T,v)`$ there to maximise the good reference contribution. At each prepared site take a fresh independent reference history and independent acceptance marks at $`v`$. For the exterior test use the original clock of its own $`u`$ for the next $`s`$ units and forbid marks below $`1/M`$, putting $`v`$ first. These tests use distinct clocks at different sites. On the retained event that the reference history is good and assigned to $`v`$, the exterior test succeeds, and all the internal acceptance marks succeed, use its reference core count as its contribution. Otherwise use zero. The conditional law can thus supply one independent copy of a nonnegative integrable variable $`Y_s`$ at every site, with
```
E[Y_s] >= (p/k) exp(-eta s) [F(s)-k-O(exp(-s))].
```
Each contribution counts actual cores in its branch. A failing trial can affect neighbouring old vertices through the exterior. Any such arrivals during preparation are allowed by the clock requirements, and during the comparison a successful branch has no interaction across its cut edge. Thus the sum of the independent lower contributions is at most the actual core count at the block endpoint.

Take $`R_s<\infty`$ such that $`\mathbb E\min(Y_s,R_s)\ge \mathbb E Y_s/2`$, for $`s`$ large with positive right-hand side. From $`C`$ old cores there are at least $`C/6`$ trials. The conditional probability of obtaining fewer than $`C\mathbb E Y_s/24`$ cores at the endpoint tends to zero as $`C\to\infty`$, by Chebyshev for that many independent truncated variables. This conclusion concerns the original endpoint counts and therefore holds in their own filtration.

The core count tends to infinity almost surely. After the first birth its total promotion rate is always at least one; at size $`n`$ the conditional chance of a promotion in one discrete step is at least $`1/n`$. Conditional Borel–Cantelli gives infinitely many promotions. The Yule process visits every finite size and has no finite explosion.

On the grid of spacing $`h+s`$, let $`I_j`$ indicate failure to multiply by at least $`\mathbb E Y_s/24`$ in a block. Its conditional expectation tends to zero almost surely by the preceding paragraph. The fraction of failures tends to zero almost surely: the centred indicators are bounded martingale differences, so their averages tend to zero (apply martingale convergence to their sum with weights $`1/j`$, then Kronecker's lemma). By monotonicity a failed block costs no core vertices. For any chosen $`s`$ with $`\mathbb E Y_s/24>1`$, this proves
```
liminf log C(t)/t >= log(E[Y_s]/24)/(h+s)  almost surely.
```
For fixed small $`\eta<\mu`$, take a deterministic sequence $`s\to\infty`$, then a countable choice of parameters with $`\eta\downarrow0`$. The existence and positivity of $`\mu`$ give the lower bound $`\mu`$. (If $`\mu=0`$, the lower bound is automatic.) For the upper bound use Markov's inequality at integer times with $`F(j)=e^{(\mu+o(1))j}`$ and Borel–Cantelli, then monotonicity. Thus $`\log C(t)/t\to\mu`$ almost surely.

The time to reach size $`n`$ is the sum of independent exponentials of means $`1/j`$ for $`2\le j<n`$. Their centred sum converges almost surely since the variances sum to a finite number. The hitting time divided by $`\log n`$ tends to one, giving the claimed discrete-size limit.
